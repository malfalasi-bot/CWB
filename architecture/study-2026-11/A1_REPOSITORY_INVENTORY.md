# A1 Repository inventory: how content is stored, structured and served

Study: architecture/study-2026-11. Date: 22 November 2026. Scope: the whole repository at commit `e67a3e8` ("Links: Repertory page"), 81 commits, 1,579 files. F1 Histories of making is the only module with content. All counts are measured from the files, not taken from READMEs; the method is in §6 and the appendix.

## 0. Summary

The repository holds about 1.6 million words of Markdown and about 0.7 million words in CSV registers. Almost all of it is research, planning and specification. The learner-facing text that exists is small: one ballet page (1,216 words, the only file under content versioning), the Edo unit (71 beats, 4,083 words, plus 19 cast stories of 2,657 words), one F1.13 lecture (9 stations, about 2,000 words of narration) and 28 grouping-page files (27 current) that mix learner text with editor apparatus.

The Atlas data model was designed in Deep Dives 1 §3 (decision Q2) as an eight-part node card: identity, six readings, four text tiers, typed relations, media, rights, learning hooks and upkeep. What was built is a 22-column flat CSV row (`schema/canon-node.schema.json`) whose only prose field, `making_significance`, is "one or two sentences" (median 17 words, maximum 60). There is no field for the four text tiers. There is no relation type, media record or version. Nothing holds a lecture, a chapter or a unit. Everything bigger than a row lives outside the Atlas. Each surface keeps it in its own format: the Edo unit's YAML with a zod schema, the lecture's JSON, the ballet topic's Markdown dossiers and its works register, the grouping pages' Markdown and the specs' Markdown tables. The Atlas hub reaches these through name matching and hard-coded links. The hub ships 1,218 marks. About 3,900 of the 4,666 canon rows (83 per cent) are not in its data at all, because only place-anchored kinds can be drawn on the globe.

The owner's complaint is accurate, and the cause is structural. The Atlas is built as a map of short records. Long-form knowledge has no type there, so it accumulates in folders that the Atlas cannot see. Section 3 ranks the concrete cases.

## 3. Where the structure limits content

Ranked by how much each case blocks the owner's requirement: an Atlas that can hold and serve all the information a subject needs, from a card to a full lecture or unit, without forcing it onto a map.

### L1. The Atlas record has no long-form or document type (blocks everything above a card)

- `schema/canon-node.schema.json` has 22 properties. The only explanatory prose field is `making_significance`, described as "Why it matters for making, one or two sentences". Measured over 4,666 rows: median 17 words, maximum 60 (COM133). The other text fields are short: `leaves_out` (median 7 words), `notes` (median 6), `defined_by` (median 3). `additionalProperties: false` forbids anything else.
- Deep Dives 1 §3 (`docs/04-deep-dives-1/index.md` lines 60–104), adopted as decision Q2, designed a different record. It has an identity block, "six readings", "text in four tiers" (Line ≤16, Label 50–60, Story 150–250, Deep 600–1,200), typed relations, media, rights and protocol, learning hooks, and upkeep ("checked on; version; who reviewed it"). It also gave per-type fields: an object's provenance as dated steps, a technique's operational sequence as 5–9 ordered steps, a route's stops. None of these has a column. The canon has no tier fields. There is no coordinate field (0 of 495 places have coordinates, as `research_notes/Ecosystem review/FINDINGS.md` notes), and no field for steps or provenance chains.
- `briefs/ATLAS_CONCEPT_UNIT_TEMPLATE_v2.md` §2 defines three layers: Record, Atlas cards and The unit. Only the Record layer has any store, and that store is fragmented (L6). No file anywhere holds an "Atlas card". `content_versions.py` lists `card` among its eight kinds, but 0 card files exist.
- Above the card there is nothing in the data model. A chapter (2–4 hours, template v2 §4.1), a walk, a lecture of 6–12 stations (`PAGE_TEMPLATES_v1.md` §0), a unit of 35–45 hours, a route over cards (Repertory plan §4.3) and a dossier entry are all files in folders. The Atlas cannot point to them by id, because none of them has one.

Consequence: whatever does not fit in 60 words is pushed out of the Atlas and into a surface-specific folder. The rest of this section follows from that.

### L2. Topic knowledge sits wholly outside the Atlas data (ballet, Edo)

- Ballet: 390,582 words of research, 156 works in `topics/ballet/research/repertory/register.yaml`, 155 dated events, 118 dossier entries, 18 debates, 38 myths, 65 designers. A grep finds **0 canon rows** that mention "ballet" or "Giselle", and **0 mentions** in the hub's `atlas.json` and `nodes.json`. - Edo: the unit's 41 people, 54 events, its places and its 105 sources live in `build/units/edo/content/*.yaml`. The hub joins them to the canon by exact name, hard-coded in `build_data.py` (tsutaya → PER205, hokusai → PER206, edo → POL183…). The Edo build scripts do not read `atlas/data` at all.
- The ratio of research to learner text in ballet is about 320 to 1 (390,582 research words; 1,216 learner words). This is not a fault in the research. The Repertory plan (`planning/REPERTORY_PLAN.md` §7) asks for about 85,000 learner words in about 340 cards, and the repository has no type to put them in except one Markdown file per card.

### L3. The map is the only way in, and only place-anchored kinds reach it

- `build/atlas-hub/README.md` and `build_data.py`: "Place-anchored subset only. Of 4,666 canon rows (25 kinds), only kinds place, maker community and institution (771 rows) become marks." Of those, 422 are "listed but never pinned" and 9 are dropped. `nodes.json` holds 1,218 marks (513 places, 327 objects at holders, 160 institutions, 111 route nodes, 107 maker communities).
- The other 22 kinds do not reach the hub's data at all: about 3,900 rows, including 609 people, 399 object types, 383 events, 247 techniques, 219 periods, 198 styles, 138 materials and 116 movements. Techniques and materials act only as "lenses", which are regex rules over row text (`atlas.json` lenses, for example "rows whose text names workers, wages, crews or unnamed hands").
- The list view is described as "primary, complete", but its hierarchy is the map's: worlds → units → stories → nodes. A learner cannot open a movement, a technique, a person or a work as a page in the hub. `docs/06-atlas-logic` ("What ships first") ships Place, Time, Connections, Story and Grouping pages together. The hub built Place (map and globe), with routes and a year setting layered on it. It has no Time surface of its own, and no grouping page can be reached from it.
- The 3D report (`reports/Atlas 3D map and game mechanics.md`, the attachment `bde74a8d-…`) chose this on purpose ("Only place-anchored kinds become marks… no 4,228-dot cloud"). That is right for a globe. It becomes a limit only because the globe is also the only entrance.

### L4. Relations are words in a notes column

- 1,442 canon rows mention 2,360 other canon ids, almost all as `links: BEL008; MKR012; STY083` inside `notes`. There is no edge type, direction, source or confidence for a relation, although the node card (Deep Dives 1 §3) requires "typed edges to other nodes, each with its own source and confidence". Atlas Logic ("The grouping families") also makes membership of a grouping "a relation with its own confidence".
- The hub's "rumour log" ("why this mark") is rebuilt at build time from text matches and the Edo name map. The `units` column (a semicolon list) is the only structured link between a node and the curriculum.
- Works to versions (ballet remake families), people to works, objects to holders through provenance steps, and claims to the sentences that rest on them have no shared relation model. In `build/units/ballet/build.py` the sentence-to-claim link is a dictionary of 40 phrases in Python ("no wood appears" → `bal-box-materials`). Change a phrase in the page and the link breaks without an error.

### L5. Fixed word tiers and per-surface length rules, with no tier above Deep

- The four tiers are fixed in three places: the voice guide's surface table, Deep Dives 1 §3 and template v2 §2. They are the right reading lengths for a card. The trouble is that they are the whole scale the Atlas has. The largest unit of text the Atlas design allows is Deep at 1,200 words. A ballet dossier entry runs about 2,000 words (D2 is 24,498 words for about ten works under 13 headings). A grouping page runs 6,000–9,000 words. A chapter runs 2–4 hours.
- Every surface has its own length rule, each enforced or stated separately. Edo beats are 40–100 words (zod lint in `build-content.mjs`). Edo cast stories are 120–220 words (`people.yaml` header), against the voice guide's Story at 150–250. A walk step is 60–120 words. A lecture station is 100–200 words or 60–90 seconds. Template v3 sets a concept passage "as the spec sets". So the same person is written to three different lengths in three places (canon `PER206` at 60 words or fewer, Edo cast story, Atlas Story tier).
- Rings make depth depend on curation status. Ring 1 gets all tiers, Ring 2 a line and label, Ring 3 a link (Deep Dives 1 §3; `PAGE_TEMPLATES_v1.md` §0). The canon `tier` column (R1/R2/R3) means "walk / card with member grid / card with link". The column decides presentation, not content. A subject with a large research body but no R1 flag has nowhere to put it.

### L7. Each surface invents its own content model

- Edo: zod types for `Beat`, `Segment`, `Act`, `Guess` (seven discriminated variants), `Argued`, `Person`, `Tile`, `Rebuild` and `Overture` (exactly nine shots).
- Lecture: JSON with `stations[]` (`id, n, title, idea, seconds, narration`). Claims appear as `[2]`, mapped to `F1.13-C02` by convention.
- F1.13: `lectures.yaml`, `beats.yaml` and `scenes.yaml` were derived by hand from the lecture JSON, a second shape for the same lecture.
- Ballet: a Python script that turns Markdown into HTML with regex and one hand-drawn SVG.
- Grouping pages: bold-label blocks in Markdown, 15–17 per page, in the order of `PAGE_TEMPLATES_v1.md` §0.
- Specs: a Markdown table parsed by regex (`parse_specs()` in `build_data.py`). The hub hard-codes every unit's status as "in development".
- The skill's scene schema (`references/scenes.md`) is another. The Composer's `model.json` is another.
- The Edo redesign report predicted that per-unit effort falls "to 40–65 hours per unit once the schema, scene registry and pipeline exist". The pipeline that exists belongs to Edo alone. Ballet, F1.13 and the grouping pages do not use it.

### L8. Timelines, eras and events have no data type

- Timelines exist in five shapes. Edo `timeline.yaml` has 5 lanes, 4 censor bands, a price series, 54 events. Ballet `timeline.json` has 11 eras, 6 work lanes, 5 event lanes, 155 events and remake families. The F1.13 lecture's `Timeline.tsx` uses bands B01–B11. The register schema's `kind: date | span | number` with `display` and `alternates` (in the ballet and F1.13 YAML) is the closest thing to a shared event value, and only two files use it.

### L9. Versioning covers one file

- `content_versions.py` discovers only `modules/*/topics/*/learner/**/*.md`, and `check` reports "1 content file(s)". Everything else a learner reads or will read has no record-level version: the Edo story (71 beats), the Edo people cards, the F1.13 lecture, 28 grouping-page files, 38 specs, canon rows and register rows. The ballet page's own superseded drafts survive three times (`history/0.1.0.md`, `0.2.0.md`; `planning/board/round1–3/_page-v1..v3.md`; `build/units/ballet/dist/`).
- Canon rows carry no `version`, `checked_on` or `reviewed_by`, though the card design's "Upkeep" part requires them. Changes are visible only as git diffs of 20 wide CSV files.
- The database has no history table and no version column. Its `loaded_at` records load time.

### L10. The database can hold only a snapshot of flat text

- Every column in the migration is `text not null`. The one exception is `schemas.content jsonb`. There are no foreign keys between tables, no array or relation columns (semicolon lists stay strings), no media or asset table, no tables for units, topics, unit registers, works, events or learner records, and no version history.
- `documents` stores whole files in one `content` column with a full-text index. That works for search but not for addressing a section, a card or a claim.
- The data is stale. The seed holds 4,228 canon nodes against 4,666 in the CSVs, 78 documents against 341 Markdown files now, and 270 files in the manifest against 1,579. By its own README, 54 of 85 seed files were never loaded. Row-level security with no policies means nothing can be served from it. In practice it is an unloaded mirror, not a content store.

### L11. The runtime caps what one surface can carry, and learner state has no home

- Each Artifact has its own origin and storage, a 255-file publish cap and a 16 MB per-file cap (`ARCH_V4.md`; 3D report §"What the Artifact runtime allows"). The Edo v3 dist held 237 files. The v4 target is 200 or fewer.
- The Repertory's roughly 340 cards as one file each would exceed one publish. Progress crosses between Artifacts only as `#ink.<unit>.<ids>` tokens (`build/units/shared/LINKS.md`).
- The Concept DNA and SRC schemas define the learner's record, but no surface writes it. Say-backs and closing contributions (template v3 table, template v2 §4.1) have no destination.
- The Composer keeps the 12 October decisions in its Artifact database (`LINKS.md`), and the live documents stay in Claude Docs. Some of the program's state is therefore outside git altogether.

### L12. Grouping pages are large, unconsumed and cannot write back

- 28 page files (about 180,000 words): 23 on the v1 templates of `PAGE_TEMPLATES_v1.md` (11 movements, 11 civilisations, 1 band) and 5 older round-2 pages, one of them superseded. Each holds a facts block, members, claims table, nodes, open objects, lecture storyboard and fact-check list. The template's best rule is "a page is written once at Ring 1; the card and the link-out are cut from its facts block, never written separately". No code does the cutting, and no surface reads the pages.
- The pages propose canon fixes in prose. For example, `civilisations/indus_valley.md` says "The canon's units field for CIV048 lists F1.3, F1.5 and F1.13 only: correction proposed". No route takes such a line back into the CSV.

## 5. What already works and must be kept

1. **Harvest discipline** (`atlas/harvest/`, `harvest.yml`, `backbone.yml`). Identifiers are never typed. Every harvest result arrives as a pull request a person accepts. There are offline tests on saved real responses (13 for `harvest.py`), per-host rate limits and a descriptive User-Agent. The Met provenance reader fails loudly rather than passing silently. Getty and Wikidata outages are recorded, not papered over.
2. **The licence switch** (`schema/licence-classes.json`, `atlas/tools/licence_class.py`). Six classes, one dataset and two builds. There is a recorded fallback for every PERMISSION-NC item and the per-record rule ("an old date or an anonymous maker proves nothing"). This should become a field on every media record of any new model.
3. **The 1970 test as structured fields** (`provenance_test`, `test_basis`, `history_gap`, `action` in the world set). It already models provenance as a decision with its basis.
4. **The claims register record shape** (`ballet.yaml`, F1.13 `claims.yaml`, `references/claims.md`). Records are keyed by the claim, not the year, with `kind`, `value`, `precision`, `calendar`, `alternates` with their own sources, `display`, `where_used`, `editor_note` and `rule`. Sources carry `kind`, `interest`, `date_accessed` and `archive_url`. There is a signed header with `spotcheck_sample`. This is the best-designed structure in the repository and the natural base for claims inside any long-form content.
5. **Content versioning** (`tools/content_versions.py`, CI job). It has a body hash, `derived_from` with git blob shas, history snapshots, stale-research warnings, semver rules (patch, minor, major by teaching effect) and tags `<content_id>@<version>`. The mechanism is sound. Its reach (one folder pattern, one file) is the limit.
6. **CI checks**: the schema is generated from `codes.py` and checked for sync, and canon rows are checked against id patterns and kinds. `validate_canon.py` covers banned phrases, coercion words without a human-flow flag, `leaves_out` required for civilisations, polities and movements, typed QIDs, and signed-year dates.
7. **The Edo content lint** (`build/units/edo/scripts/build-content.mjs`). A typed schema runs before the build, with reference resolution, a sentence-length rule and the "no two consecutive beats with the same instrument state" rule. It should be lifted out of Edo and run in CI.
8. **Two views from one source** (learner and editor, `WORKFLOW.md` standing rules, `build/units/ballet/build.py`). Build notes never reach the learner. The claim id on every factual sentence is kept for the editor.
9. **The topic folder** (`topics/<topic>/research`, `planning`, `learner`, with generated `MANIFEST.md` and `CONTENT_INDEX.md`). Learner text sits next to its research and stays traceable.
10. **The dossier headings and the Repertory card set** (`ATLAS_CONCEPT_UNIT_TEMPLATE_v2.md` §4.4). They give a de facto schema for a "work" with thirteen sections. These sections are what a long-form record type would need.
11. **"Written once at Ring 1, cut from the facts block"** (`PAGE_TEMPLATES_v1.md` §0), and "chapters never re-tell a card; cards never re-tell a chapter's argument" (template v2 §4.4). Both are single-source rules that a structured store could enforce.
12. **The hub's honesty rules**: no invented coordinates (422 rows listed, not pinned), objects placed at holders and never at an origin, approximate places drawn as blots, the list twin as the primary view, and the hand-off token the learner carries.
13. **The protocol and sensitivity vocabulary** (`x-sensitivity`, the human-flow flag, content notes, the AI reader outcomes CLEAR / REVISE / LINK-OUT / REMOVE). Any new type must carry these.

## Appendix: method

- File counts: `find . -type f -not -path './.git/*'`, grouped by path prefix.
- Words: Python `len(text.split())` over each text file, using the script in the session scratchpad (`count.py`). Binary files count as 0 words. Markdown-only figures come from `find … -name '*.md' | xargs cat | wc -w`. Whitespace counts overstate prose in CSV and YAML (keys and ids count as words). Where prose matters, the MD-only figure is given.
- Canon statistics: Python `csv.DictReader` over `canon_*.csv`. Field lengths are `len(value.split())`. Id mentions are a regex over the 26 prefixes followed by three digits, excluding the row's own id.
- Register and record counts: `csv.DictReader` row counts; YAML records counted by top-level key patterns (`^f13-`, `^  bal-`) or by loading with PyYAML (Edo story, people, timeline).
