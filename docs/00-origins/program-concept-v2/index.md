# Program Concept v2 — full depth, two lanes

2026-09-26 · @Mohammad AlFalasi

## The verdict on v0 and v1

The original syllabus (v0) has the right depth and the wrong delivery; the rebuild (v1) has the right delivery and the wrong depth; the fault in v1 was treating "accessible" as "shorter", when the real fix is to write once at full depth and let the learner choose how far in to go.

| Dimension | v0 (original syllabus) | v1 (rebuild) | Keep for v2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Depth and professional appeal | Postgraduate; studio-scale briefs; tools to fabrication level; venture realism | Entry level; 15-hour lenses; tools trimmed to a thread; venture to literacy | v0's depth, all of it, as the Mastery tier of every module |
| Audience | Designers only, implicitly advanced | Designers plus the people who brief and judge | v1's two lanes |
| Structure | Six tracks, duplicated tails, no outcomes or hours | Four levels, outcomes and hours, artifact-driven | v1's structure, holding v0's content |
| Practice areas | Four studios: art, space, object, body | Six lenses, adding identity and digital product | Six areas, each with a lens (Practice) and a studio (Mastery) |
| Tools | Eleven modules, each a career | One free-tools thread, five electives dropped | Core thread at Practice; the dropped electives restored at Mastery with free tools |
| Venture and release | Two tracks, 15 modules, near-verbatim duplication | Five short literacy modules | Five modules, Practice as literacy, Mastery as full simulations and a real handover package |
| Register | Manifesto adjectives, dense, memorable, exclusionary | Plain, sometimes flat | Precise professional vocabulary, no adjectives; the rule in §4 |
| Geography | Gulf, MENA, SWANA as core identity | Global first, region parked | Global spine with a regional slot in every unit; the regional vertical as the first expansion |
| Delivery | Assumes cohorts, live crits, kits, specialists, budget | Self-paced, AI partner, no budget, free sources | v1's delivery, with the AI partner taking the fabricator, client and stranger roles the studios needed |
| The artifact | Concept DNA plus anchor, family, dossier | Concept DNA, growing per unit, lane-specific additions | v1's growth model with v0's full dossier at Mastery |
| Assessment | Critical defence, dossier, live panel | Tests and drift scores, no grades | Both: tests at every depth, a defence at Mastery, still no grades |
| Method (Track 02) | The strongest part, complete | Kept, lightened, given a data structure | v0's completeness on v1's data structure |

**What was lost in v1 that mattered.** The studio-scale brief (a real site, a real object family, a capsule collection); the invisible systems taught to working depth (back of house, egress, BOM, tolerances, tech-packs); the tool electives; the sense that the program is for people who will actually build; and the regional specificity that made it unlike anything else.

**What v0 lacked that v1 supplied.** A learner who is not a designer; outcomes, hours and sequence; the artifact as the through-line; free sources; a delivery that works without a room, a budget or a faculty; and two practice areas the industry commissions most.

## The combining principle

Write every module once, at the depth of the original, and give it three depths of entry: Orientation (the lab, the vocabulary, one case: enough to see the idea), Practice (the professional content: enough to use and to brief it), and Mastery (the studio-scale brief, the tools, the defence: enough to build and be judged on it); the lanes do not change the content, they change the default depth and the Apply task.

| Depth | What it contains | Time share | Who it is for by default |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orientation | The provocation, the lab, the term named after the lab, one case, one short Apply | About 15% of a module | Anyone; the briefer's floor; a taster for makers |
| Practice | The full concept set, the world set of cases, the method, the make or brief Apply, the partner's Mirror and Stranger | About 45% | Briefers' default; makers' minimum |
| Mastery | The original's studio material: long-form briefs on a real site, object family, body or brand; tools electives; the invisible systems to working depth; the partner as Fabricator, Client and CFO; the defence | About 40% | Makers' default; briefers who commission at scale |

**Why this is not dumbing down.** The Mastery tier is the original curriculum, complete, in plainer language. Orientation is not a simpler version of it; it is the first fifteen minutes of it, designed so that a non-designer can start. The learner who stops at Orientation has met the real idea, not a cartoon of it.

**Lanes on top of depths**

|  | Make lane | Brief-and-judge lane |
|---|---|---|
| Default path | Practice through Foundations; Mastery through Method, one studio, the tools core and the defence | Orientation through Foundations; Practice through Method, one lens and the venture modules; Mastery for R3 (briefing and handover) |
| Apply task | Produce | Write the brief, or judge a supplied piece with the four tests |
| Artifact | DNA, anchor, adjacent translation, dossier | DNA, brief, judged set, commissioning record |
| Can they cross? | Yes; any learner may take any depth of any module |  |

**What keeps the professional appeal.** The studio briefs at Mastery are the same ones a design master's would set; the world sets are large and global; the tools go to fabrication, capture and analytics; the venture modules run real numbers; the vocabulary is the profession's own; and the regional vertical, when built, gives the program a position nobody else holds. What makes it accessible is only the entry ramp and the removal of adjectives.

## Vocabulary policy

Keep the profession's precise terms, because they are the professional appeal and a briefer needs them to talk to designers; drop the manifesto adjectives, because they add heat without meaning; and introduce every kept term by a lab before it is named.

| Keep (precise, professional) | Drop (adjectives and intensifiers) | Rename (obscure for its own sake) |
|---|---|---|
| typology, morphology, archetype, affordance, ergonomics, proxemics, tectonics, palimpsest, patina, semiotics, grammar, constant, variable, threshold, anti-DNA, anchor, hero, core, carrier, tech-pack, bill of materials, tolerance, egress, back of house, CapEx and OpEx, minimum viable implementation, handover, provenance, lifecycle, attribution | rigorous, uncompromising, brutal, lethal, absolute, obsessive, profound, bulletproof, hyper-, relentless, unglamorous, seductive (as praise), sublime (as praise) | "somatic truths" → the body as metric; "epistemology" in headings → how we know; "ontology" in headings → what an object is; "generative world systems" → method; "orbital architecture" stays but is defined in one line; "Modular" → Module |

**Rules**

1. A term is first met in a lab, then named, then defined in the glossary; it never appears in unit copy before its lab.
2. One term per idea; no synonyms for style.
3. Sentences under 25 words in unit copy; briefs and provocations may be written with more voice.
4. The original's register survives in two places: the provocation that opens a unit, and the studio briefs at Mastery, where a strong voice sets the stakes.
5. Every kept term has a one-line "say it to a designer" note for the briefer lane.

## Nine angles to read the curriculum from

The same 21 modules should read as a coherent whole from every angle a learner, a partner or you might take; if one angle shows a hole, the map is wrong, not the angle.

| Angle | The question it answers | What v2 shows |
|---|---|---|
| By lane | Where does a maker or a briefer spend their time? | Makers: Practice foundations, Mastery method and one studio. Briefers: Orientation foundations, Practice method, one lens, venture; Mastery only in briefing and handover |
| By depth | What do I get for 45, 200 or 480 hours? | Orientation: the ideas and the vocabulary. Practice: the method and one medium, usable. Mastery: the original program, built and defended |
| By the artifact | Which section of the DNA does each module fill? | Foundations fill candidates and the perimeter; Method turns them into rules and tests; areas add the anchor or the brief; venture adds rights, handover, lifecycle and the dossier |
| By medium | I only care about products (or brands, or spaces): what is mine? | One area's lens and studio, plus the tools it needs (S2 needs T1, T2, T5, T10; S6 needs T3, T4); everything else optional |
| By knowledge type | Is this history, perception, method, practice or business? | Historical: F1, F4, S-lineage units. Perceptual: F2, F3. Method: M1–M4. Practice: S1–S6, tools. Business and law: R1–R5. Each type has its own research plan |
| By traditional vs innovative | What could be a normal online course, and what needs an interactive or the AI partner? | Roughly 40% traditional (cases, explainers, briefs), 60% innovative (labs, simulators, the partner's three voices, the Stranger test); the studios are the most innovative part because the partner replaces the fabricator, client and crit |
| By geography | Is this a global program or a regional one? | Global spine; a regional slot in every unit's world set; the Gulf, MENA and SWANA vertical as the first expansion, filling those slots and adding regional studio prompts |
| By career stage | I am a student, mid-career, a founder: where do I enter? | Student: Orientation then Practice in order. Mid-career designer: Practice foundations fast, Mastery method and studio. Founder or brand lead: briefer lane, Mastery in R1–R3 |
| By time budget | I have five hours, a weekend, a quarter, a year | Five hours: one module at Orientation. A weekend: M2 at Practice. A quarter: the Practice path. A year at 10 hours a week: the Mastery path |

**Holes the angles reveal, and the fixes already in the map.** A briefer who wants a studio in identity (S5) has no Mastery path shorter than a maker's; add a commissioning variant of each studio brief. A student with no project cannot run Method; supply three seed worlds. Tools are invisible from the lane angle; show them as a sidebar in every area.

## Alternatives

The depth-dial model is the recommendation, but four other shapes are defensible, and one of them (core plus pathways) is closer to how professional programs are actually structured; the table gives each its honest case.

| Shape | How it works | Strength | Cost | Suits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Depth dials (recommended) | One curriculum, three depths per module, lanes set defaults | Written once; nobody gets a lesser version; every module sells alone; both audiences share cases and labs | Harder to market ("what is it?"); the app must handle depth switching; Mastery content is the bulk of the work | A trial of a repeatable method; a mixed audience |
| B. Two products | "Foundations and Method" for everyone; "Studios" for makers, sold separately | Clear positioning; the professional product keeps its edge undiluted | Splits the artifact; briefers never see the studios they commission; duplicated marketing | If the first product must launch fast to one audience |
| C. Core plus pathways | Method is mandatory; learners choose a pathway (an area plus its tools plus its venture units), like a master's with specialisms | Closest to professional education; strong identity per pathway; natural for accreditation later | Less flexible; a briefer must pick a pathway too; more sequencing rules | If the aim is to look like a school |
| D. Studio-first | Learners enter through a studio brief in week one and are sent to Foundations and Method units as the brief demands them | Highest motivation for makers; learning is pulled, not pushed | Chaotic for non-designers; hard to build (every studio must link back to every foundation unit); needs the whole program before launch | Experienced designers only |
| E. Region-first (the original) | The Gulf, MENA and SWANA vertical is the identity; global material supports it | Unique position; deep cultural authority; strong partners possible | Narrows the audience; the commissioned regional assets are the expensive part and cannot be free; parked by your decision | When budget and regional partners exist |

**A hybrid worth considering: A with C's pathways as named routes.** Keep the depth dials, but publish four named routes through them (Maker, Briefer, Founder, Student) so the marketing question has an answer without changing the structure. Routes are just defaults; the dials stay.

**Alternatives inside the map**

- Six areas or four? Keeping the original four keeps focus; adding identity and digital product keeps the briefers, who mostly commission those two. Recommended: six, build the original four first.
- Tools as a level of their own, or threaded? Threaded at Practice keeps them tied to use; electives at Mastery keep the depth. A separate tools level reads as a bootcamp and was the original's weakness.
- Method before or after Foundations? Before, for experienced makers (they have a project); after, for everyone else. The dials allow both: Method at Orientation can be taken first as a preview.
- One artifact or two? One DNA for everyone, with lane-specific attachments. Two artifacts would split the cohort's most useful interaction, makers and briefers testing each other.

## What v2 changes for research, interactives, the AI partner and the build order

The v1 concept's research plan, free-resource strategy and AI pipeline still hold; v2 adds the Mastery tier, which roughly triples content volume and needs four things v1 did not: studio-scale briefs on free real inputs, larger world sets, long-form interactive patterns, and two more partner roles.

**Research.** World sets grow from 20–30 to 40–60 objects and cases per module, still from open collections; every unit's set carries an empty regional slot. Studio briefs need a free "site pack": an OpenStreetMap plan and a CC0-documented building for S1, a common object and a public 3D scan for S2, the learner's own body for S3, a public site for S4, an existing public-domain identity to re-world for S5, a well-known flow for S6. Venture modules need the public guides and open templates from v1 plus real formulas in the simulators.

**Interactives.** The 25 patterns from the analysis all return; three long-form patterns are added for Mastery: a Studio Workspace (the project, its site pack, prototypes, revision history and partner threads in one place), a Handover Auditor that reads a full package, and a Defence Room where the partner's panel questions the dossier. The Stranger test gains a peer mode for the day there are enough learners.

**The AI partner.** Three voices at Practice (Mirror, Stranger, Client); two more at Mastery (Fabricator: tolerances, materials, what will not build; CFO: what it costs, what to cut). Still no grades. The Fabricator and CFO replace the specialist consultations the original assumed.

**Build order** (Practice first everywhere, Mastery where it proves the most)

1. Fix the DNA data structure and the site-pack format.
2. Practice tier of Foundations and Method, in full.
3. Mastery tier of M2 and M4 (the spine at full depth).
4. Practice tier of all six areas; Mastery studio for one (S2 Object is the cheapest to do at home and the easiest to test).
5. Practice tier of venture; Mastery of R3 and R5.
6. Remaining Mastery studios and tool electives, one at a time, through the module factory.
7. The regional vertical, filling the slots.

**Effort, honestly.** At 2–3 days a week with me doing the production, the Practice tier of the whole program is a few months of your review time; each Mastery studio is a month of its own. Orientation comes almost free, since it is the first fifteen minutes of every unit.

## Decisions to close v2

1. The shape: depth dials (A), or A with named routes, or core plus pathways (C).
2. The areas: six, or the original four first.
3. Method's place: before Foundations for experienced makers (allowed by the dials), or strictly after.
4. The first Mastery studio to build: S2 Object (recommended), or the one you would most enjoy judging.
5. The register: agree the keep and drop lists in §4, or add to them.
