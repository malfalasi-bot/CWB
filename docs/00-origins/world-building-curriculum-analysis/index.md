# World-Building Design Curriculum — Instructional Analysis & Build Plan

2026-09-26 · @Mohammad AlFalasi

## Executive read

What you have is a strong, distinctive content inventory for a postgraduate-level program in world-building design practice; it is not yet a curriculum, and the fastest route to one is to treat Track 02's Concept DNA as the spine and rebuild everything else around it.

**What the material is.** Six tracks, 39 modules and roughly 250 named units. The through-line is a governing artifact, the Concept DNA (Track 02), which must survive translation into four practice studios (Track 03: art, architecture, object, fashion), be executed with computational tools (Track 04), and survive money, law and public release (Tracks 05–06). Track 01 supplies the foundations (history, form, senses, material, systems). The whole thing is deliberately anchored in the UAE, Gulf, Arab world, MENA and SWANA, which no comparable online program currently does.

**What it reads like.** A hybrid of a studio-based MA (RCA, Central Saint Martins, Design Academy Eindhoven, Bartlett, Parsons Transdisciplinary Design), a strategy-consultancy operating system (brand architecture, DNA, contributor briefs) and a founder curriculum (capital, IP, cash flow). That hybrid is its selling point and its main production risk: three audiences, three registers.

**Diagnosis of the syllabus as written**

| Issue | Where | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Topics, not outcomes | Every unit | "Mastering…", "the rigorous…" describe ambition, not what a learner can do or show | Rewrite each unit as 1–3 observable outcomes + one evidence type (see §2, §9) |
| No learner, level, time or modality | Whole document | You cannot size a module, price it or choose interactives without these | Decide the five questions below before module design |
| Near-verbatim duplication | 5.8 ≈ 6.1; 5.10 ≈ 6.4 + 6.5 | Two capstones; learners meet the same text twice | Merge Tracks 05 and 06 into one Venture & Release track (§7) |
| Lifecycle/circularity taught six times | 1.5, 3.3.10, 3.4.12, 4.9, 5.9, 6.3 | Repetition without progression | One horizontal Planetary Weight thread with a level per track |
| Ergonomics/affordance taught three times | 1.3, 3.3.2, 3.4.2 | Same | Foundation in 1.3, applied per studio |
| Tech-pack/handover taught three times | 3.2.10, 3.4.11, 6.2 | Same | One Handover Grammar unit with studio-specific packs |
| Track 03 has no header and only four studios | Track 03 | 1.1 promises graphic, moving image, performing arts, digital culture; no studio delivers them | Add at least Brand/Graphic and Digital Product/Interface studios, or scope them out explicitly (§11) |
| Track 04 is eleven tools, each a career | Track 04 | Learners will drown; a LIDAR unit and a drawing unit are not equal | Split into Core Toolkit (5) and Elective Deep Tools (6) (§6) |

**What is already excellent and should be protected.** The Concept DNA method, with its constants/variables/thresholds, the four boundary tests (belongs, edge, failure, seductive-but-off-DNA) and the blind-interpretation test, is a teachable, assessable, proprietary method. The mandatory adjacent-practice translation in 2.4 is the best single assessment idea in the document. The regional centering is a real market position, not decoration.

**Decisions to make first**

1. Learner and level: early-career designers, studio founders, brand and cultural-sector leads, or students? Postgraduate-equivalent is implied throughout; confirm it.
2. Product shape: one long program, or stackable products (10-hour micro-courses → 40-hour modules → 120-hour studios → diploma)? Stackable is recommended; it lets you ship Track 02 first.
3. Modality: self-paced, cohort, or hybrid? Studio pedagogy needs live critique; recommended is self-paced interactives plus fortnightly live crits plus an AI critic between them.
4. Physical making: kits and field assignments (Gulf sites, archives, workshops) or digital-only? The studios are hollow without making; budget for it.
5. Language: English-first with Arabic parallel, or bilingual by design? 5.7 already treats bilingual architecture as content; the program should practise what it teaches.
6. Pilot: build Module 2.2 (Concept DNA) first as a four-week micro-course with the DNA Builder interactive (§8, §13); it is the spine, it is self-contained, and it proves the method.

## Program architecture

Restructured, the program is five levels and roughly 720 learner hours: Foundations (100 h) → Method (160 h, one continuous project) → one primary Studio plus one adjacent translation (160 h) → a Core Toolkit threaded through all of it (112 h + 32 h electives) → Venture & Release with a defended capstone (about 150 h). That is a postgraduate-diploma-sized program; the same modules also sell individually.

**Module types used below.** F = Foundation (knowledge and analysis), M = Method (procedural, project-based), S = Studio (making and critique), T = Tool (skills, drills), V = Venture (decision and simulation). Hours are learner hours (content + practice + critique), not production hours.

## Track 01 — Context & Core Fundamentals

Track 01 is where online programs lose people, because it is where lecture-shaped content hides; the rule for this track is do first, then read, with every module opening on a lab or a field prompt in the learner's own city.

## Track 02 — Generative World Systems (the spine)

Track 02 is the product: teach it as one continuous 16-week project in which the Concept DNA is a versioned document (v0.1 to v2.0) that every later track edits, and grade generativity (can others build from it?) rather than beauty.

**The Concept DNA needs a schema before it can be taught or built as software.** Proposed structure, drawn only from what the syllabus already names:

1. Premise — proposition, stance, central contradiction, human scenario, non-goals
2. Constants (unbreakable), Variables (adaptable), Thresholds (maximum deviation before the concept breaks)
3. Grammars — formal, material, construction, chromatic, spatial, atmospheric, sensory, behavioural, interaction, temporal, narrative, verbal; each marked required, optional or not applicable for this world
4. Anti-DNA — what the system is incapable of being
5. Alignment — relationship to Practice DNA, Collection DNA and Masterbrand DNA
6. Category obligations — functional duties by product or experience type
7. Test set — one exemplar each of belongs, edge, failure and seductive-but-off-DNA
8. Contributor brief and agent prompt architecture — the blind-interpretation package
9. Version history — what changed, why, and which evidence forced it

**Suggested cohort rhythm.** Weeks 1–3 Phase I, weeks 4–7 Phase II, weeks 8–10 Phase III, weeks 11–16 Phase IV; two live crits per phase; an AI critic available between crits; one peer blind-interpretation exchange per phase.

## Track 03 — Practice Studios

All four studios share one shape — brief → research → iterate → crit → refine → document — and one online problem: making cannot happen on a screen, so each studio needs a real site, base-build or body, a physical kit, a simulation layer for what the kit cannot do, and fortnightly live critique with an AI critic between sessions.

**Shared studio pattern.** A 12-week studio: weeks 1–2 site and lineage, 3–6 concept and first prototypes, 7–10 resolution and simulation, 11–12 handover pack and defence. Async pin-up gallery with a structured feedback protocol (what it is trying to do, what it is doing, what to change); one live crit every two weeks; one specialist Q&A per studio (engineer, fabricator, pattern cutter, sound designer). Grading is against the learner's own DNA first, then craft, then feasibility.

## Interactive pattern library

**Design rules for every interactive**

- Do, see, explain: the learner acts first, sees a consequence, then reads the concept.
- Peer overlay: wherever a judgement is made (a sort, a threshold, a rating), show the cohort's distribution afterwards; disagreement is the teaching moment.
- AI critic hook: any artifact produced can be sent to a critic persona briefed on the learner's own DNA.
- Export: each interactive writes to the project (DNA field, evidence file, ledger entry, revision log).
- Bilingual from day one: RTL layout and Arabic type rendering are platform requirements, not a later phase.
- Mobile for reading and sorting; desktop for 3D, drawing and building.

## Teaching methodology

The program should be built with backward design and constructive alignment (outcome → evidence → activity), delivered as a studio translated online (brief, iteration, pin-up, crit), and assessed on a versioned portfolio rather than tests; the frameworks below are the ones to write into the design brief for every module.

**Frameworks to adopt**

| Framework | What it gives this program | Where it shows up |
|---|---|---|
| Backward design (Wiggins and McTighe) and constructive alignment (Biggs) | Every unit starts from an essential question, names its evidence, and only then chooses content and interactives | The Essential question and Evidence rows in §3–§7 |
| Studio as signature pedagogy (Shulman; Schön's reflective practitioner) | Learning happens by making and by public critique of the making; the tutor is a critic, not a lecturer | Fortnightly live crits; async pin-up gallery; the AI critic between |
| Threshold concepts (Meyer and Land) | A short list of ideas that, once grasped, change how the learner sees everything; teach these slowly, with troublesome cases | The list below |
| Cognitive apprenticeship (Collins, Brown and Newman) | Model, coach, scaffold, fade: worked examples first, then the learner's project, then independence | Worked exemplar per module (one full case walked through), then the learner's own instance |
| Experiential cycle (Kolb) | Do, observe, conceptualise, try again; the unit anatomy follows this order | Lab first, reading second |
| Community of inquiry (Garrison) | Online cohorts need social, cognitive and teaching presence engineered, not assumed | Peer pods of four or five; named critics; structured feedback |
| Mastery and re-submission | Studio work is pass, revise or resubmit against a rubric, not a percentage | Rubric below; two resubmissions allowed |

**Threshold concepts of this program** (the ideas the whole curriculum turns on)

1. Design history is a web with obscured strands, not a line (1.1).
2. An archetype has a structural DNA you can push far but cannot delete (1.2).
3. The body, not the eye, is the metric (1.3).
4. Materials and words are signs before they are surfaces (1.4).
5. A concept is a generative system with rules, thresholds and anti-rules, not a picture (2.2).
6. Generativity is testable: if a stranger or a machine cannot build from it, it is not finished (2.2, 4.4).
7. The collection is designed before the object (2.3).
8. The invisible systems — back of house, bill of materials, cash flow, code — are design material (3.x, 5.x).
9. Circularity is the brief, not the footnote (the Planetary Weight thread).

**Anatomy of a learning unit** (60–90 minutes, the building block of every module)

| Step | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Provocation | 3 | One question or one image that the unit will answer |
| Lab | 15–25 | The interactive, before any explanation |
| Concept | 10–15 | Short reading or video that names what the lab showed |
| Case | 10 | One regional and one global exemplar dissected against the concept |
| Apply | 20–30 | The same move on the learner's own project; the interactive exports the result |
| Overlay | 5 | The cohort's choices shown against the learner's |
| Critic | async | AI critic response on the artifact; one peer prompt |
| Log | 2 | One line in the revision log: what changed and why |

**Cohort rhythm.** Weekly unlock on Monday; peer pod on Wednesday (45 minutes, four or five learners, a rotating facilitator); live crit or office hours alternating on Fridays; revision log due Sunday. A 12-week studio has six live crits and one specialist Q&A. Self-paced learners get the same units with the AI critic and asynchronous peer overlay, and can join a crit cohort for a fee.

**Critique protocols.** Use a fixed structure so feedback is teachable: statements of meaning (what the work does), the maker's questions, neutral questions from critics, then opinions only with permission (Lerman's Critical Response Process). Every crit also runs the four boundary tests from 2.2 as a lens: does the piece belong, sit on the edge, fail the DNA, or seduce while being off-DNA. Train the AI critic personas on the same protocol, each with a declared agenda: the Historian (1.1), the Fabricator (3.x, 4.10), the Buyer (2.3, 5.5), the Community (1.1, 5.6), the Planet (the thread), and the Stranger (blind interpretation).

**Assessment rubric** (every module uses the same five criteria; module briefs specify the evidence)

| Criterion | What it looks for | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| Generativity | The DNA or system lets others build correctly from it; tests and blind interpretation documented | Rules only the author can apply |
| Craft | Resolution appropriate to the phase; command of the medium and the tools used | Fidelity substitutes for thinking |
| Feasibility | The invisible systems are addressed: body, code, BOM, cash, site | Feasibility is deferred to "later" |
| Cultural authority | Sources credited; consultation documented; references distinguished from appropriation | Regional forms used as surface |
| Evidence and reflection | Alternatives and abandoned directions shown; revision history visible; claims supported | Only the final image is submitted |

**What to avoid.** Long recorded lectures (cap videos at eight minutes); quizzes as assessment; a single final submission with no visible history; separating tools from projects; and the temptation to write learning copy in the manifesto register of the source document — keep that voice for briefs and provocations.

## How curricula of this nature are built

Programs like this take about seven months to a paid pilot cohort and 12–18 months to a full catalogue; what separates the ones that ship from the ones that stay syllabi is a single accountable curriculum lead and a gate between every phase that nobody can skip.

*[Embedded: node/4e09bec8-122d — build roadmap · 6 phases, 4 gates]*

Each gate is a decision, not a meeting: Discovery cannot end until the learner, product shape and modality are written down; Architecture cannot end until every module has outcomes and the shared rubric is signed by the track leads; the pilot build ends when the eight priority-1 patterns run end to end with real content; the pilot cohort ends with completion and satisfaction numbers that decide whether Scale starts.

**What each phase produces**

| Phase | Deliverables | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | 12–15 learner interviews across the target personas; competitor and price scan; positioning statement; product shape (stackable modules) and modality decision; language decision; commissioned-asset scoping | Curriculum lead with a researcher |
| Architecture | Program specification (this document, finalised); one module descriptor per module (aims, 3–5 outcomes, evidence, hours, prerequisites); the shared rubric; the Concept DNA schema; the pattern library specification; the Planetary Weight and Handover Grammar threads defined | Curriculum lead, track leads, learning experience designer |
| Module design (pilot) | For Module 2.2 and its Track 04 companions: unit-by-unit storyboards on the unit anatomy; scripts; interactive specifications with data models and export formats; exemplar case fully worked; AI critic persona briefs | Learning experience designer, instructional designer, track lead for Track 02 |
| Pilot build | Patterns 1–8 as components; 2.2 content instantiated; DNA Builder and Blind Interpretation Lab live; cohort tooling (pods, crits, pin-up gallery); kits not needed for 2.2 | Developers, producer, editor, Arabic editor |
| Pilot cohort | 20–30 paid learners over four to six weeks; two live crits; instrumentation on every interactive; exit interviews; a revision list | Curriculum lead, one tutor, one critic |
| Scale | Track 02 complete, then one studio at a time, Track 01 in parallel as self-paced, venture track last; commissioned assets delivered; institutional partnerships and any accreditation route pursued | Whole team |

**How real programs of this nature work.** Studio-based master's programs (RCA, CSM, Design Academy Eindhoven, the Bartlett) are built from a program specification and module descriptors validated by an academic board and read by an external examiner; every module states aims, outcomes, assessment and hours, which is exactly what the source document lacks. Cohort-based online programs (the model behind the better professional design and strategy schools) run a small paid pilot before building the catalogue, treat the live crit as the product, and measure completion rather than enrolment. Interactive learning content is expensive: a planning figure of 40–100 production hours per finished learner hour for interactive units is a common industry rule of thumb, approximate, and the reason to build reusable patterns rather than bespoke pieces.

**Team for the pilot** (smallest viable): curriculum lead (owner), Track 02 lead, learning experience designer, one full-stack developer plus one 3D or creative-coding developer, editor, Arabic editor, part-time producer, one tutor for the cohort. Add track leads and guest critics per studio in Scale.

**Cost drivers to model early:** commissioned regional assets (§10); developer time on the L-sized patterns (2, 10, 22–25); generative model usage per learner; kit fulfilment into the GCC; guest critic fees; image and text rights; any accreditation or partnership fees.
