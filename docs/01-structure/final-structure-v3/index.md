## Version genealogy and audit method

Five documents preceded this one, and v2.1 carried most but not all of them; the audit below checked every unit, principle and recommendation in each against v2.1 and found 38 items missing, weakened or unresolved, none of which required changing the four-level shape, so v3.0 is v2.1 plus those items and a set of structural rules.

| Version | What it is | What it contributed that survives |
|---|---|---|
| v0 — the original syllabus | Six tracks, 39 modules, about 250 units, postgraduate register, Gulf-centred | The content at full depth; the Concept DNA method; the twelve grammars; the four boundary tests and blind interpretation; the studios' unit lists; the venture and release realism |
| The analysis (Instructional Analysis & Build Plan) | Diagnosis of v0; per-module essential questions; 25 interactive patterns; teaching frameworks; resource needs; gaps; build phases | Threads (Planetary Weight, Handover Grammar); the pattern library; threshold concepts; unit anatomy; the five criteria; the gap list; the pipeline phases |
| Concept v1 | Rebuild for two audiences, global scope, self-paced, no budget | The two lanes; the artifact growing per unit; Level 0; the research plan by module type; the free-resource strategy; the AI partner's first three voices; the Module Factory |
| Concept v2 | Full depth restored through three depths per module | Depth dials; vocabulary policy; nine angles; named routes; seed worlds; commissioning variants; build order; alternatives considered |
| Master Structure v2.1 | Unit register: 21 modules, 10 tools modules, 10 engines, about 430 units | The register itself; the artifact schema; tools evolution paths; compound engines; media and lecture studies; research log rules |

**Audit method.** Each earlier document was read unit by unit and recommendation by recommendation; each item was marked present, partial or absent in v2.1; partial and absent items received a fix (a new unit, a renamed unit, a rule, a thread or an appendix). Fixes were then checked for duplication against each other and against the register. The result is §2. Numbering stays stable: a new unit takes the number of the unit it follows plus a letter (F1.3a), so every reference in earlier documents still resolves.

## Logic and efficiency review

The four-level shape and the depth dials hold; what v2.1 lacked was the connective tissue — threads, closing tests, prerequisites, reuse marking and a rule for briefers inside the studios — and those are the structural changes adopted here.

| Finding | Problem it caused | Adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-cutting concerns (planetary weight, handover, attribution, accessibility, writing, AI collaboration) appeared as scattered units | A learner meets them six times without progression | Six horizontal threads with named checkpoints per level (§8); the units stay where they are, the thread gives them order |
| Modules had no closing test | Nothing marks a module as finished for either lane | A closing test per module, both lanes (§6) |
| No prerequisites in the register | Sequencing was implied, not stated | A prerequisite column (§6) and four sequencing rules (§5) |
| Labs reused across modules were indistinguishable from new ones | Build effort could not be counted | Reuse marked in the build inventory (§11): about 110 distinct labs across 25 patterns, plus 10 engines and 3 long-form patterns |
| Studio briefs served makers only | Briefers had no Mastery path in the areas | Every studio brief has a Make variant (ANC) and a Commission variant (CMB); the same site pack, a different task |
| Each area's medium-specific rules were implicit | The original's Practice DNA idea was lost | A Practice DNA unit at the start of every area's Practice tier |
| Peer features assumed a cohort | Self-paced learners would hit empty overlays | Pooled, anonymised outputs from all learners feed the overlays; the partner's Stranger voice stands in when the pool is thin (§9) |
| The commerce, margin and expansion content was gone | The venture track could not answer "where does the money come in" | Five units restored (gap items 6–9) |
| Tools carried no data-rich modelling | BIM and PLM, the bridge to real handover, were missing | T4.2a |
| Voices were named inconsistently (Historian, Fabricator, CFO appeared without definition) | The partner had no spec | Seven voices defined once (§9) |
| Hours lived in v2, not in the register | Slices could not be sized | Hours by depth per module (§6) |
| Assets that cannot be free (regional readers, colour study) had no home | They were silently dropped | Program-produced assets list with what is done now and what waits for the regional vertical (§10) |

**Not changed, deliberately.** The number of levels; the 21 + 10 module set; the six areas; the artifact schema; the regional slot as the last unit of every module; the no-grades rule; global first, region later.

## The twelve principles of v3.0

Everything in the program follows from twelve rules; if a future change breaks one of them, the change is wrong or the rule needs a versioned revision.

1. **One curriculum, three depths.** Every module is written once at the original's depth. Orientation is its first fifteen minutes, Practice its usable core, Mastery its studio and defence. The Introduction, Practitioner and Comprehensive versions are slices by depth, never separate texts.
2. **Two lanes, one spine.** Makers produce; briefers write and judge. Same concepts, cases and labs; different Apply step and artifact attachments. Anyone may take any depth of any module. Every studio brief has a Make and a Commission variant.
3. **Four named routes.** Maker, Briefer, Founder, Student: published default paths through the depths, changing nothing structurally. A learner without a project takes one of three seed worlds.
4. **The artifact is the through-line.** The Concept DNA (schema in Master Structure §2) grows at the end of every unit, is tested at the end of every module, and is defended at the end of the program. Makers add anchor, family, adjacent translation and dossier; briefers add brief, judged set and commissioning record.
5. **The unit's shape follows its knowledge****.** There is no fixed sequence. Each unit opens with the move that is most impactful for its particular knowledge and research (the opening moves below), and orders its parts to suit. Four things are invariant: every unit contains a lab or simulation, an Apply step by lane, a partner step, and a log entry; and a term is named after the learner has met it, wherever that is possible. Every kept term enters the glossary, which is a networked compendium.
6. **Tests, not grades.** Every module closes with a test (§6); the partner works through five lenses (generativity, craft, feasibility, cultural authority, evidence) and never issues a verdict.
7. **Seven voices.** The partner's voices (§9) replace the studio's critic, fabricator, client, finance director, historian, planet and stranger. They ask, test and reflect; they do not grade.
8. **Six threads.** Planetary Weight, Handover Grammar, Attribution and Authority, Accessibility, Writing and Argument, AI Collaboration run across all levels with checkpoints (§8), so recurring concerns progress instead of repeating.
9. **Precise vocabulary, no adjectives.** The profession's terms stay (typology, tectonics, affordance, palimpsest, tech-pack, MVI); manifesto intensifiers go; obscure headings are renamed (the v2 lists). The original's voice lives in provocations and studio briefs only.
10. **Free and open, with a record.** Every asset is sourced from open collections, public-domain texts, open data or free software, or produced by the program; every asset carries licence, source and the units that use it.
11. **Global spine, regional slot.** Every module's last unit is a regional slot; the world sets are global across the whole timeline; the Gulf, MENA and SWANA vertical fills the slots when it is built.
12. **Evolving by version.** Research is uncapped and logged; findings change the structure through recorded versions (Master Structure §12); new units take letter suffixes so references never break.

The research log of each module says which move each unit takes and why; when a unit's research shows a different move would land harder, the unit changes, and the change is versioned.

**Sequencing rules.** Foundations before or alongside Method; Method before any area's Practice tier; an area's Mastery studio after M2 and M4 at Practice; tools attach to the unit that first needs them; venture modules after at least one area at Practice; R5 last. Experienced makers may open with M2 at Orientation as a preview.

**Frameworks and threshold concepts** (from the analysis, retained): backward design and constructive alignment; studio as signature pedagogy translated to the partner; threshold concepts taught slowly — history is a web, archetypes have DNA, the body is the metric, materials and words are signs, a concept is a generative system, generativity is testable, the collection precedes the object, the invisible systems are design material, circularity is the brief; cognitive apprenticeship (worked exemplar, then the learner's own); Kolb's cycle in the unit anatomy; mastery with re-submission instead of marks.

## The AI partner

Seven voices, one rule: they ask, test and reflect, and never say good or bad; between them they stand in for the critic, the fabricator, the client, the finance director, the historian, the planet and the stranger a studio would otherwise need in the room.

| Voice | Asks | Where it appears | What it can see |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Mirror | "Here is what your work is doing; is that what you meant?" | Every Apply step; M1.8; M4.7 | The learner's artifact and the unit's concept |
| The Stranger | "I have never met you; here is what I built from your rules." | M2.15, M2.18; every Sx.21-type unit; R5.7 | The DNA or brief only, never the conversation |
| The Client | "I am commissioning this; what do I get, what does it cost, what could go wrong?" | Briefs; M1.16; R3.3; R3.6a; S4.8 | The DNA, the brief, the venture fields |
| The Fabricator | "This will not build; here is why, and what it would take." | M4.8; every studio brief; R3.6; T10 | The engine state, materials, tolerances |
| The CFO | "This costs that; cut this and you break these constants." | S1.29, S2.34, S3.32; R2; R3.12 | The Venture Console and the DNA constants |
| The Historian | "Where does this come from, who made it, whom did you ask?" | F1; M1.14; S2.11; S3.15; R2.7 | The world set, the sources, the attribution record |
| The Planet | "What does this weigh, and where does it go when it dies?" | F5; Sx circularity; R4 | The Planetary Ledger and the BOM |

**Rules.** No verdicts; every response ends with a question or a test the learner can run. The five lenses (generativity, craft, feasibility, cultural authority, evidence) shape what each voice looks at, not a score. Voices declare their agenda. Everything generated is logged, attributable and scoreable for drift. Free-tier limits are shown, not hidden.

**Standing in for peers.** Overlays (the cohort's sorts, thresholds, ratings) draw on a pooled, anonymised set of all learners' outputs; when the pool is thin, the Stranger produces the comparison set and says so. Peer exchange in M2.18 is the Stranger until real exchange is available; live crit cohorts are a later, optional product.

**Standing in for specialists.** The Fabricator, CFO and Historian carry the specialist consultations the original studios assumed; each has a knowledge pack per area (materials and processes, cost norms, source lists) that the research logs feed.

## Consumption matrix

What a learner needs to learn comes in five kinds, and how they consume it comes in six situations; every unit is published in three consumption forms so that the same content meets the learner on a phone for five minutes, at a desk for an hour, and in the hand while making.

**What they need to learn** (by level and lane)

| Kind of knowledge | What it is | Heaviest in | Maker needs | Briefer needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Know-that | Facts, histories, names, dates, sources | Foundations, lineage units | Enough to place their own work | Enough to talk to designers and check claims |
| Know-why | Theory, argument, judgement | Foundations, Method, venture | To defend choices | To judge choices |
| Know-how | Procedures, moves, tool skills | Method, areas, tools | The full procedure | The procedure's shape, to brief and audit it |
| Know-who and know-where | Sources, makers, communities, institutions, archives | Attribution thread, regional slots | To credit and consult | To commission and credit |
| Dispositions | Habits: test before trusting, subtract, log changes, ask who made it | Everywhere; the partner's questions | Both | Both |

**How and where they consume** (situations, with what fits each)

| Situation | Time | Attention | What fits | Forms | Depth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phone, in transit | 5–10 min | Fragmented, one hand | A provocation, one lab, one card, one prediction prompt | 15, 13, 3, 6, 11 in scrub mode | Orientation |
| Desk, focused | 45–90 min | Full | The whole unit: lab, concept, cases, apply, partner | 1, 2, 4, 9, 10, the labs | Practice |
| Hands busy, making | 30–120 min | Ears free, eyes on the work | Audio companion, step-through on a second screen, the designed brief printed | 8, 2, 17, 16 | Practice, Mastery |
| Walking or on site | 20–60 min | Outdoors, moving | Audio walk, field prompts, capture tasks | 8, 12, capture tools | Mastery (M1.14, S1.9, S4.26) |
| Reviewing before a decision | 10–20 min | Skimming, comparing | Comparison wall, glossary compendium, the artifact's own fields, the judged set | 10, 13, the DNA Builder read mode | Any |
| Revisiting as reference | 2–5 min | Look-up | Glossary entry, card, checklist, formula panel | 13, 4, checklists | Any |

**Three published forms per unit.** The five-minute form (Orientation layer: the unit's opening move, one lab or card); the session form (the full unit anatomy); the reference form (glossary entries, cards, checklists and the artifact fields the unit filled). The app serves the form by device and session length; a learner can switch. Studio briefs also exist as printable documents; making units have an audio companion; field units have an offline pack.

**Modes of consumption a unit should offer** (at least three of six): read, watch, listen, play, make, browse. Foundations units lean read-play-browse; Method units play-make-read; studio units make-play-listen; venture units play-read-browse.

**By career stage and lane** (defaults, not limits): a student reads and plays most; a mid-career designer makes and skims theory; a founder browses, plays the simulators and listens; a briefer reads cards, browses walls and plays the judging labs. Routes (§5) set these defaults; the three published forms make them possible without duplicating content.

**Design consequences.** Every unit's spec now carries its consumption forms and its presentation forms alongside its media list; the glossary is built as reference-first (look-up in two taps); the Orientation layer is written to be complete on a phone; audio is generated for every argument card and every making unit; and the pattern library gains the presentation catalogue as a second component set.
