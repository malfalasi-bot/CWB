## How to read the master structure

This is the comprehensive version: every unit of the program, at every depth, with what it teaches, which artifact field it feeds, how it is delivered, and what it needs; the condensed versions are not separate documents but slices of these tables, selected by the Depth column.

**Depth and slices.** O = Orientation, P = Practice, M = Mastery. The Introduction slice is every O unit (about 45 h). The Practitioner slice is every O and P unit with one area (about 200 h). The Comprehensive version is everything (about 480 h with one studio and two tool electives). A single module at any depth is its own product. Change a unit here and every slice changes with it.

**Delivery codes** (a unit usually has two or three): L = lecture material (text, short video; §11 says what qualifies) · C = case, an object or project from the world set told as a story · I = interactive lab (single concept) · S = simulation (compound, several concepts coupled; §9) · P = the AI partner in one of its voices · K = physical making with things at hand · D = a document the learner produces · T = a tool unit is used here.

**Media codes** (§10 has the sourcing): img = images from open collections · drw = drawings, plans, sections · 3d = models · vid = short video · aud = audio · dat = data · ani = animated diagram · pack = a site, object, body or brand pack for a studio brief.

**Artifact field.** The Concept DNA section each unit writes to, by the codes in §2.

**Research status.** Every module is Open: research is uncapped and logged (§12). Status moves to Sprinted (one research sprint done), Drafted (units written), Tested (labs run with people), Live.

**Versioning.** This structure is v2.1. A module's research may add, split, merge, move or re-depth units; each such change increments the structure (v2.2, v2.3…) and is recorded in §12's change log. Nothing here is final by design.

**Regional slot.** Every module's last unit is a regional slot: the same concepts on the learner's own place, and the hook for the Gulf, MENA and SWANA vertical later.

**Counts.** 4 levels, 21 modules, 10 tools modules, 10 compound engines, about 430 units. Numbering is module.unit (F1.3, S2.14) so units can be referenced from anywhere.

## Compound simulations

Single labs teach one concept; the program's central claim — that a world is a system in which form, body, material, money and consequence move together — can only be taught by simulations that couple several concepts, so ten engines are defined here, each built as one model with layers that units switch on one at a time.

**The principle.** An engine holds one state (a site, an object, a garment, a DNA, a venture) and several coupled models. Early units switch on one layer and the engine behaves like a single lab. Later units switch on more, and the learner sees the couplings: change the wall thickness and the cost, weight, shipping volume, carbon and load safety all move; change a threshold in the DNA and the Stranger's outputs drift differently across media. Each engine's layers list is also its build order, so the first version is small.

| Engine | State it holds | Layers (each added by a unit) | Units served | What it teaches that single labs cannot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. | 2. ## Lecture material study

Lecture material is whatever must be told rather than discovered — sequenced history, argument, definitions, procedures, warnings — and the program keeps it to about a fifth of learner time by converting each kind into the form that lets the learner meet it through doing first.

**What counts as lecture material**

| Kind | Examples in this program | Why it must be told |
|---|---|---|
| Sequenced narrative | F1's pivots and centres; S3.11 history of silhouette; S5.11 identity histories | Order and causation are the content |
| Theory and argument | S1.9 phenomenology; S4.12 relational theory; F2.4 typology theory; S2.10 critical design | The claim and the counter-claim are the content |
| Definitions and taxonomies | M2.6 the twelve grammars; M3.3 roles; R2.5 IP types | Shared vocabulary is needed before practice |
| Procedures | M1.3 research routes; R3.8 handover anatomy; M1.5 ethics and consent | Steps, in order, with the reasons |
| Cases | Every C unit | The specific is what makes the general stick |
| Warnings and ethics | F1.18 attribution; T3.5 bias; R2.7 sovereignty | Consequences must be named |

**How each kind converts**

| Kind | Conversion | What remains as reading or video |
|---|---|---|
| Sequenced narrative | Told through the Timeline Atlas and object stories; the learner navigates before reading | One 400-word card per pivot or centre: why it mattered, what to look for |
| Theory and argument | An argument card (claim, counter-claim, one case) followed by the lab that makes the theory felt: phenomenology through the Light Sandbox, relational theory through the Audience Ritual Builder | Short original essays, because the canonical texts are not free and a summary that replaces them is not allowed |
| Definitions and taxonomies | Sort first, then reveal: the learner sorts examples into bins, the taxonomy is named after | A glossary entry and a one-line "say it to a designer" |
| Procedures | Embedded as the interactive's steps; the tool is the procedure | A checklist to keep |
| Cases | World-set dossiers tagged to units; never lectured | The dossier page itself |
| Warnings and ethics | Decision simulators: the Spectrum Sort, the Sponsor Negotiation, the MVI Negotiator | A principle stated once, in a sentence |

**Format rules for what remains.** Text first, with optional narrated audio; video only where motion carries meaning; nothing over eight minutes; every lecture element names the lab that precedes it and the artifact field it feeds; the original's voice is allowed in provocations and studio briefs, nowhere else.

**Share of time, estimated across the comprehensive version.** Lecture material about 20%; cases about 15%; single labs about 25%; compound simulations about 20%; making and documents about 15%; the partner about 5%. Foundations is heavier in lecture and cases; Method and the studios are heavier in labs and engines.

## Research log and evolution rules

Research is uncapped; what is capped is losing it, so every module carries a research log, every finding that changes the structure is recorded as a version, and the structure itself expects to be wrong in places.

**The research log per module** (one living document each): questions open; sources found, with licence and quality grade; world-set candidates and the units they would serve; claims sheet (every factual statement in unit copy with its source); findings that argue for a structural change; open questions for you. **How a finding changes the structure**

| Finding | Structural move | Version effect |
|---|---|---|

| A unit that is really two | Split | minor |
| Two units that are one | Merge; keep both numbers as aliases | minor |
| A concept sitting at the wrong depth | Re-depth (O to P, P to M, or back) | minor; slices update automatically |
