# AI reader protocol for F1 held items

Version 1 · 2 October 2026 · applies to F1 units in batches 1–4 (F1.1–F1.4, F1.5–F1.9a, F1.14–F1.31, except F1.17)

## 1. Purpose and limits

Many F1 items (55 on a full count) were held for "a reader from the community concerned". There are no community readers yet. An AI system finds the community's or nation's own published guidance, reads each item against it, picks one of four outcomes, and records what it read so a human can check every step.

**This is not a community reader, and nothing in it is consent.** Its limits:

- An AI belongs to no community. Much of what matters (restricted knowledge, family and clan rights, current disputes) is never published, so it cannot see it.
- Published guidance is often a state's, a museum's or an outsider's, not the community's. The log says who wrote each source.
- Search misses voices. An unfound source may say the opposite.
- It can misread tone and context, especially outside English.
- An AI outcome is interim. It never "clears" an item for good.

So the pass only moves items toward caution or holds them. It never adds sacred, restricted or funerary material, and never names a living person who has not published their own name.

## 2. What counts as guidance

Guidance is a published text by, or formally adopted by, the community or nation concerned, opened at source. In order of weight:

1. **The community's own statement or code:** an assembly pronouncement, a council's research code (the San Code), or a museum the community governs (the A:shiwi A:wan Museum, Ájtte).
2. **A national or tribal body acting for it:** the Navajo Nation Heritage and Historic Preservation Department, the Royal Court of Benin, the Manhyia Palace, AIATSIS, a Sámi Parliament.
3. **The nation's heritage law or official statement:** Guatemala's Decreto 26-97, Yemen's antiquities law, Nigeria's 2023 declaration.
4. **Guidance written with the community:** an ICOM Red List, Te Papa's iwi guidance, Local Contexts.
5. **Community-authored writing that is not policy:** a community newspaper (Navajo Times, Ka Wai Ola) or a member's scholarship. It informs, but the log marks it as not guidance.

News reports, museum labels, encyclopaedias and outsiders' scholarship can supply facts. They cannot speak for a community.

**Record:** the URL; whether it was opened in full or read through a search extract; a quotation of 25 words or fewer; the question; what stays uncertain. **Never invent a URL.** If nothing is found, say so and apply the cautious default.

## 3. The four outcomes and the cautious default

| Outcome | Meaning | What the spec must show |
|---|---|---|
| **CLEAR** | The planned treatment already fits the guidance found. | No wording change; a section-9 line. |
| **REVISE** | Show it, but with changed wording or an added content or cultural note. | The exact new wording, written into the spec. |
| **LINK-OUT** | No image and no detail on our pages: a sentence naming the item and a link to the holder's or the community's own page. | The image removed from the media plan; the link target named. |
| **REMOVE** | Drop the item from the unit. | The item deleted, with a section-9 line saying why. |

**The cautious default.** Under uncertainty, pick the more cautious outcome (CLEAR < REVISE < LINK-OUT < REMOVE). In particular:

- If no published guidance is found, the item gets **LINK-OUT**. **REVISE with a content note** is allowed instead only when two things are true: the item is not known to be sacred, ancestral or restricted, and F1's funerary rule (Q21) already allows it to be shown with its origin and a note.
- If the guidance found is a state's or a museum's rather than the community's, treat the decision as interim and make it no less cautious than the default.
- An object that may be an ancestor, or that may have sat on an altar or in a grave, and that came out of a documented looting, gets **LINK-OUT** at least.
- A living person's own words are not reproduced until the route to consent named in the spec has been used. Until then, link to where they were published.
- Names, designs or knowledge that a community holds (cultivar names, design names, patterns) get **LINK-OUT** unless the community itself publishes them for general use.

## 4. The "Cultural note" pattern

The programme does not hold a Local Contexts Hub subscription. Local Contexts Notices can only be generated by subscribed institutions, and their icons, titles and text may not be altered. Labels belong to Indigenous communities alone. So **we use no Local Contexts icon, title or text, and we never apply a Label.** Where a spec says "Notice", read it as "Cultural note" until a subscription exists.

A Cultural note is our own plain sentence, set apart from the caption:

> **Cultural note:** [Community's own name] [relationship to the item, in one sentence]. [What we do and do not show, and why, in one sentence.] [Where to go: the community's or holder's own page.] About cultural labels: localcontexts.org.

Rules for the note:
- Put the community's own name first, spelled the way the community spells it (for example Diné, Hwéeldi, Diné Bikéyah).
- Write no more than three sentences, in plain words, in our own voice.
- Do not say or imply that the community approved anything.
- Do not interpret designs, rites or meanings.
- Link to the community's page, not to an outsider's, wherever such a page exists.
- Put a separate **Content note** before any hard material: violence, death, looting, burial or coercion. It names people as people, never as goods.

Example (F1.30, winter counts): *"Cultural note: each winter count belongs to its keeper's band and family, and its stories are theirs to tell. We name each count by its keeper and nation, as the holder records it."*

## 5. The standing withdrawal route

This route applies to every F1 item, not only to the held ones. It follows the rule in F1.31: **withdrawal requests are acted on within 48 hours.**

- **Who can ask:** anyone at all. A requester does not have to prove membership, identity or standing, or give a reason.
- **How to ask:** use a link on every unit page, at the foot of every Cultural note, and in the course footer: *"Ask us to change or remove this item."* It goes to a monitored inbox: [contact to be set by the course team before G3].
- **Within 48 hours of receipt:** the item is taken down or replaced by the line *"This item has been withdrawn while we review a request."* The requester gets a reply. The request is logged in AI_READER_LOG.md with the date, the item ID and the action taken. The requester's personal details stay out of the log.
- **After that:** a human reader, or the people who asked, decide whether the item comes back, changes or stays out. The default is that it stays out. A withdrawal is never reversed on the strength of an AI pass alone.
- **Weekends and holidays** do not extend the 48 hours. Someone must be named to cover them.

## 6. How a human reader takes over

A human reader from the community, or a reader the community names, starts from AI_READER_LOG.md.

1. **Read the row, then the source.** Check that the source says what the row claims, and whether it truly speaks for the community.
2. **Decide again, from scratch.** A reader may make an item more open or more restricted; their decision overrides the AI pass.
3. **Record it in the log:** the reader's name (or "a reader from [community]"), date, outcome and approved wording. Mark the AI row "superseded"; never delete it.
4. **Edit the spec.** Replace the section-9 line "AI reader pass…" with "Reader review, [date]: [outcome]". Change the wording as the reader directs.
5. **Credit and payment.** Readers are credited, if they wish, and paid as F1.31's authorship tiers set out. Their time is not volunteered for them.
6. **Order of review:** first all LINK-OUT items about objects that may be ancestors or come from altars or graves; then every REVISE that rests on no community guidance (marked "none found" in the log); then the rest.
