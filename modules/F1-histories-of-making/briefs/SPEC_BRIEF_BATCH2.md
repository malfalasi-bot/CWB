## What each unit must carry

- **F1.19.** The Great Exhibition (1851) as an event of making: link to F1.15's taxonomy reading, don't repeat it. The machine in at least four worlds, not only Britain:
  - Indian handloom against the Lancashire mill;
  - Meiji Japan's export industries;
  - a West African or Latin American case;
  - Jacquard as a hinge to F1.5's algorithmic recurrence.

  Cotton picked by enslaved people is linked to F1.13 and F1.23, with the human-flow rule. Use the National Jukebox rule (module file row 4.7) if you use sound.
- **F1.19a.** Military procurement as the largest patron. Also:
  - dazzle and camouflage as applied ornament (link F1.15);
  - rationing and utility schemes;
  - reconstruction and prefabrication;
  - propaganda typography;
  - refugee and displaced making;
  - the arms trade as a network.

  **Steppe:** artisans moved by the Mongol conquests (craftsmen spared and relocated, for example to Karakorum or Tabriz), with sources.

  Deep Dives 2 names the Diné Long Walk (1864–68) as a link. Handle it with content notes and never frame war as good for design.
- **F1.19b.** The designer as a profession and how design was taught. Cover:
  - guilds and the academies: link F1.3's hierarchy-of-the-arts case, don't repeat it;
  - the schools of design (the canon holds about 20 schools, SCH ids);
  - Bauhaus, Vkhutemas and Ulm;
  - and at least four schools beyond Europe and North America. Candidates to verify: Kala Bhavana at Santiniketan; NID Ahmedabad; the Mayo School in Lahore, now NCA; the College of Art at Kumasi (KNUST); Bezalel; the Cairo School of Fine Arts; Mingei's pedagogy; the Sir J.J. School in Mumbai.

  Who was admitted, and who was not: women at the Bauhaus is already in F1.18, so link it. Gropius's manifesto (1919) must follow the quote-by-jurisdiction rule. Propose the canon rows the unit needs.
- **F1.20.** Modernisms as plural, never one line with provinces. Carry:
  - socialist, Soviet and Non-Aligned design after 1945;
  - design and the nation after independence (Chandigarh, Dakar, Brasília, Kumasi, tropical modernism);
  - **Steppe/Central Asia:** Soviet modernism in Tashkent or Almaty (mosaics and panels), from sources you can verify.

  The comparison wall (form 10) and an argument card (form 13).
- **F1.21.** The consumer and the platform. Carry:
  - the fashion system: couture, the department store (GAP072, GAP073), and link F1.18;
  - typography and type technology into the digital age, including Arabic, Chinese and Indic type (hot metal to Unicode);
  - advertising and the invention of the consumer, using the Jukebox "Uncle Josh in a department store" link from F1.4;
  - the platform economy;
  - training data as copying: link F1.14 and give both readings.

  The Client is the partner voice.
- **F1.22.** Making as extraction, data first (map and explorable). Commodity chains in at least four worlds. Candidates to verify:
  - Congo rubber and cobalt;
  - guano or nitrates in Peru and Chile;
  - Indian indigo;
  - **steppe/Central Asia:** Soviet cotton and the Aral Sea, and/or rare earths at Baotou in Inner Mongolia.

  Fill PLW (planetary weight) for one object. The Planet is the partner voice. Numbers only from sources opened, with dates.
- **F1.22a.** Use, wear, repair, resale, inheritance, dowry. Also:
  - the household as a site of making;
  - furniture and interiors;
  - the play-and-the-child lens;
  - assistive and adapted objects (the body-and-access lens, module file row 6.4);
  - kintsugi and darning;
  - the Diné blanket that became a rug (Deep Dives 2 link).

  **Steppe:** the ger or yurt and its felt, as a lived-with object that is repaired and moved. UNESCO lists Mongolian ger making (verify the year from UNESCO's own page); PR-017 is proposed. Open objects via form 5 (before/after wear) where possible.
- **F1.23.** The credit map: who is left out of design's credit. Carry:
  - the coerced-making strand (slavery, plantations, camps, prisons), named plainly with content notes;
  - people never described as goods (human-flow rule);
  - disabled makers;
  - studio assistants and factory hands;
  - women: link F1.18, don't repeat it.

  Apply both lanes: a credit map of the learner's own project. Cases in at least four worlds.
- **F1.24.** The museum as author. Collecting, the provenance chain as a query (every Cleveland and AIC record carries one), and restitution. Cases:
  - the Benin bronzes and the Igun Street guild (link F1.6's PR-003);
  - the Gothenburg return (Deep Dives 2 link; verify what was returned and to whom);
  - Luristan as a looting case (WS-091 is flagged);
  - a heritage brand's claims (module file v3.5);
  - a settler-colonial case;
  - **Steppe:** the Crimean Scythian gold lent to the Allard Pierson Museum, Amsterdam in 2014 and the Dutch court rulings on where it goes. Verify the dates and outcome from court or museum sources.

  Handle funerary objects by the contextual rule. Never show human remains; the Pazyryk burials, for example, are named only.
