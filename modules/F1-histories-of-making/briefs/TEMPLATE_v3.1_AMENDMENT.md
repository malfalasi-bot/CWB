# Template v3.1 amendment: four rows, the tap rule, and the station tour

2026-10-03 · Applies to all 38 F1 unit specs · Amends briefs/SPEC_BRIEF.md (template v3) · Source: AUDIT_CONTENT_2026-10-03.md §3 and §8

A unit is now a tour of stations that the learner watches, then explores, then makes from, then checks. The spec table gains four rows that hold the tour. Nothing in the claims, the open objects, the reader decisions or the Historian's rules changes.

## 1. The four new rows

They sit in the spec table after the "Media" row (or after the last row where a table has no Media row), in this order.

| Row | What it must hold |
|---|---|
| Storyboard | 6 to 12 stations. Each station is one line: a number, a title, one idea, one visual (an open image, a model, a map layer or our own drawing, by id), the lens it sits on (time, place, movements, objects), the data it draws (claims by id; canon rows; register rows), and a length of 60 to 90 seconds (100 to 200 narrated words). The opening move is station 1. At most one one-tap prediction per chapter, placed before a station, and at most one retrieval question after it, both in tap form. A world chapter carries one simultaneity station ("what else was being made at this date") and at least two stations that teach a movement or a civilisation as the thing itself, not as the caption of an object |
| Narration script | The full script, 800 to 900 words per five minutes of lecture (about 170 words a minute), in the programme voice (docs/02-program/voice-guide). Every sentence that states a fact ends with its claim id in brackets, matching section 3. Confidence words follow W5. The script ends with a "What we simplified" note of 50 to 120 words that names each simplification and where the full reading sits (a claim, a case, a reference page). Until the script is written, the row reads "To write against template v3.1" |
| Assets and licences | Every image, 3D model, map layer, drawing and sound the tour uses. Each entry: holder, id (accession, object id, tile set or file), licence class as the holder states it (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA, KOGL Type 1, our own, link out) and a fallback (a still for a model, a text list for a map, a drawing for a link-out, a caption for a sound). Nothing NC or ND is embedded; it is linked. The open objects of section 5 seed the row; our own drawings and maps come from section 7 |
| Reference pages opened | The movement, civilisation (and culture, polity, dynasty, community, diaspora), network, event, period and world-band pages the tour opens, by name and canon id, each marked "exists" (a page is in grouping-pages/) or "to write". Institutions, beliefs, places and people are nodes, not pages, unless a grouping page exists for them (the waqf, INS044). The list is drawn from section 4 (nodes) and the walk, and is the unit's order for the page batches of audit §10 |

## 2. Rule changes

1. **Watch, explore, make, check.** Every unit is a tour of 6 to 12 stations over a map, a timeline or an object, one idea and one visual per station. Watch: the chaptered lecture, 5 to 10 minutes. Explore: the lens the lecture was on, now live, with its reference pages one tap away. Make: the lab and the Apply step. Check: five to eight one-tap questions in the same form as the predictions, answered on the map or timeline.
2. **Predictions are one tap.** A prediction is one tap on a map, a timeline or three plausible options, with the reveal shown within seconds. Never typed, never prose, never more than one per chapter. The claim, the counter-claim and the case keep their wording; only the learner's act changes.
3. **No guess gate.** No lab asks the learner to reconstruct, order or fill before it has shown the record. The reveal comes first. An ordering strip may follow as an optional one-drag puzzle after the explanation.
4. **Typed answers only in the method units.** F1.25 to F1.31 (nine specs: F1.25, F1.26, F1.27, F1.28, F1.28a, F1.29, F1.30, F1.30a, F1.31) keep their labs, Apply and Partner whole, because tagging, logging and writing are the skill taught. Their opening-move predictions still take the tap form.
5. **The five-minute form** becomes the first chapter of the lecture. The session and reference forms stay.
6. **The reference layer** stays behind every tour: claims, open objects, nodes, protocol screen and glossary, as section 10 and the Consumption forms row already hold them.

## 4. How the script fills the rows on the first pass

- **Storyboard:** "To write against template v3.1", with the station count the spec implies: the walk's steps where a walk exists (plus the three stations to add in a world chapter), or the opening move, the cases, the concept and the lab where there is none, held within 6 to 12.
- **Narration script:** "To write against template v3.1" and the three rules (words per five minutes, claim ids, the simplified note).
- **Assets and licences:** the open objects of section 5 with holder, id and licence class; our own drawings and maps as section 7 lists them; sound as the Media row states it; fallbacks "to set".
- **Reference pages opened:** every canon id of the page kinds found in section 4 and the walk, grouped as movements; civilisations, cultures, polities, communities; networks; events and periods; world bands, each with its canon name and "exists" or "to write". Names in the walk prose without a canon id are not resolved by the script and are added by hand when the storyboard is written.

**Applied 3 October 2026.** The script (scratchpad amend31/amend31.py, with pre-edit copies of every spec beside it) amended all 38 specs. F1.25 is the first full station tour (ten stations; a 1,317-word narration with claim ids and a "what we simplified" note; the Object Autopsy in reading mode); F1.13 is next, as audit §11 orders. Where a chapter keeps its walk in a table cell (F1.6's Lab row, F1.9's Cases row, the Walk row of F1.8, F1.11 and F1.12a), the "Station to add" lines sit in that cell; where it keeps it in a "The walk" table (F1.7, F1.12) or in section 7 (F1.5, F1.9a, F1.10, F1.13), they follow the table or list.

## 5. Lint additions (to docs/02-program/voice-guide/index.md §6)

**Script**

14. The spec table holds Storyboard, Narration script, Assets and licences and Reference pages opened, in that order, after Media.
15. A written storyboard has 6 to 12 stations; each station line carries a title, one visual id, a lens, at least one claim id and a length of 60 to 90 s. A world chapter's storyboard contains "simultaneity" and at least two stations whose title is a movement or a civilisation name.
16. At most one prediction per chapter; every prediction sentence in the Opening move row and the storyboard contains "tap" and none contains "type", "types", "write", "writes" or "fill".
17. A written narration script has 800 to 900 words per five minutes of its stated length, carries a claim id in brackets on every sentence that states a date, a name, a place, a number or a quotation, and ends with a "What we simplified" paragraph.
18. Every Assets entry has a holder, an id, a licence class from the allowed set and a fallback; no entry's licence contains "NC" or "ND" unless the entry is marked "link out".
19. Every Reference pages entry has a canon id and is marked "exists" or "to write"; "exists" is accepted only when a file in grouping-pages/ cites that id.
20. In a world chapter, both Apply rows begin "Optional:" and the walk contains "Station to add: simultaneity".
21. Section 9 of every spec carries the template v3.1 log line.

**Person**

22. Does every station teach one idea, and does the visual show that idea rather than decorate it?
23. In a world chapter, do the two movement or civilisation stations teach the thing itself (who named it, its span, what the name hides, its making), not an object with the name as caption?
24. Does the simultaneity station name makers elsewhere at the same date, with sources, and not rank them?
25. Is the "What we simplified" note honest about what the lecture flattened, and does it point to where the full reading sits?
