# F1.13 Networks — lecture storyboard

Built 4 October 2026 from `specs/F1.13.md` (claims 1–25, section 2 protocol screen, section 5 open objects, section 7 visual plan, reader decisions AR-080 to AR-086) and `AUDIT_CONTENT_2026-10-03.md` §3, §8 and §9. The lecture is the Watch step of the unit journey: a tour of nine stations over one map, one timeline and one chain, one idea and one visual per station, 60 to 90 seconds each, a one-tap prediction at most once per station, and eight one-tap closing questions. It says only what the claims support, with their confidence words.

**Form.** Station tour (audit §3). One canvas, four lenses (audit §8): this lecture lives on the Place lens (the map), with the Time lens (the timeline strip) always visible and the Chain lens drawn over the map where a supply chain or a skill chain is the idea. The visual system is fixed (audit §9): shapes, one legend, one narrator, one map. Dark background, a quiet grey base, one accent (the jar's cobalt), one highlight (amber, reserved for the people layer and for reveals).

**The four flows on the map.** Object = solid accent arc. Material = dashed accent arc. Skill = dotted grey arc. People = their own mark (an amber ring at each place, a dotted amber tie between them), never an arrow shared with goods (spec §2 map rule; H9). On the Atlantic station no people line is drawn at all: the count is in words (spec §7 step 8).

**Runtime.** 9 stations, 555 s planned (9 min 15 s), plus a 20 s title and a 70 s closing-questions reel.

## Stations

Durations are planned narration time at about 165 words a minute, with pauses. Lens: Map (place), Time (timeline strip), Chain (boxes and arrows over the map), Object (a record card: holder, accession, date, licence; the image slot is left for the CC0 file, not fetched in this build).

### Title (0:00–0:20)

Timeline strip appears with the eleven bands B01–B11; the scrub marker sits in B08. The map fades in as land outlines, no borders. Title: "F1.13 Networks: objects, materials, skills and people". Legend builds its four lines. No narration beyond the title line.

### Station 1 — Where did the blue come from? (0:20–1:25, 65 s)

- **One idea.** A network is four flows at once, and this jar's blue came from Iran.
- **Visual.** Map, Eurasia framed. One dot, Jingdezhen. Object card: Cleveland 1962.154, jar, 1300s, CC0. A dashed accent line runs west from Jingdezhen to an unlabelled point. Date stamp "1300s"; scrub marker in B08.
- **Data.** Route table R01; claims 1, 2, 3; object WS-020 (Cleveland 1962.154).
- **One-tap prediction.** "Where did the blue come from?" Three dots on the map: A Jingdezhen's own hills, B Iran, C Europe. Pause 4 s. Reveal: the dashed line resolves to Kashan, Iran; label "imported from Iran, likely from Kashan (Cleveland's record) [2]". Highlight on B.
- **Look-for.** The dashed line marks a material, not an object.

### Station 2 — Blue before China (1:25–2:25, 60 s)

- **One idea.** Blue under a glaze was in Kashan's workshops a century before the jar; which mine is argued.
- **Visual.** Map zooms to an Iran inset. Object card: Cleveland 1915.581, footed bowl, late 1100s–early 1200s, CC0. Kashan dot; Qamsar and Anarak appear as fuzzy marks labelled "argued", with the two readings as captions. Timeline: a span "Kashan bowl, late 1100s–early 1200s" with faded ends; a mark at 1301, "Abu'l-Qasim's treatise".
- **Data.** R02, R03; claims 4, 5; object WS-021.
- **Prediction.** None.
- **Look-for.** Two fuzzy marks: an argued claim gets two readings, not one dot.

### Station 3 — By sea (2:25–3:30, 65 s)

- **One idea.** Monsoon routes carried crockery, earthenware and porcelain to Kilwa, and a text of the mid-1st century lists enslaved people among exports.
- **Visual.** Content note card (spec §2: steps 3–8), 5 s. Map, Indian Ocean framed. Three solid arcs draw into Kilwa from Arabia, Iran and China (origins placed by region). A wreck mark off Belitung with a solid arc from China's coast; label "linked, not shown". A people mark on the coast with the words "enslaved people listed among exports, mid-1st century CE; count not recorded". Timeline: spans "Kilwa coins, 11th–14th c." and "Kilwa's merchants, 13th–16th c."; a fuzzy mark "Belitung wreck, 830s or late 800s".
- **Data.** R04–R08; claims 6, 7, 8; nodes PLC076, PLC248; NET005, NET009, NET011.
- **Prediction.** None.
- **Look-for.** The people mark never shares an arrow with crockery.

### Station 4 — By land (3:30–4:35, 65 s)

- **One idea.** A skill travels as a dated line; people moved by order are a mark of their own.
- **Visual.** Content note card, 4 s. Map, Central Asia framed. Dotted grey route Chang'an → Tianshan (R09) draws; label "5,000 km, 33 sites". Dotted grey skill arc Central Asia → Baghdad (R10); Talas as a fuzzy "argued" mark. People marks: Central Asia and Persia → China, tie drawn apart (R11). Timeline: span "corridor formed 2nd c. BCE–1st c. CE, used to the 16th c." with faded ends; a mark at 794–95, "Baghdad paper mill, earliest documented"; a fuzzy mark at 751, "Talas, argued".
- **Data.** R09–R11; claims 9, 10, 11; NET001, NET003, DIA001, TEC184.
- **One-tap prediction (timeline).** "The earliest documented paper mill in the Islamic world: which band?" Options B06, B07, B08. Pause 4 s. Reveal: B07, 794–95, Baghdad [10].
- **Look-for.** The skill is a dated line; the people are a mark of their own.

### Station 5 — Across the sand (4:35–5:40, 65 s)

- **One idea.** Gold crossed the Sahara, and the argued question is whether demand made the routes or routes made the goods.
- **Visual.** Content note card, 4 s. Map, Sahara to Cairo framed. Dashed accent arc Sahel → Cairo (R12), label "gold, 1324–25". A people mark in the Sahara, words only (R13). Majorca dot with the Catalan Atlas caption quoted as the mapmaker's (R14). Then the question card: two readings side by side, "demand built the routes" and "routes made the goods", each with the evidence this station gave. Timeline: marks at 1324–25 and 1375.
- **Data.** R12–R14; claims 12, 13, 14; NET010, POL036, AS-003 (link out).
- **Prediction.** None.
- **Look-for.** The caravan line and the dated gold: hold both readings of how they relate.

### Station 6 — Across the Pacific (5:40–6:50, 70 s)

- **One idea.** Silver went west and porcelain east on the same ships, and so did people, on terms the goods never had.
- **Visual.** Content note card, 4 s. Map re-centres on the Pacific (the world wraps). Chain over the map: Potosí → Manila (silver, dashed) → Acapulco (porcelain, silk, ivory, spices, solid) → Puebla (the basin). Object card: Met 12.3.1, basin, Puebla, ca. 1650, public domain. People marks at Manila and Mexico with a dotted amber tie, drawn below the goods arcs, labelled "enslaved people from many communities, grouped as chinos; from 1672 counted as Indians". Timeline: span "galleons, 1565–1815" from the Met essay's title, marks at ca. 1650 and 1672.
- **Data.** R15–R18; claims 15, 16, 17; object WS-022; NET036, PLC244, PLC176, PLC163, MAT029.
- **One-tap prediction.** "How much of the silver mined in New Spain and Peru went to Asia?" Options: a tenth, a third, nine tenths. Pause 4 s. Reveal: "as much as one-third", by one estimate, probable [15].
- **Look-for.** The people mark beside the silver arc, never on it.

### Station 7 — The blue goes north (6:50–7:55, 65 s)

- **One idea.** The blue leaves China as export ware, and a skill reaches Arita with the people who were taken.
- **Visual.** Map, East Asia to Europe framed. Object card: Rijksmuseum AK-RBK-16385, dish with two birds, c. 1600–1625, CC0. Solid accent arc China → Amsterdam labelled "route not recorded" (R19). Dotted grey skill arc Korea → Arita (R21) and, apart from it, people marks Korea → Arita with the name Yi Sam-pyeong (R20). Timeline: a span "Kraak dish, c. 1600–1625" with faded ends; marks "1590s, invasions" and "early 1600s, Arita".
- **Data.** R19–R21; claims 18, 19; NET054, PLC203, OCC071, OCC072, PER191.
- **One-tap prediction (timeline).** "When was the dish made?" Options B08, B09, B10. Pause 4 s. Reveal: B09, c. 1600–1625 [18].
- **Look-for.** The people mark from Korea to Arita carries a name where the record gives one.

### Station 8 — The Atlantic (7:55–9:15, 80 s)

- **One idea.** The count is given in words, the goods as type, and one man by his name and his own text.
- **Visual.** Content note card, as learners see it (spec §2), 8 s, with the skip line. Map, Atlantic framed, dimmed; **no line, no arc and no mark for the 12.5 million**: the count appears as type, "about 12.5 million people departed, 1501–1866; about 10.7 million disembarked (SlaveVoyages' estimate)". The goods list as type (R23–R26): Indian cotton cloth; Manchester copies; brass manillas; guns, gunpowder and alcohol marked "probable". Then two named dots, Futa Toro and the Carolinas, with "Omar ibn Said" and no arrow (R27). Object card: Library of Congress 2018371864, manuscript, 1831, public domain; the caption as AR-081 sets it. The title page is not shown and not quoted. Timeline: span "1501–1866" with hard ends (the estimate's own bounds); marks 1807, 1831, "1863 or 1864, argued".
- **Data.** R22–R27; claims 20, 21, 22; NET035, NET055, OBT159, MAT027, PLC127, PLC360.
- **Prediction.** None, by design.
- **Look-for.** His two places as dots with his name, and no arrow.

- **Prediction.** None.
- **Look-for.** The four boxes: each flow in this lecture has a different verb.
