The edict · sixth month of 1842
Single sheets of actors, courtesans and geisha concern public morals. Their publication, and the sale of existing stock, are forbidden.
Paraphrase of the transcribed wording; the Japanese text is read in full before narration is recorded. In the eleventh month: a price cap of 16 mon a sheet, seven or eight colour impressions at most, nothing larger than a triptych. The three theatres are moved to Saruwaka-chō; Danjūrō VII is banished (probable). C-EDO-080 · C-EDO-081

Before the reforms
Actors and beauties are the trade's two best-selling genres; an ordinary ten-colour sheet sells for about 24 mon; the three theatres stand at Sakai-chō, Fukiya-chō and Kobiki-chō in the centre of the city. Mizuno Tadakuni becomes senior councillor in 1839. Drag right.

Which genre did the edict leave alone as morally uplifting?
{{verdict}}
History and warrior subjects were read as instruction in loyalty, so they passed; the trade put the banned actors' faces on them. Landscapes were not exempt by name, they were simply not named, which is why Hiroshige's road and Hokusai's mountain became the century's safest products.
