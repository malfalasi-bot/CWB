Edo Japan and the floating world 江戸

How does a city turn pictures into a mass market, and who gets named when it does?

A print was never one hand. A publisher planned it and answered for it, a designer drew it, a carver cut it, a printer pulled two hundred a day, and a buyer paid a bowl of noodles for it. The state stamped it, banned half of it, and capped its price. Then the world bought it by the hundred thousand.

Content note · shown before three stations and one interactive
The pictures' subjects were women under indenture in a licensed quarter. The course names them as people, gives the contract's documented terms, and shows both scholarly readings without ruling. Nothing is used as decoration. A skip is always offered. Shunga is named once as a fact of the trade and not shown.
