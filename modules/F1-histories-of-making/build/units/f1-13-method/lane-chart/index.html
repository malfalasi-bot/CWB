Porcelain clay found at Arita

The Met reads it one way, and the date is contested: Korean potters found porcelain clay at Arita early in the 1600s. The canon and Korean sources: the potters were taken by force in the Imjin War, 1592–98; the find is a later story with its own date.

Contested wording (AR-083): our verb is "taken"; the Met's "came" is quoted as the Met's. The potters' move is a tick in the people lane; the clay find places nothing on the chart.

      The reading

      The cobalt for the jar's blue was imported from Iran, likely from Kashan.

      Cleveland's record for the jar, made at Jingdezhen in the 1300s, says its cobalt was imported from Iran, likely from Kashan. The focus reads that one sentence as four ticks. A century before the jar, the Kashan bowl, late 1100s–early 1200s, was painted under the glaze with a blue rim; its bar has fuzzy ends because the holder's "late" and "early" are not years.

      In 1301 Abu'l-Qasim's treatise placed lajvard near Kashan. Which mine, Qamsar or Anarak, is contested (F1.13-C04), so the mine is a card and only the treatise's year is a tick. Jiang et al. 2020 tested imperial wares of the Xuande reign, 1426–35, and found imported cobalt.

      Between the bowl and the jar the route from Kashan to Jingdezhen is undated, so the journey draws dashed between two dated ends. Four of the lecture's stations fall inside the span, and the cursor on 1301 is the only clock on the page.
