# F1.13 Networks: step 6, the storyboard

Written 20 October 2026 (Claude Fable 5.1) against `research_notes/F1.13 method run/` (research.md, benchmark.md, inventory.json, plan.md) and the lecture data `build/lectures/src/lectures/f1-13.json`, to the moment grammar and schema of `scenes.md`. `scenes.yaml` beside this file is the data of record; this file quotes, beside every glyph, mark or micro-element, the content line that demands it. Quotes are the lecture's captions and narration (station n as "L.n") or the register's claim rows (F1.13-Cnn) or an inventory row (inventory.json). Places are the gazetteer's ids; every point carries its source line there, and the three "placed by region" points used here (Qamsar, Futa Toro, Joseon, China) are soft blots.

Rules applied throughout: open licences only, and no image is in the repo, so every scene ends on a frame that says "the holder's photograph, placed at build" with the holder's credit line (holder · accession · date · licence, from inventory.json); no invented placement, date, route or number; captions ≤ 25 words with sources and a claim id (`f13-<event id>` or `F1.13-Cnn`); the confidence word in the sentence wherever a value is below documented ("probable", "contested"); "earliest known", never "first"; a range is a bar, a circa a halo, a contested value a card over a dotted bar and never a tick or a clock; one camera move, one primary label and ≤ 3 others per shot; at most two highlight kinds on screen; glyphs only from the 14-glyph library in `scenes.md` (ship, junk, steamer, crate, pavilion, shop, book, fan, pigment, coin, soba bowl, flame, seal, bubble); the globe turns before a leg draws and never draws a leg behind the limb; dim, don't hide; `reduced` on every shot; the human-flow rule of research.md (a person is the chip, never a glyph riding a leg; a human count is a range on a card, never a bubble that grows or a crawl that climbs; a content note once; no goods grammar for people).

**Two schema extensions**, used on three scenes and flagged here for the editor: `content_note` at scene level (the protocol's level-2 note, shown once before the first shot, as the lecture does at `note` time 0) and `clock.claim` (the register id behind a readout the caption does not itself carry, so the lint can check the clock as it checks a caption). Where a caption carries two register values (a span with two documented ends) its `claim` is a list.

**One glyph the library lacks.** The lecture draws a "people mark" (its `map.people` and `map.peopleMark` commands) for people moved without a name: "three weaving communities, moved by Mongol order · names not recorded" (L.4), "enslaved people from many communities · grouped as chinos" (L.6), "Korean potters, taken · Yi Sam-pyeong named" (L.7). The six highlight kinds give a **person** (the chip, ink, no motion, the name) and the library gives no glyph for people, by rule ("no person glyph rides a coerced or trade leg"). For a named person (Omar ibn Said; Yi Sam-pyeong) the chip serves. For an unnamed group this storyboard uses a **people card**: a paper card beside the leg or at the place, ink, no glyph, carrying the lecture's words and "count not recorded" where that is so. It is not drawn as a new glyph; it is proposed to the editor as a seventh highlight kind ("people, unnamed: a card, ink, no motion, the group's own description; opens the content note and the claim"), because two of the four scenes and three of the nine stations need it. The jar motif (`jar`, plan.md) is the unit's identity motif on the band, not a scene glyph, and appears on no map.

**Where the globe must turn.** S2 twice (Potosí → Manila, 172°; Manila → Acapulco, 129°) over the Pacific; S4 once (China → Amsterdam, 79°). S1's one leg is 55° (Kashan to Jingdezhen) and one settle holds both ends; S3 turns nothing.

## S3 · Omar ibn Said

**A person's journey**, stored as kind `incident` because no leg is drawn. The human-flow rule's test. **Span** 1501–1866 (the count's span, which the clock covers on the before shot; his ticks c. 1770, 1807, 1831). **Surfaces** globe (world, the Atlantic, the lecture's focus 42° W 24° N) → timeline → stage. **Told** L.8; register C20–C22; AR-081. **Understand:** one man the record names, Omar ibn Said, wrote his own life in Arabic in 1831 while enslaved; the count of the trade is given in words, and no line is drawn for people.

**Why no journey line, and no seal-red path either.** Research.md: "a person is the chip, never a glyph riding a leg; a human count is a range on a card". Inventory j-omar's rule: "a person, the chip; content note AR-081; his name and his Arabic text lead; no glyph; no goods grammar". And the lecture's own closing line for the station: "Look for his two places, Futa Toro and the Carolinas: dots with his name, and no arrow." The indigo journey line is the grammar of goods and is excluded outright. The incident template allows "the consequence as one leg (a move)" in seal red, and the task allows a seal-red path if one is drawn; this storyboard draws none, because the lecture's line says "no arrow", because a seal-red line from Futa Toro to Charleston would still read as a route across the Atlantic that the course has just said it will not draw ("We draw no diagram: people are a count in words, never a line on a map", L.8), and because the one dated leg (1807) is fully told by a tick, a pulse at the place reached and his chip there.
