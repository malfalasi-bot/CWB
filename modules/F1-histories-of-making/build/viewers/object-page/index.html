Belt Buckle with Animals

A gilt bronze buckle, 202 BCE–9 CE, from the Ordos region.

Two long-eared animals fill a plate of gilt bronze, 6.1 by 11.8 cm. The record does not name the animals, and we do not guess a species. Who cast and gilded it is not recorded. Its record names one owner, Dr. Vladimir G. Simkhovitch of New York, who sold it to Cleveland in 1952. Where it was found is not recorded.

Content note: belt plaques like this are known mostly from burials. This buckle's findspot is not recorded, so it may have been taken from a grave. No images of burials or bodies are shown. You can skip the viewers and go to the record line; the autopsy keeps every date.

Made. The record's culture line: China, Ordos region, Western Han dynasty (206 BCE–8 CE). Its date line: 202 BCE–9 CE. The two Han start dates, 202 and 206 BCE, are both the record's own; the Historian pre-read checks them (F1.25 §9).

Maker: not recorded. The record's creators field is empty. Both readings of who made it are in the Labour layer above.

Not recorded. The record's find_spot field is empty, so the findspot is not recorded. How and when the buckle left China is not recorded either. Belt plaques like this are known mostly from burials, which is why the content note stands at the top of this page.

This is the earliest gap in the chain, and the one a return request would turn on.

Dr. Vladimir G. Simkhovitch. The record's entry, word for word: (Dr. Vladimir G. Simkhovitch [1874–1959], New York, NY, sold to the Cleveland Museum of Art), dated ?–1952. When he came to hold the buckle is not recorded; the question mark is the record's.

His dates beyond this record were not verified (F1.25 §10).
