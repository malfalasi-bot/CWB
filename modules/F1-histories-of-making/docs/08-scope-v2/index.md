## What "everything" means

Everything is reachable because the Atlas already has three depths. A node costs one register row at R3, a card and member grid at R2, and an authored walk only at R1. So the canon can list every entity the field names, and effort goes where a unit teaches.

- **R1:** taught in a unit, with a grouping page and a 6–9 step walk.
- **R2:** a card and a member grid; needs five or more verified members and a source.
- **R3:** a card generated from the register row, linking out to Wikidata, PeriodO, Getty or the UNESCO graph.

Every node starts at R3 at least. It moves up when its evidence is verified, never because it is famous.

The canon is the minimum, not the ceiling. Beyond it, Ring 3 search reaches every open record in the connected collections, and anyone can propose a new row. A row is added with a source, never with a guessed identifier.

## Civilisations: all 93 names

Every name a learner might search for as "X civilisation" gets a card and a grouping page. None is left as a bare search result. This keeps Atlas Logic Q7 intact: the word stays a label that opens onto the precise groupings beneath it, never the Atlas's frame. Covering all of them is cheap because the members already exist in the canon: the polities, cultures, horizons and networks each name is built from.

Each card carries four things: who uses the name, what sits underneath, what the name leaves out, and the argument about it. Nok's card, for example, says the name implies a state where the evidence shows farming communities. The "Western" card lists the African, Islamic and Byzantine roots the label borrows from and the colonised makers whose objects fill its museums.

Umbrella and loaded labels (Mound Builders, Pre-Columbian, Old Europe, Moorish, Western) are kept on purpose. Learners search for them, and their cards teach why the label is a problem. "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander" is not a self-description as a civilisation. Its card points first to the more than 250 language groups' own names and carries the consultation notice.

Thirteen names get a walk (R1), because a unit teaches through them:

- The eight already seeded: Indus Valley, Egyptian, Maya, Andean, Chinese, Islamic, Greek and Roman, Swahili.
- Western, for F1.3's "civilisation as an argument".
- Great Zimbabwe, for the myth that denied African builders.
- Nok, for "making does not need a civilisation".
- Mesopotamian, for F1.9a's account.
- Polynesian, so the Pacific has one.

## Objects: from 64 records to about 700–900 verified

The world-set register holds 64 objects today, 45 of them exemplars. Five layers take Ring 1 to about 700–900 verified objects, each with a licence, a 1970 provenance test and a sensitivity check. The layers overlap, so the total is an estimate to be confirmed by the first harvest.

| Layer | Rule | Estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Taught sets | 8–10 exemplars per world chapter, 10 chapters including F1.13 | 80–100 |
| Thematic units | About five per unit across F1.1–F1.5 and F1.14–F1.31 | 120–140 |
| World hubs | 20–40 reference objects per world (Deep Dives 1, option C) | 180–360 |
| Cell floor | At least one verified object in every sub-region × period cell that had people in it (502 of 506) | 150–250 beyond the layers above |
| Object-type floor | At least one verified example of each of the 323 object types | 80–120 beyond the layers above |

Ring 2 then takes every open record the harvest matches to a canon node, likely in the tens of thousands. Ring 3 is search across the connected open collections. At Ring 3, "all objects" means all the open ones, with the rest linked out.

Three rules keep this honest:

- **The record's bias is shown, not hidden.** Where no licensed object exists (most twentieth-century community-made work, much of Oceania), the cell shows a living practice, a drawing of our own or a link out. The gap is labelled as the record's gap.
- **Licence per record.** An anonymous maker or an old date proves nothing (finding 3.3). The Met's public-domain filter returns non-public-domain objects, so every hit is checked at record level.
- **The recall deck stays at 60.** It is the Orientation spine; world hubs get their own optional decks of about 20.

## The free source ladder

The registry lists 198 free routes. 103 were checked live on 28 September 2026: 74 fully and 29 in part. 60 are safe for commercial use, 103 are safe item by item, and 35 are non-commercial only. Each medium is taken from the highest rung that has it.

1. **Open museum records (CC0 or public domain):** Cleveland, the Met, the Art Institute of Chicago, Smithsonian items marked CC0, the Rijksmuseum, the National Gallery of Art, the Walters, SMK and Paris Musées.
2. **National open licences:** KOGL Type 1 (Korea, item by item), ColBase under Japan's Government Standard Terms, and the National Palace Museum in Taipei.
3. **Attribution licences (CC BY, CC BY-SA):** photographers on Wikimedia Commons, Tropenmuseum's 51,506 files, Auckland Museum's non-taonga images, Wellcome, and figures from open-access papers.
4. **Our own production:** drawings, maps, timelines, diagrams and working translations (§9).
5. **Link out, never host:** anything non-commercial, no-derivatives, permission-only, or restricted by a community's rules.

The data backbone is free and reusable: Wikidata and PeriodO (CC0), Pleiades and geoBoundaries (CC BY), the Getty vocabularies (ODC-By, credit required), Cliopatria, Natural Earth (public domain) and UNESCO's intangible heritage data.

Five findings from the licence checks change plans:

- **Many big "free" routes are non-commercial.** These are the British Museum (images and its Sketchfab models), the V&A, Gallica, Digital Bodleian, Smarthistory, EMKP, Seshat, the International Dunhuang Programme, the Qatar Digital Library, SlaveVoyages' estimates, and David Rumsey maps. Q28 decides whether F1 can ever host them.
- **Community rules override open licences.** Te Papa keeps taonga Māori images to research and education, and Auckland Museum keeps Māori and Pacific images to personal research. Digital Benin keeps each lending museum's licence. Korea's e-museum is non-commercial unless an item is marked KOGL Type 1.
- **3D is item by item.** Smithsonian models are CC0 only where marked. Sketchfab's CC0 programme and Scan the World vary by model. Only models marked CC0 are hosted.
- **Access has tightened.** JSTOR's free reading dropped to 10 articles a month. OpenAlex needs a key and gives $1 of free use a day. Trove bars downloading content. DOAJ now charges for up-to-date bulk feeds, while its API stays free.
- **Facts typed from memory go stale.** 19 of 124 UNESCO statuses in the first draft were wrong: elements moved between lists, were extended or were renamed. UNESCO status and licences now come from the harvest, never from typing (Q34).

The full registry, with the page each licence was read from, is the workbook's Sources sheet.

## Making visuals at zero cost

What cannot be licensed can often be drawn. Every asset type has a free route, and a rule that keeps it honest.

| Asset | Free route | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Object images | Ladder rungs 1–3 (§8), shown through IIIF deep zoom where the source offers it | Licence and credit line checked per record, each January when collections update |
| Maps and borders | Natural Earth base; geoBoundaries for modern borders; Cliopatria for historical polities; Pleiades for ancient places; drawn with MapLibre and our own tiles | Historical name first, modern country second; the borders layer states that it maps states only |
| Timelines, grids, networks | Our own SVG, drawn from the registers with D3 or Observable Plot | Date ranges never drawn as points; human flows never drawn as goods |
| Pattern and technique drawings | Our own line drawings of public-domain designs and our own operational-sequence strips | Drawn from several sources and labelled "drawing after"; never traced from one copyrighted photograph |
| 3D | Models marked CC0 (Smithsonian, Sketchfab's CC0 programme); others linked | NC models (the British Museum's) are linked, never embedded in a paid product |
| Sound | The National Jukebox's pre-1923 recordings; Freesound CC0; our own narration | 1923 line checked per recording; other countries noted per item |
| Film | Public-domain films on the Internet Archive (the Prelinger collection), Library of Congress films, NASA imagery; our own motion graphics from open images | British Pathé and Folkways are paid for reuse, so they are linked only |
| Type and script | Noto fonts (Open Font License) for every script F1 shows, which keeps the app ready for Arabic | None |
| Reconstructions | Our own diagrams, built from cited evidence and labelled as reconstructions | See Q32 on generated images |

One thing zero cost does not buy is a fresh photograph of an object we cannot license. There the Atlas shows the drawing, the practice or the link, and says why.

## All recommendations, modified and expanded

This list replaces my earlier recommendations. It keeps what held from tasks 25–27, including the licence routes, the export-law line and the colonial-context screen, and adds what the full canon makes possible.

**The Atlas and its pages**

1. Keep the ten world and network chapters, each with its device. Put breadth in world hubs: one hub page for each of the 46 sub-regions, built as a grouping page of kind "region".
2. Give all 93 civilisation names a card and a grouping page, with 13 walks (§3).
3. Raise grouping-page walks from the planned 60 to about 210: the 205 drafted, the six F1.20 promotions, and the civilisation changes.
4. Add twelve function strands, one walk per function across all worlds. "Care and access" carries the body-and-access lens.
5. Add technique strands: the 13 R1 techniques as operational-sequence walks, which F1.25's fifth layer and F1.28a reuse.
6. Load the Time surface's "first known" mode with the 174 claims. Independent inventions sit on parallel rows, as F1.5's simultaneity view needs.
7. End every world chapter on a living practice, chosen from the 214. UNESCO status is harvested, never typed.
8. Apply the human-flow rule to all 161 rows flagged for it, not only the Atlantic.

**Objects and data**

1. Build Ring 1 to about 700–900 verified objects in the five layers of §5, with a floor per cell and per object type.
2. Take every medium from the highest rung of the source ladder (§8); anything below rung 4 is linked, never hosted.
3. Add four fields to every world-set record: licence route (the six rungs), sensitivity class (the eight values in the canon), Notice or Label, and canon IDs (the groupings it belongs to).
4. Keep the colonial-context screen for objects made after 1800 in colonised regions. Keep the export-law line on every card (Iraq 1936, Egypt 1983, UNESCO 1970).
5. Let the harvest jobs match every canon row to Wikidata, Getty and PeriodO identifiers. No identifier is ever typed from memory. Licences are rechecked every January.
6. Treat the canon CSVs as the source of truth. The drafting scripts are archived, because rerunning them would erase the fact-check fixes.

**Specs, voice and review**

1. Deep-dive template v3 (Groundwork Q19 and Q23) also adds a responsibility block (which cells, object types and canon groupings the unit must reach), the "what it leaves out" line, and the four record fields above.
2. Historian pack v2 (Groundwork Q24) also briefs the Historian voice as a regional specialist for each world. It checks five things: a "leaves out" line on every grouping, no ranking of civilisations, the human-flow rule, the Q21 funerary rule, and the confidence grammar. It also flags any typed fact that should come from a harvest.
3. Before G3, fact-check every R1 row, every UNESCO claim and every licence. In the first pass, 1 error turned up in 120 randomly sampled rows, but 19 in 124 UNESCO claims.
4. Run sensitivity review the zero-cost way (§7), and hold back sacred and contested steps until a reader clears them.
5. Keep the regional slot (F1.31) locally authored. The Gulf and MENA expansion later draws on the same canon: Magan, Dilmun, Al Sadu, Hafit and Umm an-Nar rows are already in it.
6. Record Q27–Q35 in the Program Hub, the only place where decisions change.

## Delivery order

Nothing in the scope is cut. The order only decides what a tester sees first, and every phase adds to the one before. On day one, every one of the 2,430 nodes exists as at least an R3 card, because R3 cards are generated from register rows.

1. **Now, needing no decision:** set up the harvest runtime. Match the canon rows to Wikidata, Getty and PeriodO. Load the UNESCO intangible heritage dataset. Harvest Ring 1 candidates for the taught sets. Run the first object coverage report against the 506 cells.
2. **Before G3:** fact-check every R1 row. Write unit specs in batches on template v3. Write the 13 civilisation walks and the walks the units need first. Send F1.6, F1.12 and F1.13 to reciprocal reviewers.
3. **Atlas v1 (static):** Place, Time, Connections, Story and Grouping pages. All nodes at R3 or above, civilisation names at R2, and the taught sets and thematic-unit objects verified.
4. **Atlas v1.x, world by world:** world hubs, then the cell floor and the object-type floor, the remaining walks, and the function and technique strands.
5. **Later versions:** the Ring 2 harvest at scale, Compare, the calendar and lineage modes, and the Gulf and MENA vertical.
