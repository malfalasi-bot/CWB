## Q28 answered: free now, able to charge later

You said F1 is not charged today and you want to keep the option. So the rule is: host only media safe for commercial use by default, and make every other item switchable rather than excluded. Three mechanics do it.

1. **A licence class on every asset.** Each image, model, sound or text carries one of six classes: public domain or CC0; national open licence; attribution (CC BY or BY-SA); our own work; non-commercial by permission; link-out only. The class comes from the harvest, not from typing.
2. **Two builds from one dataset.** The open build may show items a holder has permitted for free education. The commercial build filters them out automatically. The day F1 charges, one setting changes and nothing is rewritten.
3. **A fallback recorded for every restricted item.** Every non-commercial or permission-only item names its replacement: an open object of the same kind, our own drawing, the living practice, or a link out. The switch never leaves a hole in a walk.

One caution, and I am not a lawyer: Creative Commons defines non-commercial by purpose, so a free course run by a company can still count as commercial. Until F1's legal home is settled, treat non-commercial licences as link-out and ask holders for permission instead (§2, option 2).

**The AP-250 test.** Of the 250 works, 182 (73%) had a canon grouping for their own culture or movement; 92% matched at least a broad grouping, technique or object type; 20 matched nothing. Ancient Mediterranean, West and Central Asia, and South, East and Southeast Asia were fully covered. Africa (50%), the Pacific (55%) and later Europe and the Americas (57%) were the weakest.

## Deep-dive template v3

Template v3 replaces v2 (Deep Dives 1 §7) for every remaining unit spec and deep dive. It keeps v2's twelve steps, folds in Groundwork's six cross-cutting pre-read fixes and the round-2 lessons, and adds three steps: people, visuals and a fact-check gate.

1. **Blind map.** What a specialist of this world or theme would insist on, plus the round-2 benchmarks for its area (the AP-250 content area; Global Design History; Heilbrunn).
2. **Responsibility block.** The sub-region × period cells, object types and canon groupings (civilisation names, communities, movements, networks) this unit must reach. Say which ones the taught path covers and which the world hub covers. The list comes from the workbook's grid, by canon ID.
3. **Protocol screen, before searching.** Never human remains, sacred or secret material, or anything a descendant community asks not to show. Other grave goods and altar objects are shown with their origin and a content note (Groundwork Q21). A Notice goes on every Indigenous community's material.
4. **Claims first.** Write 12–25 claims, each with a source, how deep that source goes, a confidence level, and both readings where contested. The chapter's device is declared as an interpretive claim. "Earliest known", never "first". No success criterion takes a side in a live debate.
5. **Nodes from the canon.** Every claim names the canon node that carries it. A missing node is added as a canon row, with a source and a review-log entry.
6. **Object verification.** Check the licence, the date, the 1970 test with the history-gap field, the colonial-context screen for objects made after 1800, the export-law line, the attribution as the museum records it beside our reading, and protocol. Outcome: exemplar, case or excluded. Also record the licence class, and a fallback for every restricted item (the licence switch).
7. **Living practice.** Its holders under their own name, sources dated by year, a consent route, and at least one source in the holders' own words. UNESCO status is harvested, never typed.
8. **People.** Name the makers where the record allows and write the credit line. Record who the credit leaves out, for F1.23's credit map.
9. **The walk.** 6–9 steps, each tied to claims and nodes, with content notes where needed.
10. **Visual plan, at zero cost.** For each step, the ladder rung the image comes from: open record, national licence, attribution licence, our own drawing, map or diagram, or a link out. No generated images of historical objects, people or places.
11. **Apply and the rest.** Apply by lane (Maker and Briefer), success criteria, three consumption forms, and links out.
12. **Readings.** The Historian's pre-read on pack v2, then a reciprocal reviewer. Sacred and contested steps are held back until a reader clears them; no paid reads.
13. **Writing check.** The twelve writing rules and the lint list: banned phrases, first-sentence length, and a claim for every sentence that states a fact.
14. **Fact-check gate.** Every R1 or R2 row the unit uses, every living person, every endonym, every UNESCO claim and every licence is checked at source. These rows err at 8–14% when drafted from memory.
15. **Registers.** The canon (new rows and review log), the world set, claims, practices, the link register, and a coverage-report row.
