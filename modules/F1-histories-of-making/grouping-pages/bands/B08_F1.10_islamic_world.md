**Overview**

A bowl the size of two hands was pierced through its walls before glazing. Light passes where the glaze filled the holes. Cleveland dates it to the late 1100s or early 1200s and places it in Iran, probably Kashan. The record credits "Iranian potters" and gives no names. Its body is fritware, ground quartz with glass frit and a little clay. Potters in Iran and Egypt had worked out that white body a century earlier.

Between 1000 and 1400 the makers of this world worked for courts, markets and endowments. The band is F1's counting device; no potter at Kashan knew the year 1200 as its midpoint. At Fustat, potters fired lustre and copied Chinese imports, and weavers in state tiraz workshops wove the ruler's name into cloth. From about 1250, glass-workers in Syria and Egypt enamelled and gilded lamps for endowed mosques and madrasas. The inlayer Muhammad ibn al-Zayn signed a brass basin. At Kashan, the Abu Tahir family signed lustre tiles; Abu'l-Qasim wrote the recipes down in 1301. In Khurasan, from the second half of the 1100s, brass-workers hammered silver and copper into engraved grounds. In al-Andalus, potters at Málaga and Murcia fired lustre, and Manises took the trade by 1400. In Fez, tanners and tile-cutters worked under guilds, and the Marinids' madrasas took zellige. In Anatolia, masons and tile-cutters built Konya's madrasas and caravanserais. Al-Jazari drew his machines for an Artuqid ruler in 1206.

Three sets of groupings frame the record. Fatimids (909–1171), Ayyubids (1171–1260) and Mamluks (1250–1517) in Egypt and Syria. Great Seljuks (1037–1194) and Ilkhans (1256–1335 or 1353) in Iran. Almoravids, Almohads, Marinids and Nasrids in the west. Each name is a historians' label, and the dates differ by source.

The gap is the hands. Deeds and signatures name patrons and a few masters. Mamluk endowment deeds set builders' wages, and Geniza letters record a Jewish trader's bronze workshop in India. The enslaved boys who became mamluks were people, never goods. For Iberia and the Maghrib the open record is thin: no verified open object sits in EU-IBE for this band.
