## Movement page: the Deutscher Werkbund (1907 to 1938; refounded 1947)

Twelve designers and twelve firms signed a call in Munich in 1907 to raise German goods on the world market, and quarrelled in public over whether to standardise.

**3. Before the name**

A roller-printed cotton, designed by William Morris in 1875, is in Cleveland. Its printers are not recorded. Hermann Muthesius had seen such things in London. He served at the German embassy there from 1896 and wrote "Das englische Haus" in 1904 to 1905, as most accounts have it. The English model was Arts and Crafts: honest materials, plain form, the maker respected. Back in Berlin, Muthesius taught applied art and worked for the Prussian trade ministry. In 1907 the electrical firm AEG hired Peter Behrens as artistic adviser. Behrens designed its turbine hall, kettles, lamps and letterhead, an early corporate identity. The same year the Wiener Werkstätte was four years old, and the Dresden Deutsche Werkstätten were making furniture by machine.

**4. The name**

The name was chosen at a hotel in Munich. On 5 and 6 October 1907, designers, architects, craft workers, industrialists, dealers and writers met at the Hotel Vier Jahreszeiten. They called themselves the "Werkbund", a federation of work. Twelve artists and twelve firms signed the founding call. Muthesius, a civil servant, had prompted it and stayed away. The stated purpose, in the body's own words, was "die Veredelung der gewerblichen Arbeit im Zusammenwirken von Kunst, Industrie und Handwerk". Our translation: the ennobling of industrial work through the cooperation of art, industry and craft. Its own site adds the aim beneath: better "German quality work" on the world market. The name came from inside.

**5. The makers it championed**

The Werkbund praised quality work and sold it under firms' names. The founding firms were a cutlery maker, a type foundry, two publishers, a weaver and workshops in Dresden, Munich and Vienna. The workers who ran the presses and looms are not recorded. At Stuttgart in 1927, the official catalogue named the building firms and trades before the architects, as IBA'27 reports. Local contractors with no experience of the new materials built 21 houses in under six months. Fifty-five designers furnished the 63 flats; histories keep a handful of their names. Lilly Reich was responsible for the hall exhibition. The canon's "leaves out" line for the 1914 debate reads: "the workers who made the products". The record agrees.

**6. Named hands**

Muthesius, van de Velde and Behrens are the names the label kept. Theodor Fischer was the earliest chairman, in 1908; Friedrich Naumann wrote the programme "Deutsche Gewerbekunst" that year. Behrens designed for AEG from 1907. Walter Gropius built the Fagus works and, at Cologne in 1914, a model factory. Bruno Taut built the glass house there and van de Velde the theatre. Theodor Heuss was managing director from 1918 to 1933 and in 1949 became the Federal Republic's president. Gustaf Stotz, the Stuttgart organiser, brought in Mies van der Rohe, appointed artistic director on 8 October 1925. Paul Schultze-Naumburg, a founding artist, left in 1927 and founded "Der Block"; his Heimatschutz line, db deutsche bauzeitung says, moved toward "Blut und Boden". In 1933 only Gropius, Wilhelm Wagenfeld and Martin Wagner voted against the Nazi statute.

**7. Institutions**

The Werkbund worked through yearbooks, exhibitions and estates. Its yearbook of 1914 printed the Cologne debate. The Cologne exhibition of 1914 showed Gropius's factory, Taut's glass house and van de Velde's theatre. The magazine "Die Form" ran until a last issue of 1935. The Stuttgart council approved "Die Wohnung" on 29 July 1926 by 25 votes to 11. A society ran the show from November 1926 to February 1928. Ground was broken on 1 March 1927 and the show opened on 23 July 1927. Seventeen architects under Mies built 21 houses with 63 flats; about 500,000 people came in four months. Four more Werkbund estates followed to 1933. The Werkbundarchiv – Museum der Dinge in Berlin holds the papers today.

**8. Reach**

The federation reached for the world market and got the state. Its own site says the goal was "eine hervorragende Position auf dem Weltmarkt" (a leading position on the world market) for German products. Before 1914 its members took orders from army, navy and civil authorities, a coverage source says. Weissenhof drew a world response in 1927, and five estates followed in Brno, Wrocław, Prague, Vienna and elsewhere. Then the state took the federation. In 1933 the Würzburg assembly adopted a statute placing it in the "nationalsozialistische Bewegung" under the leader principle. Carl Christoph Lörcher, of the NSDAP and the SA, took the chair.

Content note: this section names the Nazi years. In 1934 the Werkbund was incorporated into the Reich chamber of fine arts; in 1938 the chamber's president declared it dissolved. The Reich bought the Weissenhof estate in 1939 to demolish it; the war stopped the demolition. Ten houses were destroyed in the bombing. You can skip this paragraph; the dates stay in the claims.

**9. Against**

The founders set themselves against historicism and the decay of things. The Werkbund's own history calls the founding "ein Protest gegen Historismus und Kulturverfall" (a protest against historicism and cultural decay). It names the problem as "der Entfremdung des Produktes vom Schaffenden" (the alienation of the product from the maker). Muthesius, db deutsche bauzeitung says, fought "die wuchernden Gewächse des Jugendstils" (the rank growths of Jugendstil). In our words: the counter-positions were the revival styles of the Kaiserreich and the ornament of Art Nouveau. The counter-movement was not outside but inside, at Cologne in 1914, where the artists' group answered Muthesius (section 10).

**10. What is argued**

- *Type or artist, 1914*. One reading: Muthesius's ten theses. His earliest reads: "Die Architektur und mit ihr das ganze Werkbundschaffensgebiet drängt nach Typisierung". Our translation: architecture, and with it the whole field of Werkbund work, presses toward standardisation. The other: van de Velde's counter-theses, backed by Osthaus, Taut, Poelzig, Obrist, Endell, Gropius and Behrens with reservations. His earliest reads: "So lange es noch Künstler im Werkbunde geben wird ... werden sie gegen jeden Vorschlag eines Canons oder einer Typisierung protestieren". Our translation: as long as there are artists in the Werkbund, they will protest against any canon or standardisation. The body's own chronology says both sides shared "the high demand" for quality. We do not choose.
- *Dissolved by the Nazis, or aligned*. One reading: the Werkbundarchiv says the Nazi movement denounced its thinking as "Kultur-Bolschewismus" and dissolved it into the Reich chamber in 1934. The other: db deutsche bauzeitung says it "versuchte sich zu arrangieren" (tried to come to an arrangement) and was brought into line. It was formally dissolved only in 1938, and the Volksempfänger radio was "a Werkbund product". The Werkbundarchiv itself calls 1933 to 1945 an open research question. Both readings stand.
- *The end date*. Four candidates stand. 1932, where the body's chronology stops; 1934, the canon; 1938, the liquidator; or never, the archive's "interruption". The page keeps all four.

**11. Neighbours and counterparts**

Its model was the Arts and Crafts movement (MOV005) through Muthesius's England. Its sibling bodies are the Austrian Werkbund (1930 exhibition in Vienna) and the Swiss Werkbund, whose "Gute Form" (MOV025) it took up in 1949. Its founding firms include the Wiener Werkstätte (MKR077). Its school is the Bauhaus (PLC342), begun in van de Velde's Weimar buildings and promoted by the Werkbund. Its opponents inside were the artists' group of 1914 and Schultze-Naumburg's "Block" of 1927. Its target was Jugendstil (STY037).

**12. Afterlives**

The refounding came in two steps. Regional federations formed in Bavaria and Hesse in 1947. A meeting at Rheydt that year wrote the "Rheydter Manifest", and the federal body was refounded at Kloster Ettal in 1950. Theodor Heuss, its managing director of the 1920s, was by then president of the Federal Republic. The Weissenhof estate was listed in 1958 and restored in 1981 to 1987. The city of Stuttgart bought Le Corbusier's double house in 2002 and opened the Weissenhofmuseum on 26 October 2006. On 17 July 2016 UNESCO inscribed that house and its neighbour among seventeen works of Le Corbusier. The museum notes the estate itself is not listed. In 2019 the European Union gave the five Werkbund estates its Heritage Label. In 2007 the body counted about 1,640 members in six regional federations. The estate's centenary falls in 2027. "Werkbund" is the body's own name in 2026; we report that and do not endorse it.
