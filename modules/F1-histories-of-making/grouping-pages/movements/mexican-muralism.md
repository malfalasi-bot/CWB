## Movement page: Mexican muralism

A ministry gave painters walls in 1921 and 1922, a union of painters wrote the programme in 1923, and the three names that carry the label stand in front of the assistants, the plasterers and the women.

**3. Before the name**

A skeleton in a top hat runs a printing press on a sheet of 1902. José Guadalupe Posada engraved it for the publisher Antonio Vanegas Arroyo, who is the skeleton. The Met holds the sheet and marks it public domain. Memórica, the Secretaría de Cultura's archive, records a habit. Both Orozco and Rivera stopped at Posada's workshop on their way to the Academia de San Carlos to watch him work. His prints were the cheap public art before the walls.

In September 1910 scaffolding stood in the new amphitheatre of the Escuela Nacional Preparatoria. The painter Gerardo Murillo, Dr Atl, had put it up. The Revolution began weeks later; the murals were never painted. In 1911 students of the Academia struck against its teaching. In 1921 the new state created the Secretaría de Educación Pública with José Vasconcelos at its head. The same year it invited Roberto Montenegro to paint the walls of the former church of San Pedro y San Pablo. Dr Atl painted, erased and repainted there too; his mural did not survive.

**4. The name**

The walls were the state's; the programme was the painters'. Vasconcelos held the ministry from 10 October 1921, the Colegio's page says. In 1922 he invited Rivera, Fernando Leal, Jean Charlot, Ramón Alva de la Canal and Fermín Revueltas to decorate the Preparatoria at San Ildefonso. Siqueiros and Orozco joined them. Memórica calls the union's announcement at the end of 1922 "the date considered foundational". In 1923 the painters formed the Sindicato de Obreros Técnicos, Pintores y Escultores with Siqueiros as secretary general. Its manifesto, dated 9 December 1923, went out as a poster and flyer. Its paper *El Machete* began in March 1924, and the manifesto appeared in its seventh number in the second half of June. The name "muralism" is historians'; the painters called themselves workers.

**5. The makers it championed**

The manifesto named no maker. It addressed "the Indian race, humiliated for centuries" and praised "the art of the people of Mexico" as collective. The Colegio's pages name the assistants. Rivera's *La Creación* of 1922, in encaustic, was painted with Carlos Mérida, Xavier Guerrero, Amado de la Cueva and Jean Charlot. Alva de la Canal's and Revueltas's walls "were painted with the support of the Otomí artist Máximo Pacheco". Siqueiros worked with Guerrero and de la Cueva. Siqueiros called Guerrero "the sage of the group", Memórica reports, for his command of tools and materials. No source opened names the plasterers who laid each day's lime for fresco. The gap sits in the archive, not on the makers.

**6. Named hands**

Three names carry the label and the record holds more. Rivera painted the amphitheatre in 1922. From 1923 to 1928 he painted the 235 panels of the Secretaría de Educación Pública. Alicia Azuela calls it the earliest mural with social content he made. Orozco painted the north walls of three floors of San Ildefonso's main patio between 1923 and 1926. Later he painted the chapel of the Hospicio Cabañas in Guadalajara, which UNESCO lists. Siqueiros painted the stair of the smaller patio and left it unfinished. Leal, Charlot, Alva de la Canal and Revueltas painted the walls of 1922 to 1923. Montenegro painted the tree of life. Two women stand outside the three names. Aurora Reyes won a commission through the League of Revolutionary Writers and Artists in 1936. She painted *Atentado a las maestras rurales* at the Centro Escolar Revolución. It is held to be the earliest known mural by a Mexican woman. María Izquierdo signed a contract in 1945 for the stair of the Departamento del Distrito Federal. It was cancelled.

**7. Institutions**

A ministry, a school, a union and a newspaper carried the movement. The Secretaría de Educación Pública, founded in 1921, paid for the walls and then stopped paying. After Vasconcelos left, his successor Bernardo Gastélum cut the budget and left the painters without work, El Universal records. The Escuela Nacional Preparatoria at San Ildefonso was the laboratory of 1922 to 1926, the Colegio says. The union and *El Machete* set the programme. In June and July 1924 students, "apparently Catholic" in Memórica's words, damaged the murals of Siqueiros and Orozco. Guerrero and Siqueiros mounted armed guards and were then dismissed. In 1992 the restored Colegio opened as a museum under a three-party mandate of UNAM, the federal culture body and the city.

**8. Reach**

The movement served the state and said so. The manifesto's last paragraph declared the union's support for the candidacy of General Plutarco Elías Calles. The walls were public buildings, the pay was the ministry's, and the painters' dismissal in 1924 came from the same source. Rivera later took commissions from "large private corporations", including abroad, Memórica notes. The reach ran outward through those commissions and through the Taller de Gráfica Popular's prints from 1937. It had run inward too: Charlot came from France and Mérida from Guatemala. The Chicano movement in the United States (MOV068) took the wall as its medium from 1965.

**9. What is argued**

- *When it began.* One reading is Memórica's. Montenegro's *El árbol de la vida* at San Pedro y San Pablo, from Vasconcelos's 1921 invitation, is the movement's earliest mural. The other is the Colegio's and the city's. The collective work at San Ildefonso from 1922 "is considered the birth of muralism" for its debates, collaboration and experiments. A third bound, Dr Atl's 1910 scaffolding, is a project and not a wall. We give all three.
- *Why Izquierdo's commission was cancelled.* One reading is the officials' of 1945. There were no safe conditions for the work, and the theme was "far from a shared history", as the INBAL summary reports. The other is Izquierdo's and most historians'. Rivera and Siqueiros told the regent she lacked the technique, and he cancelled. Nancy Deffebach writes that "there are elements of truth in the arguments for and against". She lists other factors: competition for walls, the two men's lack of commissions, the site's prestige. One more: Izquierdo's plan to show modern Mexico as a woman. We do not choose.
- *A revolutionary art or a state art.* One reading is the union's. It wanted an art of "propaganda ideológica en bien del pueblo", for the people and against the bourgeoisie. The other is in the same record. The walls, the wages and the dismissals were the state's, and the manifesto endorsed a presidential candidate. Siqueiros himself, Memórica reports, later judged the early murals "mystical, populist and even philanthropic" and short of "a subversive or revolutionary impulse". Both readings stand.

**10. Neighbours and counterparts**

Its neighbours sit in the same state programme. One is the Exposición de Artes Populares of 1921 (OCC193), which Dr Atl and Montenegro organised. Another is the Best Maugard drawing method taught in schools from 1923 (SCH006). Its avant-garde neighbour is Estridentismo (MOV057). Its printed counterpart is Posada's broadsheet (PER083) before it and the Taller de Gráfica Popular (MKR035) after. Its parallels abroad are the Federal Art Project murals of the United States and the Chicano movement (MOV068). Its counter-position is the Academia's easel tradition and the "art of the coterie" the manifesto repudiated.

**11. Afterlives**

The walls are museums now, and the women are being named. San Ildefonso has been a museum since 1992 and marked the centenary with "El espíritu del 22" from 10 December 2022 to 12 June 2023. The Hospicio Cabañas is on UNESCO's World Heritage List. In 2025 the Museo Vivo del Muralismo, in the SEP building, hung Izquierdo's *La música* and *La tragedia* among Rivera's and Siqueiros's walls. It named its library for Aurora Reyes, "considered the first Mexican muralist" in the newspaper's words. Memórica's centenary dossier of 2022 lists the union's members, two women among them. "Mural" sells as a city-branding word and a tourist route in 2026; we report that and do not endorse it.
