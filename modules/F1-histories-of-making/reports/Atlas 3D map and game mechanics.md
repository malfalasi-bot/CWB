# Ink the map, play the evidence

The F1 Atlas should become an animated 3D hub, but not a "game" in the arcade sense, and not a cloud of 4,228 glowing dots. The evidence and the precedents point to one design. The Atlas is a paper-and-ink globe on which the world chapters are soft territories and the thematic units are lenses. Only place-anchored nodes appear as marks, and they are clustered by zoom. Learning is shown as the map getting inked, never as points. Entering a unit parts a veil of golden clouds onto that unit's own historic sheet, the 1859 map for Edo. The meta-game sits on top of this hub. It treats knowledge as the save file, in the manner of *Outer Wilds*' ship log and *Obra Dinn*'s book of fates. In the Edo unit, three things justify real 3D or 2.5D because space is the argument there. The first is a "Print the Wave" workshop with an exploded block stack. The second is multi-plane "step into the view" scenes cut from public-domain Hiroshige prints. The third is a single uki-e perspective box. The game that would actually win attention, and that no existing title the research found offers, is 2D: a Censor's Desk and a Catalogue Desk where learners date and attribute prints by their seals, built on mechanics proven in *Papers, Please*, *Return of the Obra Dinn* and *The Case of the Golden Idol*. The learning evidence supports this split. Gamification reliably helps cognition (g ≈ .42–.49) but not motivation, and expected, completion-contingent rewards depress later voluntary engagement (d = −.36). Content that is the mechanic, rather than a quiz wrapped in fun, produced about seven times more voluntary play. A high-fidelity 3D history tour taught facts less well than a 12-minute lecture. Award juries split into craft-first and purpose-first camps. An accessible, phone-first WebGL Atlas with a full text twin can compete in both, because the craft juries do not ask for accessibility and the purpose juries prize it. Everything below fits zero cost, the allowed licence list and the Artifact runtime. One constraint changes the architecture: each published Artifact has its own origin and its own browser storage, so the Atlas cannot read what a learner did inside a unit unless the unit hands it back explicitly.

## A globe that gets inked, not a cloud of 4,228 dots

Brand-hub globes, culture spaces and game world maps each teach the Atlas one thing. GitHub's globe uses no textures. Its land is about 12,000 circles in a single `InstancedMesh`, arcs are revealed with `setDrawRange`, and antialiasing is switched off to protect frame rate ([GitHub Blog](https://github.blog/engineering/engineering-principles/how-we-built-the-github-globe/)). Stripe chose a sphere because it needs **about 17% of the area of a flat map**, cut 60,000 dots to about 20,000 vertices, and prepared a static fallback in case 60 fps could not be held ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/blog/globe)). Both suit a 390 px phone and F1's "routes draw" motion rule. The British Museum and Google's Museum of the World is the closest ancestor of the Place–Time–Connections triad. It laid out 4,500 objects across time and region with connections lighting up, but it was WebGL and desktop-only, and its Awwwards usability score was 6.84 ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/the-museum-of-the-world); [Experiments with Google](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/the-museum-of-the-world)). F1 can beat it by being phone-first and accessible. The game maps supply the progress mechanic that the house rule against confetti needs. In *Civilization VI* unexplored land is drawn as cartographer's parchment that recedes into colour as you explore ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/civilization-vi/civilization-6-art-trailer)). In *Ōkami*, completed brush feats restore cursed land to colour ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ckami)). *Ghost of Tsushima* replaced waypoints with a Guiding Wind so players stay "immersed in this world instead of just more compasses and mini-maps" ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/ghost-tsushima-creative-director-interview)). Its Kurosawa Mode shows that a whole-world style preset can be a respected, art-directed feature rather than a filter ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/ghost-of-tsushima-kurosawa-mode-explained-how-it-changes-the-game-and-why/)).

Three structural decisions turn those lessons into an atlas rather than a cloud. The first concerns the data. The canon register holds **4,228 nodes in 25 kinds**: techniques, materials, styles, periods, calendars, communities, people, places, events and more. Most of those kinds have no single point location, so the globe should not try to plot them. Only place-anchored kinds become marks. Techniques, materials, functions and beliefs stay what the Atlas Logic decision already made them, lenses that recolour any surface. The thematic and method units become lens layers over the same globe rather than extra places. Networks lights the routes. Copying draws copy-chains as dashed threads. Industry pulses the exhibition cities. The planetary present (F1.22) runs extraction flows from mine to city. The museum unit (F1.24) draws provenance arcs from source to collection, a reverse-route view of real force. Re-timing (F1.30) relabels the time scrubber in another calendar. The geographic world chapters (F1.6–F1.12) are drawn as soft, brushed territories rather than pins, and where they overlap, the Islamic world over Africa and Iberia for example, the overlap shows as translucent washes. That overlap teaches that a "world" is a reading of the past, not a border. The GMap research on map metaphors for graphs supports territories over node-link spaghetti. Users "spend significantly longer time studying the maps" and find structure unprompted, although clusters can fragment and dense graphs force false adjacencies ([Gansner, Hu & Kobourov](https://www2.cs.arizona.edu/~kobourov/pacvis10.pdf)).

The second decision is three semantic-zoom tiers. At world tier the globe shows the unit territories and a few dozen major routes, with no node marks, and about a dozen labels at once on a phone. That ceiling is a design heuristic, not a finding. At region tier nodes appear as `supercluster` bubbles by kind, with counts and expansion zooms (ISC licence) ([supercluster](https://github.com/mapbox/supercluster)). At unit tier the globe hands off to a tilted flat sheet carrying the unit's own historic map, with nodes placed in that map's coordinates. This copies MapLibre's adaptive approach, which switches from globe to Mercator near zoom 12 because the globe is a zoomed-out view and close work belongs on a flat map ([MapLibre globe guide](https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/blob/main/developer-guides/globe.md)). Every unit change uses one van Wijk–Nuij camera path: zoom out, travel, zoom in, with duration scaled to distance ([van Wijk & Nuij](https://vanwijk.win.tue.nl/zoompan.pdf)). The default treatment of time is a scrubber that draws routes as time plays. Route-heavy units can tilt into an optional space-time cube using Bach and colleagues' extraction and flattening operations ([Bach et al.](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/a-descriptive-framework-for-temporal-data-visualizations-based-on)). That cube should stay at a few dozen paths, because Kraak's open question is whether readers can parse it when many are shown ([Kraak 2003](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2003/Papers/255.pdf)). The existing uncertainty grammar carries into 3D without new vocabulary. Soft-ended date ranges become extrusions that fade, contested claims become dashed arcs drawn in the shader, confidence becomes ink density rather than hue, and approximate places become soft ink blots rather than pins.

The third decision is the transition into a unit, and the source material supplies it. Rakuchū rakugai-zu screens hang golden clouds over the city, "while scattered openings reveal notable landmarks" ([MLIT](https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/R6-00038.html)). Tapping East Asia flies to Japan. The clouds (kinun) part onto the Leventhal 1859 *Kaisei On'edo oezu*, which is public domain ([Leventhal](https://collections.leventhalmap.org/search/commonwealth:kh04p664d)), or onto the Library of Congress sheet the v3 build already ships. The veil also hides the swap in level of detail and the texture load. Each world gets its own veil and "material pack" (paper, line, palette, type), switched on entry while the hub stays a neutral archive paper. That keeps any single culture's look from becoming the default, the way Kurosawa Mode changes look but not rules. Packs may change materials and type only, never layout, encoding or interaction, and WCAG contrast must be checked per pack. Packs for the Islamic, Pacific, African and Americas chapters must come from specialists and communities. The notes found no sourced cartographic model for them, and Pacific wayfinding is living knowledge.

Two conflicts with earlier decisions need settling here. Pass 2 v2 specified "a dark canvas for the Globe". The first redesign study then criticised the Edo prototype for fading a cream raster into a navy pane nineteen times per read. The resolution is a paper globe by default, with "night paper" only as a dark-mode token set applied to every surface at once. The second conflict is the Pass 2 scenario in which the learner's guess of where a Jingdezhen bowl ends up "is scored against the route". Under the second report's rule it becomes a ghost line drawn against the true route, with no score.

### What the Artifact runtime allows, and the one thing it forces

The stack that fits is three.js (MIT; measured at **about 186 KB gzip** for a realistic scene with loaders and controls, 289 KB for the WebGPU build) ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/three/latest)), bundled with the existing Vite build. globe.gl/three-globe (MIT) is optional for arcs and HTML label layers ([globe.gl](https://github.com/vasturiano/globe.gl)). Geometry comes from Natural Earth, which is public domain ([Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/)), baked into small TopoJSON files and one land texture. The 2D Place surface stays d3-geo, as the v3 unit already does. Several tools are ruled out. Cesium's default imagery comes through the ion token service ([gist](https://gist.github.com/banesullivan/e3cc15a3e2e865d5ab8bae6719733752)), so it cannot be used. OSM tiles are both remote and ODbL. MapLibre with live OpenHistoricalMap or Terrarium tiles was recommended by the first redesign study but breaks the no-runtime-fetch rule, so any tiles must be pre-baked and shipped as published files. deck.gl's GlobeView is still marked experimental ([deck.gl](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/core/globe-view)). The OpenHistoricalMap licence also needs a ruling before any of its layers are baked in. The first study's table calls OHM CC0 ([OHM Reuse](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/OpenHistoricalMap/Reuse)), while its own stack table says to keep "ODbL OpenHistoricalMap data" as rendered tiles only. Until one layer-by-layer check settles it, treat OHM as excluded.

Performance budgets are well established. Aim for **about 100 draw calls and 100k vertices** on mobile, textures no larger than 2048 px unless they are hero assets, and DPR capped at 2. A single 4096² texture costs about 85 MiB of VRAM with mipmaps ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips), a secondary source). Only 77% of Android devices support 8192 px textures ([web3dsurvey](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgl2/parameters/MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE)), so historic sheets ship at 2048 for phones and 4096 elsewhere. WebGPU now ships in all four major engines, but coverage is incomplete, so WebGL 2 is the guaranteed path ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers)). A hand-built hub of about 20–40 draw calls fits these budgets. It needs a paper-shader sphere, one land texture, the territories in a single `BatchedMesh`, all routes merged into one geometry with a per-vertex time attribute revealed by a uniform, one `InstancedMesh` per marker family, and a DOM label overlay. The 25 kinds collapse into about six families for marking. Accessibility has to sit outside the canvas. WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7 requires a non-drag alternative, and its own example is a map with arrow buttons ([W3C](https://w3c.github.io/wcag/understanding/dragging-movements)). Accessible web maps pair the canvas with a synced list, focusable markers and about 10–20 tab stops ([Equal Entry](https://equalentry.com/accessible-maps-on-the-web/)). In practice that means Tab cycles the visible territories rather than 4,228 nodes, arrow keys rotate, Enter enters a unit and Escape climbs a tier. The list view is primary, not a fallback. Under reduced motion every flight becomes a fade-cut, routes appear rather than draw, and the guiding current becomes a static dotted thread.

The constraint that reshapes the design is storage. Each Artifact has its own origin, so localStorage written by the Edo unit is invisible to the Atlas, and a 35-unit programme cannot fit in one 255-file publish. Progress therefore has to travel by hand-off. A unit's closing "Rebuild" screen links back to the Atlas with a compact token in the URL hash naming the nodes and edges engaged. The Atlas stores the token in its own storage, wrapped in try/catch, and inks those nodes. Learners can export and import a "field notebook" code to move between devices. This costs nothing and needs no backend. It also makes inking something the learner carries, which suits the Never Alone principle discussed below. Shared cohort density can only come from the runtime's shared-state capability where that is enabled for the programme. It should be deferred until it is.

## Only three things in Edo deserve to be 3D

The case against a walkable 3D Edo rests on evidence, not only on budget. In the Université de Montréal study, knowledge rose **44% after Assassin's Creed's Discovery Tour against 51% after a 12-minute teacher lecture**. Students said a teacher helped them see what mattered, and that they would not use the tour on their own ([Variety](https://variety.com/2018/gaming/news/assassins-creed-origins-discovery-tour-effectiveness-1202861325)). Across 69 samples, games beat non-game instruction by g = 0.33, but a single session showed **g = 0.08 against 0.44 for multiple sessions** ([Clark, Tanner-Smith & Killingsworth 2016](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315582065)). Mayer's review lists science, mathematics and second languages as the domains where games outperform, and history is not on that list ([Mayer 2019](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/abs/10.1146/annurev-psych-010418-102744)). Chapman's distinction between "realist" and "conceptual" simulation gives the rule. Conceptual simulation uses abstractions such as maps and presents history as constructed ([Thersites review of Chapman](https://thersites-journal.de/index.php/thr/article/download/93/164/671)). 3D earns its cost where spatial structure is the content: the stacked colour blocks of a print, the depth bands of a composition, the geometry of a route. It does not earn it as a recreated street.

Ukiyo-e itself argues for 2.5D. The prints are flat colour planes, keyblock outlines and stacked bands of space. True one-point perspective appears only in the uki-e sub-genre, which began around 1739–40. Its practitioners favoured interiors because "it is easier to accurately apply one point perspective to architecture than to landscape" ([Wikipedia: Uki-e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uki-e)), as in Masanobu's 1748 kabuki-theatre view, which the Met releases as public domain ([Met 56534](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/56534)). *Old Man's Journey* won an Apple Design Award with a single puzzle: drag layered hills to join foreground to background ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Man%27s_Journey)). The *Pentiment* team deliberately flattened its landscapes "for clarity and visibility" instead of copying the stacked perspective of their sources ([GamesHub](https://www.gameshub.com/news/features/pentiment-interview-xbox-obsidian-entertainment-hannah-kennedy-art-director-34642/)). The production analysis estimates that a multi-plane scene cut from one print takes one to two days once the pipeline exists. A small stylised 3D street block takes weeks, and a walkable 1859 district takes months with a high risk of a generic "Japan" look. Those are reasoned estimates, not measured ones.

The engine choice follows from the file and licence rules. Godot 4's single-threaded web export avoids the COOP/COEP headers an Artifact cannot set. Its default .wasm, however, is **about 40 MB uncompressed** ([Godot](https://godotengine.org/article/progress-report-web-export-in-4-3)), more than twice the 16 MB per-file cap, and its UI exists only inside a canvas. Unity is free under $200k of revenue but proprietary ([Unity](https://unity.com/blog/unity-is-canceling-the-runtime-fee)). Rapier physics adds 1.1–1.6 MB gzip ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@dimforge%2frapier3d/latest)) for no teaching gain. GSAP is free but ships under a "no charge" licence that is not open source ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/gsap/latest)). Theatre.js core is Apache-2.0, but its editor studio is AGPL and its 1.0 has moved to a private repository ([GitHub](https://github.com/theatre-js/theatre)). The safe set is three.js with a hand-written tween and camera splines. The studio may be used locally for authoring only, if at all. The ukiyo-e look breaks down into cheap passes. The toon shader keeps two bands, "because more steps stop reading like print", and contours use `fwidth` with noise wobble ([Codrops "Still"](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/09/09/still-from-akira-to-ink-wash-building-a-generative-garden-in-webgpu/)). Outlines come from Sobel edge detection on depth and normals ([Maxime Heckel](https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/moebius-style-post-processing/)). The palette is a 16×16 colour atlas, as in abeto's *Messenger*, so mood can be retuned by editing one texture ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/messenger.html)). Paper grain is pinned to screen space so it does not swim, which is the lesson of Lucas Pope's sphere-mapped dither ([Pope devlog](https://dukope.com/devlogs/obra-dinn/tig-32/)). Horizontal kasumi mist bands restore depth, the readability lesson Shedworks learned on *Sable* ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/how-shedworks-refined-the-art-of-sable-in-pursuit-of-readability)). Assets are the team's own planes cut from CC0 Met and AIC prints, plus neutral bases from Kenney, Poly Haven and ambientCG, all CC0 ([Kenney](https://kenney.nl/support); [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/license); [ambientCG](https://docs.ambientcg.com/license)). Quaternius moved to a custom licence on 28 August 2026 ([Quaternius](https://quaternius.com/license.html)), and Sonniss audio is a proprietary EULA ([Sonniss](https://gdc.sonniss.com/)). Both now fall outside the allowed list.

Accessibility for these pieces is specified in the game guidelines. WCAG 2.3.3 names parallax as "often non-essential" motion that must be possible to disable ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). XAG 117 asks for no camera shake, bob or motion blur, plus adjustable field of view and the ability to disable automatic camera movement ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)). The Game Accessibility Guidelines rule out essential precise timing and simultaneous inputs ([GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/)). In Print the Wave this means keyboard nudges of 1 px and 10 px at the kentō marks, a snap assist, and no timer. Bokashi becomes a slider rather than a pressure gesture. Every 3D piece opens behind a "play / read" choice, and the scrollytelling path stays complete.

## Mechanics that teach are the historian's own tools

The gamification literature is clearer than its reputation suggests. Sailer and Homner's meta-analysis finds a cognitive effect of **g = .49**, which survives in rigorous designs at .42. The motivational effect (.36) and behavioural effect (.25) become non-significant once only rigorous studies count. The authors warn that "mere competition might be problematic", and the configurations that hold up are competition combined with collaboration, and game fiction ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/S10648-019-09498-W)). Wouters and colleagues found games no more motivating than conventional instruction (d = 0.26, not significant) ([ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1008015)). In Clark's data, solo competitive play came out at g = −0.06. Learners who dislike gamification give two reasons: it lacks utility, and it causes "anxiety or jealousy" ([Bai, Hew & Huang 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1747938X19302908)). A semester-long course with leaderboards and badges lowered motivation and satisfaction ([Hanus & Fox summary](https://educ201.wordpress.com/2014/11/24/assessing-the-effects-of-gamification-in-the-classroom-a-longitudinal-study-on-intrinsic-motivation-social-comparison-satisfaction-effort-and-academic-performance/)). Broken streaks cut repeat behaviour from about **66% to 58%** ([Psychology Today on Silverman & Barasch](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/202306/how-broken-streaks-sap-motivation)). That is exactly the wrong lever for adults who dip in irregularly.

Two findings decide what rewards may exist. In Deci, Koestner and Ryan's 128 studies, expected tangible rewards lower later free-choice engagement (d = −0.36). So do completion-contingent rewards (−0.36) and engagement-contingent rewards (−0.40), while informational verbal feedback raises it, most strongly for college-age learners (**d = +0.43**) ([Deci et al. 1999](https://home.ubalt.edu/tmitch/642/articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf)). The meta-analysis is contested ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589298)), but its direction matches Nicholson's warning that a reward loop must be maintained "forever" and that "play must be optional" ([Nicholson](https://scottnicholson.com/pubs/recipepreprint.pdf)). "Unit 12 complete" badges are completion-contingent and so fall in the harmful category. Feedback such as "the kiwame seal puts this before 1842" is informational and falls in the helpful one. The second finding is intrinsic integration. When Habgood and Ainsworth made the maths the core mechanic of *Zombie Division*, delayed test scores were 58% against 41% for the version with a quiz bolted on. In free choice, children played the integrated version for **75.7 minutes against 10.3** ([Habgood & Ainsworth 2011](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/3556/1/Habgood_Ainsworth_final.pdf)). Problem-solving before instruction helps secondary and university learners more than younger ones ([Sinha & Kapur via Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2021-09-productively-wiser.html)). Prequestions help the content they target (g = 0.54) but barely touch anything else (0.04) ([St. Hilaire et al. 2023](https://consensus.app/papers/details/ae47f3256d405351ba11ef75d04f388d/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). That supports the second report's rule of one optional guess per scene, made on the instrument and placed just before the reveal. The second report also called for a "Rebuild" recall at the end of each act and an optional confidence tap stored only locally. The evidence for confidence calibration was not verified in this round, so it stays optional and private.

Read against this evidence, the strongest asset of F1 is that its labs are already historians' practice. Dating by seal, comparing impressions and counting editions are real connoisseurship. Award-winning games supply a tested grammar for turning evidence into a claim without letting players brute-force it. *Return of the Obra Dinn* confirms fates only in batches of three ([Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/games/return-of-the-obra-dinn)). *The Case of the Golden Idol* signals only when "two or fewer slots are incorrect" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)). Jon Ingold of *Heaven's Vault* refused to confirm translations because "archaeology is not about getting things 'right'" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-inkle-s-i-heaven-s-vault-i-)). *Pentiment* never names its murderer ([Obsidian forums](https://forums.obsidian.net/topic/125999-pentiment-josh-sawyers-upcoming-historical-murder-mystery-rpg-set-in-16th-century-europe)). *Papers, Please* took its tension from a rule-book that tightens day by day, applied to documents an inspector checks "over and over again" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papers,_Please)). None of the existing ukiyo-e games teaches the publisher economy, censorship or seal dating. *Ukiyo-e* (2025) is a light set-collection game about Japonisme dealers ([BGG](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/444752/ukiyo-e)), and *Hokusai* is a rondel euro. That gap is where the Edo unit can be original.

Several catalogue proposals conflict with earlier decisions and need reconciling. The catalogue opens each act with a bet card. The second report dropped chapter-opening multiple-choice cards. The bet therefore moves to the end of a scene, just before its reveal, stays skippable, and is answered on the instrument with a ghost-versus-truth display. The "Thought Cabinet" earns lenses by completion. That is completion-contingent gating, and it collides with Atlas Logic's rule of one lens at Orientation and up to three at Mastery. The resolution is that every lens is always available. The unit that teaches a lens introduces it with a worked example. The learner's "kit" is a record of the lenses they have *practised*, which they may name and annotate. Bruno Simon's portfolio has achievements and a daily top-10 leaderboard ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)). Those are rejected. Only his bounded, moderated "whispers" survive, as a model for anonymous cohort density, and only once shared state exists. *Tokaido*'s "furthest-back player moves next" rule is a good facilitator pattern but needs a cohort. It belongs in a later seminar mode.

### Honesty and sensitive content as design rules

Gamifying reasoning can fabricate history unless every mechanic is bounded by explicit rules, and three of them carry most of the load. First, every choice-based mechanic ends on a "What actually happened" card. That card cites its sources and carries the claims collection's confidence tag (high, medium, low or contested), so a learner's bet is answered by the record and not by a simulation. Second, anything that did not happen is labelled "counterfactual" and links to F1.30a. Third, colour separations, depth-plane cuts, composite journeys and "a printer's typical day" are labelled reconstruction, composite or typical, with sources. These match the second report's reconstruction and simulation labels. The painful-history rules are firmer still. When *Playing History 2* made players stack enslaved people Tetris-style, the level was withdrawn amid backlash and criticism that it was "a dismal, tone-deaf failure at education" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playing_History_2_-_Slave_Trade)). The reference models run the other way. *Never Alone* unlocks documentary "cultural insights" co-authored with about 40 Alaska Native contributors ([Games for Change](https://www.gamesforchange.org/games/never-alone-kisima-ingitchuna/)), and *Attentat 1942* is built on testimony ([Attentat edu](https://attentat1942.com/edu/)). So the Yoshiwara contract stays read, not played, as two scholarly readings in columns with a content note first. It has no score, timer, outcome or collectible. Courtesan images never appear as Censor's Desk cards. The Tenpō category can be named in the rule-book text only, behind the content note. Shunga is named and never shown. The Censor's Desk must not cast coercion as a cosy puzzle. Wage and fine pressure appears as documented context, consequences for named publishers come only from the record, and the role must be named after whoever actually inspected in each period. That needs checking against Thompson's censorship essay in the *Hotei Encyclopedia* ([WorldCat](https://search.worldcat.org/title/61666175)) before any copy is written. The same caution applies across the programme. Pacific wayfinding must not become a fog puzzle to "solve", and colonial collecting must not become fun acquisition. The museum unit's cabinet should invert collecting into "should this be here?"

## Craft juries ignore accessibility, purpose juries prize it

The award landscape rewards different things in different lanes, and that decides where the effort goes. Awwwards weights **Design 40, Usability 30, Creativity 20 and Content 10**, and its evaluation page says nothing about accessibility or mobile ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)). The Webbys judge visual design partly on "improving inclusion for people with disabilities". They want interactivity that is "more than a rollover… allows you, as a user, to give and receive", and an experience that loads quickly ([Webby criteria](https://www.webbyawards.com/judging-criteria/)). D&AD Digital Design judges "execution over idea" and has an Inclusive subcategory ([D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/awards/d-ad-awards/categories-2025/digital-design)). Games for Change gave its 2025 Accessibility Award to *Botany Manor* for "no time limits and adjustable controls" ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/games-for-change-announces-2025-award-winners/)). Its 2026 Best in Learning went to *The Darkest Files*, a game of archival source evaluation shipped with lesson plans ([CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/games-for-change-2026-award-winners/); [Paintbucket](https://paintbucket.de/en/projekte/the-darkest-files)). That is the lane the Catalogue Desk and Censor's Desk could enter. The comparables show the craft bar. Getty's *Persepolis Reimagined* won FWA of the Year by making one master scene drive every view, giving every scene the same post-processing, and naming its curators and specialist advisers ([Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-getty-persepolis-reimagined.html)). abeto's Igloo Inc was Awwwards Site of the Year 2024 ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards-2024/site-of-the-year)). Its *Messenger*, a 15-minute, one-finger, "no-stress" game on a tiny planet with seven places, is listed as 2025 Developer Site of the Year on the annual winners page ([Awwwards winners](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards/winners)), although abeto's profile calls it Site of the Year, so the exact title needs checking. The London Museum won Museums + Heritage Best Use of Digital 2025 for linking objects and stories and for "generous sharing of insights" ([M+H](https://awards.museumsandheritage.com/awards/2025-winners/best-use-of-digital-uk-5/)). The Met Unframed, by contrast, drew criticism as "low-level edutainment" for gating art behind puzzles on screens too small for it ([Artnet](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/two-hours-inside-met-unframed-1936671)). That is the gate F1 must not build. Several programmes are closed and should not be planned for: MUSE has been on hold since 2023 ([AAM](https://www.aam-us.org/programs/awards-recognition/media-technology-muse-awards/)), Japan Media Arts held its last awards in 2022 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Media_Arts_Festival)), and Malofiej paused in 2021 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malofiej_Awards)).

These findings imply a short list of concrete changes. Most of the Edo unit's narrative and instrument problems were addressed by the v3 build and the second report's component grammar. What remains is mostly about scope, distinctiveness, credibility and parity, as the table below sets out.

The notes also surface factual hazards to clear before anything is built. The production note's "Print the Wave" meta-layer gives a Tsutaya-style shop an *aratame* seal. On the unit's own seal chronology that seal dates from 1853, more than fifty years after Tsutaya. The award note's "spot Prussian blue" and its 1829–30 arrival date are unsourced. The catalogue itself warns that pigment identification needs laboratory methods, so the Edo unit should teach dating by seal and trademark, and present pigment as conservation-science evidence. No source gives the number of colour blocks in *The Great Wave*, so the workshop's separation must be labelled a reconstruction until that is found. The Edo figures used in the game proposals (Kuniyoshi's 8,000 sets, 200 sheets a day, the 1824–28 blue prices, the seal periods) come from the project brief and were not re-checked in this round.

The order follows value per unit of effort, and it puts the distinctive, HTML-only learning ahead of any 3D. That was the catalogue's own conclusion, and it agrees with Clark's finding that repeated short encounters, not one large set-piece, carry the effect. All effort figures are the researchers' estimates, not measured data. Before committing to the shaders, the team should run a one-week spike measuring frame time on two or three real phones.

Measurement closes the loop, because the motivation evidence is too weak to trust by default. Each optional mechanic should be A/B tested on return visits and on delayed recall in the Rebuild, never on time-on-page ([Sailer & Homner](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/S10648-019-09498-W)). The first study's read-aloud sessions with five to eight learners stay mandatory before P2. Awards come after evidence. The order is Webby (Websites and Mobile Sites: Education/Cultural, Best UI or Visual Design) once the Edo unit and the P1 globe ship. FWA and Awwwards follow for the globe and Print the Wave, then Information is Beautiful for the Atlas visualisation. G4C Best in Learning comes into reach if the Catalogue and Censor's Desks grow into a standalone game with a lesson plan. Good Design Award eligibility for a free course is unverified.

## Conclusion

The question changes once the evidence is in. "Make it a 3D game" assumes that immersion teaches, but the strongest data say immersion without the historian's method teaches less than a short lecture. Making the method itself the mechanic, on the other hand, multiplied voluntary play sevenfold. The Edo unit's most award-worthy game is therefore the plainest one, a desk where learners read seals. The 3D budget belongs only where space is the argument: the block stack, the depth bands of a view, the uki-e box and the route-bearing globe. Juries and the evidence converge on the same quality markers. A tightly scoped loop of about 15 minutes, one material language, seamless transitions and visible expertise satisfy the craft lane. Accessibility parity is the cheapest differentiator, because the craft juries do not ask for it and the purpose juries do.

The runtime forces a second insight. Because each Artifact keeps its own storage, the Atlas cannot quietly observe a learner across 35 units. It has to be told, through a hand-off the learner carries, and that turns out to be the more honest design. Progress becomes an inked map that belongs to the learner, built from evidence they actually handled, with no points to chase and nothing to lose by stopping. The risks now sit in content rather than code. They are anachronisms such as an aratame seal in Tsutaya's shop, unsourced dates and costs, the right name for the inspector, and the licence rulings on OFL fonts and OpenHistoricalMap. Clearing them before the first shader is written is what separates an award entry from an elegant error.
