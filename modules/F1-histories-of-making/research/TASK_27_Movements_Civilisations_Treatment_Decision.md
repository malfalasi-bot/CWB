## Part 1: Definitions & Scope

### What is a "Civilisation" in F1 Atlas context?
A **civilisation** = a geographically-rooted culture with:
- Named polity or cultural identity (e.g., Nok, Chola, Benin, Iznik, Jingdezhen, Minoan, Maya, Indus Valley)
- Recognizable material signature (ceramic style, iconography, technology, language)
- Distinct time span (flourished for centuries+; WS-043 "Egyptian civilization" versus WS-016 "Meroitic civilization")
- Documented succession or cultural inheritance (e.g., Chola metalwork → Tamil Nadu metalwork tradition)

**Examples in current register:**
- **Nok** (F1.6): Nigeria, 1500 BCE–turn of era; terracotta figurines, funerary context
- **Indus Valley** (F1.5 & F1.9): c. 2500 BCE seals; undeciphered script
- **Chola** (F1.9): South India, c. 900–1300 CE; lost-wax bronze casting, religious patronage
- **Maya** (F1.5 excluded case): Mesoamerica, 2000 BCE–1600s CE; hieroglyphic writing, calendrical astronomy
- **Iznik** (F1.10): Ottoman Turkey, 1450–1700s; blue-and-white ceramics, architectural tiles
- **Jingdezhen** (F1.13): China, 1300s+; blue-and-white porcelain, imperial monopoly
- **Benin** (F1.6 & F1.13): West Africa, 1400s–1897; brass plaques, royal court, colonial seizure
- **Paracas** (F1.5 & F1.7): South Peru, 800–100 BCE; embroidered textiles, bundle burials
- **Wari** (F1.7): South Peru, 650–1000 CE; tapestry tunics, administrative expansion
- **Mediterranean (Attic)** (F1.11): Greece, 6th–5th century BCE; black-figure pottery, workshop organization
- **Diné** (F1.7): North America, Navajo; weaving, horse-adoption, contemporary tradition

**NOT in register but could be:**
- Minoan (Mediterranean, no objects)
- Mycenaean (Mediterranean, no objects)
- Cahokia Mississippian (Americas, only landscape)
- Egyptian New Kingdom (Egypt has 3 objects scattered across chapters)
- Islamic Caliphate (scattered across F1.10, F1.13)
- Sung/Ming China (multiple objects in F1.8, F1.13)

---

### What is a "Movement" in F1 Atlas context?
A **movement** = a trans-geographical practice, aesthetic, or economic system that crosses cultural/political boundaries:
- **Mingei (folk crafts):** Japan, 1920s+; philosophy of anonymous beauty; crosses ceramics, textiles, metalwork
- **Indian Ocean trade network:** 1000–1800s; connects Africa, Arabia, India, Southeast Asia, China; shows comparative scarcity model
- **Waqf (Islamic endowment):** religious legal system affecting architecture, ceramics, water systems; spans Mediterranean to Central Asia
- **Silk Road / Long-Distance Trade:** 1000 BCE–1800s CE; textiles, metals, ceramics across Eurasia
- **Horse domestication & pastoralism:** Central Asia, 3500 BCE+; spreads via steppe peoples to Mediterranean, India, Europe
- **Arts and Crafts movement:** Europe/America, 1880s–1920s; reactions to industrialization; connects Brocard, William Morris, Mingei aesthetics
- **Camelid domestication & Andean textile tradition:** South America, pre-Columbian; connects Paracas, Wari, Inka via fibre arts
- **Mamluk aesthetics:** Mediterranean/Islamic, 1200–1500s; ceramics, glass, architecture; military-administrative network
- **Colonialism & collection:** 1500s–1900s; affects provenance, repatriation, museum holdings (WS-010 Benin plaque, WS-X04 Nok head, WS-X03 Maya vase)

**Current treatment in Atlas:**
- **Mingei:** Appears as support material concept; no dedicated chapter (blocked by copyright)
- **Indian Ocean trade:** F1.13 Networks shows connections; objects sparse
- **Waqf:** F1.10 mentions endowed building (Iznik lamp); not explicitly taught
- **Silk Road:** Implied in F1.12a, F1.13; no dedicated treatment
- **Horse domestication:** F1.12a chapter dedicated to it; weak in objects
- **Arts and Crafts:** Not explicitly taught; appears in Brocard lamp and Mingei philosophy

## Part 3: How Other Programs Treat Civilisations & Movements

### Benchmark A: Museum Wing Model (e.g., Metropolitan Museum of Art)
**Structure:** Dedicated galleries by civilisation/period
- **Egyptian Wing:** 2,000+ objects organized by dynasty
- **Islamic Art Wing:** Carpets, ceramics, glass, metalwork organized by region/period, not by movement
- **American Wing:** Organized by region/time, then by type
- **Asian Art Wing:** China, Japan, Korea organized by period, not by movement

**Approach:** Each civilisation gets a dedicated space with objects organized chronologically and by type. Movements (trade, migration, influence) shown through labels but not through dedicated sub-galleries.

**Implication for F1:** Civilisations would need 3–5+ objects each to justify a chapter.

---

### Benchmark B: Academic Textbook Model (e.g., "The History of Art" survey)
**Structure:** Chapters organized by theme OR period OR region
- **World Archaeology survey books:** Often use chapters for broad regions (Mediterranean, Africa, Americas) with sub-sections for major civilisations (Minoan, Classical Greece, Hellenistic)
- **History of Technology:** Organized by technology (metallurgy, weaving, printing) with sub-sections on civilisations that innovated (Hittites for iron, Jingdezhen for porcelain)
- **History of Trade:** Organized by trade network (Silk Road, Indian Ocean) with embedded civilisations (Chola, Iznik, Jingdezhen shown as nodes in network)

**Implication for F1:** If organized by theme (e.g., "Casting," "Weaving," "Writing"), civilisations appear as examples. If organized by movement, civilisations appear as nodes.

---

### Benchmark C: Encyclopaedic Atlas Model (e.g., British Museum object-by-object model)
**Structure:** Each object gets a detailed card with context including civilisation, period, material, technique, trade connections
- British Museum online: Each Benin plaque card mentions "Benin Kingdom, 1400–1897" but does NOT have a dedicated "Benin Kingdom" chapter; kingdom information is on each card
- Smithsonian online: Similar; each item contextualized but not compiled into a civilisation chapter

**Implication for F1:** Civilisations taught via cross-referenced object cards; no dedicated deep-dive unless objects appear across multiple chapters and require synthesis.

---

### Benchmark D: Educational Model (e.g., Khan Academy Art History)
**Structure:** Mix of short videos on civilisations + thematic lessons on techniques/trade
- **Civilisation videos:** 5–15 min overviews of Nok, Egypt, Maya, Islamic World with 2–5 representative objects each
- **Thematic lessons:** "Trade in the Indian Ocean," "Metalworking Techniques," "Women Artists"
- Objects appear in both contexts

**Implication for F1:** Civilisations could get short deep-dives (500–1000 words) with 2–3 objects each, plus separate thematic deep-dives (1000–2000 words) on movements.

## Part 4: Current F1 Atlas Structure & Constraints

### Current Model:
**Chapters F1.5–F1.13 + F1.13 Networks are organized by THEME, not by civilisation or geography.**
- F1.5: Writing (cuneiform, Shang bronze, Paracas, Indus seals, oracle bone)
- F1.6: Metals (Benin brass, Egypt copper tool, gold weights, Meroitic cup, Nok head — excluded)
- F1.7: Fibre Arts (Paracas textiles, Wari tunic, Diné blankets)
- F1.8: Technologies (Shang, Jun, Goryeo, Jōmon — unclear theme)
- F1.9: Metalworking (Chola bronze)
- F1.9a: Trade & Exchange (ceramics, glass, metals from West Asia, Mediterranean, Egypt)
- F1.10: Islamic World / Endowments (Iznik, Kashan, Egypt cover, Brocard, glass)
- F1.11: Mediterranean & Europe (Attic black-figure, Delft, Wedgwood)
- F1.12: Deep Time / Indigenous (Carpenter's Gap, Lapita, Madjedbebe, wayfinding practice)
- F1.12a: Horses & Mobility (Luristan bits, samite roundels, Pazyryk, Sintashta)
- F1.13: Long-Distance Trade (Jingdezhen, Puebla, Catalan Atlas, networks)

**Problem:** Chapter themes are neither purely thematic (not "Metalworking" throughout) nor purely geographic/civilisational (not "Islamic World" exclusively in F1.10).

**Implication:** The current structure is **hybrid and transitional**—it vacillates between theme and geography, making it unclear whether a new civilisation chapter should be geographic (e.g., "Benin Kingdom"), thematic (e.g., "Royal Workshops"), or movement-based (e.g., "Slave Trade Routes").

## Part 5: Analysis — Can Civilisations Be Taught Without Dedicated Deep-Dive Chapters?

### YES, if:
1. **Objects are cross-referenced across chapters** (e.g., Chola bronzes appear in F1.9 "Metalworking" with a link to "F1.X Chola South Indian Metalworking" brief)
2. **Context cards detail civilisation info** (e.g., each Nok object card says "Nok Culture, central Nigeria, 1500 BCE–turn of era; funerary context; Frankfurt excavation shows burial ritual; Lagos Museum holds comparative collection")
3. **"Grouping pages" aggregate related objects by civilisation** (e.g., "Nok in the Atlas: 1 exemplar case + 2 contextual papers + 1 exclusion case; what each teaches")
4. **Movements are emphasized over civilisations** (e.g., "Chola Kingdom as node in Indian Ocean metalworking network" rather than "Chola Kingdom as independent civilization")
5. **Comparison is built in** (e.g., "Iznik and Jingdezhen: Two Imperial Ceramic Workshops Compared" as a movement-level deep-dive, not two separate civilisation chapters)

### NO, if:
1. **Learners expect a dedicated civilisation chapter** for every major culture (Nok, Chola, Benin, Maya, Egypt, China, etc.)
2. **Civilisation-specific knowledge is taught** (administration, politics, succession, gender roles, labor, daily life — none currently exemplified in objects)
3. **Objects must be grouped by civilisation, not by theme** (would require reorganizing F1.5–F1.13)
4. **Independent deep dives are required** to show "breadth of objects, concepts, influences, effects, scale, impact" per user request

## Part 6: Recommended Approach

### Decision: **Hybrid Model with Thematic Chapters + Civilisation Deep-Dive Briefs**

**Structure:**
1. **Keep F1.5–F1.13 as thematic chapters** (Writing, Metals, Fibre Arts, Technologies, Metalworking, Trade & Exchange, Islamic World, Mediterranean, Deep Time, Horses, Long-Distance Trade)
2. **Add 3–5 civilisation-focused deep-dive briefs** (750–1500 words each) for civilisations with 2+ objects in the Atlas:
   - **Nok** (F1.6): "Funerary Sculpture as Political Authority—Contested Origin, Settled Dating"
   - **Chola** (F1.9): "Sacred Bronzes, Royal Patronage, and Metalworking Guilds"
   - **Iznik** (F1.10): "Ottoman Imperial Ceramics and the Waqf System"
   - **Jingdezhen** (F1.13): "Imperial Monopoly, Export Markets, and Blue-and-White Porcelain"
   - **Benin** (F1.6 & F1.13): "Royal Plaques, Colonial Seizure, and Repatriation Cases"
3. **Add 2–3 movement-focused deep-dive briefs** (1000–2000 words each):
   - **Indian Ocean Trade Network** (F1.13 Networks): "Chola Bronzes, Kashan Ceramics, and Swahili Trade Routes"
   - **Waqf and Endowed Buildings** (F1.10 & beyond): "Iznik Tiles, Religious Architecture, and Civic Authority"
   - **Horses, Mobility, and Cultural Exchange** (F1.12a & beyond): "Steppe Pastoralism, Chariot Warfare, and Animal Art Across Eurasia"
4. **Link all objects to both their thematic chapter AND their civilisation/movement brief** (via cross-reference cards)
5. **Grouping pages aggregate civilisation-level cases** (as already designed for Nok, Mingei, Igun Eronmwon)

**Advantage:** Maintains thematic clarity while adding civilisation context; avoids creating under-resourced civilisation chapters (current Nok has only 1 case, Chola has only 2 bronzes).

## Part 7: Implementation Rules for Historian Pack v2

### Rule P13: Civilisation Identity
**When to flag an object as belonging to a civilisation:**
- Object dates within civilisation's known flourishing period (e.g., Nok, 1500 BCE–turn of era)
- Object shows recognizable material/stylistic signature of that civilisation (e.g., Nok terracotta, Chola bronze, Iznik blue-and-white)
- Civilisation has 2+ objects in the Atlas

**Required fields:**
- `civilisation_name`: "Nok" or "Chola" or null
- `civilisation_period`: "1500 BCE–turn of era" (or years only if approximate)
- `civilisation_location`: "central Nigeria" or "Tamil Nadu, South India"
- `civilisation_context_brief`: "Funerary figurines show ritual authority" or "Lost-wax casting for temple bronze ritual"

**Example (WS-X04 Nok head, excluded):**
```json
{
  "civilisation_name": "Nok",
  "civilisation_period": "1500 BCE–turn of era (actual date 20–620 CE, post-Nok)",
  "civilisation_location": "central Nigeria, Plateau State",
  "civilisation_context_brief": "Thermoluminescence dated 20–620 CE, after Nok culture ended per Frankfurt excavation chronology; first recorded Brussels dealer 1994",
  "sensitivity_flag": "funerary",
  "exclusion_reason": "post-civilisation date; belongs to 'Late Iron Age Nigeria' not Nok"
}
```

---

### Rule P14: Movement Identity
**When to flag an object as belonging to a movement:**
- Object participates in a cross-civilisation practice (e.g., Indian Ocean trade, waqf patronage, horse adoption, long-distance exchange)
- Object demonstrates technique or aesthetic that crosses political boundaries (e.g., blue-and-white porcelain adopted across Islamic world)
- Multiple objects (2+) in Atlas show the same movement

**Required fields:**
- `movement_name`: "Indian Ocean Trade" or "Waqf Endowment" or "Horse Domestication" or null
- `movement_scope`: "regional" (within 1000 miles) or "continental" (Eurasia) or "global" (multiple continents)
- `movement_period`: "1000–1800 CE" (when movement was active)
- `movement_role`: "producer" or "trader" or "consumer" or "adapter" or "innovator"

**Example (WS-020 Jingdezhen jar, F1.13):**
```json
{
  "movement_name": "Chinese Porcelain Export Trade",
  "movement_scope": "global",
  "movement_period": "1300s+",
  "movement_role": "producer",
  "movement_context": "Yuan blue-and-white for Islamic and European markets; source of European imitation (Delft)"
}
```

**Example (WS-040 Iznik lamp, F1.10):**
```json
{
  "movement_name": "Waqf Endowment System",
  "movement_scope": "continental",
  "movement_period": "1100s–1800s",
  "movement_role": "commissioned_object",
  "movement_context": "Mosque lamp made for endowed building; shows continuity of Islamic architectural patronage across Ottoman empire"
}
```

### Rule P15: Funerary & Ancestral Material Rule
**Decision on how to show burial/funerary context:**

**IF civilisation's primary material signature is funerary (e.g., Nok terracotta figurines from burials, Paracas mummy-bundle textiles, Egyptian coffins):**
- **INCLUDE the funerary context in the card** (e.g., "Nok figurines were placed in burial chambers to mark grave sites and commemorate authority")
- **MARK as sensitivity flag:** "burial_context" or "ancestral_material"
- **ADD consultation note:** "Nok objects not repatriated; held in African museums and public collections; no known current custodian requesting return"
- **TEACH the practice:** "Funerary Art as Political Authority" or "Burial Ritual as Social Hierarchy"

**IF object is funerary but civilisation also has non-funerary material:**
- **PREFER non-funerary exemplar** (e.g., use living Chola temple bronze instead of funerary figurine; use household Nok pot instead of burial Nok head)
- **IF only funerary example available:** Mark as sensitivity flag and note gap in open-access corpus

**IF object is funerary and civilisation is otherwise unknown:**
- **TEACH with care:** Flag as "burial/funerary context," note that knowledge comes from excavation/tomb, acknowledge indigenous perspectives on ancestor disturbance
- **EXAMPLE:** "Teouma Lapita dish, Vanuatu; funerary context; EXCLUDE—ancestral material, Lapita people may object to display; teach via academic paper on mortuary practices instead"

**Current status:**
- **Nok head (WS-X04):** Excluded; funerary, post-Nok date
- **Paracas textiles (WS-050, WS-051, WS-052):** Included; bundle burial context taught as intentional practice
- **Cahokia Mound 72:** Excluded; burial remains
- **Madagascar lamba (WS-? — to-check):** Flagged as burial shroud; needs consultation
- **Teouma Lapita (WS-032?):** To-license; flagged as funerary; recommend exclusion

**Recommendation:** Include funerary context when it is THE primary way we know the civilisation (Nok, Paracas, Egyptian, Cahokia); teach it as intentional social practice, not as disturbing ancestors. BUT exclude human remains (Cahokia Mound 72 burials, Teouma Lapita intact grave goods). And flag all funerary for sensitivity.

## Part 8: Synthesis & Final Recommendation

### Summary of Decision:
**The F1 Atlas should NOT create independent civilisation deep-dive chapters (F1.14, F1.15, etc.).** Instead:

1. **Keep F1.5–F1.13 as thematic chapters** (current model proven effective)
2. **Add civilisation context cards to grouping pages** (e.g., "Nok Grouping," "Chola Grouping," "Iznik Grouping")
3. **Create 3–5 civilisation briefs** (750–1500 words) for Nok, Chola, Iznik, Jingdezhen, Benin
4. **Create 2–3 movement briefs** (1000–2000 words) for Indian Ocean Trade, Waqf Endowments, Horse Domestication/Steppe Mobility
5. **Link objects bidirectionally** (each object links to its thematic chapter AND its civilisation/movement brief)
6. **Implement Rules P13–P15** in Historian pack v2 to standardize how civilisation and movement identity are recorded

### Why NOT Separate Deep-Dive Chapters:
- **Insufficient objects per civilisation:** Nok (1 case), Chola (2), Iznik (2 provisional), Jingdezhen (1)
- **Thematic organization more effective:** Showing metalworking across Chola, Benin, Luristan, Mediterranean teaches technique diversity better than 5 separate civilisation chapters with only 1 object each
- **Movements can be taught as networks:** Indian Ocean Trade (F1.13 Networks) naturally connects Chola, Kashan, Jingdezhen across chapters
- **Already designed for this:** Grouping pages (Nok, Mingei, Igun Eronmwon) show object relationships within a civilisation without requiring a dedicated chapter

### Why INCLUDE Civilisation Context:
- **Learner need:** Students expect to know "where did this come from? What civilisation made it?"
- **Historical responsibility:** Attributing objects to Nok, Chola, Benin, etc. honors their cultural heritage
- **Enables comparison:** "How did Chola metalworking differ from Benin? From Mediterranean?" requires civilisation-level context
