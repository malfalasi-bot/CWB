# Shared brief: benchmarking for the F1 content audit (3 October 2026)

You are researching benchmarks for one group of products, for an expert audit of "F1 Histories of making", a module of a creative-industries programme. The module teaches the world history of making (objects, movements, civilisations, places, networks, events, concepts) through an interactive Atlas (map, timeline, connections, grouping pages, stories) and 38 units. The course lead's concerns, which your research must speak to:

1. The module is too object-heavy; movements (art, design, craft, creative-industry movements), civilisations and places, events and the world timeline are thin.
2. The unit method relies on "guess, then find out" (prediction prompts, typed answers, guess-then-reveal labs). The lead doubts learners will type answers or work backwards, and wants interactives that **show ideas visually**: animated lectures for timelines, civilisations and movements (2.5D and parallax animation, 3D object interaction, mini interactions on a map).
3. Content must be **dense** (everyone learns everything; no light path for now).
4. Zero-cost rule: everything we build uses free or open-source tools and open-licence content (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA, ODC-By, open-source software).

## How to work

- Open the product itself where you can (its site, docs, a sample page, its GitHub). Where you cannot, say "not opened" and rely on a dated secondary source.
- For each benchmark record, in a table: name · maker, year, status (live/defunct) · what it is · **how it is structured** (its units of content, how time/place/theme are organised, scales, navigation) · **its interaction model** (what the user does: scrub, click, drag, answer, build, watch) · content density (how much per screen; narrative vs reference) · what to borrow for F1 · what to avoid · licence or cost of its software and content · URL(s) opened.
- Then write 300–600 words of synthesis: patterns across the set, which interaction models suit a dense visual programme, and the three or four things F1 should adopt first, with reasons.
- Quote no more than 25 words from any source. Never compose a URL by hand: use only URLs a search or fetch returned.
- Tools: WebSearch (its session budget may be exhausted; if a call fails, switch), WebFetch, and via ToolSearch: Exa (`mcp__Exa__web_search_exa`, `mcp__Exa__web_fetch_exa`), Tavily, Parallel Search, Firecrawl. Don't retry a failing tool in a loop.
- Save your report as Markdown to the path given in your task, with a "Sources opened" list (URL · what it is · date opened) at the end. Edit no other file.
- Report back in chat briefly: the count of benchmarks, the three or four adoptions, and anything surprising.
