# Benchmarks: atlases, timelines and historical maps

Group report for the F1 "Histories of making" audit. Date of research: 3 October 2026. 21 benchmarks; all opened directly (site, docs, sample page or GitHub) unless marked "not opened".

Reading key for the two tables below:
- **Scales** = how many distinct levels of zoom in *place* (world / region / country / site) and in *time* (era / century / year / day) the product exposes to the user.
- **Uncertainty** = whether fuzzy dates or disputed borders are *modelled* in the data and whether they are *shown* on screen. Most viewers model nothing; the gazetteers model it but rarely render it.

## Synthesis

**Patterns.** The set splits into three families. *Snapshot-grid atlases* (Met, TimeMaps, Smarthistory, and in print the Vitra Atlas) fix a small set of period bands and cross them with regions; every cell is dense, editorially written, comparable sideways, and navigated by clicking, not scrubbing. *Continuous time-scrub viewers* (Chronas, Running Reality, OHM, Histography, ChronoZoom, the YouTube genre) are data-driven, visually seductive and text-poor; they resolve time to the year or day, which forces hard borders and silently discards uncertainty, and their narrative is outsourced to Wikipedia or to a "tour". *Backbones* (Getty, PeriodO, Wikidata, Pleiades, WHG, Seshat) are where the field actually models movements, periods, places and fuzziness, with open licences (ODC-By, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA), but they render almost nothing. No benchmark combines all three; the two that come closest, Chronas (map + slider + article drawer + Epics) and ChronoZoom (zoomable timeline + authored tours), are the ones whose interaction models best fit a dense, visual programme, because they let an author drive (autoplay, tour) and then release the learner to explore the same canvas.

**What suits F1's brief.** The lead wants ideas shown visually, dense content, and no typed guessing. The interaction models that satisfy that here are: autoplay with speed and reverse (Chronas), guided tours over a zoomable canvas (ChronoZoom), the "elsewhere at this date" lateral jump (TimeMaps, Histomap rows), the entity drawer with tabs (Chronas), works streaming along unequal era bands (Google Time Explorer), and small parameterised map interactions with a visible result (ORBIS routes, OldMapsOnline overlay). Guess-then-reveal appears nowhere in this set; Smarthistory's pinned guiding questions are the only "prompt", and they ask for reflection, not typing.

**Adopt first.**
1. **Give the Atlas an open backbone before adding content.** Movements from Wikidata Q968159/P135 (CC0) keyed to Getty AAT ids (ODC-By, verified); periods from PeriodO (CC0, verified) with earliest/latest bounds; places from TGN/Pleiades (ODC-By/CC BY); a CC0 time-aware basemap from OpenHistoricalMap vector tiles with maplibre-gl-dates. This directly fixes the thin movements/civilisations/places layer at zero cost and gives every unit a node to hang on.
2. **Adopt a key-date lattice as the world-timeline spine** (TimeMaps' 20 dates × 3 levels; the Met's region × period cells). Every cell must be dense: map state, who is making what, key events, works, movement bars. Animated lectures then become morphs between adjacent snapshots rather than year-by-year data the team cannot source.
3. **Pair authored tours with free scrub on one canvas** (ChronoZoom tours + Chronas autoplay/drawer). The "animated lecture" is a tour; when it ends the same map and timeline stay live for the learner, with a province/movement drawer that embeds F1's own text instead of Wikipedia.
4. **Render uncertainty instead of hiding it**: period bars with fuzzy ends (PeriodO), soft or hatched edges for early borders, "attested at" markers for objects and workshops (Running Reality's "exists" idea), and parallel competing definitions where scholars disagree.

**Avoid.** Closed data (Running Reality, Google A&C images, YouTube videos); event-dots without story (Histography); object-only drill-down with no movement or place pages (Vitra online collection); and the Histomap's undefined "power" axis, unless redrawn as something measurable.
