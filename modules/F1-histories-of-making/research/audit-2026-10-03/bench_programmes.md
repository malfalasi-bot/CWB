# Benchmarks: online programmes, animated-lecture formats, and zero-cost animation tooling

Group: online educational programmes and animated-lecture formats for art, design, craft and world history, plus zero-cost production tooling for animated lectures.
Prepared for the F1 "Histories of making" audit, 3 October 2026. Everything below was checked against the product's own site, repo or docs on 3 October 2026 unless marked "not opened" (then a dated secondary source is named). No quotation exceeds 25 words.

Reading key for the F1 concerns: **[C1]** movements/civilisations/places/timeline thin · **[C2]** replace "guess, then find out" with interactives that show ideas visually · **[C3]** density · **[C4]** zero-cost / open licence.

## Part A — Programmes and formats (15 records)

### A1. Smarthistory

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| Maker, year, status | Smarthistory (US non-profit), founded 2005 by Beth Harris and Steven Zucker; live. ~3,500 essays and videos, 800 contributors, 50 editors; official art-history provider for Khan Academy. |
| What it is | Open multimedia art-history "textbook" and reference site, prehistory to today, all continents. |
| How it is structured | Three overlapping entry points: (1) **"Art Histories" tree** (left-nav) by region → period → culture → object; (2) **"Reframing Art History"**, a textbook organised in Parts by *global time-slice* (Part 2 prehistory–c.300 CE; Part 3 c.200–900; Part 4 c.900–1400; Part 5 c.1400–1800 …) with chapters by region or theme ("The Silk Roads", "World portraiture 700–1400", "Material culture of the Crusades"); (3) **Syllabi**, **Thematic Series** (conservation, at-risk heritage, Latinx art) and **The Basics**. Each content page follows one contract: *Jump To → Video → Video transcript (time-stamped) → Essay → Artwork details → Bibliography/Additional resources → Images for teaching → Cite this page → Explore related*. **Artwork details** is a faceted record: Places (Europe / Southern Europe / Greece), Period-Culture-Style (Ancient Greek / High Classical), Artwork Type, Material, Technique. |
| Interaction model | Watch (two-voice conversational video, typically 5–16 min), read, follow "see also"/"Explore related" links, cite. No quizzes on the site itself (Khan Academy adds them). |
| Content density | High. A single object page = ~10-min video + 1,500–3,000-word essay + record + bibliography. Narrative (video, essay) and reference (record, bibliography) coexist on one page. |
| Borrow for F1 | The page contract (video + transcript + essay + faceted record + related) **[C3]**; the time-slice Parts with regional chapters as the model for a world timeline that is not object-first **[C1]**; chapter-level syntheses ("The Silk Roads") that are movements/networks rather than objects **[C1]**. |
| Avoid | The videos are talking-heads-over-photographs; no animation, no maps, no timeline interaction — F1's visual-explanation ambition goes beyond it. Site search/browse is the only wayfinding; there is no atlas. |
| Licence / cost | Free, ad-free; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for all Smarthistory text and video (third-party images carry their own credits). NC clause: fine for a non-commercial programme, a problem if F1 is sold. |
| URLs opened | https://smarthistory.org/ · https://smarthistory.org/about/ · https://smarthistory.org/faq/ · https://smarthistory.org/reframing-art-history/ · https://smarthistory.org/the-parthenon-athens/ · https://smarthistory.org/parthenon-frieze/ · https://smarthistory.org/who-owns-the-parthenon-sculptures-2/ |

### A3. MoMA on Coursera — "Modern Art & Ideas" and "Fashion as Design"

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| Maker, year, status | Museum of Modern Art, New York (Lisa Mazzola; Paola Antonelli + 3); live. 489k and 297k enrolled. Part of the "Modern and Contemporary Art and Design" specialization. |
| What it is | Two beginner MOOCs built from museum educators, curators and designer interviews. |
| How it is structured | **Modern Art & Ideas**: 6 modules, ~7 h total, *thematic* not chronological (Places & Spaces; Art & Identity; Transforming Everyday Objects; Art & Society). Module 2 = 7 videos totalling 20 min (one 5-min theme intro + six 2–4-min single-work videos) + 6 readings (10–20 min each, titled as questions: "How do artists capture the mood of a place?") + optional discussion prompts; 1 assignment in the whole course. **Fashion as Design**: 7 modules, ~2 weeks × 10 h; themes (Heroes; Silhouettes; …); each module 7 videos totalling 27–38 min (2–11 min each: curator talks, "On Site" studio visits), 7–8 readings ("A Close Look at the Items …"), 1 assignment (30 min), 2 discussion prompts. |
| Interaction model | Watch short videos, read, optionally post to discussion ("Propose an Item", "What do your heroes wear?"), one short assignment per module. |
| Content density | Medium-high: 13–15 items per module; each item small. Narrative. |
| Borrow for F1 | Theme-led modules that mix objects, people and places under one idea **[C1]**; the 2–4-min single-work video as the unit of visual explanation; readings framed as questions. |
| Avoid | Discussion prompts assume a cohort; MoMA-only objects. |
| Licence / cost | Free to audit; certificate paid. Content © MoMA; no open licence. Structure only. |
| URLs opened | https://www.coursera.org/learn/modern-art-ideas · https://www.coursera.org/learn/fashion-design |

### A4. OER Project — Big History and World History (Origins / 1200 / 1750 / AP)

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| Maker, year, status | OER Project (Big History Project LLC, Gates-funded, non-commercial); live; classroom courses for teachers and students. |
| What it is | Free, standards-aligned full-year history courses with every asset downloadable and editable. |
| How it is structured | **Big History**: 7 units (What is Big History? → Universe & Earth → Life → Early Humans → Agriculture & Complex Societies → Modern Age → Future), 4–6 lessons each, organised by "thresholds of complexity" and *scale-switching*. **World History**: four variants by start date. Every lesson page is a numbered **step sequence**: Step 1 *Opener* activity (prior knowledge) → *Looking Ahead* / Unit Notebook → core video with **Before you watch / While you watch / After you watch** questions (time-stamped) and transcript → article with **Three Close Reads** (step 1 skim for gist; step 2 understand content; step 3 connect to the question/frames) → *Framing Unit N* → *Closer* activity → extension materials, essay-review rubric. Three **frames** run through the whole course: *Communities, Networks, Production & Distribution*; the first lesson of each unit is a frames video + activity. |
| Interaction model | Watch with look-fors, read in three passes, draw/complete worksheets (editable PDFs), write a short claim; "Lessons by Topic" for non-linear use. |
| Content density | High (6–8 steps per lesson, each with its own asset) but strictly sequenced. Narrative with structured reading scaffolds. |
| Borrow for F1 | The *Before/While/After you watch* look-fors as the replacement for prediction prompts **[C2]**; the three frames (communities = civilisations, networks = places/trade, production & distribution = making) as the lens that makes civilisations and events first-class **[C1]**; Three Close Reads as the dense-reading scaffold **[C3]**; the frames-drawing activity at unit start. |
| Avoid | Requires a teacher account for full access; worksheets presume a classroom; visuals are mostly stock photo montage. |
| Licence / cost | Free. Course materials "generally" **CC BY-NC** (Terms of Use); third-party content under its own licences. |
| URLs opened | https://www.oerproject.com/Big-History · https://www.oerproject.com/World-History · https://www.oerproject.com/World-History-Origins/Unit-3/Cities-Societies-and-Empires · https://www.oerproject.com/World-History-1200/Unit-1/History-Frames · https://www.oerproject.com/OER-Materials/OER-Media/HTML-Articles/Teacher-Resources/Reading-Guide · https://www.oerproject.com/Terms-of-use |

### A7. Google Arts & Culture — Stories, Projects, Entities

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| Maker, year, status | Google Cultural Institute (2011– ); live; content contributed by ~2,000+ partner institutions. |
| What it is | Image-first cultural platform: high-resolution works, Street View of galleries, and partner-authored "Stories". |
| How it is structured | Three page types seen: **Story** (e.g. "Bauhaus: The School of Modernism", Bauhaus Dessau Foundation) = a vertical/horizontal *scroll of slides*, each one image or zoom-crop + 50–150-word caption, 15–40 slides; **Project** hub ("Bauhaus Everywhere") = curated grid of stories, places, people ("The brains behind Bauhaus"); **Entity** page ("Bauhaus style, 1919–1938") = auto-aggregated movement record with date range, description, and all partner items tagged to it. | Avoid | Captions are shallow; no timeline or map across stories; content not reusable. |
| Licence / cost | Free to view; images and text © partners / Google terms — **not open-licensed**; structure only. |

### A9. TED-Ed animations

| Field | Finding |
|---|---|
| Maker, year, status | TED-Ed (2012– ); live; thousands of lessons. |
| What it is | 4–7-minute animated explainers written by an educator, produced by TED-Ed, animated by an independent studio. |
| How it is structured | Every lesson page: **Watch** (video) → **Think** (typically 5 multiple-choice + 3 open-answer questions) → **Dig Deeper** (curated links and further reading) → **Discuss** (1 guided + open discussions); "Customize this lesson" lets a teacher fork it. Credits list educator, director, animator, narrator, fact-checker. Production: educator writes an 800–900-word script, 10–20 editorial iterations, fact-check, then animation; ≥4 months per video. |
| Interaction model | Watch, then answer MCQs (auto-marked) and optional open questions; follow links. |
| Content density | One idea per video; density comes from Dig Deeper. Narrative. |
| Borrow for F1 | The **Dig Deeper** tier as the home for density **[C3]**; MCQ "Think" after, not typed prediction before **[C2]**; the 800–900-word script as a budget for a 5-minute animated lecture. |
| Avoid | The hero-animation style (full character animation by a studio) is beyond a zero-cost budget. |
| Licence / cost | Free to watch/embed; videos **copyrighted**, no download/edit/redistribution. Structure only. |
| URLs opened | https://help.ted.com/hc/en-us/articles/360005308474-What-is-a-TED-Ed-lesson · https://help.ted.com/hc/en-us/articles/360005308974-General-TED-Ed-lessons-FAQ · https://ed.ted.com/blog/2022/06/28/how-ted-ed-partnerships-work · https://ed.ted.com/lessons/the-myth-of-hercules-12-labors-in-8-bits-alex-gendler/think?question_number=2 · https://ed.ted.com/lessons/the-three-different-ways-mammals-give-birth-kate-slabosky/digdeeper?lesson_collection=awesome-nature |

## Synthesis (≈600 words)

**Patterns across the set.** First, every programme that sustains density without exhausting the learner separates a *reference layer* from a *narrative layer* and links them on one page. Smarthistory's page contract (video, transcript, essay, faceted record of place/period/type/material/technique, bibliography, related) is the clearest case; Google Arts & Culture's **entity** pages (a movement with a date range and every linked item) and OER Project's **frames** (communities, networks, production & distribution) do the same job for movements and civilisations. F1 already has the reference layer — the Atlas — but its units are object pages; the benchmarks show that movements, civilisations and events become first-class when they get their own page type with a dated range and links out to objects.

Second, the universal lesson grammar is *several short visual items*, not one long one: MoMA's modules are seven 2–5-minute videos plus six question-titled readings; CalArts' are eleven to fourteen 1–8-minute videos; TED-Ed is one 5-minute film plus a "Dig Deeper" tier. Density comes from stacking items and from a sources tier (Kurzgesagt's 15–30-page source sheets; Extra History's "Lies" episode), not from longer films.

Third, on interaction: typed free-text answers appear only where a cohort or teacher exists (Coursera peer essays, OER notebooks). The forms that work unattended are **watch with look-fors** (OER's before/while/after questions), **multiple-choice after watching** (TED-Ed, Khan), **self-assessment with an optional reveal** (OpenLearn), **scroll-to-advance** (GA&C stories), **date-filter on a map** (OpenHistoricalMap) and **orbit/zoom a 3D model** (Giza 3D, model-viewer). Nobody at scale asks a solo learner to predict and type.

Fourth, on cost: the proven zero-studio visual system is Historia Civilis — abstract shapes and a legend over a flat map, one narrator — and the OU's "Design in a nutshell" shows six 3–8-minute movement animations with transcripts can stand for years. Kurzgesagt (1,200 h per film) and TED-Ed (four months) are quality references, not models.

**Adopt first, in order:**

1. **A "Smarthistory contract" for every unit and a GA&C-style entity page for every movement, civilisation and event [C1][C3].** One template: 5–10-minute animated lecture + time-stamped transcript + dense essay + faceted record wired to the Atlas + sources sheet + "explore related". Entity pages get a date span, a map extent and linked objects — the thin strands grow without new objects.

2. **Replace "guess, then find out" with OER's lesson spine [C2].** Opener (one image, one question, no typing) → watch with three "look for" prompts → three-pass reading → multiple-choice check → closer that *shows* the answer on the map or timeline. An optional typed reflection, never a gate.

3. **A movement/civilisation animated-lecture series in the Historia Civilis / OU-nutshell style on the verified free stack [C2][C4]:** Motion Canvas (MIT) for shapes-and-maps lectures, with its player for in-page scrubbing; Kokoro (Apache-2.0) narration; MapLibre + OpenHistoricalMap (CC0) date-filtered maps and deck.gl arcs for networks; `<model-viewer>` (Apache-2.0) for CC0 scans; CSS scroll-driven animation for 2.5D parallax from Depth Anything V2 **Small** (Apache-2.0) depth maps via DepthFlow or Blender. Avoid Rive (exports now paid), Remotion unless the team is ≤3 or non-profit, and XTTS-v2 and Depth Anything Base/Large (non-commercial).

4. **A sources-and-simplifications tier on every unit [C3]:** Kurzgesagt's source sheet plus Extra History's "what we simplified" note — the cheapest way to make a dense programme trustworthy while keeping the lecture short.

**Surprises.** Rive put exports behind a $9/month plan in October 2025, failing the zero-cost rule despite MIT runtimes. GSAP became free in April 2025 (proprietary, no-charge). Piper's maintained fork went from MIT to GPL-3 in 2025. Only Depth Anything V2 Small is Apache. And the "open" programmes (Smarthistory, Khan, OER, Open Yale, OpenLearn) are all **non-commercial** CC variants — a problem if F1 is ever sold.
