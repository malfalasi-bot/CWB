# Edo Japan and the floating world: the full worked case (4 October 2026)

Why this folder exists: after the content audit, the lead judged the reference pages "still pretty weak, from concept to execution" and asked for one civilisation or movement researched in full: what the lectures should contain, how the interactives should work, which objects, events and people, and how it all falls into a collective. Edo Japan (1603–1868) read through ukiyo-e as a creative industry is that case, and the recipe at the end of the main document is what every other civilisation and movement page is now measured against.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `EDO_FULL_CASE.md` | Export of the Claude Doc *Edo and the Floating World — the full case* (rev 10): concept and thesis, cast of 58 by role, six-era spine with 78 events, 52 open objects, eight lectures (~62 min) with stations and predictions, ten interactives, how it holds together, protocol, gaps, sources. The live doc is the working copy. |
| `people_system.md` | Research report: the people and the production system (publishers, designers, carvers, printers, censors, the Yoshiwara and theatre licences, prices, print runs). |
| `objects_events.md` | Research report: open-licence objects (holder, id, licence) and the dated event list with sources. |
| `teaching.md` | Research report: how Edo and ukiyo-e are taught and exhibited now; lecture and interactive precedents. |
| `notes_objects.md` | Working notes on object selection and licence checks. |
| `BRIEF.md` | The shared brief the three research passes worked from. |

Rules that held throughout: open licences only for anything shown (the Met, Cleveland, AIC, LoC, Rijksmuseum, LACMA, Commons with tag); link-out otherwise; quotes of 25 words or fewer; no URL composed by hand; confidence words on every claim; Japanese names in Japanese order; content notes for the Yoshiwara, shunga excluded from view and named as a fact.

Next: apply the recipe (system diagram → cast with organiser → spine → object set as editions → lectures as station tours → interactives as system joints → rigour) to the twenty existing grouping pages, starting with Mingei and the Indus Valley.
