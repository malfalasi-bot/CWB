Overall verdict in one paragraph: the research underneath is award-grade and the voice is mostly right, but the piece is a well-sourced essay with a slideshow of dots beside it. Nineteen of its thirty-three beats sit on the same static 1859 raster map; the timeline is a playhead and 3 px knobs; the eight tools are counters, bar charts of interpolated numbers and a rubber stamp; the prose summarises where an award-level piece dramatises; and five of the learner-facing captions apologise for stand-ins. It is not "too complicated" in structure (one column, one thread) but it is complicated in the wrong place: eight tool widgets with their own sliders, timers and checks, each fighting the scroll driver, while the two instruments the lead asked for (an interactive world map, a timeline that shows things happening) do almost no work.

## Key question 1: Thread. Does the narrative read as one coherent thread with a dramatic question, rising stakes, recurring characters and payoffs, or as a sequence of facts? Where does it break?

### Takeaway
There is a thread on paper (eight motifs listed at the end: "the market, the four hands, the seal, the price, the blue, the ban, the export, the icon") but it is an index, not a plot: no question is posed, no protagonist carries more than one chapter, every event is told as summary, and the timeline runs backwards three times inside chapters. The one genuine narrative loop (learn to read a seal in chapter 3, use it to date a sheet in chapter 5) shows what the rest should do.

### Inferences
- The piece has motifs but no arc because it has no protagonist with a bet at stake. The case's own thesis supplies one: the publisher. A five-act structure built on four publishers' gambles would make the thread causal rather than chronological: Act I Tsutaya (the quarter's publisher becomes Edo's; fined half his assets; answers with mica portraits; ends in handcuffs 1804/1806). Act II Eijudō / Nishimuraya Yohachi (bets a Fuji series on a pigment fad; the Wave; the price falls). Act III Fujiokaya and Uoya Eikichi (the ban; 408,000 sheets; the Hundred Views). Act IV Hayashi and Bing, then Wright (the second trade, its scandal). Act V the Bank of Japan as the last publisher (the banknote). Each act ends on a question the next answers (What do you print when half your stock is illegal? Who buys 300,000 prints of a trade its own country had not thought worth a museum?).
- A second spine should be the object: one sheet of the Wave followed from Echizen paper to Met JP1847's accession (Havemeyer bequest 1929, already in the waves tool caption at [APP L367](/home/claude/creative-world/modules/F1-histories-of-making/build/prototypes/edo-story/app.js)), so "the price, the blue, the seal, the export, the icon" are stations on one sheet's life rather than nouns.
- Scenes are available in the sources and unused: the New Year 1765 exchange party (b2-4 is summary; the case's L3 station 2 has the Met Harunobu with "Meiwa 2" on the neckband, [CASE L193](/home/claude/creative-world/modules/F1-histories-of-making/research/edo-2026-10-04/EDO_FULL_CASE.md)); the 1835 letter about a carver's fee; the magistrate's 1847 report; the Fujiokaya diary entries for the hit and the flop; the 1919–20 "revamped" prints with pinholes (b6-3 is the only beat written as a story, and it is the best beat).
- Every "the course does not rule" sentence should become either a two-column "argued" station (the case specifies one per lecture: Hokusai genius vs system; Smith vs Guth on the blue; ban created vs sped the landscape; exchange vs appropriation — [CASE L189, L201, L205, L209](/home/claude/creative-world/modules/F1-histories-of-making/research/edo-2026-10-04/EDO_FULL_CASE.md)) or a one-clause "argued:" marker, never a paragraph of throat-clearing.

### Gaps
- I could not verify from the sources whether Eidelberg's doubt about Bracquemond is the only one the research file records; the text should name whoever the research file names.
- No reader-testing data exists for the prototype (no analytics, no session recordings), so where real learners drop off is unknown; the 42,638 px scroll height (my audit) is a proxy for the risk, not a measurement of it.
