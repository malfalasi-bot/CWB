# F1 Atlas as an animated 3D map/globe hub: exemplars, layout, techniques, styles

Scope note: researched 2026-10-04. Covers (a) best-in-class 3D maps, globes and spatial hubs for knowledge/culture, (b) how 35 units and ~4,228 nodes could sit on a 3D Atlas hub, with scale transitions down to unit maps such as Edo 1859, (c) libraries, licences, performance and accessibility under the claude.ai Artifact constraints, (d) East Asian / ukiyo-e visual styles and per-world styling inside one system. Gamification mechanics proper (scoring, quests, and so on) are only touched where they are spatial (fog of war, reveal, restoration). Items marked **[single source]** or **[unverified]** need checking before they are quoted in learner-facing copy.

## Q1. Exemplars: award-winning and best-in-class 3D maps, globes and spatial hubs: what each does well and what transfers

### Takeaway
The strongest precedents split into three families: (1) **globes as brand hubs** (GitHub, Stripe), which are cheap, texture-free, instanced dot-globes with animated arcs and hard performance discipline; (2) **culture/knowledge spaces** (British Museum × Google "Museum of the World", Google Arts & Culture t-SNE Map / Curator Table, Histography, Atlas of Economic Complexity), which lay thousands of objects out by time × region or by similarity and let connections light up; (3) **game world maps** (Civilization VI, Ōkami, Ghost of Tsushima, Bruno Simon's 2025/26 portfolio), which show that an *art-directed map that changes as you progress* (parchment fog that recedes, cursed land restored, wind instead of waypoints) is what makes a hub feel like a world rather than a menu. Most culture precedents are **desktop-only**, which is the gap F1 has to close.

### Cited Findings

**Globes as hubs (engineering making-ofs)**
- GitHub globe (Dec 2020): three.js/WebGL; five layers (halo, globe, Earth's regions, blue spikes for open PRs, pink arcs for merged PRs). "We don't use any textures": four lights on a sphere, ~12,000 five-sided circles for land rendered via one `InstancedMesh`, halo from a custom shader on the back side of a sphere. Land dots sampled from a world image: a circle is drawn where pixel alpha ≥ 90/255 — [GitHub Blog, "How we built the GitHub globe"](https://github.blog/engineering/engineering-principles/how-we-built-the-github-globe/) (excerpted via [Noise mirror](https://noise.getoto.net/2020/12/21/how-we-built-the-github-globe))
- GitHub arcs: `CubicBezierCurve3` between two locations, three orbit heights (longer distance → arc pulled further out), `TubeBufferGeometry` with `setDrawRange()` to animate lines appearing/disappearing; dot density varies with device performance; antialias deliberately **off** to protect frame rate — [GitHub Blog via Noise mirror](https://noise.getoto.net/2020/12/21/how-we-built-the-github-globe)
- GitHub ran a five-part series (globe, data, performance, illustration, narrative); Core Web Vitals used as a "North Star"; scroll animations use IntersectionObserver-style patterns instead of `getBoundingClientRect()` in scroll handlers (which force reflow) — [GitHub Blog, "Making GitHub's new homepage fast and performant"](https://github.blog/engineering/user-experience/making-githubs-new-homepage-fast-and-performant)
- Stripe globe: chose a sphere because it takes ~17% of the area a 2D map needs, keeps relative size/shape/orientation, and "spinning a globe is much more satisfying than scanning a map"; 60,000 dots on a sunflower spiral (`setFromSphericalCoords`) for even spacing at all latitudes; cut to ~20,000 vertices by drawing only relevant countries; throttled animation during scroll; antialias off ("performance gain was significant enough to accept a tradeoff"); arcs = D3 great-circle interpolation + CubicBezier to avoid passing through the sphere, custom fragment shader, `setDrawRange` reveal over 2.5 s (~3,000 vertices per arc); static image fallback prepared in case 60 fps could not be held; "build a fully-functional prototype early" — [Stripe Blog, "To design and develop an interactive globe"](https://stripe.com/blog/globe)
- Open re-implementations: `janarosmonaliev/github-globe` (MIT, ~1.2k stars) builds the look on `three-globe` — [GitHub repo](https://github.com/janarosmonaliev/github-globe)

**Game and WebGL world-maps as hubs**
- **Civilization VI**: the art drew on "old style maps with the wonderful illustrations"; unexplored and fogged land is drawn "traced over in that style" (parchment), and the parchment recedes into full-colour terrain as you explore — [PCGamesN on Firaxis art video](https://www.pcgamesn.com/civilization-vi/civilization-6-art-trailer); Strategic View 2D map art by Sam Gauss — [ArtStation](https://samgauss.artstation.com/projects/bZqrd)

- **Ghost of Tsushima** (Sucker Punch 2020): design goal "how can we keep people immersed in this world instead of just more compasses and mini-maps"; the Guiding Wind replaces waypoints and is also tied to history (the typhoon that dispersed the Mongol fleet); icons appear only when close to objectives; nature cues guide the player — [Android Central interview with Jason Connell](https://www.androidcentral.com/ghost-tsushima-creative-director-interview)

- **Codrops "Susurrus"** (Apr 2026): a cozy watercolour world in three.js from a single Kuwahara post-processing pass run at very low resolution, which keeps it smooth on mobile and older devices — [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/24/susurrus-crafting-a-cozy-watercolor-world-with-three-js-and-shaders/)

### Inferences
- **Transfer from GitHub/Stripe:** a texture-free, instanced "dot/ink-dot" globe with arcs drawn via `setDrawRange` matches F1's "routes draw" motion rule exactly and is proven at 12k–60k instances on mobile. Antialias off, pausing offscreen and a static fallback are standard practice, not compromises.
- **Transfer from Museum of the World:** the time-axis × region layout with connections lighting up is the closest ancestor of F1's Place/Time/Connections triad. Its weakness (desktop-only, usability 6.84) is where F1 can beat it: phone-first, accessible list equivalent, uncertainty grammar.
- **Transfer from Civ VI and Ōkami:** "fog as unrendered parchment / un-inked paper, which becomes inked as units are completed" is an art-directed progress mechanic. It fits the archive aesthetic and the "no confetti" rule because progress is shown as *the map getting drawn*, not as points.
- **Transfer from Ghost of Tsushima:** a "guiding wind" equivalent (e.g. a faint ink current or route thread pointing to the next suggested unit) gives direction without waypoints or HUD, and a "Kurosawa mode" shows that a *whole-world style mode* can be a respected, designed feature. That is the model for per-world styles (see Q4).
- **Transfer from Bruno Simon:** instancing, KTX2 and quality tiers make a rich 3D hub viable on phones; "quiet multiplayer traces" (bounded, moderated) is a precedent for "cohort as quiet density". F1 should avoid his leaderboard, per the existing decision.
- **Transfer from Histography and t-SNE Map:** both prove that thousands of items can be a navigable *field* if there is a strong organising axis (time or similarity) plus category filters. F1's 4,228 nodes need such an axis per surface.

## Q2. How to lay out 35 units and ~4,228 nodes in 3D so it reads as an atlas, not a cloud (layout, LOD, labels, camera, semantic zoom, time as 3rd dimension, uncertainty, progress/fog, entering a unit)

### Takeaway
Use a **hybrid**: a real geographic globe (or tilted, folded map) as the *hub*, where the 35 units are **regions/territories, not pins**. Most units are geographically anchored (Africa, Americas, East Asia, Pacific, steppe…). Thematic and method units (Networks, Copying, Museum/provenance, Re-timing) are better shown as **route-layers or "lenses" over the same globe** than as extra places. Nodes are never shown all at once: they are **clustered by zoom** (supercluster-style), and their labels go through a priority/collision pass. Camera moves follow **van Wijk–Nuij zoom-out/pan/zoom-in** paths. Time is either a scrubbed layer (default) or an optional **space-time cube** view for routes (Hägerstrand/Kraak; Bach et al. operations). Entering a unit = a semantic-zoom handoff from globe → region → a **textured plane of the unit's own historic map** (Edo 1859), using a culturally grounded transition device such as golden clouds (kinun/suyari-gasumi).

### Cited Findings

**Atlas, not cloud: map metaphors for graphs**
- GMap (Gansner, Hu, Kobourov, IEEE PacificVis 2010): clusters of a graph become "countries"; boundaries come from Voronoi cells merged per cluster, with random boundary points that give irregular "coastlines" and "lakes"; spectral + greedy colouring maximises contrast between neighbours; users "spend significantly longer time studying the maps" and "find non-trivial structural information without any prompting". Limits: clusters can fragment into non-contiguous regions; dense graphs force false adjacencies (planarity limits) — [Kobourov PDF](https://www2.cs.arizona.edu/~kobourov/pacvis10.pdf)
- Atlas of Economic Complexity 10.0 adds *label-defined knowledge clusters* to a node-link product space, so named regions stay legible over thousands of nodes — [Growth Lab](https://growthlab.hks.harvard.edu/news/atlas-economic-complexity-100-brings-new-data-and-product-space-design/)
- Stripe: a globe uses ~17% of the area of a flat map, which suits a 390 px phone — [Stripe Blog](https://stripe.com/blog/globe)

**Clustering and LOD**
- `supercluster` (Mapbox, **ISC**): KD-tree point clustering (demo: 6 million points in Leaflet); `getClusters(bbox, zoom)`, `getLeaves()`, `getClusterExpansionZoom`, map/reduce aggregation of properties; default 40 px radius; runs in browser or Node — [GitHub mapbox/supercluster](https://github.com/mapbox/supercluster)
- MapLibre globe switches automatically to Mercator around zoom 12 to avoid precision problems, so the globe is a *zoomed-out* view and close-in work happens on a flat map. Globe and Mercator draw the same vector data — [MapLibre developer guide: globe.md](https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/blob/main/developer-guides/globe.md)
- MapLibre roadmap describes this as an "Adaptive Composite Map Projection": globe when zoomed out, Mercator when deep. Released in GL JS 5.0.0 (Jan 2025) — [MapLibre roadmap](https://maplibre.org/roadmap/maplibre-gl-js/globe-view)

**Labels**
- Max Planck Research Networks needed a bespoke label-placement algorithm — [well-formed-data](https://well-formed-data.net/archives/689/max-planck-research-networks/)
- `troika-three-text` (MIT; licence confirmed on [unpkg LICENSE](https://app.unpkg.com/troika-three-text@0.33.1/files/LICENSE), not opened): SDF text in three.js, parses .ttf/.otf/.woff directly, layout in a web worker, outlines and strokes, curved text along cylinders, works with three.js materials — [npm troika-three-text](https://www.npmjs.com/package/troika-three-text)
- globe.gl ships a Labels layer and an HTML Elements layer, so DOM labels can be used for accessible, crisp text — [GitHub vasturiano/globe.gl](https://github.com/vasturiano/globe.gl)

**Camera choreography**
- van Wijk & Nuij (IEEE InfoVis 2003): the optimal smooth zoom-and-pan path is an ellipse-like curve in (u, w) space (zoom out, travel, zoom in); ρ trades zooming against panning; in a 26-person study, preferred ρ was widely spread; users perceive panning as linear but zoom logarithmically; speed is set as V_RMS — [TU Wien summary](https://www.cg.tuwien.ac.at/courses/InfoVis/HallOfFame/2004/04_zoom_and_pan/); [paper PDF](https://vanwijk.win.tue.nl/zoompan.pdf); [IEEE CSDL](https://www.computer.org/csdl/proceedings-article/ieee-infovis/2003/20550003/12OmNzTppyr). (Commonly reported to be the basis of Mapbox/MapLibre `flyTo` and its `curve` default of 1.42. **[unverified in this session]**)
- Newer research: OptFlowCam (CGF 2024) builds camera paths for scenes with extreme scale variation from 3D image flow — [Wiley CGF](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/cgf.15056) (abstract level only)
- deck.gl GlobeView defaults: maxPitch 60, minPitch 0, maxZoom 20; "The globe behaves like a physical ball" — [deck.gl docs](https://deck.gl/docs/api-reference/core/globe-view)

**Time as a third dimension**
- Hägerstrand introduced the space-time model (late 1960s). Its elements are the space-time path (vertical segments = stays at "stations"), the space-time prism / potential path area, and capability, coupling and authority constraints. Kraak argues the cube "has to be manipulated in space to find the best possible view" and should have linked views, query tools and slider planes for temporal selection. Open question: can users read the cube when many paths are shown? — [Kraak 2003, ICC proceedings PDF](https://icaci.org/files/documents/ICC_proceedings/ICC2003/Papers/255.pdf)
- Bach, Dragicevic, Archambault, Hurter, Carpendale, *Computer Graphics Forum* 2016 (DOI 10.1111/cgf.12804): a generalised space-time cube with operations of **extraction** (cutting sub-parts), **flattening** (across space or time) and **transformation**. It applies to geospatial data, networks and video — [Edinburgh Research Explorer](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/a-descriptive-framework-for-temporal-data-visualizations-based-on)
- Histography handles deep time with zoomable time scales plus category filters — [IIB](https://www.informationisbeautifulawards.com/showcase/771-histography)

**Progress, reveal, fog**
- Civ VI: fog is drawn as an old cartographer's parchment that recedes into colour terrain as you explore — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/civilization-vi/civilization-6-art-trailer)
- Ōkami: completed acts restore colour and life to cursed land — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ckami)
- Ghost of Tsushima: wind guides instead of waypoints; icons only near objectives — [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/ghost-tsushima-creative-director-interview)

**Entering a unit: a culturally grounded transition**
- Rakuchū rakugai-zu screens (c.1603–1623 example): "golden clouds hang over the city, while scattered openings reveal notable landmarks", which gives a bird's-eye, map-like view. The style was popular from the 16th to the 18th century — [MLIT Japan Tourism multilingual database](https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/R6-00038.html)

### Inferences (design proposal for F1)
1. **Hub geometry = globe for "where", with a flat "folded atlas" when zoomed.** Follow MapLibre's adaptive approach. Below roughly region scale, swap to a tilted flat sheet (Mercator or the unit's own historic projection). A globe for all 35 units fits a phone better than a flat world map (the Stripe area argument) and reads instantly as "global history".
2. **Units are territories with soft, inked borders, not pins.** Draw each geographic unit (F1.6 Africa/metallurgy, F1.7 Americas/fibre, F1.8 East Asia/workshop, F1.9 S/SE Asia/cotton, F1.10 Islamic world/routes, F1.11 Europe/guild, F1.12 Pacific/navigation, F1.12a steppe/horse…) as a GMap-style soft region on the globe, its outline brushed rather than political. Overlapping regions (the Islamic world over Africa, Asia and Iberia; the steppe over Eurasia) are a *feature*: they teach that units are lenses. Show overlap as translucent washes, not hard borders.
3. **Thematic and method units become layers or lenses, not places.** F1.13 Networks, F1.14 Copying, F1.19 Industry and exhibition, F1.22 Extraction, F1.24 Museum/provenance, F1.30 Re-timing are toggled *lenses* that re-draw the same globe: routes light up (Networks); copy-chains as dashed threads (Copying); exhibition cities pulse (Industry); extraction flows from mines to cities (Planetary present); provenance arcs from source to museum (Museum, a powerful reverse-route view); calendar rings re-label the time scrubber (Re-timing). This keeps 35 units from turning into 35 pins.
4. **Three zoom tiers (semantic zoom).** (a) *World*: 35 unit territories + ~10–30 major routes, no node dots, only unit names (≤ about 12 labels on a phone at a time). (b) *Region*: supercluster bubbles of nodes by type (objects, makers, places, routes…) with counts; a label budget driven by importance/priority. (c) *Unit sheet*: the unit's historic map (Edo 1859) as a textured plane, with nodes as instanced markers in the historic map's coordinates. Clusters expand via `getClusterExpansionZoom`; cluster colour or shape encodes node type.
5. **Label strategy.** Priority order = unit name > selected node > hub cities > others. Hide labels on the far hemisphere (dot product of normal with camera vector < 0) and fade near the limb. Run a screen-space greedy collision test each settle, not each frame. Use DOM/HTML labels for the top tier (crisp, accessible, CJK-friendly, translatable) and troika SDF text only for decorative or curved route names.
6. **Camera.** One `flyTo` implementation using van Wijk–Nuij (zoom out → travel → zoom in) for every unit change; duration scales with distance, clamped to about 1.2–3 s. Orbit limits: polar clamp so the north pole is never upside-down; zoom limits per tier; no free roll. With reduced motion, cross-fade cut (fade out, re-centre, fade in) instead of flying.
7. **Time.** Default: time is a *scrubber* that filters the globe (routes draw as time plays). Optional "Time-depth" view for route-heavy units (Networks, Pacific navigation, Islamic routes, Industry): tilt into a **space-time cube** where routes extrude upward by date. Use Bach's *flattening* (back to the map) and *time-cutting* (slider plane) operations. Keep the cube to ≤ a few dozen paths, as Kraak warns about legibility with many paths.
8. **Uncertainty in 3D (extends the existing grammar).** Soft-ended date ranges → extrusions that fade at their ends in the cube, and gradient tails on timeline bars. Contested claims → dashed arcs (dash in shader UV). Confidence → ink density or opacity of the line (not hue). Approximate places → a soft ink blot (radius = spatial uncertainty) instead of a pin.
9. **Progress = the map getting inked.** Unvisited units appear as uninked washi or a faint pencil underdrawing (Civ VI parchment logic). Completing acts inks outlines, then washes colour in, then draws the unit's routes (Ōkami restoration logic). This makes progress spatial and quiet, with no confetti. The cohort is shown as faint shared ink density on routes (many learners passed here), never ranks.
10. **Entering a unit.** Tap a territory → `flyTo` region → **golden clouds (kinun) part** to reveal the unit's historic sheet (Edo 1859 plane) aligned to its real location. Exiting reverses the clouds. The device comes from the material culture F1 teaches (rakuchū rakugai-zu), and it hides the LOD swap and texture streaming. For other worlds, use equivalent "veils" in that world's style (see Q4).
11. **Guiding wind equivalent.** A faint animated ink current from the current unit towards the suggested next unit (based on the knowledge-graph relation with highest confidence), in place of arrows or waypoints. It is off under reduced motion and replaced by a static dotted thread.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical study comparing globe and flat map hubs for *learning* outcomes; the Stripe and GitHub rationales are product, not pedagogy.
- No sourced numbers on how many labels are legible on a 390 px globe; the "≤ ~12" above is a design heuristic, not a finding.
- No study found on reading uncertainty in 3D space-time cubes specifically (the existing 2D grammar is a project decision).

## Q3. Techniques, libraries, licences, performance and accessibility under Artifact constraints

### Takeaway
For a zero-cost, self-hosted claude.ai Artifact, the best fit is **three.js (MIT) with hand-built layers**, optionally using **three-globe/globe.gl (MIT)** for arcs, labels and HTML layers, plus **supercluster (ISC)** and **Natural Earth (public domain)** geometry baked to small TopoJSON/GeoJSON or pre-rasterised textures. **MapLibre GL JS v5 (BSD-3)** is the strongest option if a true tiled vector map with globe→Mercator handoff is needed, but it expects tiles and style JSON. These can be self-hosted as files, but the build is heavier. **deck.gl GlobeView** is still "experimental". **CesiumJS** is Apache 2.0, but its default imagery and terrain come through Cesium ion, which is a remote token service and banned at runtime here. It is also heavy for phones. Budget for ≤ ~100 draw calls, ≤ ~100k vertices and ≤ 2048 px textures on mobile, DPR capped at 2. WebGPU now ships in all major browsers, with automatic WebGL2 fallback in three.js. Accessibility requires a synced list/table equivalent, keyboard focus on markers, button alternatives to dragging (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7, AA) and reduced-motion cuts.

**Performance budgets**
- Mobile: about 100 draw calls per frame; "<100 draw calls and <100,000 vertices if you can" (citing Don McCurdy); textures ≤ 2048 px unless hero; one 4096² RGBA texture = 64 MiB VRAM (~85 MiB with mipmaps); KTX2/Basis gives 4–8× less GPU memory; Meshopt decodes faster than Draco; cap DPR at 2; half-resolution post-processing can about double fps in fill-bound scenes; InstancedMesh = 1 draw call per repeated geometry; BatchedMesh = 1 draw call for varied geometries with one material (per-instance culling and sorting), WebGL and WebGPU since r183 — [Utsubo, "100 Three.js Tips" (updated 11 Sep 2026)](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips) **[agency blog, secondary; numbers consistent with three.js community practice]**
- MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE (WebGL2, web3dsurvey): 4096 = 100% of devices, 8192 = 98%, 16384 = 85%. **Android: 8192 = 77%, 16384 = 4%**. iOS: 8192 = 100%, 16384 = 98% — [web3dsurvey](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgl2/parameters/MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE)
- WebGPU: Chrome/Edge 113+ desktop; Chrome 121 Android 12+ (Qualcomm/ARM); Firefox 141 Windows (Jul 2025), 145 macOS Apple Silicon; **Safari 26 on macOS, iOS, iPadOS and visionOS (Sept 2025)**; Linux and Firefox Android incomplete — [webgpu.com, 1 Dec 2025](https://www.webgpu.com/news/webgpu-hits-critical-mass-all-major-browsers/). About 87% global support as of Sept 2026; three.js WebGPURenderer falls back automatically to WebGL2 — [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips) **[secondary]**

- Sketchy pencil post-processing: Sobel edges from both the colour buffer and a normal buffer (`MeshNormalMaterial` pass), gradient noise for stippled shading, displacement texture for wobbly lines. Costs: an extra scene render plus 9 texture taps per pixel — [Codrops 2022](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2022/11/29/sketchy-pencil-effect-with-three-js-post-processing/)

**Accessibility**
- WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.7 Dragging Movements (AA): "All functionality that uses a dragging movement … can be achieved by a single pointer without dragging". ### Inferences (recommended build for the Artifact)
- **Stack:** three.js (WebGPURenderer with automatic WebGL2 fallback, or plain WebGLRenderer if TSL adds too much weight) loaded from jsDelivr/cdnjs. Custom layers: (1) ocean/paper sphere with a washi-fibre shader (procedural noise, no large texture); (2) land as instanced ink dots *or* a 2048×1024 pre-rendered Natural Earth land mask (PD) as one KTX2/WebP texture; (3) unit territories as GeoJSON polygons → triangulated meshes, about 35 draw calls or 1 with BatchedMesh; (4) routes as one merged `LineSegments`/tube geometry with a per-vertex "time" attribute, revealed in the shader by uniform `uTime` (no per-arc draw calls); (5) nodes as **one InstancedMesh per node type** (≈11 types ≤ 11 draw calls for 4,228 instances, trivial); (6) DOM label overlay. Total about 20–40 draw calls.
- **Files (≤255, ≤16 MB each):** `index.html`, `atlas.js` bundle, `nodes.json` (4,228 nodes, about 0.6–1.2 MB minified; could be split per unit), `units.geojson` (35 simplified territories), `routes.json`, `land_2k.webp/ktx2`, one or two subsetted fonts (Latin + needed CJK subset; a full CJK font can exceed several MB), per-unit historic sheets (Edo 1859 at 2048 px for phones and 4096 px for iOS/desktop; deep zoom only as pre-cut tiles in the files budget). Everything is fetched relative to the page, which is allowed because it is self-hosted alongside the page.
- **Avoid at runtime:** Cesium ion, Mapbox, Google tiles, OSM tiles, CDN font services other than Google Fonts stylesheets (and remember CJK webfonts from Google Fonts are a stylesheet load, permitted, but self-hosting is safer for offline determinism).
- **Mobile quality tiers:** DPR cap 1.5–2; no post-processing on low tier (paper texture in the material instead of a full-screen pass); Kuwahara or Sobel only on a "high" tier; pause the render loop when idle (render on demand), which also saves battery. Use `navigator.hardwareConcurrency`/GPU tier heuristics plus a measured first-second fps to drop tier.
- **2D fallback / no-WebGL:** an SVG orthographic globe (d3-geo, ISC) of the same 35 territories, or a static equal-area map, with the same list. The **list/table view is primary, not an afterthought**: a sortable list of units → nodes, synced to the map selection, keyboard-first, which also serves screen-reader users (G92).
- **Keyboard:** Tab cycles the ≤ about 12 visible unit territories (not 4,228 nodes); arrow keys rotate the globe; +/− zoom; Enter enters a unit; Escape goes back up a tier. On-screen pan/zoom buttons satisfy SC 2.5.7. Focus is mirrored on the canvas by an ink ring on the focused territory and by a visible DOM focus outline on its label.
- **Reduced motion:** no auto-rotation; `flyTo` replaced by fade-cut; route "drawing" replaced by an instant draw with a brief opacity fade; the guiding-wind current becomes static; the space-time cube tilts in steps rather than animating. Honour `prefers-reduced-motion` and also offer an in-app toggle.

## Q4. Japanese / ukiyo-e / East Asian styles on maps and in 3D, and per-world styles inside one programme-wide system

### Takeaway
There are strong precedents for a *whole world rendered in a historical art language* (Ōkami: sumi-e/ukiyo-e with a paper overlay; Ghost of Tsushima: an art-directed "Kurosawa Mode" with tuned contrast, grain and wind). Edo-period cartography offers native devices that double as interface: bird's-eye views, golden clouds that hide and reveal, and woodblock-printed city maps (Ishikawa Ryūsen). The way to keep one system is to make **style a themable layer on shared geometry**: same scene graph, data and interactions, with per-world "material packs" (paper, ink, palette, line quality, transition veil, typography) switched when the camera enters a world. This works like Kurosawa Mode, a post-process + material + audio preset, not a separate build.

### Cited Findings
- Ōkami: sumi-e and ukiyo-e inspired cel-shading; paper-parchment overlay (reduced in the Wii version); the brush mechanic draws onto the frozen screen as if on paper; restoring colour to cursed land; 2007 BAFTA Artistic Achievement — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Ckami)
- Kurosawa Mode: contrast matched to period films by time of day and weather; film grain; period audio; stronger wind; "not just a standard black-and-white filter"; made with the Kurosawa estate's blessing — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/ghost-of-tsushima-kurosawa-mode-explained-how-it-changes-the-game-and-why/); [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2020/07/ghost_of_tsushimas_black_and_white_mode_has_official_blessing_of_akira_kurosawas_estate)
- Rakuchū rakugai-zu folding screens: golden clouds over the city with openings revealing landmarks, giving a bird's-eye, map-like view (16th–18th c.) — [MLIT tourism database](https://www.mlit.go.jp/tagengo-db/en/R6-00038.html); other examples are held at the [Royal Ontario Museum](https://collections.rom.on.ca/objects/354015/rakuchu--rakugai-zu-views-of-kyoto-and-its-environs) and [Cornell](https://emuseum.cornell.edu/objects/26902/rakuchurakugai-in-and-around-kyoto) (licences not checked)
- Ishikawa Ryūsen, *Edozu* (Map of Edo), 1710, hand-coloured woodcut, at the National Gallery of Art. NGA page says media unavailable, and no open-access image was found — [NGA](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/48256-edozu-map-edo). Ryūsen's *Nihon kaisan chōrikuzu* (1694) is at the David Rumsey collection — [David Rumsey](https://www.davidrumsey.com/japan/xmaps1002.html) (licence on that site **[unverified; many Rumsey images are CC BY-NC-SA, which is NOT on the allowed list]**). "Ryūsen-zu" as a genre of Japan maps — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ry%C5%ABsen-zu). An Edo map (*Enjū Oedo ezu*) is in Manchester Digital Collections — [Manchester](https://www.digitalcollections.manchester.ac.uk/view/PR-JAPANESE-00097) (licence **[unverified]**)
- Civ VI shows a parchment/illustrated-map style as fog that recedes to colour — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/civilization-vi/civilization-6-art-trailer)
- NPR techniques available in three.js (Sobel ink lines, noise-wobble, Kuwahara painterly) and their costs — [Codrops 2022](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2022/11/29/sketchy-pencil-effect-with-three-js-post-processing/); [Codrops 2026](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/24/susurrus-crafting-a-cozy-watercolor-world-with-three-js-and-shaders/)

### Inferences (design proposal: "one Atlas, many inks")
- **Global hub style:** neutral archive (unbleached paper, iron-gall/sumi ink lines, restrained mineral palette), so no single culture's aesthetic is the "default" world. This matters for a decolonial global history of making.
- **World packs (applied on entry to a unit; the hub stays neutral):** each pack = {paper shader params, line shader (brush vs pen vs engraving), 5–6 colour palette, transition veil, typeface pairing, optional ambient sound}. Examples to research with each unit's specialists (proposals only, not sourced claims):
  - *Edo / East Asia (F1.8, worked unit):* washi fibre, sumi line with dry-brush break, ukiyo-e flat colour (aizuri/Prussian-blue accent for the post-1820s print trade), **golden-cloud veil** transition, vertical label option.
  - *Islamic world / routes and waqf (F1.10):* the veil could be a geometric girih lattice that opens; route lines in the style of itinerary maps. **[needs a sourced cartographic model]**
  - *Pacific / navigation (F1.12):* swell-line patterns and star paths as line language (the stick-chart idea), but these are culturally sensitive and need community-sourced guidance.
  - *Europe / guild (F1.11):* copperplate hatching (Sobel and hatch shader).
  - *Africa / metallurgy (F1.6), Americas / fibre (F1.7), S/SE Asia / cotton (F1.9):* materials-led line and texture (forge-scale, woven grid, block-printed resist), avoiding pastiche "tribal" motifs.
- **One system rule:** packs change *only* materials, palettes, veils and type, never interaction, layout or encoding. Uncertainty grammar (dashed contested arcs, soft-ended ranges) and accessibility contrast are invariant across packs, so WCAG AA contrast has to be checked per pack.
- **Kurosawa-mode analogue:** an optional "Archive mode" (monochrome ink on paper, period-appropriate) offered as a respectful, art-directed reading mode. It doubles as a high-contrast and low-GPU tier.
