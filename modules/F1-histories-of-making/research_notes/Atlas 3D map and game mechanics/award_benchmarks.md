# Award benchmarks for an award-winning, game-like, 3D/map-based design-history course (Creative World, F1 "Histories of making")

Research date: 4 October 2026. Scope: which awards fit the project, their categories and published criteria, 2021–2026 winners and shortlisted works that most resemble it, why they won, and what keeps cultural or learning projects from winning.

Verification legend: **[V]** checked against an official award page or the organiser's own release; **[V-sec]** checked only against a reputable secondary source such as press, Wikipedia or an agency case study; **[U]** unverified or uncertain.

---

## Q1. Which awards fit, and what are their categories and published criteria?

### Takeaway
The best-fitting live programmes are:
- **Webby Awards**, which publishes seven criteria and is the only major web award whose criteria mention disability inclusion.
- **Awwwards**, which publishes weights: Design 40, Usability 30, Creativity 20, Content 10.
- **FWA**
- **Games for Change**, which now has topic categories such as "Best in Learning" and gives a separate Accessibility Award.
- **BAFTA Game Beyond Entertainment**
- **Information is Beautiful**, now run by the Data Visualization Society.
- **Museums + Heritage "Best Use of Digital"**
- **D&AD Digital Design**, which judges execution over idea and has an "Inclusive" subcategory.
- **Japan Good Design Award**, which uses four perspectives: human, industrial, social and temporal.

Several programmes named in the brief are no longer usable:
- **AAM MUSE Awards**: on hold since 2023.
- **Japan Media Arts Festival**: the last awards were in 2022.
- **Malofiej**: paused in October 2021.
- **GLAMi**: moved from MCN to MuseWeb, with no verifiable winners after 2021 found.

### Cited Findings

**Awwwards (Site of the Day, Month and Year, plus the Developer Award)**
- Scoring weights are Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20% and Content 10%. [V] — [Awwwards Evaluation System](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)
- How the honours are decided [V] — [Awwwards Evaluation System](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/):
  - Site of the Day needs minimum jury scores from at least 18 jurors, with outliers removed.
  - "The eight highest-scoring sites each month are nominated" for Site of the Month, which gets a second jury review; user votes carry weight.
  - All Site of the Month winners plus "some of awwwards favorite sites" are nominated for Site of the Year.
  - The Developer Award goes to Site of the Day winners scoring above 7 against the Developer Guidelines.
- The evaluation page says nothing about accessibility or mobile. [V] — [Awwwards Evaluation System](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)
- 2025 annual winners [V] — [Awwwards Annual Awards](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards/winners):
  - Site of the Year: **Lando Norris** (OFF+BRAND), which also won Users' Choice.
  - Developer Site of the Year: **Messenger** (abeto).
  - Agency of the Year: Immersive Garden.
- Annual nominees by year [V] — [Awwwards Sites of the Year](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/sites_of_the_year/):
  - 2024: Igloo Inc (abeto).
  - 2022: Getty "Persepolis Reimagined" (Monks).
  - 2021: Star Atlas (Hello Monday) and Umami Land (Monks).
  - The listing does not clearly separate nominees from winners.

### Inferences
- The live programmes split three ways.
  - **Craft-first web juries** (Awwwards, FWA, CSSDA) reward visual and technical spectacle. Awwwards weights Design plus Usability at 70%, and Content at only 10%.
  - **Purpose-first juries** (G4C, BAFTA Game Beyond Entertainment, M+H, the former GLAMi and MUSE) reward meaning, real-world impact, sharing with the sector, and accessibility.
  - **Hybrid juries** (Webby, D&AD, Good Design) mix both and are the most explicit about inclusion and usability.
- A zero-cost design-history course is most competitive in:
  1. Webby Websites and Mobile Sites: Education/Cultural, Best User Interface, Best Visual Design.
  2. FWA and Awwwards, if the Atlas is a real WebGL showpiece.
  3. Information is Beautiful (Arts, Entertainment & Culture) for the Atlas visualisation.
  4. G4C Best in Learning, only if the labs are real games.
  5. Good Design Award, given the Japanese subject matter and its "temporal perspective."
  6. M+H Best Use of Digital, only with a museum partner.
- BAFTA and IGF winners are almost always full commercial or indie games. A web course is unlikely to compete there. They are useful as design references, not as targets.
- The legacy museum awards (MUSE, Malofiej, Japan Media Arts) should not be planned around unless they relaunch.

## Q2. Which 2021–2026 winners and shortlisted works are the closest comparables, what did they do, and what transfers?

### Takeaway
The closest comparables fall into three groups:
1. **WebGL heritage reconstructions**, led by Getty "Persepolis Reimagined", which won FWA of the Year and Awwwards Site of the Month.
2. **Small, calm 3D web games**, led by abeto's "Messenger", the 2025 Awwwards Developer Site of the Year.
3. **Systems-driven learning games**, where the mechanic is itself the learning act: *Chants of Sennaar*, *The Darkest Files*, *Venba*, *Pentiment*.

Museum collection sites such as the Rijksmuseum (Webby 2025, GLAMi 2021) and London Museum (M+H 2025) win on interface clarity and on linking objects into a knowledge graph. That is exactly the Atlas pattern.

### Cited Findings

**A. Web, heritage and 3D experiences**

**1. Getty Villa — "Persepolis Reimagined" (Media.Monks, 2022)**
- *Awards:*
  - Awwwards Site of the Month, June 2022. [V] — [Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-getty-persepolis-reimagined.html)
  - Awwwards Site of the Day and Developer Award. [V-sec] — [Awwwards Facebook post](https://www.facebook.com/awwwards/videos/persepolis-reimagined-by-mediamonks-nl-wins-sotd-devawardfor-getty-we-made-an-im/3353466244964019/)
  - FWA of the Year and FWA People's Choice of the Year. [V-sec] — [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/news/mediamonks-and-getty-win-fwa-of-the-year-and-fwa-peoples-choice-award-of-the-year)
  - Also listed by the One Show. — [One Club](https://www.oneclub.org/awards/theoneshow/-award/48596/persepolis-reimagined/)
- *Mechanics:* a fixed camera path guided by scroll ("fly towards the Gate of All Nations"). It moves seamlessly from preloader to video to real-time WebGL, using a chroma-key colour and camera sync. — [Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-getty-persepolis-reimagined.html)
- *Content and research:* made with Getty curators and Prof. Ali Mousavi.
  - Models came from Persepolis3D and were retopologised.
  - The vegetation is historically plausible for the site.
  - The score uses period instruments (kemenche, duduk, ney, daf…) chosen with a specialist. — same source
- *Tech:* Vue.js, GSAP and WebGL 2 with custom JSON scene export. Authoring tools were Unity, Maya, Substance, World Creator and Clarisse. Performance techniques were instancing, frustum culling, LOD and normal maps. One master scene was used to generate the WebGL, 360° dome, map view and animation outputs. — same source
- *Stated lesson:* "Crucial to an immersive experience is creating seamless transitions from start to end." All scenes share the same post-processing (vignette, bloom, grain). — same source
- Accessibility and mobile handling are not documented. — same source

**2. "Messenger" by abeto (2025)**
- *Award:* Awwwards **Developer Site of the Year 2025**. [V] — [Awwwards Annual Awards](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards/winners)
- *What it is:* a free, roughly 15-minute multiplayer web game. The player is a teenage mail carrier on a tiny spherical planet with seven locations.
  - Exploration is non-linear, with "no intrusive guidance."
  - Controls work with one finger on mobile and mouse only on desktop, with an auto-following camera.
  - Instances are capped at 10 players, who talk through 3D emoji instead of chat.
  - It is framed as a "no-stress zone." — [Awwwards: Messenger](https://www.awwwards.com/messenger.html)
- *Tech:* Three.js, three-mesh-bvh, vanilla JS, C++ (WASM-generated typography), custom LOD and a colour atlas. Assets were built in Houdini and Blender. — same source
- *Reach:* "millions of impressions," thousands of daily players months after launch, and a Wikipedia page. — same source; [Wikipedia: Messenger (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenger_(video_game))

### Inferences

**Transferable lessons per comparable**
- **Persepolis:** use one authoritative master scene to produce every view: globe/map, timeline fly-through and object close-up. Hide every transition. Get named specialist consultation and say so on the page; juries and press repeat it. Budget heavily for performance work (instancing, LOD, culling).
- **Messenger:**
  - A tiny, legible world with seven places beats a sprawling one. The Edo unit could be one small "floating world" diorama of Nihonbashi, with publisher, carver, printer, censor, bookseller and the export dock.
  - Use one-finger controls and an auto camera.
  - Set no fail states and no stress.
  - Keep sessions to about 15 minutes, which maps well onto a unit.
- **Rijksmuseum and London Museum:** a knowledge graph pays off when the interface makes object-to-story links feel effortless. Juries also reward openly sharing method (M+H). Publishing an "Atlas data model and how we built it" note is cheap and helps.
- **Finding Freedom:**
  - First-person voices and a coherent illustrative style (watercolour there, washi and sumi here).
  - Classroom resources and documented accessibility compliance.
  - It went on winning for five years (2021 MUSE to 2026 Webby). Durable, well-documented work keeps earning.
- **The Darkest Files, Chants of Sennaar and Botany Manor:** the strongest learning-game pattern is **deduction from period documents**. Natural labs follow:
  - Decoding censorship seals (*kiwame*, date seals).
  - Attributing a print from publisher marks.
  - Dating a print by pigment, as Prussian blue arrived around 1829–1830.
  
  Each needs a journal or notebook mechanic that checks the hypothesis, as in Chants of Sennaar.
- **Venba:** turn a craft procedure into the puzzle. For example, register and colour-block a print in printing order (key block, then colour blocks, then *bokashi* gradation), and "restore" a lost impression.
- **Pentiment:** typography and material style can carry the history itself, for example changing letterforms and paper for each period.
- **Is the Love Song Dying?:** let the reader hold a position, then use the data to challenge it.
- **Team size:** award-winning games here are often made by 2–10 people (Rundisc's core of two; Messenger by a small studio). A focused scope matters more than headcount.

## Q3. What failure modes keep cultural and learning projects from winning, and what do juries expect on accessibility?

### Takeaway
Juries punish several things:
- slow or janky performance;
- weak mobile versions;
- templates and placeholder or stock content;
- motion that decorates rather than explains;
- polished homepages with thin inner pages.

Accessibility is formally required by the Webby criteria, D&AD's "Inclusive" subcategory, the G4C Accessibility Award, and the former MUSE values. The craft-first web awards (Awwwards, CSSDA) publish **no** accessibility criteria. An accessible WebGL experience therefore stands out rather than being expected.

### Cited Findings
- The Webby Visual Design criterion includes "improving inclusion for people with disabilities." Functionality requires that it "loads quickly" and that "any new technology used is functional and relevant for the intended audience." [V] — [Webby Judging Criteria](https://www.webbyawards.com/judging-criteria/)
- Webby Interactivity must be "more than a rollover or choosing what to click on next; it allows you, as a user, to give and receive." This rules out click-through "interactivity." [V] — same source
- D&AD Digital Design has a dedicated **Inclusive** subcategory (5103) and judges "execution over idea." [V] — [D&AD Digital Design](https://www.dandad.org/awards/d-ad-awards/categories-2025/digital-design)
- Cannes Lions Digital Craft added "Excellence in Image Description" as a new subcategory, so alt text and image description are now a craft discipline. [V] — [Cannes Lions](https://www.canneslions.com/awards/lions/digital-craft/what-you-need-to-know)
- G4C gives an Accessibility Award: Botany Manor in 2025 for "no time limits and adjustable controls," and EA Sports FC 26 in 2026. [V] — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/games-for-change-announces-2025-award-winners/); [CGMagazine](https://www.cgmagonline.com/news/games-for-change-2026-award-winners/)
- Japan's Good Design Award "human perspective" asks whether there is due consideration for users: usability, understandability and kindness ("親切さ"). [V] — [G-Mark](https://www.g-mark.org/apply/gda/screening/perspective)
- The AAM MUSE values listed "education, accessibility, and inclusiveness." [V] — [AAM](https://www.aam-us.org/programs/awards-recognition/media-technology-muse-awards/)
- Finding Freedom advertises Section 508 compliance as part of its award story. [V-sec] — [AmRev Museum](https://www.amrevmuseum.org/press-releases/museum-of-the-american-revolution-wins-webby-award-for-finding-freedom-online-experience)
- Neither the Awwwards evaluation page nor the CSSDA About page mentions accessibility. [V] — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/); [CSSDA](https://www.cssdesignawards.com/about)
- An agency blog (Utsubo; opinion, written by a practitioner, not a jury) lists the following failure modes. [secondary / opinion] — [Utsubo blog](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/award-winning-website-design-guide)
  - Beautiful sites that "take 5+ seconds to load."
  - "Mobile afterthoughts," saying judges test on mobile first.
  - Templates ("Judges recognize WordPress themes and Webflow templates instantly").
  - Decorative motion that doesn't serve content.
  - Polished homepages with unfinished inner pages, which create a "negative halo."
  - "Lorem ipsum," stock photos and machine-translated copy.
  - It also suggests performance targets of LCP <1.5 s, CLS <0.05, INP <100 ms and sustained 60 fps. These are the agency's targets, not official jury thresholds.
- The Persepolis case study, the most decorated heritage WebGL comparable, documents no accessibility or mobile work. This shows the craft awards do not require it. [V] — [Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-getty-persepolis-reimagined.html)

### Inferences
- **Failure modes for a cultural or learning course specifically:**
  1. A generic LMS or template shell around beautiful content.
  2. A 3D globe that is decorative only, so the map does not change what the learner can find out.
  3. "Gamification" that is only points or badges, so the mechanic is not the learning act.
  4. Thin units after a showcase first unit.
  5. Motion without `prefers-reduced-motion` fallbacks.
  6. WebGL without a DOM or text equivalent, which fails screen readers and Webby or D&AD inclusion.
  7. Poor mobile frame rates.
  8. Unsourced or AI-generic copy and imagery.
  9. No visible expert or curatorial credibility.
- **Accessibility as a differentiator.** Because the craft awards ignore accessibility and the purpose awards prize it, a WebGL Atlas with these features can compete in both lanes:
  - a full-parity list or table view;
  - keyboard navigation of the map and timeline;
  - a reduced-motion "paper" mode;
  - captioned and transcribed audio;
  - colour choices that do not rely on hue alone. This matters because seal red is reserved for censorship: pair it with a shape or icon.
- Mark-ups based on published but unofficial guidance are labelled as such above. The only official performance criterion is the Webby "loads quickly."

### Gaps
- Published accessibility requirements for GLAMi, IIB, FWA and Red Dot were not found.
- No official jury statement on WebGL accessibility was found. The failure-mode list relies partly on one agency blog.

## Q4. What distilled quality bar or checklist follows for Creative World F1 and the Edo unit?

### Takeaway
To be credible across both craft and purpose juries, the project needs:
- one coherent, materially specific art direction carried into every component;
- a 3D/map Atlas whose interaction is itself an act of inquiry;
- labs where the game mechanic is the learning skill (deduction from period evidence, a craft procedure);
- seamless, performant transitions on mobile;
- documented accessibility parity;
- named expert sources;
- a public "how we built it" write-up.

### Cited Findings
These are the anchors behind the checklist. Each is sourced above.
- Weights: Design 40, Usability 30, Creativity 20, Content 10. — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)
- Seven Webby criteria, including inclusion and "give and receive" interactivity. — [Webby](https://www.webbyawards.com/judging-criteria/)
- D&AD judges execution over idea and has an Inclusive subcategory. — [D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/awards/d-ad-awards/categories-2025/digital-design)
- Cannes Lions judges "execution and experience." — [Cannes Lions](https://www.canneslions.com/awards/lions/digital-craft/what-you-need-to-know)
- Good Design Award judges from human, industrial, social and temporal perspectives. — [G-Mark](https://www.g-mark.org/apply/gda/screening/perspective)
- Seamless transitions, research partners, and one master scene with many outputs (Persepolis). — [Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/case-study-getty-persepolis-reimagined.html)
- Tiny world, one-finger controls, no-stress play, about 15 minutes (Messenger). — [Awwwards: Messenger](https://www.awwwards.com/messenger.html)
- Source-evaluation learning objectives plus lesson plans (The Darkest Files). — [Paintbucket](https://paintbucket.de/en/projekte/the-darkest-files)
- A journal that checks deciphering hypotheses (Chants of Sennaar). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chants_of_Sennaar)
- Sharing learnings with the sector (London Museum). — [M+H](https://awards.museumsandheritage.com/awards/2025-winners/best-use-of-digital-uk-5/)
- No time limits and adjustable controls (Botany Manor). — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/games-for-change-announces-2025-award-winners/)

### Inferences — the checklist
The checklist is synthesis, not sourced claims. Each line maps to the anchors above.

**1. Design (Awwwards 40%; Webby Visual Design; Cannes Form)**
- Use one material language end to end: washi grain, sumi line, indigo, and seal red **only** for censorship. Carry it into UI chrome, loaders, cursors, 3D shaders (paper grain and ink-wash post-processing as a unified "look," as Persepolis did) and typography.
- Make no template-looking component. Every inner unit page must hold up to the same scrutiny as the home page.

**2. Usability (Awwwards 30%; Webby Structure/Functionality; Good Design "human")**
- Make the mobile version first-class: one-thumb navigation and an auto camera, as in Messenger.
- Lazy-load 3D, use instancing and LOD, and degrade to a 2D "paper map" on weak devices.
- Give the Atlas three synced views: globe/map, timeline and connections. Make it easy to form a mental model, which is the Webby's exact wording.

**3. Creativity and Innovation (Awwwards 20%; Webby Innovation; IGF Nuovo)**
- Make at least one interaction that is "fundamentally different," for example:
  - pulling apart a print into its woodblocks;
  - walking the Tōkaidō or the Hundred Views locations on the 3D map with prints anchored to their sites;
  - following the export route from Edo to Paris across the globe in real time.

**4. Content (Awwwards 10%, but central for Webby, G4C, IIB and M+H)**
- Use real, cited sources, with object images credited to their holding institutions.
- Name the expert reviewers.
- Write no filler; every unit page should be as rich as the worked Edo unit.

**5. Real gamification, where the mechanic is the learning act**
- Deduction labs from period evidence:
  - date a print by its censor seals;
  - spot Prussian blue;
  - attribute it to a publisher.
- A journal or notebook that validates hypotheses, as in Chants of Sennaar.
- Craft-procedure puzzles: block-printing order, as in Venba.
- A collectible Atlas that fills in as learners discover nodes, so progression is exploration rather than points, as in Messenger.
- No fail states or time limits, as in Botany Manor.

**6. Accessibility, the deliberate differentiator**
- A full DOM or text twin of every 3D and map view.
- Keyboard control and screen-reader labels for Atlas nodes.
- A `prefers-reduced-motion` "still paper" mode.
- Captions and transcripts.
- Colour plus shape coding.
- An accessibility statement in the submission.

**7. Credibility and impact (G4C, M+H, GLAMi, Good Design "social" and "temporal")**
- Classroom kit: learning objectives and lesson plans, as with The Darkest Files.
- An open method and data write-up for the Atlas, as with London Museum.
- Evidence of use, such as learners, completions or pageviews. Finding Freedom cited about 1.5 million pageviews.

**8. Submission strategy**
- **First wave:**
  1. Webby: Websites and Mobile Sites, Education or Cultural, Best UI or Visual Design.
  2. FWA.
  3. Awwwards, once the WebGL Atlas is ready.
- **Second wave:**
  1. IIB, for the Atlas visualisation.
  2. Good Design Award, given the Japan-themed unit and operator eligibility.
  3. G4C Best in Learning, if the labs grow into a standalone game.
- **Avoid planning around:** MUSE, Japan Media Arts and Malofiej, which are dormant or ended.

### Gaps
- No published jury guidance says what *minimum* depth of a course earns learning-category awards. The checklist is an inference.
- I did not verify Good Design Award eligibility for a free, non-commercial web course.
