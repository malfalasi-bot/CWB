## Verdict

**Refuse to sign as it stands: 2 of 5.** The page is well made and mostly traceable, and the lints are clean, but the lints cannot see what is wrong here: one dated event on the page did not happen (the Paris Opéra dancers did not strike in December 2023; the notice was lifted on 21 December), one origin story has no record at all (Frederick Freed "began the marks"), one sentence attributes to the company figures that are two journalists' (the 150,000–330,000 output), the only independent source for the founding gives a different year (1920) that the register does not record, four register records cite a source that does not carry the value, and the header has no archive copies and no spot-check. Fix the ranked "high" items and the register bookkeeping and this becomes a 4 that I would sign with the usual caveats.

## Ranked issues

### High

**H1. A strike that did not happen.** Page: "When the Paris Opéra's dancers struck in December 2023, their union said…"; register `bal-paris-strike-2023`, claim "Paris Opéra dancers strike over preparation-time pay", confidence documented, source an article of 20 December announcing the strike. The notice was lifted on 21 December 2023 (Sceneweb, 21 Dec 2023; Franceinfo, same day) and the performances of 23, 25 and 31 December went ahead. *Fix:* rewrite as "When the Paris Opéra's dancers gave notice of a Christmas strike in December 2023, their union said… The notice was lifted on 21 December after the house promised a budget for the time." Change the record's claim to "give notice of a strike"; add a record for the lifting (21 Dec 2023, Sceneweb, documented with Franceinfo as second source); add both to `sources`.

**H2. An origin claim with no record.** Page: "Frederick Freed began the marks after he opened his shop in Cecil Court in 1929, so a returned shoe could be traced to the hand that made it. Dancers began to order by the mark." No record in the register says who began the marks, when, or why; `pass2_verify` line 79 confirms the company's history page "does not describe the system"; Spitalfields says only that makers are "identified by the symbols they impress" and that each ballerina "chooses a maker", not that she orders by symbol. The brief cites a Pointe Magazine piece ("Custom creations") that never reached the register. This is an attribution (rule 1) and reads as an invention story ("began", rule 2). *Fix:* either fetch and register a source that says Frederick Freed instituted the marks for traceability (Pointe "Custom creations", or Freed's own makers page, if either says it), as probable; or write what the sources carry: "Each maker presses a symbol into the sole: a crown, a wine glass, a fish. Why the marks began is not in the record we have; dancers choose a maker and keep him."

**H3. Journalists' figures attributed to the company.** Page: "The company's own pages put its output anywhere from 150,000 to 330,000 pairs a year." 150,000 is Spitalfields Life (a journalist, 2013); 333,000 is the Spectator Australia (a journalist, 2019); the company's own figures are "over/nearly 250,000" (pass 1, pages not in the register) and 48,000 + 112,000 ≈ 160,000 (a Freed video reported by Dance Spirit, 2015, not in the register). Rule 8 is inverted: the sentence labels press figures as the company's. The record `bal-freed-output` sources its head value (160,000) to `freed-history`, which carries no output figure at all (fetched); the 333,000 alternate is sourced to `moscow-times-2020`, which is about Grishko. *Fix:* rewrite as "Nobody agrees how many pairs Freed makes a year: a journalist was told 150,000 in 2013 and another 333,000 in 2019; the company's own pages have said about 250,000. Every figure is the teller's." Re-source every value to the page that carries it (add Dance Spirit 2015, Spectator 2019, the two Freed pages); confidence contested, and carry the word "contested" in the sentence.

**H4. The founding date's one independent source disagrees, and the register hides it.** `bal-freed-founded` (1929, documented) rests on the company's own history page; the editor note says "corroborated by Spitalfields Life 2013", but Spitalfields says "Founded in 1920 by Frederick Freed, a sample shoemaker, and his wife Dora, a milliner, in St Martin's Lane". (Cecil Court runs off St Martin's Lane, so the place is compatible; the year is not.) An alternate exists and is unrecorded. *Fix:* add `alternates: [{value: 1920, sources: [spitalfields-2013], note: "'Founded in 1920 … in St Martin's Lane'"}]`; correct the editor note; keep 1929 as the company's own date ("Freed's history page says 1929; a 2013 press visit was told 1920"). Documented holds only on the reading that a firm owns its founding date; say so in the note, or mark probable. "Dora" should be sourced to Spitalfields, not Freed.

**H5. Register records cite sources that do not carry the value.** Found at source: (a) `bal-makers-count` alternate 28 → `freed-history` (page has no maker count); alternate 24 → `spitalfields-2013` with note "Business Insider 2021" (Business Insider is not in `sources`); alternate 26 → `pointe-book-2012`, whose URL is the "Pointe shoe" article, with a note citing the "Freed of London" article. (b) `bal-freed-output` as in H3. (c) `bal-freed-hackney` (1960s) → `freed-history` (no Hackney on the page) and `spitalfields-2013` (Hackney, Well Street; the decade of the move is not shown to me). (d) `bal-hours-per-pair` → a source titled as The Pointe Book, when the Wikipedia sentence carrying 10–20 hours is unfooted. A reader who follows these links finds nothing, which is the one thing a register must never do. *Fix:* one source id per page actually fetched; every alternate's `sources` must be the page that says that number; Business Insider, Dance Spirit 2015, Spectator 2019, the two Freed pages added or the alternates dropped; `bal-freed-hackney` either sourced to a page that gives the decade or reduced to "Hackney (Spitalfields 2013)" with no date.

### Medium

**M1. Contested and probable records without their word in the sentence (rule 2).** Contested: `bal-makers-count` ("depends on who you ask … somewhere between twenty and thirty") and `bal-freed-output` carry no "contested". Probable: `bal-hours-per-pair` ("by the usual estimate"), `bal-nycb-budget`, `bal-paris-prep-hours` carry no "probable". The editor-view tags show the words; the learner view, which is what the rule governs, does not. *Fix:* either put the word in ("the count is contested"; "probably ten to twenty hours") or recast the record as an attribution ("Linnette Roe put her company's shoe bill at…", which is then documented as what she said, and the record's claim must say so).

**M2. Single-journalist "documented" records.** `bal-sole-press`, `bal-crown-pairs-day`, `bal-crown-years`, and the editorial half of `bal-box-materials` rest on Spitalfields Life alone. It is a good source: a long-running eyewitness record with photographs by Patricia Niven, the best account of the factory in print, and the right basis for the page's central example. But it is one pseudonymous journalist ("the gentle author"), unnamed, writing what the factory told him, and the constitution's "documented" needs a holder's record, a primary source, a paper or two independent sources. *Fix:* mark the Crown figures and the 4,000 lb probable and keep the source named in the sentence, which the page already does well ("when a journalist visited…"). To raise them: the Spectator Australia (2019, "31 pairs a day, or 35" for another maker), the 2015 Freed video via Dance Spirit (700 pairs a day), and Business Insider 2021 corroborate the method and the rate; the construction (paper, hessian, paste, turned, baked) is independently given by 4dancers and can stay documented. Also correct "counted forty-one pairs a day at his bench" to "was told" or "recorded": the article narrates the figure; it does not count.

**M3. Universal negatives and counts the sources do not give.** Quiz feedback: "wood was never used" (the sources say there is no wood in Freed's block today, not that none was ever used); "a moulded plastic shank arrived with one maker only" and "most companies still dance in paste" (no record; Wikipedia says "plastic in newer shoes", plural). Body: "It is also the only count of this labour anyone has made" and "no other part of ballet's economy is counted in public at all" (false as written: the ROH publishes audited accounts; the register's own BRB appeal gives £260,000). *Fix:* "there is no wood in it, and none of the makers whose work we read use any"; "a moulded shank arrived with Gaynor Minden, probably in 1993, and paste remains the common method"; "the only count of this labour we found"; "almost nothing else in a company's making is counted in public".

**M4. The learning objective the page cannot meet.** "name three of them" (makers): the page names Crown by mark, Linnette Roe (a shoe-room supervisor) and Frederick Freed (a founder). It mentions "sixteen people a journalist named" without naming one. The brief had Michael Cripps, mark "J", 152 pairs a week (Pointe "Custom creations"); he was dropped and nothing replaced him. *Fix:* name three from Spitalfields as a journalist's record (probable): Worral Thomas, hand laster; Tony Collins, machine operator, forty years; Sheila Goodman, pointe-shoe finisher; or change the objective to "name the one place a maker's identity reaches the stage". Note also that "sixteen" and "forty years" are numbers the register lacks (rule 1; the lint cannot see spelled-out numbers), and Crown is not "named", so the count of named people is fifteen plus one mark.

**M5. The Royal Ballet figure at one remove.** The page says "The Royal Ballet says…"; the source is SOFII, a fundraising showcase reproducing the appeal. Kind "institutional" is wrong (it is a sector case study by a named fundraiser). *Fix:* add the RBO's own appeal page as the primary source when reachable (archive it), keep SOFII as the second; re-kind SOFII "sector case study, interest: fundraising showcase". Same for Heritage Crafts: "a listing body" understates it; it is an advocacy charity for endangered crafts, and its count is "as reviewed 2025".

**M6. "A worn pair with its mark, signed by Darcey Bussell".** The ROH record shows no mark, no maker, no date. *Fix:* "A pair of pointe shoes worn and signed by Darcey Bussell sits in the Royal Opera House's collection; whether a mark is on the sole the record does not say."

### Low

**L1. Register precision fields.** `bal-taglioni-sylphide` precision "month" with display "12 March 1832"; `bal-gaynor-patent` "month" with a day in hand (21 April 1998); `bal-spitalfields-visit`, `bal-roe-2018`, `bal-paris-strike-2023` "month" with year displays. Set precision to what the source gives (day for the first two).

**L2. Statements shoehorned into number records.** `bal-box-materials` value 0, `bal-turn-shoe` value 1, `bal-dancers-sew` value 1, all "kind: number, precision: year". They are statements; the schema wants a `statement` kind or they should be editor notes under a real record. As they stand a reader of the register sees "value: 0 … display: no wood".

**L3. Small wording drifts from source.** "a night in a warm room dries the paste hard" (source: baked overnight in an oven, 4dancers c. 70 °C); "since, probably, the 1870s" (register range 1870s–1890s; say "the later nineteenth century"); "hessian, a coarse jute cloth" is fine; "in darned slippers with a leather sole and no block" (Pointe says darned and leather-soled; "no block" is the writer's inference and should read "before the block"); the shank paragraph (full, three-quarter, half; slit by the dancer) rests on Wikipedia and has no register row, acceptable for a description but note it in the editor view.

**L4. Taglioni's date deserves a better source than two blogs.** 12 March 1832 is documented in every reference work (Guest, *The Romantic Ballet in Paris*; the Opéra's own chronology; the BnF). Two secondary web pages make it "documented" on the two-sources reading only; a reference-work or BnF citation would make the strongest date on the page as strong as the patent.

**L5. Header.** `signed_by`, `signed_date`, `spotcheck_sample` null (correct for a draft; the colophon says so). Every `archive_url` is null, against the research skill's "sources (ids into sources.yaml with kind, URL, date accessed, archive URL)"; Spitalfields alone carries most of the page and is a personal blog that can vanish. Access dates are all 2026-10-20 and present. *Fix before freeze:* archive every source (Wayback), record the URL; run and record the spot-check on every documented record; then sign.

**L6. Prose lint.** Median sentence 13 words (floor 14) and 926 words (ceiling 900): the short sentences are a style choice that reads well here; the length will fall when H3 and M3 are tightened. Not a sign-off matter.

## The three changes that would most improve it

1. **Make every record's source the page that says the value, and record the alternates the sources actually give.** H1, H3, H4, H5 are all one failure: the register was filled from the research notes rather than from the pages, so ids drifted (Grishko's article sourcing a Freed figure; Freed's page sourcing a count it does not carry; a 1920 founding date nobody wrote down; a strike nobody held). Re-walk all 26 records with the URL open; add the five missing sources (Sceneweb 21 Dec 2023, Dance Spirit 2015, Spectator Australia 2019, Business Insider 2021, the two Freed pages); archive each.
2. **Put the confidence word, or the teller, in every sentence a non-documented record drives, and downgrade the single-journalist "documented" rows to probable with the journalist named.** The page's own thesis is that figures belong to whoever gives them; the register should hold itself to that. Keep the construction documented (two independent accounts), make Crown's 41 and 24 and the 4,000 lb probable, make the NYCB and Paris figures attribution records.
3. **Deliver the objective "name three of them" with three names from the 2013 visit, labelled as a journalist's record**, and give the mark its true, smaller story: the marks identify makers; dancers choose a maker; who began the system and why is not in the record. That sentence is more honest and better teaches the page's lesson than the Frederick Freed origin story does.

## What to keep

- The structure: materials → the mark → the money → the history → what could not be shown. It is the right order and the "could not show" section is the best thing on the page.
- The rule-8 discipline in "What a company pays, by its own account": the figures are labelled as the companies' and the labelling is itself taught ("tell a date a record supports from a figure a company gives about itself"). Keep that objective; it is the unit's spine.
- The treatment of the pointe dispute: "among the earliest known … the date is contested, and who did it earliest keeps moving" is exactly how the constitution wants a dispute carried in a sentence.
- The honesty about the firms' dates ("probably right but not yet checked at source") and about the patent ("1993" is a launch, 1998 is the grant): the register is right where the trade press is wrong, and the page says so without fuss.
- The quiz: one question, three plausible answers, feedback that teaches; no score.
- The link-out handling of the ROH object and the colophon's plain statement that no human has read the page.
- Spitalfields Life as the central example. Keep it; name it; grade it as what it is.
