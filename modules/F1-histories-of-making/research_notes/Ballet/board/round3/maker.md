# Maker's review, round 3: "The shoe" (v3)

Reviewer: theatrical shoemaker and costume maker, twenty years in a company wardrobe and shoe room, now teaching. Read: `board/round3/_page-v3.md`, the built `dist/the-shoe.html` (schematic SVG read in source) and `shot-1440.png` (figure cropped and inspected at full size).

**Verdict.** I would hand this to an apprentice as a first reading. The construction paragraph is now in bench order (pleats, sole, turn, oven) and says nothing the sources cannot carry; the figure is a shoe at last, with a heel, an opening, a throat, a vamp and a platform, and the numbered key is the right way to label a section; the makers are named and the pay sentence ("He is paid by the pair") is the one that explains forty pairs a day; the Heritage Crafts sentence is now what the listing says; the gender line ("the benches in 2013 were men's, and nobody in binding or finishing has a mark") is true, sourced and the best single sentence about the division of labour on any page of this unit. What keeps it from a 5 is one drawing fault that undoes half the figure's purpose, one paragraph that still lists the dancer's work without its reasons, and one wrong word in the order. The deferred items (sizes, widths, specials and the docket; the fitter; Suffolk by name; the "pick it up" test; lining and insole) are honestly recorded as open and I accept the deferral, with the note below.

## Remaining issues

### 1. The figure shades the hollow as well as the block, so the toe still reads as solid.
Figure, path 4: `M430 134 ... L584 190 ... C430 212 C452 192 462 170 458 152 ... Z` with `fill-opacity=".08"`, and the label `3` placed at (500,170), inside the innermost shell.

The three nested U-shaped shells are right: that is a block, a band of layers around the toe. But the fill covers everything from the outer shell to the platform, including the cavity inside the innermost shell, and the "3" sits in that cavity. On the screenshot the toe is one grey mass with three outlines in it, and the key tells the learner that grey mass is "the block ... built around the hollow the toes sit in". The hollow is therefore invisible, which is the fault round 2 raised, moved one step. Fix: fill only the band (outer shell minus innermost shell, `fill-rule="evenodd"` with both subpaths in one path, or fill the band and overpaint the cavity with the page background), leave the cavity the same white as the rest of the shoe interior so the eye reads one continuous space from the opening into the toe, and move the "3" onto the band near the top of the vamp. Ten minutes' work, and the drawing then teaches the thing the quiz asks.

### 2. The dancer's work still has no reason attached.
Text: "The dancer sews her own ribbons and elastics, darns the platform and breaks the pair in, on her own time".

Three rounds, same sentence, and it is still a list. The learner needs the why or the Paris figure that follows it reads as fuss: ribbons sewn where her ankle needs them to hold; the platform darned for grip and so the satin does not shred in an act; the shank bent where her own foot bends, because the maker built to a docket, not to her. One clause each, no new source needed; it is what "breaks the pair in" means. Fix: "sews her own ribbons and elastics where her ankle needs them, darns the platform for grip, and bends and softens the pair to her own foot, on her own time".

### 3. "The finished shoe is turned" is the wrong word in an otherwise correct order.
Text: "the leather sole is pasted and hammered on at the bench, and the finished shoe is turned the right way round."

It is not finished when it is turned: the shank goes in after turning, and the shoe then goes through the binding and finishing rooms the next paragraph names. Fix: "and the shoe is turned the right way round and the shank put in. The shoes go into an oven overnight". If the register cannot carry "shank after turning" (no source read says it), then simply "and the shoe is turned the right way round", and let the shank sentence that follows stand on its own.

### 4. Small
- "two businesses in the country": the listing also counts three apprentices at Freed; one clause, and the word "endangered" stops meaning "dying".
- "No photograph of a shoe being made is on this page": a link is not a licence. The research has three films a learner can watch (Freed's 2015 introduction film, the 2021 Business Insider factory film, the RBO "How Royal Ballet dancers prepare their pointe shoes"); name and link them in "What is not here" so the drawing is a stand-in for something the learner can go and see.
- Figure key, item 5, "shank, the stiffener under the arch": the dashed line floats a little above the sole with nothing drawn to hold it; since the insole is deferred, the caption's "not a particular shoe" covers it, but when the insole arrives it belongs between 5 and 6.
- "A pair lasts probably ten to twenty hours": the hedge is right, the word order is not; "A pair probably lasts".

## On the deferrals
Lining and insole not drawn because not in the sources read: correct discipline, and the figure survives without them. Sizes, widths, specials, the docket and the fitter deferred to a later page: acceptable, but that page is where "a dancer chooses a maker" becomes a fact about an order form rather than a sentiment, so it should be the next page on shoes, not an afterthought. The "pick it up" test: I will write it for whichever page takes it; six lines.

## Keep
All of v2's keeps, plus: the construction paragraph as it now stands (bar the one word); the numbered key; "He is paid by the pair"; "the benches in 2013 were men's, and nobody in binding or finishing has a mark"; "so even the company's count is contested"; "yet says trade is buoyant"; the first-name note in "What is not here", which is the right way to carry a journalist's record.
