## 0. The verdict in ten lines

1. The research is the strongest single body of case material in F1: sourced, confidence-marked, two readings kept, 29 of 148 register works made outside western Europe, North America and Russia. None of it should be lost, and none of it needs to be.
2. The plans were written against the research, not against the program. The latest plan (`CASE_WORLD_PLAN.md`) never names an F1 unit, the spec template, the four personas, the seed worlds, P5, the Atlas, the Timeline Atlas, the Object Autopsy, the card tiers, a partner voice, the closing test, or M1.11a. A text search of all four plans for these terms finds only "regional slot", the two lanes, and one line that gives the Historian voice a bookkeeping job.
3. In their place it builds parallel structures: acts, sittings, a braid, a case card, ten object studies with their own five moves, three card depths, 24 "concept threads", a six-persona board, a separate claims store, a separate route.
4. It conflicts with four things already settled or in force: the lab-or-simulation invariant (principle 5), local authorship of the regional slot (F1.31; F1.17 §2a), the Concept DNA schema (`learner_world.kind` is `own` or `seed`), and the "one hub" rule. It also works from a stale F1 size (34 hours; v3.6 is 4 / 22 / 22, about 48).
5. The program already has a home for the plan's central thesis ("the concept is the product"): **M1.11a World-building lessons from film, games and performance: bibles, rules, stakes, time** (GRM.n, GRM.t; lab: Bible Anatomy), with a link row from F1.4 already in the link register.
6. Inside F1, ballet has a usable case for over twenty of the 35 units and a strong one for about fifteen. It is a lead case in three: **F1.23** (the maker's mark), **F1.4** (performance as a teacher of making: notation and repertoire) and **F1.17** (who commissions). F1.23 is the natural home of the material already built; F1.4 is the natural home of the "works" research.
7. A separate ballet strand is not needed in F1. If the owner wants depth, it should be a named Mastery path through F1's existing Mastery units (F1.27, F1.28, F1.28a, F1.29), about 6–8 hours, not a 35 + 20 hour deep path.
8. For the four personas, the latest plan's 7-hour "F1 core" is a block with no position in F1's order, opening on vocabulary and method. Each persona would likely leave before the part useful to them, which sits in the deep path.
9. Assessment is the largest gap: no closing contributions, no success criteria, no partner tests, nothing into F1's closing test, and act products that write fields owned by M1–M4.
10. Recommended shape: put ballet into the units it serves, through the spec template, at about 2.5 hours of F1 Practice time. Import the library into the Atlas at the voice guide's four tiers. Hand the concept, collection and product material to M1.11a, M2, M3, M4, S2, S3 and S5 through the link register. Send five decisions to the Hub (section 5).

### 1.3 Conflicts with what is already settled

1. **Lab invariant** (principle 5, approved at G1). "This unit is read, not played" (`BRIEF.md`) and `RETHINK.md`'s view that interactives explain nothing a captioned photograph cannot are arguments to change the principle. Until the Hub changes it, every ballet placement needs a lab. The cheapest compliant labs already exist: the credit map, the autopsy, the Spectrum Sort, the comparison wall.
2. **Regional slot authorship** (F1.31: "the program builds the frame ... and leaves the content empty"). The Gulf route case should become structure prompts and Atlas nodes, not program-written narrative.
3. **Concept DNA schema.** A *Giselle* DNA cannot validate (`kind` enum). It is also a historical reading, not a world under construction, so it would teach learners that a DNA is a description of a finished work. Keep it as a case, compared with Salt Road in M2, unless the Hub decides otherwise.
4. **P5.** The Hub row reads "recommended exemplar: Salt Road (P4, P5)". A second exemplar is a new decision, not a consequence of the ballet research.
5. **F1 size.** `CASE_WORLD_PLAN.md` §1 and `decisions/BENCHMARKS.md` use "F1 is 34 hours (4 / 14 / 16)", the v3.0 figure. The Module File gives v3.6 as 4 / 22 / 22 (line 203). The conclusion that ballet cannot fit as a 44-hour module survives; the arithmetic should be corrected.
6. **Link register rule.** "Every lineage unit opens the Atlas, not its own timeline" (Phase 4, item 6). The Prologue's anchor-date map is its own timeline.
7. **Opening move.** The built page (`the-shoe.md`, line 12) still opens with "Before you read on, choose one", which breaks the owner's own 21 October rule cited in `UNIT_PLAN.md` §1. The plans and the page disagree; the Hub has not ruled between "Prediction first" and "no opening question".

### 2.2 The natural home

There are two, for two kinds of material.

- **F1.23 is the home of what is already built.** The shoe page's thesis ("the dancer is on the poster, the designer is in the programme, the maker is a symbol on a sole") is F1.23's essential question in one object. Its register already carries the sources the unit's credit map needs. It also answers F1.23's reach in a way the unit lacks: a living workshop with named benches, beside Dave, Arezzo and Jingdezhen. Cost to place: low.
- **F1.4 is the home of the "works" research.** F1.4 asks what a maker can learn from a performance, and its link row to M1.11a already exists ("the Jukebox recordings reappear"). Ballet's works carry exactly the methods F1.4 names (rules, repertoire) plus one it lacks (notation). F1.4 gives the taste in F1; M1.11a gives the depth in Method, where premise, rules and grammar belong.

F1.17 is the third lead because the briefer lane needs it: ballet's commissioning documents (Mazarin's brief, Pillet's star vehicle, Vsevolozhsky's letter, Diaghilev's committee) are briefs as historical objects, which is F1.17's first concept part.

### 2.3 Is a separate ballet strand needed?

**Not in F1.** Three reasons, each from the program's own documents:

1. **F1's argument.** Its threshold concept is "a web with obscured strands, not a line". A 7-hour core plus a 55-hour deep path on one European-origin art form makes ballet larger than F1's Mastery tier (22 hours) and larger than any world chapter. The plan sees this risk itself ("ballet at the scale of one F1 strand among many") but then sizes it far above that scale.
2. **The evidence the plan cites.** Comparison across cases, not depth in one case, is what transfers. Ballet cases distributed across about twenty units, each beside a case from another world, deliver that comparison twenty times. A strand delivers it once per wall.
3. **Field ownership.** The deep path's products (premise, rules, obligations, collection map, adjacent translation, version history) are M1–M4 work. Doing them in Foundations breaks the sequencing rule ("Foundations before or alongside Method; Method before any area's Practice tier").

**If a separate path is wanted**, it belongs in one of two places:
- **Inside F1, as a named Mastery path ("ballet lens")** through units that already exist: F1.27 (the pointe counter-history), F1.28 (grade the Petipa-plan chain), F1.28a (reconstruct a Feuillet phrase and label it), F1.29 (a consented interview), F1.30a (the brief a counterfactual house would issue). About 6–8 hours, built from the same cards, no new structure. This follows the precedent of the "S5 lens" and "S6 lens" in the persona table.
- **After Method, as an elective case world** for learners who reach M1.11a and want a whole history of one performance practice. The six acts are a sound outline for it. It should wait until M1 and M2 exist, so that its products write fields those modules have defined.

## 4. What is missing or overlooked

### 4.1 Structure

- **No spec for any placement.** Nothing has an essential question, an opening move, a responsibility block, open objects with licences, a claims sheet in the claims register, or a fact-check list. The research holds most of the content these sections need; it has not been poured into them.
- **No protocol screen.** The research touches material that the spec template screens before any search: people treated as property (the Moscow school's foundlings, "the teacher probably paid per finished dancer"; serf theatres; Ezrahi's "slaves", contested by Tomoff), which needs rule H9; yellowface and Orientalist make-up images (N6 applies: Deep tier only, attributed, with a reason); *The Rite of Spring*'s invented rite; Khmer court dance named "Royal ballet" by UNESCO (W8, H18); living workers named from a blog's portrait series; living artists (Masilo, Khan). F1 decision 10 sets a precedent for living makers named by a third party: "Name them as EMKP does, with a 'contact before naming beyond this' note". The shoe page uses opt-out ("may have their name taken out ... by asking"), which is weaker than the F1.29 standard ("it reaches the record only on their terms").
- **Images.** The plan proposes 148 cards, each with "one image with its licence", while noting that no open image exists for any post-1945 work and that Goncharova, Benois and Picasso designs stay in copyright until 2031–2044. Most cards would be link-outs. The visual plan is listed as "the next research job"; it decides what can be built, so it should come before the card count.
- **Glossary.** The working vocabulary (livret, divertissement, répétiteur, corps, turnout, pointe, notation) has no glossary entries with "say it to a designer" lines. The glossary is reference-first, so this is where Daniel would meet the terms, not in a vocabulary sitting.
- **Accessibility.** Ballet is movement and music. The plan has no rule for sound-only or motion-only content; F1.4's rule ("sound is never the only channel"; captions, transcripts, audio description) should carry over, and the made body (injury, the narrow body type, disability) is F1's natural feed into the Accessibility thread's level 1 (F3.3), which the plan sends to F3 without a checkpoint.

### 4.2 Sequence

- **No position in F1's order.** F1 runs Orientation (F1.1, F1.2), then Practice (F1.3–F1.26), then Mastery (F1.27–F1.31). The plan does not say whether the ballet core comes before or after the credit map, the commissioning regimes or the autopsy that it relies on. Its products assume F1.17 (the brief), F1.23 (the credit line) and F1.25 (reading an object) without naming them.
- **Method before Foundations.** Five of the six act products are Method work. The sequencing rule allows Method alongside Foundations, not inside it.
- **No Orientation layer.** The plan has nothing a phone learner can finish in five minutes. That is where Amara and Daniel live.
- **The default route reverses chronology** (Act II, then Act I as "what Paris inherited"). That is defensible inside a long path, but it is a third ordering beside F1's own and the Atlas's.

### 4.3 Assessment

- **No closing contributions.** Every F1 unit ends with one SRC line in a fixed form (F1.4: "Method: [verb phrase], from [case] ..."; F1.17: "Regime: ... Brief: ... Money: ... Credit: ... Assumed: ..."; F1.23: "[Project]: credited to ...; named hands ..."). The plan's products have no line format, no field entry (unit, lane, depth, text, sources, confidence, per the schema's `entry`), and no confidence word.
- **No success criteria.** The template asks for three observable criteria that take no side in a live debate (H20). The plan has eight ready-made disputes and no rule that the learner's preferred reading is not scored.
- **No partner tests.** Each F1 partner step ends in a test the learner can run (F1.23's Historian: name one hand the record leaves out, cite the document, one sentence with a confidence word). The plan has none.
- **Nothing into F1's closing test.** The maker's test is an Object Autopsy on an unseen object; the briefer's is a sort of eight cases on the plane with reasons and consultation notices. Ballet could supply one case to the briefer's sort (a *Nutcracker* character dance, or *Le Train Bleu* remade with Chanel's permission) and, if an open record is found, one unseen object. The plan does not mention the test.
- **Judged sets overreach.** Six briefer "judged sets" inside one case world write JDG, which the schema reserves mainly for each module's closing test.
- **The transfer test is internal.** The vertical slice measures transfer by "fill a card for *La Sylphide* from given sources". That tests transfer within ballet. The program's test of transfer is the learner's own world: the closing contribution, and whether it is reused later (success criterion "Behaviour": referenced in a later artifact field by half of completers).
- **Writing thread.** Foundations owe "one argument card answered per module". Ballet's disputes (Beauchamp or Feuillet; who was earliest on pointe; reconstruction as evidence or as a new work) are ready-made argument cards. The plan lists them and does not use them so.

### 4.4 The learner's own world and the Concept DNA

- **The seed worlds are absent, and they fit.** *Night Library* (a library that opens only after dark) meets gas, the white act and "the night"; *Second Skin* (repair-first clothing for delivery riders) meets the dancer who finishes and darns her own tool, the shoe used up in a night, and the maker's mark as accountability; *Salt Road* (a salt cooperative reviving a route) meets the endangered craft, piecework and the material chain. A learner without a project would see why ballet is in front of them.
- **"Your world" tasks have no field entry.** "A livret for your world" has nowhere to go in F1. Placed in F1.4, the same impulse becomes a GRM.n rule ("Method: notation ... carries ... leaves behind ..."), which F1.4 already writes.
- **Borrowing is not routed through SRC.** When a learner takes a rule from *Giselle* or a naming grammar from Gaynor Minden, that is a source with a relation (reference, quotation, adaptation, translation or appropriation risk) and possibly a consultation flag. The plan's borrowing rule is about modules borrowing cards; it says nothing about learners recording what they borrow.
- **The exemplar risk.** The plan names the risk of learners copying *Giselle* and answers it by showing a second case. The larger risk is that a finished, famous work read backwards into DNA fields teaches that a DNA describes a finished work. The program's DNA is a generative system tested by the Stranger. *Giselle* is best used in M2 as a comparison beside Salt Road, with the Stranger test run on both.
