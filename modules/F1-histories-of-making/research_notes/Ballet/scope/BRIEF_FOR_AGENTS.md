# Scoping brief: ballet as a body of knowledge for F1 "Histories of making"

The course: a global history of making taught as a web of objects, makers, places, routes and institutions; threshold concept "design history is a web with obscured strands, not a line". Threads that run through every unit: Attribution and Authority (who is named in the record and who is not), Writing and Argument, Planetary Weight (what was mined, grown, burned), Accessibility. The learner is a foundation-year design student, not a dancer.

Your job is NOT to write a page. It is to map what there is to know about ballet from your angle, so the unit can be planned.

Deliver a Markdown file with these sections:
1. **The knowledge map.** The topics a learner must know (tier 1, cannot be skipped), should know (tier 2), could know (tier 3). For each: one line on what it is, why it matters for a history of making, and the two or three best sources (books, journal articles, archives, exhibition catalogues, theses; the scholarly base, not magazine articles). Name authors, titles, years. Say which are open access.
2. **The concepts.** The ideas and debates in your angle (not facts): e.g. the body as instrument, codification, authorship of a ballet, the ephemeral and the record, orientalism, the pointe shoe as technology. For each: who argues what, in which work.
3. **People and makers** the record names and does not name, from your angle.
4. **Objects and archives**: holders of relevant objects and documents, and which publish under open licences (CC0, PD, CC BY, CC BY-SA) or offer open images; name the collection and how to find items. Do not invent accession numbers or compose URLs by hand; give URLs only when you have actually seen them in a result.
5. **How it is taught elsewhere**: two or three university syllabi or textbooks that cover your angle and what they ask students to read.
6. **Gaps and traps**: what the obvious accounts get wrong, what is contested, what is missing from the record.
7. **Size**: your estimate of how many hours of learning your angle deserves in a foundation-year design course, and what one page, one session or one unit could carry.

Rules: quotes under 25 words; mark each claim documented (seen at source), probable (secondary), contested, or argued; never say "first", say "earliest known"; Japanese names in Japanese order; do not compose URLs by hand. Use web search widely, including Google Scholar, JSTOR, Project MUSE, DOAJ, OAPEN, archive.org, library catalogues, museum collection sites. Write the file to the path given in your instructions and reply with a 200-word summary.

**Save as you go.** Create your output file within your first five tool calls with the section headings, and append to it after every few searches. If you are cut off, what you found must already be on disk.
