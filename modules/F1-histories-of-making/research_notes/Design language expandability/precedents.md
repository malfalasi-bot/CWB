# Precedents for teaching kinds of knowledge beyond the single object

Research notes for extending the F1 design language. Everything below is drawn from pages fetched on 2026-10-20 (Sources at end). Page-anatomy lines read top to bottom.

## 2. Page anatomy per knowledge kind, and what to do differently

**Movement / style / school.** Precedents converge (Tate, MoMA, GA&C, Smarthistory): one-sentence definition · open or closed date range · 3–5 titled sections or sub-themes · 8–12 labelled key works · related terms with teasers · artists · regional variants as siblings. *Do differently:* add a "who named it, when" line (Art Deco was coined in the 1960s per the V&A teaser; "Renaissance" as a separate era dates to the mid-19th century per Le Goff) and a "what it leaves out" box; mark the range as open where GA&C does ("1860 – …").

**Archaeological culture / civilisation.** Met chronology and Smarthistory place pages: region title · map thumb · overlapping bands · overview · dated events · neighbours in time and space. *Do differently:* label the entity type ("archaeologists' label", as Things That Talk does with its question mark), show the AAT-style guide-term path (by region › by period), and cite the defining source as PeriodO would. Never derive a people from a pot style without saying it is inferred.

**Living community and practice.** Mukurtu and Digital Benin: community-preferred name and language first · protocol and sharing settings · creator/contributor · cultural narrative separate from institutional description · TK/BC Labels in a sidebar · ordered multi-voice community records · oral history as audio. *Do differently:* nothing structural; the course's job is to adopt the fields (section 3) and show the protocol *before* the content.

**Polity / dynasty.** Met band: bars with reign/dynasty spans, overlapping where polities coexist; key events dated; rulers list linked. *Do differently:* give each bar its source and a confidence word ("traditional date", "disputed"); show polities as overlapping bars, never a succession chain.

**Material / technique.** AAT and V&A facets: preferred term · variants by language · scope note · hierarchy with guide terms · sources per term · associative links typed ("produces", "distinguished from", "require"). *Do differently:* make the associative types visible as chips ("made with", "distinguished from") and attach dates to them as AAT allows.

**Network / route.** Pleiades, Pelagios, ORBIS, Republic of Letters: typed, time-bounded connections built from shared references · map plus a non-map view (cartogram, scatter, histogram) · filters by time and source. *Do differently:* always state the sample and the model's limits in the panel (ORBIS reviewer; Voltaire authors), and colour by source rather than by "route".

**Lineage.** MGP: node · degree/institution/year · advisors · students table with descendant counts · edit prompt. *Do differently:* allow more than one teacher, non-academic relations (workshop, apprenticeship, "learned from"), unknown links drawn dashed, and drop flags.

**Period / calendar.** PeriodO and WHG Temporal tab: label as the source wrote it · source · region as written + gazetteer · earliest/latest at each end · original calendar beside CE · broader/narrower. *Do differently:* see section 4; also show the period's alternates from other authorities on the same bar.

**Comparison.** Neues Museum and Met secondary links: a shared question or theme · paired objects · stated similarities and differences · caveats. *Do differently:* make the caveat ("no direct contact: parallel, not connection") a required field, and align the two bands to one scale, which no precedent does on screen.

**Provenance / restitution.** British Museum fields, Digital Benin, Linden-Museum: production › findspot › associated event › acquisition name › acquisition notes; names with roles and biographies; a milestone list of returns; current custody state. The Smithsonian's Shared Stewardship policy adds that legal title does not settle the question and that a denied request must be explained with findings. *Do differently:* put the acquisition chain and the associated event at the top, name the claimant community, and show status (held / loan at partner request / returned) with a date.

**Counterfactual.** No museum precedent; teaching literature (MiddleWeb; the Gropius-as-woman experiment, IJADE 2024) uses a "what if" posed after the evidence, judged better/worse, with the warning that students may remember the hypothetical as real. *Do differently:* give counterfactuals a distinct visual grammar (dashed, labelled "imagined"), anchor them to one evidenced branch point, and never let them share the period colour.

**Learner's own layer.** Mukurtu community records (ordered, multi-voice on one item), MGP's "we welcome additional information", Things That Talk's "continually expanding" zone. *Do differently:* a learner record sits beside, never over, the institutional record; it carries author, date and confidence; it can be a Notice but never a Label.

## 3. Cultural protocol rules a designer can apply

From Local Contexts, Mukurtu and the CARE one-pager:

1. **Community name first.** Edo designation beside English (Digital Benin); original script, transliteration and language per name (WHG). The community's term is the heading; the museum's term is the alias.
2. **Protocol before content.** A Mukurtu item cannot exist without a Community and a Cultural Protocol; sharing is "Any" or "All" of the protocols. Design the protocol badge into the header, not the footer.
3. **Restricted means restricted, not hidden.** Mukurtu lets an open item carry a strict media asset: the record and metadata stay discoverable, the media does not. Show a placeholder that says why.
4. **Notices are the institution's; Labels are the community's.** Institutions and researchers apply TK/BC Notices (and "Attribution Incomplete", "Open to Collaborate"); only a community applies Labels, through the Hub. The course may *notice*; it may never *label*.
5. **Do not alter.** Icon unchanged; Notice text used exactly; Label text is the community's customised text, with its name and Hub URL (DataCite fields: rights text, rightsURI, rightsIdentifier).
6. **Attribution as correction.** The TK Attribution Label exists to fix past omissions: name the makers and the community, and carry that forward.
7. **Consent is a process, not a tick.** CARE A1 names free, prior and informed consent; Digital Benin describes consent and trust processes for oral-history interviewees. Show who consented to what, when.
8. **Multiple narratives, ordered.** Community records on one item, displayed in a sequence the community sets.
9. **Harm warnings.** Digital Benin uses pop-up warnings for derogatory historical language and keeps colonial terms as data, flagged.
10. **Benefit and governance (CARE C, A2, R, E).** State what the community gets, who controls the data, and review harms from the community's perspective.

## 4. Periodisation

PeriodO's model: a *period* is an assertion made by an *authority* (a citable source: book, article, web page, with locator) that a named span applies to a region. Each period keeps the source's original label (plus alternates with language and script), the source's spatial wording verbatim plus gazetteer places, and a start and a stop, each with the source's verbatim phrase and a parsed earliest/latest year. Broader/narrower links only inside one authority; "derived from" across authorities. Permalinks, editorial notes, and a PROV history of edits.

To show a period honestly, the Atlas's period card should carry:

- **Label as the source wrote it**, then the course's English alternate.
- **Named authority** with locator (page/figure) and a link.
- **Region** as written, plus the mapped coverage.
- **Range as a bar** whose ends are drawn fuzzy when earliest ≠ latest (WHG uses dotted lines or shading), with the original calendar beside CE.
- **Alternates** stacked under the bar: the same name from other authorities, differing ends visible.
- **An argument line**: Le Goff's point that cutting time is never neutral; Kopaczyk and Krygier's that the reasons for any division should be examined. Each period gets one sentence on *why this cut*.
- **Boyd Davis's caution** that uniform time-space makes events look equally weighted; allow non-uniform scales and keep doubt visible.

## 5. What fails

- **Infobox clutter**: a box of facts replacing the argument; the British Museum page buries the 1897 looting at the bottom of 18 fields.
- **Flags as culture**: MGP's country flag per institution; anachronistic national flags on pre-national peoples.
- **Single dates for spans**: "1860" where "1860 – …" is honest (GA&C's open range is the good case).
- **"First" claims**: the Neues Museum press release's "for the first time" framing versus its own caveat that parallels are not contacts.
- **Movements as a line with one origin**: regional Baroques dated identically c. 1600–1700 flatten real spread; polities need overlapping bars (Met), not succession boxes.
- **Deterministic models presented as evidence**: ORBIS's reviewer on mixed-period sources; Republic of Letters on misleading averages.
- **Unlabelled lists**: Smarthistory's long unlabelled essay/video run; label by type and date.
- **Institutional term first**: English designation ahead of Edo, or the museum's spelling imposed on partner data.
- **Restitution as a single event**: show the sequence and the present state (Linden-Museum).
