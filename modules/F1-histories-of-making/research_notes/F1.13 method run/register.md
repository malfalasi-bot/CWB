What the lint cannot see, and the editor must: the lecture's timeline marks and routes carry no `claim` ids, so the mark `arita` (1616) and the route label "porcelain clay found at Arita, early 1600s" resolve, by year alone, to the documented Kraak dish (1600) rather than the contested Arita record; plan.md already says the Arita event is a card, not a tick. The build should add `claim` to every `timeline.mark`, `timeline.span` and `map.route` command so the lint judges by id, as `claims.md` §3.3 requires. Three-digit years (751, 794–95, the 830s, the mid-1st century) are outside a four-digit scan and rest on the beat lint. With `--clock`, four of the lecture's `timeline.scrub` years (800, 1700, 1800, 2015) match no record's pos; they are the station stamps' resting places, not beats, so they are not counted as errors in the default run, but a scene's beats must sit on a record's pos (`claims.md` §2).

## Follow-up, 20 October 2026: citation years and the Indian Ocean page

**The lint.** `lint_claims.py` now skips citation and counting years: a year directly after an author-style token ("Jiang et al. 2020", "(Hecht, 2003)", "and Bhan 1991", "after Engseng Ho, 2006", or one of the surnames listed in `CITE_NAMES` with a comma), after a journal, holder or publisher name ("PNAS 2020", "UNESCO 2021", "The Met, 2023", "UNESCO World Heritage List, 1561"), a counting or verifying date ("counted … 20 October 2026", "verified at source 3 Oct 2026", "read 3 October 2026"), any year inside an HTML element whose class or id contains sources, cite, credit, counted, verified or colophon, and any value under a YAML key of those names. A claim's day-date ("On 17 October 1132 he freed Ashu"; "bought 30 June 2017") stays strict, and so does a holder's name used in a sentence ("Cleveland dates it late 1100s").
