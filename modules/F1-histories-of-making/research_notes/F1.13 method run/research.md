## What the content is

A cross-world unit whose spine is one object (the Cleveland jar) read through four flows: objects, materials, skills and people. Its nine stations cover six networks (the cobalt route into China; the Indian Ocean; the Silk Roads corridor and papermaking; trans-Saharan gold; the Manila galleon; the Atlantic) and end on a living practice (lenj building, UNESCO 2011). The content is rich in journeys (every station but two has a route), in argued pairs (the cobalt mine; who made the routes; Hippalus; whose coast), in counts that are people (12.5 million; "a third of the silver"; 110,000 Hadramis), and in human flows: 174 of 743 rows carry the human-flow flag, 119 living-community, 67 sacred, 69 Indigenous-community, 57 conflict-looting.

## Rules this unit adds to the language

- **The human-flow rule** (module file §5.4): flows of people are never drawn with the grammar of goods flows; enslaved people appear as people, with a content note; the cotton, sugar and textile flows the trade financed are linked, not merged. In the scene vocabulary: a person is the chip, never a glyph riding a leg; a human count is a range on a card, never a bubble that grows or a crawl that climbs.
- **Names lead** (AR-081, the Library of Congress object): Omar ibn Said's name and his Arabic text lead; the English title page is not shown; a leaf with Qur'anic text is shown whole, never cropped.
- **Excluded cargo**: the Belitung cargo (commercial salvage) is named in text with no image and no drawing of our own (protocol level 5).
