# Edo: motion, layers and focus

A study, written before any build, of where animation belongs in the Edo unit and the Atlas, what the layers on the globe and map are, and how the timeline sets the focus and the pace. It follows the decisions of 12 October 2026 (section 1) and draws on three inputs in this folder: `inventory.md` (every event, journey, object and group in the content), `atlas_audit.md` (what the Atlas can already do) and `references.md` (documentary and atlas-interactive precedents).

## 2. What there is to move

The inventory counts 96 events (54 on the timeline, 42 told only in the story or lectures), 20 journeys, 80 objects, 16 chronological groups, 8 lectures and 10 interactives. Two numbers decide the design:

- **47 of the 71 story beats are not map beats.** They are reading, people, objects and sources. Most of the unit should not move at all.
- **Only 13 of the 20 journeys are drawn in v4**, and none of them is drawn as a sequence of dated stops. The 1867 exhibition, Van Gogh's copies, the Tokunō set, Fenollosa and Wright have no arcs. The Perry route is the only one with dates per stop, and the Atlas ignores them.

So the animation budget is small and concentrated. It is spent on the 20 journeys, on the 16 groups, on about a dozen incidents with a place, and on six object demonstrations. Everything else holds still.

## 3. The moment grammar

Six kinds of moment. Each has one surface, one trigger, one thing that moves, and a pace. A beat has at most one of them.

| Moment | Where | Trigger | What moves | Pace | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Opener** (act, lecture, chapter) | Stage, the map at the act's scale | Entering the act | One slow push-in on a still map, then the title card lands. Nothing else. | 6–8 s, then held | Act I over Japan with the lords' roads still; Act IV over the Pacific |
| **B. Journey** | Map or globe | The beat that names it; or the Atlas layer | A route drawn stop by stop; each stop gets a date and a number; the rest dims | 1–1.5 s per leg, held at each stop | The blue, the junks, the Tōkaidō, Hayashi's 218 shipments, Perry |
| **C. Incident** | The 1859 sheet or the Kantō map | The beat; or a timeline tick | The camera settles on the place, the mark pulses once, the caption carries the date | 2 s settle, one pulse, held | The Meireki fire (three starts in order), Tsutaya's move, the theatres moved |
| **D. Demonstration** | Stage (object), never the map | The beat; or a room | An object taken apart or rebuilt in steps the reader takes | Reader-stepped, never auto | The Wave in five layers, the block stack, a margin's seals, the price card |
| **E. Run of events** | Timeline focus, with the map answering | Selecting a group, or an act's Rebuild | The lane chart zooms to the span; items tick in order; each tick changes one thing on the map or a lane | 2.5–4 s per tick, with a pause bar | Censor regimes, the 1842 ban and its effects, the afterlife 1867–1928 |
| **F. Still** | Stage | Default | Nothing; a crossfade at most | — | 47 of 71 beats |

Rules that follow from the precedents (references.md §2) and from v4's own critique:

1. One focus per beat. If a beat needs two things to move, it is two beats.
2. The camera settles before the caption lands. Captions are never read during a flight.
3. Dim, don't hide. Other routes and marks fade to a quarter; the learner keeps the context.
4. Every journey is stepped at dated stops, at a constant time per leg. Distance does not set duration.
5. The reader holds the pace. Auto-step exists only inside a focus (E) and shows a pause control; nothing auto-advances longer than five seconds without one (WCAG 2.2.2).
6. The timeline is the clock. Any moment that has a date moves the "now" cursor, and the cursor moves the map. There is no second clock.
7. Reduced motion keeps every caption and every end state, and replaces every flight and draw with a cut or a fade.
8. Toggles live only in Explore. In the story, a layer appears because a moment needs it, and leaves when the moment ends.
9. Ambient motion (globe drift, paper grain) stays under 4° a second and only in Explore and the Atlas world view.
10. An opener that explains nothing is cut. The overture's shots are reassigned (section 7).

## 4. The layers

A layer is a set of marks with one meaning. The globe, the maps, the stage and the timeline each show a subset. The year is the master switch; a scene or a lens is the second; the learner's ink is the third. Nothing is a toggle in the story.

| Layer | What it holds | Globe (world) | Globe (region) | Unit map (Japan / Kantō) | 1859 sheet | Timeline | Driven by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Washes** | Nine chapters as brushed territories | always, faint | faint | — | — | — | nothing |
| **Routes** | 51 schematic routes with start and end years | drawn to the year | drawn to the year | the four Edo routes | — | the "world" lane | year, lens |
| **Places** | 796 pinned nodes, clustered | clusters | marks | 31 world and 18 Japan places | 20 city pins | — | year, tier |
| **Journeys** | 20 dated, stepped movements (new) | one at a time, as a scene | one at a time | one at a time | city moves (3) | ticks on the lanes | scene, timeline focus |
| **Events** | 96 dated incidents with a place (new as data) | the 8 exhibitions only | marks pulse at their tick | marks pulse at their tick | three fires, two moves | all 54 timeline events | timeline focus |
| **Lectures** | 8 lectures pinned to the place they are about | a lectern mark at Edo, Nagasaki, Paris, Boston | the lecture's places | — | — | the lecture's span | the Atlas layer menu; the lecture layer in the unit |
| **Lenses** | 15 thematic units that recolour the marks | yes | yes | — | — | — | lens (Atlas only) |
| **Ink** | What the learner has engaged | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes | hand-off tokens |
| **Focus mask** | Dims everything outside the current moment | yes | yes | yes | yes | the span | scene, focus |

Two layers are new as data: **journeys with dated legs** and **events with coordinates**. The inventory already has both (`inventory.json`), and the Atlas audit found that the Atlas carries no events at all and only Perry's stops with dates, as text. Building these two tables in `build_data.py` is small work (S), and everything in section 3 depends on them.

### How a layer appears

A layer never switches on. It arrives in one of three ways, and leaves the same way:

- **Because the clock reached it.** The cursor crosses 1824 and the junks' arc draws. The cursor crosses 1842 and the price lane steps down. The cursor leaves the span and the layer fades to a quarter, then out.
- **Because a scene asked for it.** The scene "The blue" brings the route layer for one route, the focus mask around it, the price lane under it, and nothing else. When the scene ends the mask lifts.
- **Because the learner inked it.** A place the learner has read stays inked in every view. This is the only persistent change.

On the globe, the **Lectures** layer is the documentary entry the brief asks for. A lecture is pinned where it happens. Choosing it from the world view does what the Atlas can already do for a unit (the van Wijk flight, the veil) and then does what it cannot yet: plays the lecture's scenes over that part of the globe, stop by stop, with the caption, the date and the step control. The unit opens at the end, or at any stop, through the hash hand-off that already exists.

## 5. The timeline as the clock

The lane chart of v3/v4 (state, publishers, prints, the world, afterlife, with the price lane and the print-run bubbles) comes back as the only timeline. Three changes:

1. **The fast sweep goes.** `startPlay` in the Atlas and the autoplay in v2's widgets both ran the cursor across 400 years in seconds. The precedents reject this, and so did the review: nothing can be read at that speed.
2. **Focus.** Selecting an event, a group or a journey (from the chart, from a beat, from the Atlas) zooms the chart to that span, never narrower than ten years, and lists the items in the span as ticks. The map and the stage answer the cursor.
3. **Stop-and-play.** Inside a focus, Play steps the cursor from tick to tick, holds 2.5–4 s at each (longer when the caption is longer), and shows a pause bar. Step moves one tick. Hold stays. Escape leaves the focus and restores the full span. Everything is on the keyboard: arrows step, space pauses, Escape leaves.

The cursor carries a date caption and the era name (the nengō lane already exists for this). When the cursor is inside a focus, the chart dims outside the span, and the map dims outside the focus's places.

A **group** is a focus with a story: the censor regimes (four seals, four ticks), the ban and its effects (1841 → 1842 edict → the cap → the theatres → 1847 report → 408,000 sheets), the afterlife (1867 → 1884 → 1887 → 1898 → 1906 → 2024). A **journey** is a focus whose ticks are stops. An **event** is a focus with one tick, which simply settles the map and holds.

## 6. Pacing

| Thing | Duration | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Camera flight, city to city | ≤ 2 s | Mapbox/MapLibre defaults (references §4) |
| Camera flight, continental | ≤ 4 s, capped | same; the Atlas's `flyTo` already runs 0.7–2.6 s |
| Settle before caption | 400 ms | convention 2 |
| Route leg | 1–1.5 s, constant | convention 6 |
| Mark pulse | once, 600 ms | v4 motion tokens |
| Caption dwell | reader-held; auto-step 2.5–4 s with pause | WCAG 2.2.2 |
| Opener push-in | 6–8 s, then held | NYT locator cards |
| Chapter card | 2–3 sentences, one date, one number | conventions 7–8 |
| Ambient drift | ≤ 4°/s, Explore and Atlas only | Mapbox idle rotation |
| Reduced motion | cuts and fades, same captions, same end states | web.dev |
| Run length | ≤ 12 ticks per focus; a longer group is split | StoryMapJS ≤ 20, Esri ≤ 30 |

## 7. Storyboards

Eight moments, chosen because together they use every moment kind, every surface and both new layers. Frames are drawn in the study page; the text here is the spec.

### S1. Act I opener: a city built to be an audience (A, Japan map)
1. The Japan map, still, 1600. Kyoto, Osaka and Edo marked; Edo pale.
2. The cursor moves to 1635. The two roads draw to Edo, one leg each, 1.5 s. Caption: "1635: lords ordered to live in Edo in alternate years."
3. The push-in on Edo, 6 s. Edo inks. The act title lands: "I · A city built to be an audience".
4. Held. The scene sheet for b1-1 is readable beside it.
Needs: nothing new. The v4 sankin arcs exist; the opener is a camera path and a caption.

### S2. The Meireki fire, 1657 (C, the 1859 sheet)
1. The sheet, Hongō, Koishikawa and Kōjimachi as soft blots (their coordinates are approximate and drawn so; no burnt area is painted, because no source places it).
2. Tick 1: Hongō pulses. Caption: "18 January 1657: the earliest fire, in Hongō."
3. Tick 2: Koishikawa. Tick 3, next day: Kōjimachi. Caption: "Three fires over two days; six or seven tenths of the city burn."
4. The cursor crosses 1657 → the Yoshiwara mark moves from near Nihonbashi to the fields behind Asakusa (journey `yoshiwara-move`). Caption with the date.
Needs: the three blots and the move exist in v4. New: the tick order and the stop-and-play.

### S3. Tsutaya's move, 1783 (C, the sheet)
1. The sheet at the quarter's gate, 1774. The shop mark, the inline card at first mention.
2. The cursor reaches 1783; one leg draws from the gate to Tōri Abura-chō, 1.5 s; the mark pulses. Caption: "1783: into the city, to the street of the big houses."
3. Held. The character chip opens the sheet; the lane chart shows the tick in the publishers' lane.
4. Later, the cursor at 1791: the mark halves (the fine) with the caption from the Kansei group.
Needs: nothing new on the sheet; the price/fine caption is a group tick.

### S4. The blue route, 1704–1831 (B, world → Japan)
1. World, Berlin. Caption: "Around 1704 a colour-maker's red came out blue." The rest of the map at a quarter.
2. Stops draw: Amsterdam, the Cape, Batavia, Nagasaki. Each 1.2 s, each with a date where the sources give one and "undated" where they do not.
3. 1824: a second arc, from the China coast, draws in 1.2 s; the Nagasaki price lane on the timeline steps from 87 to 31 monme by 1828. Caption carries both numbers.
4. 1829: the fan; 1831: the advertisement. The camera flies Nagasaki → Edo (undated leg, drawn dashed) and the Wave appears on the stage.
Needs: journey legs with dates (new data), the price lane as a focus (exists), dashed "undated" leg style (new).

### S5. The sixth month of 1842 (E, timeline focus with the sheet)
1. The chart zooms to 1838–1850. Six ticks listed. The sheet beside it.
2. Tick 1841: "Tadakuni begins the Tenpō reforms." Tick 1842.5: the edict's wording card. The state lane inks.
3. Tick 1842.9: the price lane steps 24 → 16 mon. Tick 1842: three arcs on the sheet, Sakai-chō, Fukiya-chō and Kobiki-chō to Saruwaka-chō, 1.2 s each.
4. Tick 1847.1: the magistrate's count. Tick 1847.6: the 408,000-sheet bubble grows. Caption: "By 1847 six or seven tenths of sales are nameless actors."
Needs: the group as data (inventory has it), the stop-and-play, the wording card (exists).

### S6. One sheet, many hands (D, the stage)
1. Act II, the beat "one sheet, many hands". The Wave whole.
2. Step: the five depth layers separate (the overture's `wave` shot, reused here where it explains the print's depth, not as an opener).
3. Step: the key-block proof in sumi over the colour layers (the overture's `blocks` shot, the peel reconstruction).
4. Step: "Open the Workshop" for the full reconstruction; "Compare three Waves" for the edition.
Needs: nothing new. The overture's two WebGL shots become stage demonstrations with reader steps.

### S7. The afterlife, 1867–1895 (B + E, Pacific-centred world)
1. 1867: one arc Edo → Paris. Caption: "The state that censored the prints ships them to Paris as its exhibit."
2. 1870: the Marseille line draws through Suez. 1867–69: Pacific Mail to San Francisco, then the railroad to Chicago and Boston, one leg each.
3. 1884–95: Hayashi's shipments repeat Yokohama → Paris; a counter climbs to 218. Each repeat is 0.4 s; the counter is the caption.
4. 1887: two Hiroshige sheets and an Eisen go to Paris and come back as oil (the copies shown on the stage).
Needs: four new journeys as data (1867, mm, pm, vangogh-copies), the counter caption.

### S8. From the Atlas: "The blue" as a layer over the globe (A + B, the Atlas world view)
1. The globe at world tier. The layer menu lists the eight lectures and the twenty journeys by place. Choosing "Lecture 5 · Blue".
2. The globe widens to hold Berlin and Edo; every other route dims to a quarter (the focus mask); the chapter card lands: three sentences, one date, one number.
3. The stops play as in S4, on the globe, with the step control. The timeline strip under the globe shows the span 1704–1831 and the cursor.
4. "Open the unit at this scene" hands off with `#from.atlas.b3-3` (the token exists; the unit needs the return handler).
Needs: the scene system in the Atlas (audit §3: keyframe sequencer, clock, pause, caption track, highlight/dim, mask, `#sc-` token, event and journey data, scene loader), about 10–20 days.

## 8. What changes on each surface

- **The unit stage.** Beats keep the v4 scene sheets. The two overture shots become stage demonstrations in Act II (S6); the other seven overture shots are cut. Each act opens with a moment A over its map scale. The in-lecture interactives return as inline widgets placed where the story needs them (the blue-route scrubber, the price card, the margin hotspots, the export counter), each with a "What this shows" line and no autoplay.
- **The 1859 sheet.** Gains tick order for the fire and the three moves, and the stop-and-play. No new placement is invented; approximate places stay blots.
- **The unit timeline.** The lane chart returns with focus, stop-and-play and the nengō lane. The cursor is the only clock. Every map beat and every widget listens to it.
- **The Atlas globe.** Completed first (audit §1: the guiding ink current, the static fallback, the five missing lenses, the Cast Codex feed from other units, lens-kit naming). Then the scene system and the two data layers. Then the lecture and journey layer menu at world tier.
- **Rooms.** Unchanged, linked from the beats and from the demonstrations.
- **Lectures.** The eight v0 lectures (10,400–11,700 narration words at the case's own rate) through the v1 station player, as a layer opened from the act bar ("Lecture") and from the Atlas. The player's stations are the same ticks as the timeline focus, so a station can move the map.
