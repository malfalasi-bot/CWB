# Edo: the scene study

Written 2026-10-12 against `inventory.md` / `inventory.json` (20 journeys, 16 groups, 96 events, 80 objects), `story.yaml` (71 beats), `EDO_FULL_CASE.md` (8 lectures, 60 stations), `images.json` (48 images plus the three-impression `waves` set) and `sources.yaml` (118 ids), and obeying the moment grammar of `STUDY.md` §3–§7: one focus per beat; the camera settles, then the caption; dim, don't hide; journeys stepped at dated stops at a constant time per leg; the reader holds the pace; the timeline is the only clock. `scenes.json` beside this file carries the same ranking and the same treatments as data.

Rules applied throughout: open licences only (everything in hand is CC0 or public domain; glyphs to make are our own drawings, CC0); no invented placement (every `approx: true` place is a soft blot; every undated leg is dashed and the readout shows a span, never a guessed year); quotes ≤ 25 words; the inventory's confidence words; "earliest known", never "first"; Japanese names in Japanese order; the Yoshiwara is a labelled district and a mark that moves, never entered or dramatised; no points, no timers.

The lead's examples are answered where the content demands them: the globe turns and follows the route (T2, T3); micro flames burn where the content says a fire broke out (T8); crates ride the steamer line with a counter (T2); a seal is cut or stamped as the content says (T5). Where the content does not demand a thing it is not drawn: the theatres of 1842 were ordered out, not burned (T1); the 1867 exhibit is Japan's pavilion, so no French flag (T2).

## Part 1. The ranking

Five criteria, 0–3 each. **(a) significance**: 3 = in the unit's question, an act's bet or a lecture's thesis; 2 = a lecture station or an era turn; 1 = a side note. **(b) density**: dated legs or ticks, numbers, named people and objects, quotations. **(c) assets**: images in `images.json` that belong to the item: 0 = none, 1 = one, 2 = two or three, 3 = four or more (ids listed). **(d) visual potential**: what would be seen moving, and whether a globe turn is needed (legs span more than one view). **(e) sourcing**: 3 = documented throughout; 2 = documented with an argued or probable member; 1 = probable or undated throughout.

### What should not be a scene, and why

- **Duplicates, folded into a scene above them.** meireki-outbreaks = fire-1657 (T8). theatres-move and price-sequence are ticks of ban-effects (T1). fuji-series, blue-price, china-junks and voc are legs and ticks of blue (T3). tsutaya-move is a tick of tsutaya-life (T6). hayashi, mm, exhibition-1867 and vangogh-copies are the Paris half of afterlife (T2). wright-arc = wright; with pm, fenollosa and tokuno it is the America half of afterlife, a ninth treatment on the T2 template, not written here.
- **Too thin or undated.** voc (three undated legs, no people); engravings (undated, probable, "Dejima or China", no image); paper (both legs undated, the Pacific crossing "not recorded": it stays a dashed line on the Act V still with its honesty line); fenollosa (three ticks, one of them "not stated"); exhibition-1867 alone (one leg, no counts).
- **Structure, not story.** five-acts, six-eras, nengo: the act bar, a superseded spine, a lane.
- **Already a demonstration (moment D), not a scene.** print-runs is the edition lab; wave-census is the compare lab plus a counter caption; kansei-punishments is the band-and-lifespan timeline of b2-5 and b2-8.
- **Sensitive.** yoshiwara-move: the quarter is read, not played. Its one leg survives only as the last tick of the fire (T8), as a labelled mark that moves, with the content note, no interior, no figures.
- **Opener, not scene.** sankin is Act I's opener (STUDY S1): two faint roads and a push-in. The retinues cannot be drawn: the domains are not in the unit.
- **First reserve.** perry (12): the purest globe-follow journey and the only one dated at every stop, but context to the trade rather than the trade; it loses the tie to fire-1657 on significance. Its shot pattern is the T2 template with eleven stops.

## Part 2. Treatments

Surfaces are STUDY §4's: globe (world), globe (region), unit map (Japan / Kantō), the 1859 sheet, timeline, stage. "Clock" is the readout the cursor carries, the only clock. Durations per STUDY §6: settle 400 ms, leg 1–1.5 s, pulse 600 ms once, auto-step 2.5–4 s with a pause bar, otherwise reader-held. In each demand list the quote is exact beat or station wording and the arrow gives element · surface · date. To hold each treatment under 500 words the prose keeps surface, clock, what moves, caption and sources for every shot; the labels (max four, one primary), dims, durations and camera verbs for every shot are in `scenes.json`, which is the data of record.

### T1 · The sixth month of 1842

**Run**, one city incident inside. **Span** 1841–1850 (focus 1838–1850). **Surfaces** timeline, 1859 sheet, stage. **Told** b5-1 to b5-8; L6 S1–S6; I5, I7, I8. **Learn:** the state banned two best sellers and capped the rest; the trade answered with nameless actors, warriors and cats, not mainly landscapes.

### T3 · The blue

**Journey** (`blue`, with `fuji-series`, `blue-price`, `china-junks`, `voc`). **Span** c. 1704–1831. **Surfaces** globe (world, rotate 75; Berlin and Edo in view) → timeline (price lane) → stage. **Told** b3-2g to b3-6, b4-0; L5 S1–S4; I6. **Learn:** the Fuji series was sold on a Berlin pigment that reached Edo only by sea and became a print colour when Chinese imports cut its Nagasaki price by two-thirds after 1824.

**Demands**

## Part 3. What repeats

Patterns across the eight, for the framework to template.

1. **Date + number on every caption.** Each shot's caption carries the clock's date and one figure: a price (24→16 mon, 87→31 monme), a count (218, 408,000, 660, 118, 140), a share (six or seven tenths), a range (30,000–100,000). The caption is the counter; nothing counts elsewhere.
2. **Every city incident holds the "before" first.** T1 shot 4, T6 shot 1, T8 shot 1 settle on the sheet for 2 s with the marks in their old places before anything moves. The move is read against a state the learner has seen.
3. **Every route scene is a globe that turns to follow, at a constant time per leg.** T2 and T3 pan to keep the leading stop centred; distance never sets duration (1.2 s a leg). Undated legs are dashed and the readout shows a span ("1704–1824"); dated legs step the readout. The one road without dates (T4) steps by place with the clock held, and says so.
4. **Every export or world scene keeps context when it cuts to a region.** T2 shot 5 cuts to Europe with an inset locator; the full route stays drawn at a quarter.
5. **Every scene ends on an object on the stage.** The map hands off to the proof: the cat, the cover, Red Fuji and the Wave, the Kyoto sheet, the empty catfish margin, the Sharaku, the Shower. Moment B or C becomes moment F or D at the end.
6. **Argued after evidence, never before.** b3-4 after the price shot, b5-7 after the count and the bubble, b7-6 after the counts. The scene supplies the evidence line; the argued station asks for the verdict.
7. **Guesses are the existing nine or none.** Five of the eight carry one (b5-2, b7-1g, b3-2g, b2-4, I4's map tap); three carry none because the content supports none (T4, T7, T8). No guess is invented, and none is a timer or a score.
8. **A label budget of one primary and three, resolved by sequence.** Close pairs (Sakai-chō/Fukiya-chō, Nihonbashi/Shinagawa, Berlin/Amsterdam, Port Said/Suez, Kanagawa/Yokohama, Shin-Ōhashi/Atake, Asakusa/Shin-Yoshiwara/Saruwaka-chō) are labelled one at a time as their leg draws, or share one leader; they are never both labelled at once.
9. **An honesty line in every scene.** Approximate blots, dashed undated legs, stand-ins (the early-1830s sheet for the 1829 fan; the 1802 shop for 1783; the 1859 map for 1657 and 1750), "linked, not shown" (the oils, the Tokunō set), and the argued count. The framework needs one caption-footer slot for it.
10. **Micro-elements come from a fixed glyph set, each drawn only where a line demands it.** Ship, junk, steamer, crate, pavilion, shop, book, fan, pigment, coin, soba bowl, flame, four seals (one cut, three stamped), bubble. Fourteen glyphs, all our own drawings, CC0. No flag appeared because no line demanded one.
11. **Sensitivity is handled by the same three moves everywhere.** The quarter is a labelled district and a mark that moves, with the content note once and no interior; punishments are named with their verbs and no glyph; death tolls are ranges on a card.
12. **The run cap splits long groups.** Afterlife (15 ticks) becomes Paris and America; the Tōkaidō's 55 stations become nine print stations with the road between. Every scene has 3–7 shots and ≤ 9 ticks.
13. **Reduced motion is the same scene.** Every flight is a cut, every draw a fade, every flicker a static mark; captions and end states are identical.
