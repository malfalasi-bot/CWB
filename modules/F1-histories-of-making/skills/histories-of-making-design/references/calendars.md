# Dates, calendars and periods

## The readout

The clock readout is **CE · the source's reckoning**, in that order, the reckoning in `--ink-3`:

| Case | Readout | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| A Japanese era year | `1842 · Tenpō 13` | the nengō lane supplies the era |
| A Chinese reign era | `868 · Xiantong 9` | reign name romanised as the source does |
| A Hijri span | `1585–95 · 993–1004 AH` | both ends converted; "AH" after |
| A regnal year | `year 7 of Amenhotep III · c. 1384 BCE` | the year name as written first when no absolute date is documented |
| A lunar year | `1797/98 · Kansei 9` | a lunar year that crosses two CE years is written as both |
| A Maya Long Count | `9.12.11.5.18 · 683 · GMT correlation` | five places, then CE, then the correlation named |
| A circa | `c. 1830–32` with a halo on the tick | the register's precision decides |
| An argued date | `argued · 1704 / 1704–06 / c. 1706` | alternates on tap; never drives a tick, a tile or a clock |
| Nothing dated | `clock held` | a spatial stepper says so |

A conversion the register does not hold is not shown. BCE is written out; "BC" and "AD" never.

## Periods are assertions

A period is an assertion by a named source, for a named region, with a date range (the PeriodO model). On any surface a period shows:

1. its label as the source gives it, with the original-language label beside it;
2. **as defined by** [the source, its date];
3. the region it was defined for (an age always: "the Iron Age of the Sahel", never "the Iron Age");
4. its range as a bar with fuzzy ends where the source says "early" or "late", never a point;
5. alternates stacked beneath when sources disagree, each with its source;
6. one line on why the cut is there ("why this cut");
7. what it leaves out.

Period bands (the course's eleven global bands) are for coverage and comparison only and are labelled as the course's own.

## Spans and ticks

A range is a bar; a point is a dot; a circa is a dot with a halo; an open-ended living practice is "1860 – …" with the bar running to the edge; a contested range is dashed. An argued value is never a tick. Ticks in a run are at most nine; longer runs split.
