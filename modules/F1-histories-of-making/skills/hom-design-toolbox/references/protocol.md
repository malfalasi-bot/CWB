# Protocol for living practices and sensitive material

Generalised from the Edo unit's Yoshiwara rule ("read, not played; a content note once; no interior") with Mukurtu, Local Contexts and the CARE principles.

## Six display levels

Every object, practice, place or person tied to a living community, a sacred use, coerced labour, or a colonial seizure is assigned one level in the register before it is designed:

| Level | What the page does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Notice | The community's own name first; the course's TK/BC Notice text used exactly, icon unaltered; the object shown | a Diné blanket sold at a trading post, with the Attribution Notice |
| 2 Content note | A content note once, before the object, in the community's terms; no interior views | the Yoshiwara as a district and a mark that moves |
| 3 Display basis | A mono line stating why the thing can be shown: "made for sale"; "shown with permission, [who], [date]" | a contemporary maker's work |
| 4 Link-out only | The record and metadata are discoverable; the image is not held or shown; a placeholder says why and links to the holder | the Tlahuitoltepec blouse; kōwhaiwhai |
| 5 Excluded | Named in text with no thumbnail and no drawing of our own; the gap said in words | minkisi; Okina; the Chester head |
| 6 Held | Unpublished, awaiting a named reader; the page shows "held for review by [reader]" | anything the community has not yet reviewed |

## Rules on every level

- The course **notices** (institutional TK/BC Notices, "Attribution Incomplete", "Open to Collaborate"); it never **labels**: Labels are the community's, applied by the community through the Local Contexts Hub, and their text is never edited.
- Restricted means restricted, not hidden: the record stays discoverable, the media does not, and the placeholder says why.
- Attribution as correction: name the makers and the community wherever a holder's record omitted them; carry the correction forward to every surface.
- Consent is a process: show who consented to what, when; in kit mode (F1.29) the stepper is consent → questions → recording → community review → release, each step written to the learner's record; nothing is released without the review line.
- Multiple community narratives on one item are shown in the order the community sets.
- Harm warnings for derogatory historical language; the colonial term stays as data, flagged, after the own name.
- Benefit and governance: the page says what the community gets and who controls the data.
- No interior of a sacred or domestic space; restricted techniques are watched, not made; no recitation of sacred text; no generated image; no person glyph rides a trade leg; punishments are named with their verbs and no glyph; death tolls are ranges on a card.
- Never a game: no protocol step is scored, timed or rewarded.

## Brief mode (F1.26, F1.16)

For a commission or a rite the learner designs within: each requirement is one of "fixed by the rite", "open to the maker" or "not ours to decide", with its source; the third category is never collapsed into the second.
