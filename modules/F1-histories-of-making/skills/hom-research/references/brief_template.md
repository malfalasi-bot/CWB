# The brief (one page)

**Unit.** F1.N, title; the spec's question in one line.

**Thesis.** One sentence a learner could repeat.

**Claims worth teaching.** Five to seven, each one sentence, with its strongest source and its confidence word.

**Objects.** Three, each: holder · accession · date · maker · licence (rights_uri, rights_checked); image in hand or not.

**The argument.** One question with its two readings and their sources.

**The living maker.** Who, where, how documented, protocol level.

**Counts.** Those solid enough to teach (paper or primary named) and those that are PR (labelled as whose).

**What the sources leave out.** The unnamed, the unreached, the undated.

**The spine.** Which reading order the subject has (an object taken apart; a workshop walked; one work re-made; a bench read outward; a route followed; a dispute posed; lecture and beats) and the case for it against the course's six readings: material, labour, meaning, trade, technique, use.

**The learner's path.** About twenty minutes: the order a first-time reader meets the material; which object opens it; where a map, a timeline or an interactive explains what prose cannot, and where it would not; what the reader makes at the end; the three objectives as verbs.

**What this brief still needs.** Images to fetch and verify; a reader to name; a decision for the editor.

**Colophon.** Model, tools, date; rows by confidence; URLs fetched and used; what was blocked; what no human has checked.
