# The research schema, revised (20 October 2026)

The Edo inventory (events, journeys, objects, groups, people, arguments) was built to feed a timeline, a map and a claims register, so it collected only what those hold: things with a date, a place, a holder or a number. It has no slot for concepts, techniques as knowledge, works and their re-makings, materials and supply, institutions and economics, bodies and labour, or the learner. A researcher given those six tables will not go looking for an idea. Twelve kinds of content replace it; the dossier collects all twelve, the synthesis keeps what serves the thesis.

| # | Kind | Must carry |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concepts | the term; the field's definition; the history of the word; who argued it; what it leaves out |
| 2 | Techniques and practices | what is done, in what order, with what, by whom, how learned; the documents that record it (notation, treatises, films) |
| 3 | Materials and supply | what it is made of; where that comes from; who makes it; what it costs; who counted |
| 4 | Objects | holder · accession · date · maker · licence, verified at the holder |
| 5 | Works and repertory | the work; each making of it (date, place, makers, bodies, budget); what survives |
| 6 | People and roles | dancers, makers, designers, patrons, teachers, as the record names them; the unnamed in their own column |
| 7 | Institutions and economics | schools, companies, workshops, firms; founding, patronage, budgets, contracts, pensions, appeals |
| 8 | Places and movements | where; the dated journeys of people, skills, goods and works |
| 9 | Events | dated incidents with precision and confidence |
| 10 | Arguments and historiography | disputes with both readings; the frameworks and period names and who made them |
| 11 | Bodies and labour | hours, injury, pay, age, consent; the protocol level |
| 12 | Gaps and the learner | what the sources leave out; what a first-time reader believes coming in; what the subject can and cannot teach about making |

Every row keeps the rules: source URL, confidence word, "earliest known", who counted and when.

## The spines against the course

The course reads an object through material, labour, meaning, trade, technique and use. Spine A (the body outward) carries technique, materials, makers and labour and ends on a living maker; weakest on patronage. Spine B (one work re-made: *The Sleeping Beauty* 1890 → 1921 → 1946 → today) carries patronage, institutions and the re-making of a work under different conditions, the course-level idea Edo's three Waves also carry; weakest on images and technique. Spine C (one bench: Freed's Hackney factory) carries material, technique and the maker's name with the most concreteness and is the best twenty-minute read; weakest on the art form and reliant on one firm's numbers. They are three chapters of one unit: C → A → B. The unit's question across all three: who made this, and why don't we know their names.
