# F1 Unit Specs, batch 3: overview

2 October 2026 · template v3, to the F1.17 standard

Nine specs make up F1's method units: F1.25, F1.26, F1.27, F1.28, F1.28a, F1.29, F1.30, F1.30a and F1.31. In each, the main interactive is a tool the learner uses on their own world, and the cases are worked demonstrations of the method.

Each spec has:
- the spec table and the ten template-v3 sections;
- 18–20 claims (173 in all);
- open objects whose licences were read at the holder's own record;
- a fact-check list for G3.

The brief required a steppe or Central Asia case in four units (F1.25, F1.28, F1.29, F1.30). All four carry one, and three more units touch the steppe as well.

**Tooling note.** The Met's collection search API now returns HTTP 410. Records fetched by object ID still load. So Met objects can only be found through web search and then checked one by one, and most open objects in this batch come from Cleveland (CC0) and AIC.

## Corrections found in the canon, registers and program documents

**Program documents (fix first):**
- **Finding 2.4 and the "Protocol frameworks adopted" row are wrong about Local Contexts.**
  - Labels are free and for Indigenous communities only. The programme can never apply a Label.
  - Notices need an institutional Hub subscription, required as of 2025:
    - free tiers: Individual and Collections Care;
    - paid tiers: Small US$1,250, Medium US$5,000 and Large US$10,000 a year.
  - The icons are all rights reserved and may not be altered.
  - SRC142 already has this right.
- **Phase 4 Foundations, 163-1:** it says copyright is held by both interviewer and narrator. The OHA's 2018 Statement on Ethics names the narrator as the copyright holder. Reword the Kit's form.
- **The Oral History Kit's withdrawal route:** no OHA text names one. Label it our own rule, resting on consent law and UNESCO's "sustained" consent.
- **PR-001 and the Phase 4 practices table conflate Al Sadu with its safeguarding programme.**
  - Al Sadu (UAE): Urgent Safeguarding List 2011 (6.COM 8.21), Representative List 2025.
  - The 2025 Register entry is the separate programme (2011–2023).
- **Module file 6.1:** MIT is the licence of EditionCrafter, not of the 2020 Making and Knowing site, whose content is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. "Over 900 recipes" was not found on any page opened.
- **Module file 5.1:** EMKP is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (SRC104 verified), which is not open under our rule.
- **PRA044:** Smithsonian Open Access licences vary record by record. It is not blanket CC0.

**Paper lineage (F1.27, F1.30a):**
- **INV037:** Baghdad (c. 794) is not the earliest paper outside China, because Samarkand probably made paper earlier (Bloom). Rephrase.
- **CL-156:** it reverses the direction. Central Asia had paper before Baghdad.
- **TEC185:** its start date of 750 encodes the Talas legend.
- **TEC186:** c. 1150 is late. Bloom has paper in Spanish manuscripts in the late 900s, and Mariani dates the Xàtiva mills to c. 1050.
- **INV038:** its bark-beater date conflicts with a source giving the 6th century CE.
- **DIA001:** "c. 313" should read 312–314.
- **PRA093:** hanji is nominated, with the decision due at the 21st Committee (2026).
- **PRA094:** washi's "extended 2025" is unverified.
- **MAT105:** the "defined by" field is empty.

**Steppe and Central Asia:**
- **WS-104 and Deep Dives 2 finding (2):** Cleveland *does* hold CC0 Ordos bronzes, nine records, including 1952.115, 1962.46 and 1916.1189.
- **PR-017:** world code "F1.ST" should be F1.12a.
- **PLC258:** Rubruck says Boucher was captured at Belgrade and calls him a slave. The excavated "Great Hall" was re-identified as a Buddhist temple.
- **PRA160 and COM209:** UNESCO credits men too, with preparing wool and pressing felt.
