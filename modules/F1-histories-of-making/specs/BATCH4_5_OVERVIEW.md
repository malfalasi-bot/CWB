## Corrections that change what we teach

- **Omar ibn Said (F1.13).** His enslaver was General James Owen, the governor's brother. The Library of Congress blog has the brothers the wrong way round. His death year is 1863 or 1864, depending on the source.
- **Tornabuoni and Innocenti contracts (F1.17).** These are now two separate claims:
  - Tornabuoni, 1 September 1485: 1,100 florins, May 1486 to May 1490.
  - Innocenti, 23 October 1485: "his own hand", and ultramarine at about four florins an ounce.
- **Belanda Hitam (F1.30a).** The source is now van Kessel. There were about 3,080 recruits, mostly from present-day Ghana and Burkina Faso. That returning soldiers created West African demand for wax prints is now **contested**: van Kessel calls it a myth.
- **Fangmatan (F1.27).** The tomb dates to 179–141 BCE. Whether the fragment is paper, and whether it is a map, are both disputed, so the claim is **contested**.
- **Kente (F1.18).** Smarthistory says most weavers are men. Ghana's 2024 UNESCO file says women are "in charge primarily of weaving". The claim is **contested**.
- **Thonet (F1.1).** There was no 1841 patent. The Austrian privilege is dated 16 July 1842. Solid-wood bending dates to 1855 or 1856.
- **Rana Plaza (F1.18).** The ILO gives "at least 1,132" killed.
- **Getty v. Stability (F1.14).** Getty was granted permission to appeal on 16 December 2025. No appeal judgment had been found as of 7 July 2026.
- **Book of the Eparch (F1.11).** It has 22 chapters. Nicole published it in 1893.
- **Status changes.**
  - The Katanga cross (NMAH) is no longer open: its image carries reuse restrictions.
  - AIC 1924.459 is now open.
  - The NMK roof tile has no recorded findspot.
- **UNESCO.**
  - Al Sadu: Qatar is confirmed in the joint file 02158, and the UAE element was transferred to the Representative List (20.COM 7.c.1).
  - Washi: re-inscribed in 2025.
  - Yurts: the element now includes Karakalpak yurts.
  - Hanji: decision due at the 21st Committee, 30 November–5 December 2026.
