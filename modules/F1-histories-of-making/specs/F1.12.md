## F1.12 Australia and the Pacific, read through navigation

We read Australia and the Pacific through navigation: knowledge of sea, stone and water, held in memory and practice and carried in canoes.

**Opening cultural note (our words; AR-067):** "Cultural note: Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander readers should know that this unit names people who have died. It shows no images of people. Each community named here holds its own protocols, and we link to them first." Link: https://localcontexts.org/. We do not use Local Contexts Notices (claim 23); every Indigenous node carries our own plain "Cultural note".

**Excluded:**

- **Human remains:** Teouma, Willandra and Wairau Bar burials; toi moko. Named only.
- **Sacred or secret material:** tjurunga (OBT192), Torres Strait masks, Rarotongan staff gods, Nukuoro figures. Not named in the walk.
- **Restricted Aboriginal or Torres Strait Islander designs:** none shown; clan designs and Western Desert sacred designs stay with their units.
- **Robinson's 1841 drawings of people** ("'Bunurong' men spearing eels"): not shown. His drawings of weirs and baskets are linked, not reproduced.
- **A navigator's chart, decoded:** not taught. The Smithsonian calls the charts "the secret knowledge navigators … held" (claim 16).
- **Cleveland 1982.113, "Kangaroo", Western Arnhem Land, 1900s (CC0):** screened out. No artist named; Country given only as a region. Needs a Bininj reader.
- **Harakeke cultivar names:** held in F1.29.

**Shown, with origin and cultural note:**

- **Wuatam sherds (Museums Victoria X 32087):** excavated from deposits near Rakival village. Whether any came from burials is not recorded. Cultural note: "Cultural note: these sherds come from Wuatam (Watom), Rakival land, of the Gunantuna (Tolai) people. In 2020 the Rakival People wrote of wanting to learn 'what remains unrevealed of our island'; read their statement first." (AR-073)
- **Canoe splashboard (Cleveland 1966.130): link-out only, no image (AR-071).** The holder says splashboards "offered spiritual protection to the voyagers", and no Kiriwina guidance was found. Cultural note: "Cultural note: kula is a living exchange among island communities, including Kiriwina carvers and voyagers. The holder says such boards offered spiritual protection, so we show no image and link to Cleveland's record."
- **Aute specimen (Te Papa SP074689):** a plant, not a taonga. Cultural note: aute is named in Māori and Pacific barkcloth practice.

- **Madjedbebe card (link-out only):** Cultural note: "Cultural note: Madjedbebe is on Mirarr Country. The Mirarr Traditional Owners gave permission for the dig, and their corporation publishes its own account of the site's age. We give both dating readings and the Mirarr's own words, and rule on none." (AR-069)
- **Budj Bim (step 4):** Cultural note: "Cultural note: Budj Bim is Gunditjmara Country. Gunditj Mirring Traditional Owners Aboriginal Corporation describes Gunditjmara people building the system to 'farm and harvest Kooyang'. Robinson's 1841 drawings are linked, not shown; read Gunditj Mirring's own pages first." (AR-070)
- **Stick charts (step 7):** Cultural note: "Cultural note: Marshallese stick charts were teaching tools; the knowledge stayed with master navigators. Waan Aelõñ in Majel, a Marshallese canoe programme, teaches navigation today. We decode no chart; see their page." (AR-072)
