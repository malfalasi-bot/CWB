## F1.12a The steppe and Central Asia, read through the horse

We read the steppe through the horse (what it needed made, what it made possible, what was made from it), and the chapter's best-known "first" is still argued.

**2. Protocol screen**

**Excluded, named only:** human remains in the Sintashta, Pazyryk and Yanghai burials, including tattooed skin and the woman buried on the Ukok plateau, whom many Altaians call Ak Kadyn (the White Lady). Her remains were removed in 1993 and are held in Gorno-Altaisk; in 2014 an Altai council of elders voted to rebury her, and a court refused in 2016. Altaians disagree among themselves; we name her only (AR-075). No tomb interiors. Sacred material: deer stones, shamanic objects (BEL036), Mongolia's sacred-site worship. The Pazyryk carpet (WS-100): from a burial, described and linked out, never shown beside burials.

**Shown, with origin and a content note:**

- **Ordos horses and plaque (Cleveland 1958.79; Rijksmuseum AK-MAK-285, AK-MAK-1330):** findspots not recorded; such pieces are known mostly from burials, so they may be grave goods. Note, as learners see it: "Content note: gilt bronzes like these are known mostly from burials. Where these were found is not recorded, so they may have been taken from graves." (AR-078)
- **Roundels with hunters (Cleveland 1959.124):** found sewn on a tunic in a grave in Egypt. Note: "Content note: Cleveland records that these roundels were found sewn on a tunic in a grave in Egypt. Who opened the grave, and when, is not recorded." (AR-078)
- **The coat (Cleveland 1996.2.1):** once a set with trousers and boots; findspot not recorded. We do not guess a tomb. Note: "Content note: where this coat was found is not recorded. It was once a set with trousers and boots; we do not guess a tomb." (AR-078)
- **Rubruck:** "Tartars" and "the slaves" are quoted as his words; the enslaved are named as people. Rockhill's 1900 introduction is not reproduced (F1.28).
- **The Turkestan Album:** made for a conquering governor; the felt-makers are unnamed and their consent unrecorded.
