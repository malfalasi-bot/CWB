## F1.20 Modernisms, plural

Modernism was made in many centres, for revolutions, empires and new nations; this unit sets those modernisms side by side, not on one line with provinces.

**Spec**

| Essential question | When a state, a school or a new nation said "modern", what did it take from the past, what did it refuse, and who made the things? |
| Opening move | **Object first.** Three objects on one wall, unlabelled: a small colour lithograph of a tightrope walker (Cleveland 1961.154, 1923); an early-18th-century Ōtsu print on a hanging scroll (Met 1975.268.147); a silk ikat hanging from Bukhara (Cleveland 2006.150, 1800–1850). **2. Protocol screen**

**Excluded, and why:**

- **Photographs of Soviet-era mosaics, murals and buildings:** their makers' rights and Uzbek and Kazakh freedom-of-panorama rules are not verified. Linked out, never reproduced.
- **Uli on bodies:** Igbo women's body painting (COM029). No photographs of people; wall uli by link-out only, with a Cultural note (our own wording; not a Local Contexts Notice): "Cultural note: uli is a practice of Igbo women artists. The Nsukka and Zaria artists who drew on it were mostly men, and the women they learned from often go unnamed."
- **Images of Tupinambá people from colonial accounts:** Antropofagia's metaphor drew on them. Named in text, not shown. Content note: "Antropofagia took its image of 'cannibal' Tupinambá from European colonial accounts. The Tupinambá are a living people of Brazil, not a metaphor."
- **Korean objects removed during Japanese colonial rule (1910–1945):** no Joseon object is shown in the Mingei case; the case is carried by Japanese folk work.

**Shown or named, with content note:**

- **Navoi Theatre:** Kun.uz says Soviet Koreans contributed and Japanese prisoners of war "assisted". Content note, as learners see it: "Japanese prisoners of war, held in the USSR after 1945, worked on the theatre under compulsion. 'Under compulsion' is our reading; Kun.uz writes that they 'assisted'." Soviet Koreans were deported to Central Asia in 1937 (canon COM190). Named as people; link F1.23.
- **Stalin's formula:** quoted, attributed and dated, as the rule of a state that also ran the Gulag (canon POL151). Content note.
- **Ikat hanging and suzani (Cleveland 2006.150, 2016.89):** a reception-room hanging and a dowry textile (Cleveland); the canon (STY165) names the suzani makers as women, unnamed. Cultural note: living Central Asian practices (PRA162, PRA164).
- **Casablanca School and Amazigh craft:** Cultural note on Imazighen material, link-out only.
- **Chandigarh furniture:** content note on removal and sale; the canon's word "looting" and the Met's neutral wording are both given (F1.24 link).
