## F1.20 Modernisms, plural

Modernism was made in many centres, for revolutions, empires and new nations; this unit sets those modernisms side by side, not on one line with provinces.

**Spec**

| Essential question | When a state, a school or a new nation said "modern", what did it take from the past, what did it refuse, and who made the things? |
| Opening move | **Object first.** Three objects on one wall, unlabelled: a small colour lithograph of a tightrope walker (Cleveland 1961.154, 1923); an early-18th-century Ōtsu print on a hanging scroll (Met 1975.268.147); a silk ikat hanging from Bukhara (Cleveland 2006.150, 1800–1850). Prompt: "Which of these belongs to modernism?" **Claim:** modernism is one movement with one family tree. Pevsner's 1936 book ran it from William Morris to Walter Gropius: "So our circle is complete." **Counter-claim:** there were many modernisms, and each built its own ancestry. **2. Protocol screen**

**Excluded, and why:**

- **Photographs of Soviet-era mosaics, murals and buildings:** their makers' rights and Uzbek and Kazakh freedom-of-panorama rules are not verified. Linked out, never reproduced.
- **Uli on bodies:** Igbo women's body painting (COM029). No photographs of people; wall uli by link-out only, with a Notice.
- **Images of Tupinambá people from colonial accounts:** Antropofagia's metaphor drew on them. Named in text, not shown.
- **Korean objects removed during Japanese colonial rule (1910–1945):** no Joseon object is shown in the Mingei case; the case is carried by Japanese folk work.

**Shown or named, with content note:**

- **Navoi Theatre:** Kun.uz says Soviet Koreans contributed and Japanese prisoners of war "assisted". Content note: prisoners of war worked under compulsion (our reading). Soviet Koreans were deported to Central Asia in 1937 (canon COM190). Named as people; link F1.23.
- **Stalin's formula:** quoted, attributed and dated, as the rule of a state that also ran the Gulag (canon POL151). Content note.
- **Ikat hanging and suzani (Cleveland 2006.150, 2016.89):** a reception-room hanging and a dowry textile (Cleveland); the canon (STY165) names the suzani makers as women, unnamed. Notice: living Central Asian practices (PRA162, PRA164).
- **Casablanca School and Amazigh craft:** Notice on Imazighen material, link-out only.
- **Chandigarh furniture:** content note on removal and sale; the canon's word "looting" and the Met's neutral wording are both given (F1.24 link).
