## F1.26 Attribution and consultation as protocol, on the CARE principles

Credit given after an outcry is repair; a protocol is the set of steps you run before the work, and every step leaves a record.

**Spec**

| Row | Content |
|---|---|
| Essential question | Before you use another people's form, whom do you ask, through whom, and what do you write down? |
| Opening move | **Problem first (forms 15, 2).** A brief: "a men's sandal inspired by Indian heritage footwear, for a June show." Prediction prompt: "The show credits no one. What happens in the next ten months?" Step-through reveal: Prada's show on 22 June 2025 calls them "leather sandals"; a letter to the Maharashtra Chamber of Commerce says they "are inspired by traditional Indian handcrafted footwear, with a centuries-old heritage"; on 10 December 2025 Prada signs with LIDCOM and LIDKAR, the state corporations holding the Kolhapuri chappal's GI tag (2019). **Claim:** credit and a deal after the fact settle it. **Counter-claim:** consent given after the use is not prior consent, and a GI holder is not the same as the makers. Case: Kolhapuri chappal (F1.9). |
| Lab or main interactive | **The Protocol Builder (step-through).** Eight steps after a step 0, each tagged with a CARE letter; each needs evidence or "not done". **0. Place it** on the F1.2 plane (relation, harm); a living source community opens the protocol. **1. Name the source (A):** own name first, then the specific holders (iwi, siida, guild, GI holder); "not known" raises an Attribution Incomplete flag. **2. Read first (R):** the community's own code, Labels, law or UNESCO file. **3. Stop lines (A, E):** sacred, ancestral, funerary or restricted material ends the use. **4. Ask through their institutions (A):** free, prior, informed and "sustained"; "no" is a complete answer. **5. Agree credit (R).** **6. Agree benefit (C):** pay, training, language, a return. **7. Record it (R):** Notice or the community's Label; SRC fields. **8. Future use (E3):** review date, withdrawal route. Output: a rerunnable checklist. |
| Concept | 650 words. (1) **CARE (2019):** the four principles; A3, R1, E3; written for Indigenous data, so our wider use is labelled ours. (2) **Notices and Labels:** Labels belong to Indigenous communities and are extra-legal; Notices are for institutions; communities never pay. (3) **Free, prior and informed consent:** UNDRIP Articles 11 and 19; UNESCO adds "sustained". (4) **UNESCO 2003:** the definition, craft as a domain, Article 15; nominations need consent (U.4, R.4). (5) **Attribution as law:** Ka Mate. (6) **The F1.2 plane** decides when the protocol starts. Rule: the protocol records what was asked and answered; it never says what a community permits. |
| Cases | Object stories (form 3). **1. A statement required by law (F1.12).** Under the Haka Ka Mate Attribution Act 2014, commercial publications, public communications and public films of Ka Mate must state that Te Rauparaha composed it and was a chief of Ngāti Toa Rangatira. Performance and education are exempt; the only remedy is a declaration. Credit without veto. **2. A contract before the film (F1.11).** In 2019 Walt Disney Animation Studios agreed with the Sámi Parliaments of Finland, Norway and Sweden and the Saami Council: an advisory group (Verddet), a North Sámi *Frozen 2*, exchanges. Coda: Sweden's National Museums of World Culture transferred 541 Sámi objects to Ájtte (2023, 2025). One knife (1902.04.0001) still carries CC BY on its record; its keeper is now a Sámi museum. **3. A community writes the protocol (F1.6).** The South African San Council (Khomani, !Xun, Khwe) launched the San Code of Research Ethics on 2 March 2017: council approval, real consent, benefit sharing, results returned. Strip (form 10): the Passamaquoddy (Peskotomuhkati) TK Labels on Fewkes's 1890 cylinders (F1.7); a Met basket marked "Passamaquoddy (?)"; Kyrgyz felt carpets on UNESCO's Urgent Safeguarding List since 2012 (F1.12a). |
| Apply — maker | **Draft your protocol.** One source in your concept with a living community. Run the steps; for each, the evidence (link, date, named body) or "not done". Propose an attribution line, marked "proposed, not agreed" until someone agrees it. 300 words plus the checklist. |
| Apply — briefer | **Apply it to a real brief.** Run the steps on the Prada case as the public record shows it: at which step does action appear, and on what date? Then run it on your own brief that names a living tradition, and add one clause: who is asked, through which body, by when, and what goes back. |
| Partner | **Historian (consultation coach).** Question: "Whom did you ask, through which body, and what did they say?" It walks the CARE questions and points to the community's own publications, Labels or Notices. It never approves a plan and never states what a community permits. **Test it ends with:** every step marked "done" names a body, a date and a source; any step without them comes back "assumed". |
| Forms | 15 (prediction), 2 (step-through: the opener and the Builder), 3 (object stories), 10 (the strip). |

**8. Success criteria**

1. For one source, the learner names the specific body to ask, or writes "not known", and links that body's own published protocol, or writes "none found".
2. Every step of the learner's checklist is marked done, not done or not applicable, and each "done" carries a body, a date and a source.
3. The briefer's run on the Prada case places each documented action at its step and date, and marks the steps with no public record "not on record".
