## F1.27 Counter-history research piece on one lineage

A lineage is a set of links someone drew; a counter-history redraws them one link at a time, with a source and a confidence for each.

**Spec**

| Row | Content |
|---|---|
| Essential question | Who drew the line you were taught for your lineage, when did they draw it, and what does it leave out? |
| Opening move | **Artifact first (form 13).** The learner's Lineage layer opens with their line so far. Beside it, the received line for paper: "Cai Lun invented paper (105 CE)" → "Chinese prisoners taught Samarkand (751)" → "Italy, the birthplace of Western paper". **Claim:** a lineage is a chain of firsts. **Counter-claim:** each edge is an attribution written by someone at a date. Prompt: "Who wrote each edge, and when?" One tap per edge on a timeline; the reveal follows within seconds: a 5th-century court history (PER161); al-Tha'alibi, 11th century, as later writers report him; a 2016 magazine title whose own text puts Spanish mills about 1050. The learner then tags their own edges. Case: paper. |
| Lab or main interactive | **Atlas authoring** (form 4) in the learner's Lineage layer. Nodes: object, place, person, practice or any of the twenty grouping kinds. Edges: made at, learned from, copied from, descends from, traded to, credited to, contested by. **No edge saves without a source** (URL or TXT id), its depth and a confidence; a contested edge needs both readings. An unsourced edge is drawn dashed, "assumed". "First" is replaced by "earliest known" with a range. Nodes flagged sacred, ancestral or funerary hide their image; Indigenous material gets a Cultural note by default (protocol §4). Each node writes an SRC row; each revised edge writes a DOS line. The learner then authors a **walk** of 6–9 steps (Atlas Logic: "Learners write their own walks"). |
| Concept | 700 words. **Counter-history:** a history told from what the dominant account leaves out (glossary); it works on edges more than nodes. **Four moves:** (1) *date the attribution*: who wrote the edge, and how long after; (2) *look upstream* for an earlier node; (3) *look sideways* at a parallel lineage that tests the category; (4) *read the credit's silences*. **The caution** (F1.3): "It is not sufficient for design institutions to simply include a greater diversity of actors or perspectives" (2019 manifesto). Adding nodes is pluralism; changing a category, or who holds authority over the record, is a different claim. The piece says which it attempts; the unit does not rank them. **The piece:** a walk, 1,200 words and an attribution record (who made, held and credited each node; source, depth, confidence, rights, protocol, consultation). **Assessment:** the three success criteria and the Historian's test; no marks for which position is taken. |
| Cases | Worked demonstrations (form 3). **1. Two attributions (F1.8; F1.12a).** The Hou Hanshu credits Cai Lun with paper in 105 CE; a Western Han fragment from Fangmatan, if it is paper, is roughly 250–280 years earlier. The Talas story comes from al-Tha'alibi, about 250 years after 751; Sogdian letters on paper (312–314 CE) and the Panjikent archive (to 722) predate it. Move: retire a "first", relabel an edge "said to be", add earlier nodes. A writer gets her name: Miwnay. **2. Paper's other makers (F1.10; F1.11; F1.6).** Two Cleveland Qur'an folios, parchment (800s) and paper (1100s), either side of the earliest dated paper Qur'an (971–72). The Missal of Silos (late 10th or 11th century), Xàtiva about 1050, and Fabriano's "birthplace" title. Saharan copyists on Venetian "tre lune" paper; a watermark made for Nigeria with a Hausa maxim. Move: credited-to edges for makers, copyists and traders. **3. Is it paper? (F1.7; F1.12; F1.6).** Āmatl, beaten rather than pulped, called "proto-paper"; Landa's burning at Maní (1562); Hñähñu makers at San Pablito now. Beside it, a Hawaiian kapa beater (1971.152) and Baganda barkcloth (1929.368). Move: the definition drawn as a contested edge. |
| Apply — maker | **The piece, on your own lineage** (a material, technique, form or type your world draws on). Draw the received line in 3–5 edges and date each attribution. Add six or more sourced nodes from three or more world chapters. Revise two or more edges. Author a 6–9-step walk. Write 1,200 words: the line you were taught, what changed, and whether you added nodes or changed a category. Attach the attribution record. |
| Apply — briefer | **The piece, on a lineage a brief claims** ("since 105 CE", "the birthplace of", "invented by"). Build the same piece, then 200 words to the brand: which parts stand as documented, which must read "earliest known" or "said to be", and whose credit the copy should add. Attach the attribution record. |
| Partner | **Historian.** Question: "Which of your edges was written by someone with a reason to draw it, and when?" It cites each edge's source, gives both readings where contested, and never says which lineage is right or whether a piece is decolonial enough. **Test it ends with:** every node and edge has a source and a confidence; every "first" becomes an earliest-known range; unsourced edges come back "assumed". |
| Forms | 4 (Atlas authoring, the walk), 13 (card), 3 (worked cases), 12 (map), 11 (attribution dates against event dates). |
