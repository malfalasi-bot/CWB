## F1.28a Reconstruction: making to know

Remaking a technique from a text, an object or a holder's account shows what the record left unsaid, and the result is an interpretation that must say what was decided.

**Spec**

| Row | Content |
|---|---|
| Essential question | What does making something again teach that reading about it cannot, and how do you keep the remake from passing as evidence? |
| Opening move | **Making first (form 15).** Cleveland 1987.5, a bronze toad, possibly Padua, c. 1500–1550. The label: "Drowning the toad in ammonia left the animal's body intact." Prompt: "Which step is a guess?" One tap on a three-step strip; the reveal follows within seconds (writing the full sequence and marking each guessed step moves to the Reconstruction Log). Reveal: Ms. Fr. 640 (Toulouse, after 1579) drowns a lizard "in brandy or 'good vinegar & urine'" and poses it "on a clay slab". For sand it says only: "not too fatty … not too lean". **Claim:** a recipe is a full set of instructions. **Counter-claim:** a recipe is a note to someone who already knows how. Our reading: "ammonia" and "urine" are two vocabularies for one step. |
| Lab or main interactive | **Reconstruction Log**, built on the operational-sequence strip (finding 6.2). The learner orders the steps of one technique from one source and tags each: *text says*, *object shows*, *holder says* or *I decided*. A variables panel allows one change per numbered trial. Every *I decided* joins a "silences" list. The export is labelled "Reconstruction, an interpretation". Two demonstrations: **Indus beads (F1.9),** two to ten hours per centimetre of agate with stone drills, from experiments run with Khambhat bead makers; **a khipu (F1.7),** Cleveland 1940.469: cord, ply and knot can be remade, the reading cannot. |
| Concept | About 650 words. (1) Making as knowing: the Making and Knowing Project's position. (2) Four kinds of remaking: experiment, demonstration, revival by holders, rebuild after loss (the Atlas carries the last). (3) Method: one variable at a time; record what the text omits; tag every step; label the result. (4) The limit: results show what was possible, not what happened. (5) Whose hands: elders, bead makers and shipwrights hold what the text lacks; credit them by name where they allow it. (6) Safety: recipes with lead, mercury or live animals are read, not made. |
| Cases | **1. A lizard in the sand (F1.11).** Ms. Fr. 640 (c. 1579–1588) and its 2020 edition. Smith and Beentjes reconstructed its life-casting recipes; in 2015 Giulia Chiostrini and Jef Palframan cast a rose from fols. 155r–155v. Objects: the toad (1987.5), a Palissy-circle platter (1986.57), a Jamnitzer-workshop mortar (1951.444). **2. Smelting with elders (F1.6).** Haya smelters in north-west Tanzania fired furnaces for Peter Schmidt in the 1970s. Schmidt and Avery claimed preheated air and carbon steel 1,500–2,000 years ago; Rehder answered that preheating added about 10°C. Schmidt called the smelts performances "far removed from experience, routine, and memory". Both readings stand. **3. Two canoes (F1.12).** Hōkūleʻa (1975), "performance-accurate"; in 1976 Mau Piailug navigated her to Tahiti. To some crew it was an experiment; to others, a reawakening. Hawaiʻiloa followed: no koa was large enough, and rope-making had been lost. **Strip (F1.10):** the *Jewel of Muscat*, built from a 9th-century wreck with no stern, masts or sails; beside it a Changsha ewer (1966.145, F1.8). |
| Apply — maker | **One technique, rebuilt.** From a primary text (Theophilus, Hendrie 1847; Cennini, Herringham 1899; Ms. Fr. 640 in the edition; or a text from your own world in the TXT register), rebuild one step-sequence at kitchen-table scale. Log three trials, one variable each. Hand in the strip, the silences list (what the text did not say), and 150 words on the one decision that most changed the result. Label it an interpretation. Excluded: toxic metals, live animals, sacred or restricted techniques. |
| Apply — briefer | **Judge a "traditional technique" claim.** Take one claim from a real brief, label or campaign ("hand-forged the old way", "made as our ancestors did"). List the evidence a reconstruction would need: a dated source, an object with the technique's traces, a remake or a living practice that reproduces them, and named holders who were asked. Mark each "shown", "asserted" or "missing". 200 words: what you would let the copy say. |
| Partner | **Fabricator.** Agenda: "This will not build; here is why, and what it would take." Question: "Which step did the text not tell you, and how did you choose?"
