## F1.6 Africa, read through metallurgy

Read through metal, Africa's making is a set of inventions and exchanges on its own terms, and how each object reached a museum is part of what it teaches.

**2. Protocol screen**

**Excluded, and why:**

- **Nok terracottas:** the Frankfurt project ties them to "complex burial rituals", and most museum pieces are looted. Cleveland 1995.21 (WS-X04) is taught in F1.24.
- **Igbo-Ukwu's burial (Igbo Richard; ARC034 flags funerary):** named only.
- **Ògbóni staffs and the Songye power figure (Cleveland):** restricted and sacred use.
- **Great Zimbabwe's soapstone birds:** sacred; the 2026 return included ancestral remains (OCC169).
- **Haya smelting rites; the Igun guild's initiation:** private; named, not described.

**Shown under Q21, with origin on the card and a content note:**

- **Benin head, Cleveland 1938.6 (WS-011):** made for a royal ancestral altar; the record calls it "a personal religious object". **Link-out only:** no image; named in words and linked to https://clevelandart.org/art/1938.6 and https://digitalbenin.org/eyo-oto. The palace describes the looted objects as bearing "an enormous spiritual significance", and Nigeria's 2023 declaration vests ownership of looted Benin objects in the Oba. The plaque carries the case. Content note on the link: "Content note: this head was made for a royal ancestral altar and was taken from the Oba's palace in 1897."
- **Meroitic cup, Met 13.125.37 (WS-016):** grave good from Faras, Cemetery 1, grave 1007. Moved to the Atlas; the lamp carries Kush in metal. Content note: burial.
- **Chisel, Met 27.3.12 (WS-003):** found in Tomb MMA 101. Content note: from a tomb.
- **Axe, Cleveland 1929.364 (WS-014):** "a status or ancestral symbol during rituals or dancing". Content note: ritual use.
- **Walters 54.1549:** shows a bound captive. Content note: the captive named as a person, the violence plainly. Findspot not recorded.
- **Benin plaque (WS-010):** palace object. Content note: 1897; people were sold for manillas, and are named as people.

For the Bahaya: "Cultural note: the smelts of the 1970s were made by Haya elders at a researcher's request. We name them as smelters and describe no rites."
