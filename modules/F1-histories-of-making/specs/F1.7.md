## F1.7 The Americas, read through fibre

We read the Americas through fibre: in the Andes, dyed cotton cloth is the earliest known craft on record, and across the hemisphere cloth and cord carried rank, tribute and accounts.

**Excluded:** Paracas mummy bundles and bodies (human remains; named only). Marajoara urns, which held ancestors' bones (named only). **Shown, with origin and content note:**

- **Paracas cloths (Cleveland 1946.226–228):** grave goods; removal and export not recorded; the 1929 law. Cultural note.
- **Wari tunic (Met 2021.146):** probably from a grave; the record does not say.
- **Khipu (Cleveland 1940.469):** findspot not recorded. AR-001's Cultural note (F1.1 §2).
- **Walters uncu:** record text and link-out until the Walters dates the purchase. Cultural note: "Cultural note: Inca rulers gave cumbi tunics like this to mark rank. This tunic's history before an undated New York purchase is not recorded, so we link to the Walters' record and show no image." (AR-095)
- **Huexotzinco Codex:** a Nahua legal record, with a Christian banner; tribute taken under conquest. Content note, as learners see it: "Content note: in 1531 Spanish officials took this tribute from the people of Huejotzingo under conquest; the panels are the Nahua town's testimony against them. You can skip this step." Cultural note: "Cultural note: Nahua tlacuiloque of Huejotzingo drew these panels as evidence in a lawsuit. The banner shows a Madonna of feathers and gold, paid as tribute; we show the panels whole and do not interpret the image." (AR-093)
- **Diné blanket and rug:** made for wear and sale. Cultural note: Diné, a living community. Content note: Hwéeldi, numbers from a Diné-authored source.

Cultural notes are in our own words; we use no Local Contexts Notice, icon or Label (protocol §4).
