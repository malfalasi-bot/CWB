## F1.9 South and Southeast Asia, read through cotton

Indian cloth was worn from Cairo to Maluku, and its records say where it ended up more often than who made it.

**2. Protocol screen**

**Excluded, and why:**

- **The Mehrgarh bead and burial:** from a grave. Told in words only; no image of burials or remains.
- **The Sripuranthan Nataraja:** a consecrated temple image, stolen and returned. Named, with the NGA's own statement linked; no image. Cultural note (our own wording): "Cultural note: this Nataraja is a consecrated image of a temple at Sripuranthan, Tamil Nadu. We show no image of it; its temple community has not been asked."
- **Patola kept as ritual heirlooms in Eastern Indonesia** (for example Freer F2025.1.2): linked out. No community has been asked.

**Shown, with display basis and content note:**

- **Fustat fragments:** cloth for wear and furnishing. Content note: most such fragments were found near graves (claim 6); neither record gives a findspot.
- **Chola bronzes:** made for temple worship and procession; shown with that function named. Content note: how each left its temple is not recorded.
- **Hamzanama folio (Met 24.48.1):** a court romance, not a sacred text.
- **Patolu (Met 2012.164):** a trade and gift cloth. Cultural note (our own wording; not a Local Contexts Notice): "Cultural note: in eastern Indonesia, families have kept patola as heirlooms with ritual uses. This cloth's keepers are not named in its record."
- **Batik and jamdani:** living practices, with a Cultural note; described from UNESCO and the weavers' own words.
- **Human flows:** Ashu, enslaved and freed by Ben Yiju, is told on the Indian Ocean page. The VOC's conquest of Banda (1621) is named as violence (PLC246; F1.13), with this content note: "Content note: in 1621 the Dutch East India Company conquered the Banda Islands to control nutmeg. Many Bandanese were killed, enslaved or driven out."
