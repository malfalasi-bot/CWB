# The rethink: what the framework should become

Written 20 October 2026 after the F1.13 run and the ballet research. The question was put plainly: is the structure worth it, is it applicable to everything, why so rigid, what should change.

## The verdict

The language has two halves and they have come apart. The **truth half** works and should stay: every date and number a record with its source and confidence; nothing invented; open licences only; a protocol for living, sacred and coerced content; the lint that reads the pages. In the F1.13 run it caught five errors a reader would have trusted. The **structure half** has overgrown: 33 components, a seven-step method, nine lints, a scene schema, seven beat kinds. It was derived from one unit (Edo, an object-and-route unit) and then stretched to every other kind of knowledge by adding a component for each gap, instead of asking whether the shape fitted. The result reads as apparatus first and content second: the Indian Ocean page opens on "as defined by", "membership confidence", "what it leaves out" and a rights line before a learner sees a bowl.

The presentation study put the same verdict in other words: the three clearest things on the web about ballet are an object taken apart in making order, a workshop walked room by room with one number per panel, and an essay whose images carry full captions and whose caveats sit in the sentence. None of the interactives surveyed explained anything a captioned photograph could not. The films did.

## What stays: a constitution of twelve rules

1. Every date, number and attribution is a record with its source and its confidence word; prose never carries one the register lacks.
2. Documented is unmarked; probable, contested and argued carry their word in the sentence; "earliest known", never "first".
3. Nothing is invented: no date, place, placement, route, image, drawing of a subject, or quotation the sources do not give.
4. Open licences only; restricted holders are linked, with the reason; an unverified accession is a stop.
5. Every object: holder · accession · date · materials · licence, under the image, never over it; "maker unknown" where it is.
6. Living, sacred, funerary, coerced and seized content carries a protocol level before it is shown; the course notices, never labels; people moved by force are people, never goods.
7. A dispute is shown as two sourced readings, neither coloured, never marked right.
8. Counts name who counted and when; a company's or a fundraiser's number is labelled as theirs.
9. Every page says what its sources leave out.
10. Paper, ink, two accents; one thing large; captions under; the reading in prose at 17/1.55; works at 390 px; reduced motion keeps every caption.
11. No points, badges, streaks, timers, confetti; nothing auto-advances past five seconds without a control.
12. The learner's twenty-minute read comes before any system: a page you can read and judge before anything is built at scale.

Everything else in the skill becomes a toolbox: available, not required.

## What changes

**Content first, then shape.** Each unit begins with research (two passes: a dossier, then a synthesis) and a one-page brief: the thesis, five to seven claims with their strongest source, three objects, one argument, one living maker, what the sources leave out, and the spine the subject itself suggests. The ballet brief is the first of these. Only then does anyone choose pages, and the pages take the subject's shape: Edo wanted a map and a timeline; ballet wants an object taken apart, a workshop, a short span list, and no globe.

**The apparatus goes behind the content.** "As defined by", membership confidence, the rights line, the claim ids: all present, all a tap away, none in the reader's path. The reading order borrowed from the London Museum: wearer → material → why that material then → construction → care. One number per panel. Hedges in the sentence. The maker named in the first paragraph.

**The Atlas is the index, not the mould.** Units link into the Atlas's places, people, materials and groupings; the Atlas's kinds do not dictate a unit's pages. A grouping page (a movement, a network) is one shape among several, used when the subject is a grouping.

**The build loop shrinks.** One readable page per unit, built from the brief, read by you, revised; the evals and lints run behind that gate, not in front of it. No five-agent fan-outs producing nine pages before anyone has read one. The F1.13 run proved the pipeline; it was not something anyone could learn from in twenty minutes, and that is the test.

**Research is curated by the thesis.** Pass 1 collects (events, people, objects, counts, arguments, gaps); pass 2 verifies the unverified, fills the gaps the first pass named, studies how the subject is already presented, and writes the brief. What does not serve the thesis stays in the dossier.

**The skill is rewritten in that order:** the constitution (one page), the brief template, the reading-order patterns (object taken apart; workshop walked; one work re-made; a bench read outward; a route followed), the toolbox (the components, scenes, lane chart, beats, with the Edo and F1.13 runs as the worked examples), and the lints. The method's seven steps collapse to four: research, brief, one page, lint.

## What this means for what exists

Edo keeps its spine; its apparatus moves behind the stage. F1.13's register, lint and scenes stand as the toolbox's worked example. The ballet unit is built the new way, from the brief, as one page, before anything else is touched. If that page reads, the skill is rewritten around it.
