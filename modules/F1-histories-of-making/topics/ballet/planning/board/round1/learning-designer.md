# Learning-design review: "The shoe" (Ballet, 1 of 4), round 1

Reviewer: learning designer. Read: `build/units/ballet/the-shoe.md`, `dist/the-shoe.html`, `dist/shot-1440.png`, `dist/shot-390.png`, `research_notes/Ballet/BRIEF.md`, `skills/hom-writing/SKILL.md`. Date: 21 October 2026.

## Verdict

**3 / 5.** A well-written, honest reading with a thesis that lands and recurs; but two of its three objectives cannot be met from the page, the one "guess" is not retrieval, the learner is never asked to do anything, and the hardest paragraph on the page has no picture. It teaches; it does not yet make the learner work.

## Ranked issues

### High

**H1. Objective 2 promises what the page says does not exist.**
Quoted: *"explain why a ballet's makers are rarely named, and name three of them."*
The page then says of its one maker: *"His name is not in the record."* and ends the history: *"Every one of those is a firm. The hands are still unnamed."* The only people named are a founder (Frederick Freed), a shoe-room supervisor (Linnette Roe), a dancer (Darcey Bussell) and three firms. The brief's named maker (Michael Cripps, mark "J", claim 3) is not on the page. A learner who tries the objective on themselves fails, and the failure is the page's fault. The "explain why" half is also unmet: the page shows the pattern (poster, programme, sole) but never gives a reason (piece-work in a factory, the brand being the firm, theatres crediting the designer as author). *Fix:* either put Cripps and one or two of the 2013 names into "The mark on the sole" as the brief planned, with the hedge "known from one journalist's visit", and add one sentence of reason; or rewrite the objective to what the page can deliver (see the rewrite below).

**H2. The guess is placed after the paragraph that answers it, under a heading that quotes the answer.**
Quoted: heading *"Paper, paste and satin"*, then *"There is no wood in it. The block is layers of paper and hessian…"*, and 120 words later *"Before you read on, choose one. The hard toe of a pointe shoe is made from: … paper, cloth and flour paste."*
This is recognition of the section title, not retrieval. "Before you read on" is also untrue: everything it tests is above it. The feedback does depend on the answer (two responses), but the "wrong" response merges wood and plastic and answers about a *shank* ("a moulded plastic shank arrived… in 1993") when the question asked about the *toe*, so the plastic-chooser is told something that does not correct their error. *Fix:* move the guess to the top of the section, directly after *"Pick up a pointe shoe and the hard toe feels like wood."* and before *"There is no wood in it"*, as a prediction; rename the heading so it does not carry the answer ("What the toe is made of" or "Inside the block"); give the plastic option its own response ("one maker moulds the whole box in plastic and foam; the rest of the trade still uses paste"). Then add a genuine return later: at the end of "What a company pays", ask *"How long did the page say a pair lasts a principal?"* (one performance / a week / a season) — information from two sections back, which is what SKILL §7 asks for.

**H3. Objective 3 is the page's best idea and the learner never gets to do it.**
Quoted: *"tell a date a record supports from a figure a company gives about itself."*
The page models the skill beautifully (*"These are the companies' own numbers, told to donors and journalists, and nobody has audited them."*; *"That is a union's figure in a dispute."*) but there is no point at which the learner sorts anything. The wording is also muddled: it contrasts a *date* with a *figure*, when the skill is the same for both, and "tell X from Y" reads as "say X, from Y" on first pass. *Fix:* one task (see "What would make it active" below), and reword the objective as a sorting verb.

**H4. The highest cognitive load on the page has no picture.**
Quoted: *"The block is layers of paper and hessian… stuck with flour-and-water paste and covered in satin… made inside out on a wooden foot-shaped form called a last… The stiffener under the arch is the shank… She stands on the platform, the flattened front of the block."*
Seven new terms (block, hessian, paste, last, sole, shank, platform) in 180 words, for a reader who has never held the object, with nothing to look at. Every term is glossed in a clause, as the rules ask, which is why the sentences run long. This is the one place on the page a diagram is required, and it needs no rights: a labelled line drawing of a shoe in section (block, platform, shank, sole, ribbons) drawn for the course. With that drawing, two of the glosses can leave the prose. The brief's own wording ("the maker's mark gives the unit its thesis in a picture") also calls for a sole with its mark; if no photograph can be licensed, a drawn row of the marks (crown, wine glass, fish, letter) is enough.

### Medium

**M1. "Pointe" is never explained.**
The page uses *pointe shoe* from the first objective and never says what pointe is until the fourth section (*"rising onto the tips of their toes"*). SKILL §2 names "pointe" as exactly the term to gloss. A novice reads 500 words about a shoe without knowing what the shoe is for. *Fix:* one clause in the opening paragraph: "the pointe shoe, the hard-toed shoe a dancer stands on the tips of her toes in".

**M2. Build state leaks into the learner view.**
Quoted: *"their own dates are probably right but not yet checked at source"*; *"The lithograph of Taglioni on her toes is free to use and is being fetched."*; *"a link, no picture"* (there is no link in the body; the link is in Sources).
For a novice these read as the author's to-do list, and they confuse the page's legitimate hedges (what the record says) with the project's verification backlog (what the editor has not done yet). SKILL §9 says build notes never reach the learner view. *Fix:* "their own dates come from the firms themselves" (a hedge about the source, which the learner can use); cut the fetching sentence; link the Bussell pair in the sentence that names it.

**M3. Uncertainty is hedged correctly but never scaffolded.**
The page says "probably" four times, "contested" once, "by the usual estimate", "nobody has audited them", "a union's figure in a dispute". Each hedge is in the sentence, as the rules require, and each is accurate. But the learner is never told that the page is sorting its facts into two kinds on purpose, so the hedges read as the author's nerves rather than as the method objective 3 wants them to learn. *Fix:* one sentence at the end of the opening paragraph: "Some numbers on this page come from records; some come from the companies themselves. The page says which, every time." That turns every later "probably" into practice.

**M4. Sequence: the origin should come second, not fourth.**
The brief's path is object 1 (the shoe, three sentences) → object 2 (Taglioni, the concept before the technology) → construction. The page runs construction → mark → money → origin. The money section is the easiest reading on the page and the origin section is where the big idea lives (*"The idea came before the technology"*), so the novice meets the hardest vocabulary first and the most memorable idea last, where fatigue is highest. *Fix:* either move "Where the shoe came from" to follow the opening paragraph (with the Taglioni lithograph, which is the page's one certain image), or keep the order but open with the three-sentence object (what it is made of, how long it lasts, whose mark) before the construction detail, as the brief's step 1 specifies.

**M5. The guess is not accessible once answered.**
In `the-shoe.html`: the three options are real `<button>` elements (good), but on click every button is set `disabled`, so keyboard focus drops to the document body, and the response paragraph is un-hidden with no `aria-live` region and no heading, so a screen-reader user hears nothing. The group has no `fieldset`/`legend` or `role="group"` tying the question to the buttons. *Fix:* wrap in `<fieldset><legend>` (or `role="group" aria-labelledby`), do not disable the chosen button (keep `aria-pressed` and move focus to the response), and give the response container `role="status"` or `aria-live="polite"`.

**M6. Low-contrast small text.**
Eyebrow text ("BY THE END YOU WILL BE ABLE TO"), the "Sources" tone and the colophon use `--ink-3: #7A7162` on `--paper: #F1EADB`, which is about 4.0:1, below the 4.5:1 AA threshold for text at that size (and the eyebrow is uppercase, letter-spaced and small, the worst case). Body text (`#1D1A15`) is about 14:1 and fine. *Fix:* use `--ink-2` for the eyebrow and colophon, or darken `--ink-3`.

### Low

**L1. The Sources block is one paragraph of nineteen links.** On the phone screenshot it is an unbroken slab; the link text is descriptive (good), but it cannot be scanned. *Fix:* a list, one source per line, or collapse behind a `<details>`.

**L2. The thesis is split across the page.** The opening carries the materiality half (*"Ballet looks like the least material of the arts… It runs on some of the most labour-intensive making in the theatre"*); the naming gap, which the brief calls "the unit's subject", first appears in paragraph 5 (*"That is the pattern these pages follow"*). It recurs well after that ("His mark is on thousands of feet"; "The hands are still unnamed"). *Fix:* one more clause in the opening: "…and the people who do that making are almost never named."

**L3. "Principal" is unglossed.** *"a principal can finish a pair in one performance"*: a novice does not know this is the top rank. One clause.

**L4. Sentence length.** The Brugnoli sentence is at the 40-word ceiling; the Capezio/Grishko/Minden sentence is 37 words and carries three dates and a hedge. Split both.

**L5. No self-check on the page.** The rules put the self-check at unit level, which is fine, but a four-page unit read on a phone over several sittings benefits from a two-line "can you now…" at the foot of each page.

## Reading time and chunking

Learner-view text is about 900 words before Sources (the markdown is 1,006 words including the title and "Next" line): roughly four minutes at 220 words a minute, five with the guess and a skim of the sources. That is right for one page of a twenty-minute unit, and at the top of SKILL §6's 400–900 range. On the phone it is a single scroll of about 4,300 px with five headings, one bordered box and no image: in practice one chunk, because nothing on the page changes register. The headings chunk it for a reader who already knows the subject; for a novice the chunks need a different texture each (a drawing, a guess, a task), not just a heading.

## What would make it active (one task, not a quiz)

Add one short task at the end of "What a company pays, by its own account", before the history, tied to objective 3:

> **Sort these four.** Each is on this page. Write "record" or "the company's own figure" beside each, and in three words who said it.
> 1. Freed opened in Cecil Court in 1929.
> 2. The Royal Ballet uses more than six thousand pairs a year.
> 3. Taglioni danced *La Sylphide* on 12 March 1832.
> 4. Freed makes up to 330,000 pairs a year.
>
> *Worked example first:* "Crown makes forty-one pairs a day — a journalist's count, one visit, 2013."

It is a two-minute task, it uses only what the page gave, it rehearses the page's method rather than its facts, and it hands the learner the habit the next three pages depend on (the tutu hours, the strike loss, the injury rates). If the unit's closing "thing the learner makes" is the attribution line, this is its rehearsal.

## Handling of uncertainty for a novice

The hedges are honest, in-sentence and never bracketed, which is the hard part done. What is missing is the frame (M3) and the separation of two kinds of doubt (M2): "the date is contested" (the sources disagree; a learner can hold this) versus "not yet checked at source" (the project has not finished; a learner cannot act on this and should not see it). "Probably, the 1870s" in the guess feedback is the right content with awkward commas: "since the 1870s or thereabouts" reads as a hedge, not a stammer.

## Where a picture, diagram or film is required, and where the page rightly does without

Required: (1) a labelled section drawing of the shoe at "Paper, paste and satin" (H4), drawable in-house, no rights question; (2) the sole with a maker's mark at "The mark on the sole", because it *is* the thesis, and a drawn row of marks will serve if no photograph can be used; (3) the Carnavalet Taglioni lithograph at "Where the shoe came from", already planned and CC0. Film would earn its place for the making (turning the shoe inside out, pressing the sole), which is touch and time, as SKILL §8 says; the ROH factory film is the candidate if it can be embedded, else a link in the sentence rather than a description of its absence. Rightly without: no timeline for five dates in one paragraph (a list carries them), no globe, no interactive on the money section (prose with named sources is stronger than a chart of unaudited numbers), and "What this page could not show you" is the right device, once the build-state sentences are out of it.

## Accessibility summary

Headings: `h1` then five `h2`, in order, no skips: good. The objectives block has no heading of its own (an eyebrow `<p>`), acceptable. Links: descriptive text throughout, but one paragraph of nineteen (L1). Guess: native buttons, good target size on the phone, but no group label, no live region, focus lost on answer (M5). Images: none, so no alt-text issue, and no visual channel at all. Contrast: body fine; eyebrow, sources tone and colophon at about 4.0:1 (M6). Dark mode tokens exist. Line length at 1440 is about 68 characters: good. The hidden claim chips are `hidden` in the DOM so screen readers skip them: fine.

## The three changes that would most improve it

1. **Make the objectives true and give each one a place on the page**: name a maker (Cripps, "J") with the hedge, add a reason the names are missing, and add the sorting task so objective 3 is performed, not described.
2. **Move the guess to before the information, under a heading that does not answer it, and add a spaced return two sections later**: a prediction at the top, retrieval further down.
3. **Draw the shoe**: one labelled section at the construction paragraph, a row of maker's marks at the sole, the Taglioni lithograph at the origin. Three pictures, two of them drawn in-house, and the page's chunking and load problems both ease.

## Proposed rewrite of the objectives block

> **By the end you will be able to**
> - describe what a pointe shoe is built from and how long a pair lasts, and say who makes it and how that maker is known;
> - explain why ballet names its dancers and designers and not its makers, and give the one place a maker's identity reaches the stage;
> - sort a figure about ballet into "a record supports it" or "the company says so", and say who counted it.

Each verb has a place on the page to be practised: the first in the guess (prediction) and the drawing; the second in "The mark on the sole", once the reason and a name are in it; the third in the sorting task.

## What to keep

- The opening paragraph: the thesis in three sentences, first object within 150 words, first sentence under 16 words.
- *"Pick up a pointe shoe and the hard toe feels like wood. There is no wood in it."* The best pair of sentences on the page; it is the guess waiting to happen.
- The whole of "The mark on the sole" paragraph 1 and the "poster / programme / sole" triplet.
- *"They are worth knowing because no other part of ballet's economy is counted in public at all."* and *"It is also the only count of this labour anyone has made."* Both teach the method in one line.
- *"The idea came before the technology."* Keep it, and move it earlier.
- The honesty of the last section, minus its build notes.
- Every hedge being in the sentence, never a bracket or a superscript.

## Rules in SKILL.md I would change

1. **§2, objectives.** Quoted: *"three lines, each a verb a reader can test on themselves (… name the four trades and who the law named; explain why the maker's name is missing)."* The rule asks for a testable verb but not for a check against the finished page, and its example "name the four trades" was copied here as "name three of them" onto a page whose content is that the names do not exist. Add: *"After the page is written, read each objective against it: the page must contain the thing the verb acts on, and give the reader one place to do it before the self-check. An objective the page cannot deliver is rewritten or the page is."*

2. **§7, guesses.** Quoted: *"A guess is a commitment: no answer visible before the choice; two responses, one for each way the reader chose; no hint that quotes the answer."* It says nothing about where the guess sits, and a section heading is not treated as a hint. Add: *"A guess sits before the paragraph that answers it (a prediction) or at least two sections after it (a return), never inside the section that teaches it; the heading above a guess must not carry the answer; where there are three options, each wrong option gets its own sentence."*

3. **§4, the hedge.** Quoted: *"The hedge is in the sentence: 'probably goose, pending analysis'; 'the Met's record says'; 'historians disagree about when'."* "Pending analysis" licenses exactly the leak in M2. Add: *"A hedge says what the record says or does not say. The state of the project's own checking ('not yet checked at source', 'being fetched', 'pending') is an editor-view note, never a learner sentence."*

4. **§7, the ending.** Quoted: *"The unit ends with something the learner makes."* Unit-level only, so a page may carry no task at all, as this one does. Add: *"Each page gives the learner one thing to do with the page's own material, under two minutes, with a worked example first; a guess is not that thing."*

5. **§6, length.** Quoted: *"A page's reading is 400–900 words."* Add: *"A page with no picture is read as one chunk however many headings it has; above about 600 words the page needs at least one element that is not prose (a drawing, a guess placed as a prediction, a task), or it is split."*

6. **§2, terms.** Quoted: *"The five terms the page will use that the reader does not know (… pointe, turnout), each glossed in a clause the first time it appears."* The rule is right and the page broke it on its own example term; the lint should check it. Add to §10: *"the prose lint lists the page's technical terms and the sentence that glosses each; a term with no gloss fails."*
