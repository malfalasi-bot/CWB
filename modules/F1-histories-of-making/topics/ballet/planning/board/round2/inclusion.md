# Inclusion and ethics review · "The shoe" v2 · round 2

Reviewer: inclusion and ethics seat. Read: `board/round2/_page-v2.md`, the register (`atlas/data/register/units/ballet.yaml`, including the new `people` block), and, again on the web, the Spitalfields Life portrait series (Ray's words checked verbatim), the May 2013 factory essay, and the Heritage Crafts listing. 21 October 2026.

**Verdict.** The page now says true things about naming: Ray is Ray, the names are credited to the journalist who wrote them down, Dora Freed is back in her own shop, the Grishko names are in, the output figures are attributed to the people who gave them, the strike that did not happen no longer happens. The thesis sentence ("you learn the name behind it from a journalist, if at all") is now earned. What keeps it from a 5: two of the sentences added for inclusion are wrong in detail (the finishing-room headcount and the Heritage Crafts reason), the page still says nothing to the people it names about why they are in it, the body and the geography of making are still absent, and the figure slot is empty so the page's image ethics cannot be judged.

## Issues

### Still wrong, or new and wrong

**1. The finishing-room sentence is false as a headcount and misplaces Ozel Ahmed.**
> "In the finishing rooms, where the shoes are bound and paired, the people the visit named were women: Sheila Goodman, a finisher of about forty years, and Ozel Ahmed, a supervisor."

The May 2013 essay names, in those rooms, Luthu Miah (supervisor, binding room), Varsha Bahen (finisher), Rashimi Patel (pairing), Sheila Goodman (finisher) and Philip Goodman (chargehand, forty years, started the same day as Sheila). "The people the visit named were women" is not true (Luthu Miah and Philip Goodman are named there too), and Ozel Ahmed is supervisor of the theatrical-shoes department, not the finishing rooms. The structural point I asked for in round 1 is still only implied: at Freed the word "maker" means the man at the bench, and nobody in binding or finishing, where most of the named women are, has a mark. Fix: "Past the makers' benches the shoes go to the binding and finishing rooms. The visit named Sheila Goodman, a finisher of about forty years, Varsha Bahen, a finisher, and Rashimi Patel, who pairs the shoes; Ozel Ahmed supervises the theatrical department. Nobody in those rooms has a mark. At Freed, 'maker' means the bench, and the benches are men's." Add Varsha Bahen and Rashimi Patel to the `people` block; the register should not name only the two women the page happened to use.

**2. "The closing of workshops" inverts the source.**
> "Its reason is not a lack of demand but the supply of materials and the closing of workshops."

Heritage Crafts says materials are harder to source and dearer, and that "there are fewer independent dance shops here in the UK and other countries", meaning retailers, after COVID. "Workshops" tells the learner the makers' own places are closing, which is the dying-craft story the sentence was rewritten to avoid. Fix: "but the supply of materials and the closing of the independent dance shops that sold them." And "in the UK", not "Britain's list ... critically endangered" without the qualifier: the listing is national.

**3. The page names ten people from press articles and says nothing to them.**
> "the company has not published their names, so this page carries them as a journalist's record."

That sentence is right as provenance and silent as ethics. My position on the question the chair asked: naming them is right. Ray, Taksim, Fred and Daniel sat for portraits, answered an interviewer's questions, and were published under their names in a series that has been republished twice; Ray said, "I could never have dreamt of this." Sheila Goodman and Ozel Ahmed were named in a factory visit the company evidently hosted. These are public, on-record, participatory sources, and leaving the names out would repeat the firm's erasure in the name of protecting the people erased. But the page should say three things it does not. (a) That they spoke to a writer for a portrait, not to this course, and that the page uses their words as published. (b) That the course will remove or correct a name at the person's request, with a route a shoemaker would actually use: a plain email line on the page, not "open an issue on the course repository" (the colophon's current route). (c) That the makers are given by first name because that is how the series recorded them; otherwise the page reads as a class marker, since every dancer, manager and supervisor on it has a surname (Darcey Bussell, Linnette Roe, Sheila Goodman) and the men at the bench do not. Fix, in "What is not here": "The makers at Freed's factory are known from two visits in 2013, by a writer and a photographer in May and by a novelist and the same photographer in July; they spoke for a portrait series, not for this page, and are given here by the first names the series used. The company has not published their names. If any of them would rather not be named here, write to [address] and the name comes out."

**4. The figure slot is empty, so the page's image ethics cannot be passed.**
> "@@FIGURE@@"

If the image is one of Patricia Niven's portraits, it is not open and needs her licence and the sitter's knowledge; it must not go in on the strength of a press URL. If it is a Freed company still or the ROH film frame, the page will be pairing a company image with names the company does not publish, which is fine but should be captioned as such ("the firm's photograph; the names are a journalist's"). If it is the Carnavalet Taglioni, it belongs in "Where the shoe came from", not here. The board cannot sign a page with an unresolved image slot in the section about makers.

**5. "One visit by one writer and one photographer" is wrong.**
> "The makers at Freed's factory are known from one visit by one writer and one photographer in 2013"

Two visits (May and July 2013), two writers (the Gentle Author; Sarah Winman), one photographer (Niven), plus a third journalist in 2019 (the Spectator). The register knows this (`spitalfields-2013`, `spitalfields-makers-2013`, `spectator-2019`); the page collapses it. The count matters because the page's claim is about how thin the record is. See the fix under 3.

**6. The body is still not on the page where the shoe meets the foot.**
> "Nothing about the shoe is permanent: sweat softens flour paste."

Unchanged from round 1. The page's own first paragraph promises "the body, the craft and the money meet in it" and the body is still the one of the three not delivered. One sentence and a pointer: "Ankle and foot are the commonest injuries that keep a Royal Ballet dancer off stage; the numbers are on page 4." Without it, "weightlessness, a creature of air" still stands uncosted.

**7. The geography of making is still a geography of founders' shops.**
> "Then the firms: Salvatore Capezio's shop near the Metropolitan Opera from 1887; Frederick and Dora Freed's shop in Cecil Court from 1929; Grishko in Moscow from 1988 or 1989"

Unchanged. Grishko reports over 500 staff across Moscow, the Czech Republic and North Macedonia (a plant at Demir Hisar announced as nearly 300 jobs); the volume brands make offshore. The page now names two Moscow workers, which is good, and still gives no sense that most of the world's pointe shoes are made by people in none of its four cities. One sentence: "Grishko's shoes are made in Moscow, the Czech Republic and North Macedonia by about five hundred people; its website names its founder." (For the chair: no source supports a factory in Bosnia.)

### Smaller

**8. "A women's technique" essentialises what is a description of practice.**
> "Pointe is a women's technique, so this unpaid hour is women's work almost without exception."

The second half is exactly right. "A women's technique" makes it a property of the technique rather than of who is cast to do it (men have danced on pointe professionally since the Trockadero in 1974, and NYCB's 2022 agreement is gender-neutral). Fix: "Pointe is danced almost entirely by women, so this unpaid hour is women's work almost without exception."

**9. Ray's joke is carried as a thesis.**
> "that the marks exist 'so they know who to blame' if a shoe comes back."

The source reads: "so that if anything is wrong with the shoe they know who to blame!" It is a joke with an exclamation mark. The page's use is fair (the mark is a traceability device; he says so), but a worker's wry line should keep its register: "as Ray put it, 'so they know who to blame'". And the page no longer says who began the marks or when, which is right; the register note withdrawing v1's 1929 claim is good.

**10. Piecework is still not said.**
> "The one number missing from every account is what a maker is paid for a pair."

Good sentence. The May 2013 essay (per pass2_body §1.6) has the makers on piecework at individual benches; "paid for a pair" implies it without saying it. "Paid by the pair, and what a pair pays them is in no account" closes the loop with the forty-a-day figure: forty pairs is a piece-rate day.

**11. "Nobody has audited them."**
> "These are the companies' own numbers, told to donors and journalists, and nobody has audited them."

Unchanged from round 1. The Royal Ballet and Opera files audited accounts with the Charity Commission; what nobody outside has checked is the shoe line. "No one outside the companies has checked them."

**12. "Between twenty and forty" stretches a present range with a 1990 figure.**
> "somewhere between twenty and forty"

The forty is the Christian Science Monitor, 1990, per the register. A thirty-five-year-old count in a range presented as "depends on who you ask and in which year" is defensible only if the years are named; otherwise say "twenty-some" and leave 1990 in the register.

**13. Tense for people recorded in 2013.**
> "Ray, whose mark is the crown, had made shoes in the Hackney factory for twenty-six years when ... He told them he makes forty pairs a day"

Fine as reported speech, but "whose mark is the crown" and "Taksim's mark is an anchor" are present tense about men last recorded thirteen years ago. "Whose mark was the crown" costs nothing and does not retire anyone who is still there.

## What I would change next, in order

1. Rewrite the finishing-room sentence as in issue 1 and make the "maker means the bench" point explicit; add Varsha Bahen and Rashimi Patel to the register's `people`.
2. Add the consent paragraph (issue 3) with a real contact route, and resolve the figure (issue 4) before the page is signed.
3. Fix "workshops" to "shops" (issue 2), then add the two sentences the page still owes: the foot (issue 6) and where the world's shoes are made (issue 7).

## What is now right and should stay

Ray by name, with the record's provenance in the same sentence; "the maker is a symbol on a sole, and you learn the name behind it from a journalist, if at all"; Sergei Murza and Olga Monakhova; Frederick and Dora Freed's shop; the output figures attributed to the company and to visitors separately; "the one number missing from every account is what a maker is paid for a pair"; the strike notice told as a notice; the second learning objective, which the page now meets; "What is not here" without universal negatives; the register's editor notes that record what v1 got wrong and why, which is the kind of audit trail a community-review protocol asks for.
