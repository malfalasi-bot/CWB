# Learning-design review, round 2: "The shoe" (Ballet, 1 of 4)

Reviewer: learning designer. Read: `research_notes/Ballet/board/round2/_page-v2.md`, `build/units/ballet/dist/the-shoe.html`, `shot-1440.png`, `shot-390.png`. Round-1 review at `board/round1/learning-designer.md`.

A strong 3, not a 4. The page now has real names, a real diagram and a guess that is a prediction rather than a recognition test, and objectives 1 and 2 can be met closed-book. What keeps it at 3: objective 3 is still only modelled, never practised, and the page's own text now breaks the rule it asks the learner to learn; the heading and figure under the guess still carry its answer; after the first screen the learner does nothing for a thousand words; the round-1 accessibility and contrast issues were not touched; and the page is 25 % over budget.

## Objectives, checked closed-book against the finished text

1. *"say what a pointe shoe is made of, how it is built, and how long it lasts"* — **Met.** Materials (paper, hessian, paste, satin, leather), the build order (last → block → pleats → turned → sole → oven), and "ten to twenty hours… one performance" are all on the page, in one place, with a figure that uses the same words. The learner practises only the materials half (the guess); "how it is built" is read, not done, but a closed-book reader could give the order.
2. *"name three people who make them, and say who writes their names down and who does not"* — **Met, with effort the page could spare the reader.** Eight names (Ray, Taksim, Fred, Daniel, Sheila Goodman, Ozel Ahmed, Sergei Murza, Olga Monakhova) are packed into two paragraphs of 150 and 80 words, interleaved with marks, years and quotes. Three will survive if the maker-and-mark pairs are laid out as a list (see issue 5). "Who writes them down" (a journalist, a news agency) and "who does not" (the firm) are both stated plainly. The reason the firm does not is still not given, but the objective no longer asks for it.
3. *"tell a date a source supports from a figure a company gives about itself"* — **Not met.** The page models the distinction well (Freed's 250,000 against visitors' 150,000 and 333,000; the Royal Ballet's "figure it publishes to raise money"; "a union's figure in a dispute") but never asks the learner to make it, so the skill is shown and not learned. The wording is still muddled (a *date* against a *figure*; "tell X from Y" reads as "say X, from Y"). And the page itself fails the test: see issue 2.

## Issues

### 1. (High, carried over) The learner still does nothing after the first screen.

Quoted: the only interactive element is *"Before you read on, choose one."* at the top; from "Paper, paste and satin" to "Next: the tutu" there are 1,000 words and no task, no return, no question.
The round-1 sorting task was not added. Objective 3 is a skill, and a skill needs one rehearsal with a worked example. *Fix:* at the end of "What a company pays, by its own account", add:

> **Sort these four.** Each is on this page. Beside each write "a source supports it" or "the company's own figure", and who said it.
> 1. Taglioni danced *La Sylphide* on 12 March 1832.
> 2. Freed makes about 250,000 pairs a year.
> 3. The Royal Ballet uses more than six thousand pairs a year.
> 4. Pointe-shoe making is critically endangered in Britain.
>
> *Worked example:* "Ray makes forty pairs a day — he told a writer, one visit, 2013."

Two minutes, nothing new, and it is the rehearsal for every figure in the next three pages. It also puts an activity in the second half of the page, which is where attention is lowest.

### 2. (High, new) The firms' founding dates are now asserted flat, and they are the firms' own.

Quoted: *"Then the firms: Salvatore Capezio's shop near the Metropolitan Opera from 1887; Frederick and Dora Freed's shop in Cecil Court from 1929; Grishko in Moscow from 1988 or 1989, a contested year."*
The Sources line gives Capezio's "About us ('Established 1887')" and Freed's own "History" page as the authorities. Round 1 asked for the build-state hedge ("not yet checked at source") to go; it went, and nothing replaced it, so a page whose third objective is to separate "a source supports it" from "the company says so" now presents two company-supplied dates as fact, in the paragraph right after the one that teaches the distinction. A learner who has just learned the method and applies it to this paragraph catches the page out. *Fix:* "Then the firms, each dating itself: Capezio… from 1887 by its own account; the Freeds' shop in Cecil Court from 1929, the year the firm gives…" One clause, and the paragraph becomes a second example of the method instead of a breach of it.

### 3. (High, half carried over) The guess's answer is still on screen while the reader chooses.

Quoted: guess options *"wood, shaped and padded / moulded plastic / paper, cloth and flour paste"*, then immediately beneath the box the heading *"Paper, paste and satin"*, and beneath that the figure label *"block: paper, hessian, flour paste"*.
In the 1440 screenshot the heading sits about 40 px under the third button; on the phone it is in the same viewport too. The guess moved up; the answer did not move out of sight. SKILL §7: "no answer visible before the choice." *Fix:* rename the heading ("Inside the block", "Built on a last", "Made inside out") and either move the figure below the first construction paragraph or keep the figure where it is and let the heading change do the work; the figure label is two paragraphs down and is acceptable once the heading no longer repeats it.

### 4. (Medium, carried over) "Pointe" is still never explained.

Quoted: *"say what a pointe shoe is made of"* (first line of the page); *"Pointe is a women's technique"* (the first time the word stands alone, 650 words in, undefined); *"rose onto the tips of her toes"* (900 words in, and the sentence never uses the word).
A novice reads the whole page without being told that "pointe" means dancing on the tips of the toes, which is the one fact the whole object exists for. SKILL §2 names this exact term as the one to gloss. *Fix:* one clause in the opening paragraph: "The shoe is the place to start: the pointe shoe, the hard-toed shoe a dancer stands on the tips of her toes in, is where the body, the craft and the money meet."

### 5. (Medium) The makers are named in a way that is hard to hold.

Quoted: *"Ray, whose mark is the crown, had made shoes in the Hackney factory for twenty-six years when a writer and a photographer visited his bench in 2013. He told them he makes forty pairs a day, and that the marks exist "so they know who to blame" if a shoe comes back. Dancers learn the marks and ask for a maker by one. Taksim's mark is an anchor; Fred's is the letter F; Daniel, four months at the bench, had the butterfly."*
Objective 2 needs three names retained; the page offers eight across two paragraphs, mixed with four marks, three numbers and a quotation. *Fix:* keep Ray as the narrative and then set the pairs as a short list the eye can return to:

> - crown · Ray, twenty-six years
> - anchor · Taksim
> - F · Fred
> - butterfly · Daniel, four months

A list is also the natural target for a spaced return on page 3 ("which mark was Ray's?").

### 6. (Medium) Length: 1,124 words, and it drags in three places.

Reading time is now about five and a half minutes before Sources, against a four-page, twenty-minute unit: tolerable for page 1, but the overrun is not spent on the objectives. Cut, in this order, for about 110 words:
- *"The notice was lifted and the Christmas performances went ahead."* Narrative; the learner needs the figure and its hedge, not the outcome.
- *"Gaynor Minden sold a moulded shank in 1993 and had its foam liner patented in 1998."* 1993 is already in the guess feedback; the patent serves no claim on this page.
- *"And no source read for this page gives the figure that would complete it: the price of a maker's hour."* It duplicates *"The one number missing from every account is what a maker is paid for a pair."* 400 words earlier. Keep one; the ending is the stronger place, so cut the first.
- *"whether she was the earliest is contested"* can stay; *"The block that made it safer came half a century later, from Italy."* is unsourced and vague and could go, or gain "by the usual histories".
That brings the page to about 1,010 words. Going further means losing the Grishko names, which objective 2 uses (one non-London example) and should keep. Say 1,000 is the honest budget for this page and amend the rule rather than the page (see below).

### 7. (Medium, new) The schematic's labels are unreadable on the phone.

Quoted: figure labels *"block: paper, hessian, flour paste"*, *"shank (under the arch)"*, *"last (wooden form)"* at 390 px.
The SVG is a 640-unit viewBox scaled to about 350 px, so the label text renders at roughly 6–7 px in the 390 screenshot. The figure is the fix for the page's highest-load paragraph and it does not work on the device SKILL §0 says the learner has. *Fix:* number the parts in the drawing (1–7) and put the key as a list in the figcaption, or set the label text at a size that holds at 350 px (about 20 units in a 640 viewBox) and let the lines run outside the shoe.

### 8. (Medium, carried over, untouched) The guess is not accessible once answered.

In `the-shoe.html`, unchanged from round 1: every button is set `disabled` on click, so focus drops to the body; the response paragraph is un-hidden with no `aria-live`/`role="status"`; the question is not tied to the buttons by `fieldset`/`legend` or `role="group" aria-labelledby`. *Fix:* as round 1: group and label, do not disable the chosen button, give the response `role="status"` and move focus to it.

### 9. (Medium, carried over, untouched) Contrast.

`--ink-3: #7A7162` on `--paper: #F1EADB` is still about 4.0:1 for the eyebrow ("BY THE END YOU WILL BE ABLE TO"), the Sources tone and the colophon; below AA for small text, and the eyebrow is small, uppercase and letter-spaced. *Fix:* use `--ink-2` for those, or darken `--ink-3` to about `#625A4C`.

### 10. (Low) Objective 3's wording.

Quoted: *"tell a date a source supports from a figure a company gives about itself."*
Rewrite: *"sort a figure or a date into 'a source supports it' or 'the company says so', and say who counted it."* Then the task in issue 1 is that sentence performed.

### 11. (Low) Uncertainty is hedged but not framed.

The page now says "by one account", "by the usual estimate", "by the usual account", "a contested year", "probably from the 1870s on", "nobody has audited them", "a journalist's record". Each hedge is right and in-sentence. The reader is still never told the page is sorting on purpose, so for a novice the hedges read as the author being careful rather than as a method to copy. *Fix:* one sentence at the end of the opening paragraph: "Some numbers on this page come from records; some come from the companies themselves. The page says which, every time." Issue 2 shows why the frame matters: it would have caught the firm dates in editing.

### 12. (Low) Figure caption and the last.

Quoted: *"A pointe shoe in section. Schematic, drawn from the makers' descriptions; not to scale and not a particular shoe."*
The dashed last is drawn inside the shoe, and a novice can read it as a part that stays. Add: "the last is taken out once the shoe is made." The SVG `<title>`/`<desc>` are good alt text and should stay.

### 13. (Low) Figures that changed between versions.

Round 1: *"The maker known as Crown had made shoes for twenty-four years… forty-one pairs a day."* Round 2: *"Ray… twenty-six years… forty pairs a day."* One of these is wrong, or two sources disagree, in which case the page should say so as it does for Grishko. A question for the researcher, not a page fault; flagged so the claims lint catches it.

### 14. (Low, carried over) Sources are one paragraph of twenty-five links.

Unscannable on the phone screenshot. A list, or a `<details>`.

## What is new and right

- The guess is now a true prediction: *"Pick up a pointe shoe and the hard toe feels like wood. It is made from:"* seeds the wrong answer deliberately, and the wrong-answer response now talks about a shoe, not a shank.
- The schematic uses the same seven terms as the prose, in the same order, with real alt text; this is what round 1 asked for and it halves the load of the construction paragraph on desktop.
- Objective 2 is now honest, and the "who to blame" quotation gives the mark a reason in the maker's own words.
- *"None of those names is on the company's own pages."* and *"The makers' names are the ones you have to go looking for."* The thesis now recurs four times and lands harder each time.
- The Grishko names make the point that this is not one firm's habit.
- *"Its reason is not a lack of demand but the supply of materials and the closing of workshops."* A good correction of what a novice would assume.
- "What is not here" is now a section about the sources' limits, not the project's to-do list.

## What I would change next, in order

1. Add the sorting task (issue 1) and reword objective 3 (issue 10). This is the one change that moves the page to 4.
2. Hedge the firm dates (issue 2) and add the framing sentence (issue 11), so the page keeps the rule it teaches.
3. Rename "Paper, paste and satin" (issue 3), gloss "pointe" in the opening (issue 4), and set the makers as a list (issue 5).
4. Fix the figure for the phone (issue 7), the guess's accessibility (issue 8) and the contrast (issue 9): none of these needs the writer.
5. Cut the 110 words in issue 6.

## Rule note for SKILL.md

§6 *"A page's reading is 400–900 words"*: this page cannot meet objective 2 (names, with the hedge that they come from one visit) and objective 3 (two kinds of figure, each with who counted it) under 1,000 words, because honest attribution is wordy. I would change the rule to *"400–900 words of reading, to 1,000 where the objectives require names and attributions in the sentence; above that the page splits"*, and keep the round-1 proposal that a page over about 600 words carries at least one element that is not prose in its second half.
