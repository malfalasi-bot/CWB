# Maker's review, round 2: "The shoe" (v2)

Reviewer: theatrical shoemaker and costume maker, twenty years in a company wardrobe and shoe room, now teaching. Read: `board/round2/_page-v2.md`, `build/units/ballet/dist/the-shoe.html` and `shot-1440.png` (the schematic, cropped and inspected at full size), `atlas/data/register/units/ballet.yaml`. I checked the Heritage Crafts listing and the two Spitalfields pieces again.

**Verdict.** The round-1 wounds are closed: the press is gone, Ray has his name, four makers and two finishers are on the page, the oven is an oven, Freed's number is Freed's, the quiz comes before the answer, and "sweat softens flour paste" is the right sentence. The text would now pass as a first reading for an apprentice. The drawing would not. It is the one thing on the page a learner will remember, and it shows a pointe shoe as a sealed pod with a solid toe, which is the exact misconception the quiz above it exists to correct. Three new errors have come in with the rewrite (Ozel Ahmed, "closing of workshops", "one visit"), and the round-1 gaps on sizes, fitting, the dancer's reasons and what a good shoe looks like are still open. Fix the drawing and the three new errors and this is a 4.

## Issues, in order of weight

### 1. The drawing shows the block as a solid lump. It is a hollow cup.
Figure: the toe region is filled (`fill-opacity .10`) edge to edge, with six curved layer-lines running right across it from the upper to the sole. There is no space inside the toe for the toes.

A block in section is a thick wall, a U on its side: layers of hessian and paper pasted up around the toe of the last, so that when the last comes out there is a cavity the foot goes into, bounded by the block on top, sides and platform end, and by the insole underneath. What the drawing shows is a toe stuffed solid, which is what a learner who guessed "wood, shaped and padded" already believed. Fix: draw the block as a band of three or four layer-lines following the inside of the upper, about a fifth of the toe's depth thick, with an open interior; shade the band, not the cavity. Optionally dot in the outline of a foot so the cavity reads as "where the toes are".

### 2. The drawing has no opening. It is not recognisably a shoe.
Figure: one closed almond outline from heel point to platform; no throat, no vamp edge, no heel seat, no drawstring.

A pointe shoe in side section has a low heel at the back, the upper rising to the vamp edge over the toes, and an open top where the foot goes in with the drawstring in its casing along the edge. The drawn profile tapers to a point at the heel like a canoe. A learner shown this and then handed a shoe would not match them. Fix: draw the top line as the vamp (from platform back to the throat), then an open edge running down to a short vertical heel with its seam; label vamp, throat/drawstring and heel. These are the words the dancer uses when she orders a special ("deeper vamp", "lower heel"), so they earn their place.

### 3. The drawing puts the last inside a finished shoe.
Figure: dashed "last (wooden form)" sits inside the outline together with the shank, the pleats and the sole.

That stage does not exist. While the shoe is on the last it is inside out and has no shank in it; by the time the shank is in and the shoe is right way round, the last is out. The drawing collapses two stages into one object and the caption says "a pointe shoe in section", so the learner takes the last for a component. Fix: either drop the last and draw the finished shoe (preferred, with the cavity from issue 1), or draw two small panels: "on the last, inside out" and "turned, shank in". Also: "wooden" is still unsourced (round 1, M2); nothing in the register says what the lasts are made of. Label it "last (the form the shoe is built on)".

### 4. The drawing and the text are both missing the lining and the insole.
Text: "a pointe shoe begins as a satin upper pulled over a last ... Inside the toe the maker builds the block".

"Inside the toe" of a shoe that is inside out is a sentence a learner cannot picture. The upper is satin outside and cotton lining inside, stitched together before it reaches the bench; the block is built up on the toe between them. Without the lining, "inside the toe" means nothing, and without the insole (tacked to the last first; the shank is fixed to it, the three nails you can feel in a Freed heel) the shank floats. Fix: "begins as a satin upper with its cotton lining, pulled inside out over a last to which the maker has tacked an insole; the block is built up on the toe, between satin and lining". Add "lining" and "insole" to the figure.

### 5. The sole is placed after turning. In a turn shoe it goes on before.
Text: "The shoe is made inside out, then turned the right way round, and the leather sole is pasted and hammered on at the bench."

The sentence reads sole-after-turn. In a turn shoe the sole is attached while the shoe is inside out on the last; turning is what puts the sole's edge and the pleats where you can see them on the finished shoe. The register's `bal-turn-shoe` only supports "inside out, then turned"; it does not support this order, and the sentence as written contradicts the method it names. Fix, in bench order: "The satin is pleated under the toe and hammered flat, the leather sole is pasted and hammered over the pleats, the shoe comes off the last and is turned the right way round, and the shank goes in." Then the oven.

### 6. Ozel Ahmed is in the wrong room and given a gender the source does not.
Text: "In the finishing rooms, where the shoes are bound and paired, the people the visit named were women: Sheila Goodman, a finisher of about forty years, and Ozel Ahmed, a supervisor."

In the May 2013 piece Ozel Ahmed is "Supervisor, Theatrical Department (20 years)", the character-shoe side, not finishing; the binding-room supervisor named is Luthu Miah. Nothing in the source gives Ozel Ahmed's gender, and the name is not one you can read it from. The gendered division is real and worth teaching (the benches are men; binding and finishing are mostly women; the 2019 headline was "big burly blokes"), but it must stand on names that hold: Sheila Goodman (pointe-shoe finisher), Varsha Bahen (finisher), Rashimi Patel (pairing). Fix: "In the binding and finishing rooms the people the visit named were mostly women: Sheila Goodman, a finisher of about forty years, Varsha Bahen, finishing, Rashimi Patel, pairing." Move Ozel Ahmed out of the sentence or to the theatrical department. This is a new error, not in v1.

### 7. "The closing of workshops" is not what Heritage Crafts says.
Text: "Its reason is not a lack of demand but the supply of materials and the closing of workshops."

The listing says materials are harder to source and dearer, that trade is "buoyant" with "plenty of customers", and that there are fewer independent dance *shops* (retail) as buying moves online. No workshop closures are mentioned. It also counts three apprentices at Freed and says "each craftsperson is responsible for one skill area only: cutting, measuring, making, first and second binding, cleaning and finishing", which is the division of labour the page is describing without a source. Fix: "Its reasons are materials, harder to source and dearer, and a trade that is now two firms; it counts three apprentices at Freed." Name the second firm, Suffolk, still missing from v2 (round 1, L4).

### 8. "One visit by one writer and one photographer" is wrong, and the register knows it.
Text: "The makers at Freed's factory are known from one visit by one writer and one photographer in 2013".

The register lists two pieces: the May 2013 factory visit (the Gentle Author) and the July 2013 maker portraits (Sarah Winman, photographs Patricia Niven), and the series "The Pointe Shoe Makers of Hackney" ran to at least ten parts, the latest in 2025. Fix: "known from a journalist's factory visit and a portrait series in 2013, continued since; the company has not published their names."

### 9. Sizes, widths, specials and the fitter: still absent (round 1, M3).
The page now names makers and marks but never says what a dancer orders. From Freed's own catalogue: UK 1 to 8½ in half sizes; widths medium, X, XX, XXX; a special order can specify the maker, length, width, block, platform, vamp, side and heel measurements, insole and drawstring, accurate "to within 3 mm"; two-thirds of Classic output is made to a dancer's specification, which arrives at the bench on a docket (July 2013). That paragraph is what makes "dancers ask for a maker by one" mean something: she is buying a pair of hands and a docket, not a size. No fitter appears, though Sophie Simpson is in the research. Fix: one paragraph after the marks, with the docket.

### 10. The dancer's work still has no reason (round 1, M4).
Text: "The dancer sews her own ribbons and elastics, darns or glues the platform and breaks the pair in, on her own time."

Still a list without a why. The maker builds to a docket; the dancer finishes the fit to her foot: ribbons at the angle her ankle needs, the platform darned or glued for grip and to stop the satin shredding, the shank bent or cut where her foot bends, the wings softened so she can roll through demi-pointe, the sole scraped. Thirty to forty-five minutes of sewing a pair before breaking in; thirty pairs at a time. Fix: two sentences with the why, then the Paris figure lands.

### 11. A learner still cannot tell a good shoe from a bad one (round 1).
No test anywhere on the page. Six that an apprentice learns in a week: stand the shoe on its platform on a table, it should stand by itself with the platform flat and square to the shank; pleats even, tight and flat, no gaps, no paste bleed on the satin; block symmetrical, the same give under the thumb on both wings; sole centred, stitching even; shank flush inside with no ridge; the pair matching (pairing is a job on the page: Rashimi Patel's). Fix: a short boxed list under the figure, "What to look for when you pick one up".

### 12. Smaller
- "the leather sole is pasted and hammered on at the bench": true, and the register has no record for it; add one (`bal-sole-bench`, Spitalfields May 2013: the Ballet Department has no machinery, three tools) so the chip exists.
- "a maker is paid for a pair": the research has the word the page needs, piecework (May 2013). "Paid by the pair, on piecework, at a rate no account prints" explains forty pairs a day and the waiting lists in one clause.
- "Pointe is a women's technique, so this unpaid hour is women's work almost without exception": fair, but men sew their own elastics too; the difference is scale, not kind. "almost all of this unpaid hour is women's" is safer.
- "Each of Freed's pointe-shoe makers has one": the marks are given, not chosen, and belong to the firm; a dancer scratches the mark off the sole to keep a maker to herself (July 2013). Both are better than the sentence that is there.
- The figure's `desc` says "the layered paper-and-cloth block inside the toe"; after issue 1 is fixed, make it say "around the toe".
- Shank label "under the arch": under the foot's arch, inside the shoe, on top of the sole. The dashed line in the figure runs from the sole up to the block, diagonally, and reads as a layer between sole and last. Draw it flat along the inside of the sole.

## What I would change next, in order
1. Redraw the figure: finished shoe, open top with vamp and heel, block as a hollow cup with the cavity visible, lining and insole labelled, shank flat on the insole, last removed or shown in a second small panel. The page's best idea, done wrong, is worse than no figure.
2. Correct the three new errors (6, 7, 8) and the sole-before-turn order (5); they are each a sentence.
3. Add the specials paragraph (9) and the dancer's why (10), then the six-point "pick it up" list (11). After that I would hand it to an apprentice.

## Keep
Everything kept in round 1, plus: the quiz now placed before the section; "sweat softens flour paste"; Ray's "so they know who to blame", which is the truest line on the page; "Freed itself says ... visitors have reported"; "the price of a maker's hour" as the missing number; the strike notice told correctly (given, lifted, performances went ahead); the Grishko names, which make the thesis international in one sentence.
