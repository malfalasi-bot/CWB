The Repertory: ballet's whole history in its works

Beside the thirteen-chapter unit, which stays as it is: a timeline of the ballets and the events around them, deep dives on 118 works, and the debates, myths, design and art that run across four and a half centuries. This page is the plan, with a working first version of the timeline. Four decisions are yours at the end.

The line

156 works and 155 dated events in eleven eras, from the Italian dance masters to 2026. Filled dots are works researched in depth. Lines join a work to its remakes. Choose a thread to follow one idea across the whole history, select any dot or diamond to open its card, and scroll sideways for the later eras.

In short

  • The thirteen chapters stay. The Repertory is part two of the ballet topic: the chapters teach the concept, and the Repertory teaches the range.
  • The research is done for this pass. About 209,000 new words from ten research passes. There are deep dives on 82 more ballets, and the 35 already taken apart gained their disputes, facts and comparisons. Two passes ran across the whole history: one on readings, disputes and myths, one on design and art. A benchmark looked at how books, museums and platforms tell an art's history through its works.
  • Every work answers the same questions. How it came to be, where its ideas came from, the world it built, its design and art, how it was received, its versions, how it has been read and disputed, what to set it beside, and what a maker can take from it. Facts worth telling come last.
  • Comparisons and contradictions are content. The material includes 18 debates with named holders on each side, 38 myths checked against the record, and 13 long comparisons across periods. Each thread can be followed on the line.
  • It becomes the template for any Atlas concept with many works: a movement, a school, a design house.

How it fits beside the thirteen chapters

The benchmark weighed four designs. A second unit that tells the history again would compete with the chapters and fix a closed canon. A timeline inside the chapters alone leaves the deep dives with nowhere to live. A browsable explorer with no paths overloads newcomers. The fourth is a hybrid: one timeline, many cards, and a few guided routes. One of those routes is the fifty-work course that a second unit would have been.

Part one · the concept

Ballet as a made world

Thirteen history-first chapters, about 41 hours. Giselle is the worked example. They answer how ideas, bodies and things become a world, and how it is kept alive.

Part two · the range

The line, a card for every work, the debates, the myths, the design and art, and eight routes. They answer what was made, when, by whom, how it looked, how it was argued over, and what came after it.

Found in this pass

A sample of what the deep dives turned up. Each item carries its confidence word and the file it comes from. The first is the kind of correction the Repertory exists to teach.

  • The word "riot" was first attached to the 1913 Rite of Spring premiere in 1922. The police file for the night is missing, and the later Paris and London performances were calm. Contested: "uproar" is safer than "riot".X1 myths · Levitz 2017
  • Odile was not a black swan in 1895. The black, feathered Odile came from Moscow in 1920 and from New York in the 1940s, so the white-against-black poster image is a twentieth-century design choice.D7 · documented, checked against JSTOR Daily
  • The story that admirers in St Petersburg cooked and ate Marie Taglioni's shoes first appears in a London paper in 1855, thirteen years after the tour it describes.X1 myths · contested
  • In 1930 a labour-safety inspector asked for a dance in The Golden Age to be cut, because Galina Ulanova's pulse had reached 170 in rehearsal.D4 · documented, from the minutes
  • Two weeks before the premiere of Prokofiev's Romeo and Juliet in Leningrad, the orchestra passed a written resolution refusing to play it.D4 · documented, Mariinsky exhibition
  • The famous white-tutu photographs of Margot Fonteyn in Marguerite and Armand (1963) were publicity stills. The costume kept from the stage is black net with a dark velvet bodice.D5 · documented
  • In 2024 a court in Nanterre ruled that Ravel alone wrote Boléro. Music, choreography and décor are "three distinct works", so the designer's heirs could not extend the copyright.D2 · documented
  • Marc Chagall moved Aleko to Mexico City in 1942 partly because New York union rules would not let him paint his own backdrops.X2 · documented, LACMA
  • Ninette de Valois privately called the Turkish ballet Çeşmebaşı (1965) "my bastard Petrouchka", and did not share that model with her Turkish team.D8 · documented
  • The man who ran Soviet television programming in 1991 says he aired Swan Lake twice on the first day of the coup because nothing else of the right length was ready.X1 events · documented, checked against Rossiyskaya Gazeta, August 2026

The template for every Atlas concept with many works

The Atlas concept-unit template becomes v2.1. Any concept with many works (an art form, a movement, a school, a design house) gets a Repertory beside its chapters. That means a line of eras and lanes, a card for every work under the same fixed headings, the cross-history cards, and routes over them. The research it needs is a works register, one dossier per work, a pass across the history for readings and disputes, another for design and art, a benchmark, and a published rule for which works are in, shown with the estimated total.

The timeline's eras and lanes are reusable. An opera or a fashion topic would keep the five event lanes and relabel the works.

  • The research is not yet checked by a person. Research agents wrote it in one pass, with every claim marked documented, probable, contested or argued. Probable items, the date conflicts the passes logged, and every image licence need checking at source before a card reaches a learner. Three surprising claims were checked against independent sources and held.
  • The record contradicts itself, sometimes on a company's own pages. The deep dives log both readings rather than choosing one.
  • The register is fuller now. 137 sourced corrections from the deep dives were applied, 10 were settled by rule, and one (the dates of Chopiniana) is left for a person.
  • The canon leans west. The regional counterweight is still thin before 1940 and in the Gulf. The next candidates are Iran's Bijan and Manijeh (Tehran, 1975), India's Ramayana (1952–53), Indonesia's Prambanan Ramayana (1961) and Brazil's Uirapuru (1935). No company-made Gulf ballet on a Gulf subject was found.
  • Most media are link-only. The Met's Bakst designs, which earlier research assumed were free to use, are link-only until each record shows Open Access.
  • No study tests this design with adult design learners. The first build of the line and R0 should be tried with five learners.

Your decisions

Each has a default that stands unless you say otherwise. They are recorded in topics/ballet/planning/REPERTORY_PLAN.md.

1 · Where it sits

The Repertory as part two of the ballet topic, sharing one front door with the thirteen chapters, or a separate unit.

Default: part two of the topic.

2 · The fifty-work course

R1 as an optional route, or as a certificated sub-unit with say-backs at each era and an Apply task in each lane (about 3,000 more words).

Default: optional, because every boundary between parts loses learners.

3 · The eras

The eleven eras as named on the line, replacing the register's eight periods.

Default: adopt them.

4 · The template

Every Atlas concept with many works gets a Repertory of its own (template v2.1).

Default: adopt it; movements and schools are next.
